Figures, AUM and transformation

Use the actual diagrams rather than invent missing shapes.

What the book teaches and why

The book joins geometric images with questions about inside and outside, point and circle, material and spiritual relations, and involution and evolution. The figures are part of the exercise: OCR fragments cannot replace their lines and relationships.

AUM and the related past/present/future indications place sound and temporal relationship within an esoteric setting. The source does not make every geometrical shape a universal code to apply without study. Original source-page images are included for these drawings.

Read the relationship before assigning a meaning

Begin with what can be seen. In the lower pair on source page 181, the rays point outward in one drawing and inward in the other; the second also has a circular boundary. Direction and boundary are separate features to notice.

Steiner associates these figures with questions about inside and outside, above and below, and material and spiritual relations. The page does not give a complete key that makes every circle or arrow stand for one fixed spiritual meaning. Describe the relation first, then study the particular question.

If every arrowhead is reversed but the outline stays the same, is the relationship unchanged?

Read the explanation

No. The boundary is unchanged, but inward and outward movement have exchanged places. Naming only the circle misses that change. The observation does not, by itself, decide what a spiritual interpretation should be.

When this practice or study fits

Study when you want to understand image-based practice after learning connected thinking and the difference between preparation and meditation.

Preparation

Look at the source figure and its specific question together. Do not reconstruct it from broken OCR marks.

How to work with it

These steps are our teaching arrangement of the source method. Specific historical schedules retain their source context.

  1. Describe exactly what lines and relations the figure shows.
  2. Reconstruct the relation inwardly, then attend to the associated question.
  3. Keep source observation and your interpretation separate; use the actual page again when uncertain.

A worked example

When a circle is related to a point, consider how a boundary and a centre belong to one form rather than collecting unrelated meanings of circles.
