Start Now! — Meditation and Spiritual Exercises

A chapter-based teaching course through the whole anthology: 38 lessons in 17 book sections, with practices, their purposes and the contexts in which to choose them.

How to follow this book

Study the chapters in the book’s order, or use the practice directory to find a particular question. Keep one understood main meditation while learning about the others. Reading a later chapter does not require adopting its practice or certify a spiritual attainment.

  1. Begin with purpose, a short sitting and clear thinking.
  2. Develop the supporting qualities and a rhythm that fits your duties.
  3. Choose a daily, weekly, seasonal, symbolic or devotional practice after reading its preparation and source context.

Introduction by Christopher Bamford

A book of paths, not a single routine

Understand how to choose freely among the book’s practices.

When to use it: Begin here before selecting a meditation, and return when deciding whether to add or change a practice.

Prologue — Framing the Work

Yearning, the I and a path of knowledge

Read the framing passages as an orientation to the purpose of practice.

When to use it: Use these passages for study before a formal sitting or when your motivation has become unclear.

Meditation Instructions and Explanations

A short daily sitting

Learn how a sentence becomes a meditation.

When to use it: Choose this as a beginning main practice or return to it when a more elaborate routine becomes scattered.

Meditation Instructions and Explanations

The Wagner morning sequence and regularity

Distinguish a particular fifteen-minute instruction from the general short sitting.

When to use it: Consider this fuller sequence when you understand its spiritual language and can give it a regular place without displacing duties.

The Way of Thinking

Trust in thinking

Understand thought as an activity rather than only information.

When to use it: Use when preparing for word meditation or when you confuse reading many ideas with practising attention.

The Way of Thinking

Observation, concepts and freedom

Connect meditation with the book’s philosophical preparation.

When to use it: Use as study alongside meditation, particularly before the Rosicrucian chapter’s emphasis on independent thinking.

The Way of Reverence and Its Fruits

Reverence, inner peace and listening

Practise receptivity without abandoning discernment.

When to use it: Use in daily encounters and as preparation before a chosen meditation.

The Way of Reverence and Its Fruits

The seed and the mature plant

Work with visible form and a process not yet visible.

When to use it: Choose as a main nature-based meditation after learning quiet observation and the difference between imagination and perception.

Developing the Chakras, or Lotus Flowers

The sixteen-petalled lotus and eight soul processes

Understand the practical conduct behind the spiritual-organ language.

When to use it: Use as supporting everyday schooling when you can observe your conduct without trying to diagnose a chakra state.

Developing the Chakras, or Lotus Flowers

Heart qualities, deliberate attention and harmony

Relate the remaining lotus teachings to the exercises they actually name.

When to use it: Use to deepen supporting practice after understanding the eight processes and the six exercises.

The Six Essential Exercises

Six capacities alongside meditation

Follow the supporting exercises as a sustained education of character.

When to use it: Use alongside one main meditation, beginning gradually and preserving ordinary duties.

The Backward Review, or Retrospect

The evening backward review

Learn to reconstruct rather than merely replay the day.

When to use it: Use in the evening when you want to connect inner practice and learning from conduct.

Living the Year Spiritually

Five morning and evening meditations

Choose a paired daily verse rather than gather every formula into one sitting.

When to use it: Choose a pair for a regular daily rhythm when its spiritual language is understood. The chapter does not require all five pairs every day.

Living the Year Spiritually

Six months, eight processes and seven weeks

Distinguish the chapter’s different schedules.

When to use it: Use a schedule when its actions are understood and its rhythm fits your life. Choose the programme explicitly rather than superimpose all of them.

Living the Year Spiritually

The seven planetary moods

Understand a complete weekday cycle in its original timing.

When to use it: Consider when you want sustained devotional study of the sevenfold sequence and can understand the hierarchy and planetary language.

Living the Year Spiritually

Twelve moods and monthly virtues

Distinguish two different twelvefold practices.

When to use it: Study the moods when the cosmic-poetic framework is meaningful; choose a virtue for daily ethical work when a concrete quality needs attention.

Living the Year Spiritually

The Calendar: a whole year of weekly verses

Continue beyond the four introductory examples.

When to use it: Choose as a continuing main rhythm when you want to live with one verse through a week and understand the festival and seasonal setting.

Living the Year Spiritually

Christmas, solstice and Isis-Sophia

Read festival verses with their own spiritual setting.

When to use it: Use for festival study or an understood devotional focus when its context matters to your question.

Meditations from the Esoteric School

Living sentences and the higher Self

Understand the difference between a spiritual formula and self-assertion.

When to use it: Choose a living sentence for sustained study when you can keep its whole relationship in view. Use the higher-Self formula only with its spiritual meaning understood.

Meditations from the Esoteric School

Figures, AUM and transformation

Use the actual diagrams rather than invent missing shapes.

When to use it: Study when you want to understand image-based practice after learning connected thinking and the difference between preparation and meditation.

Meditations from the Esoteric School

I AM, short themes and the protective image

Distinguish bodily references, spiritual images and ordinary conduct.

When to use it: Study the bodily-reference sequence carefully before choosing it; use a simple understood theme if that is a better present fit.

Meditations from the Esoteric School

Wisdom in light; thinking, feeling and willing

Learn the feeling that accompanies a mantra.

When to use it: Choose when a compact phrase can remain clear and meaningful over time; study the whole triad before using one line.

Meditations from the Esoteric School

Body and spirit, human work and cosmic work

Read the larger mantras as structured transformations.

When to use it: Use for extended spiritual study and consider a whole understood section for practice only after its relationships are clear.

Meditations from the Esoteric School

The three-part mantra, sound and butterfly

Keep advanced symbolic sequences intelligible and distinct.

When to use it: Study after the earlier work on clear thinking and meaningful imagery. Choose a sequence only when you can explain its symbolic movement.

The Rosicrucian Path

Study and independence in the Rosicrucian path

Understand why thinking precedes symbolic practice.

When to use it: Use before selecting Rosicrucian symbols or when you need to examine the relationship between guidance and freedom.

The Rosicrucian Path

The Rose Cross and its morning–evening forms

Build the symbol before working with it.

When to use it: Choose after understanding symbolic preparation. The variants are alternatives for considered study, not compulsory additions to the main symbol.

The Rosicrucian Path

The signature, philosophical fire and water

Read alchemical images as spiritual transformations.

When to use it: Use for informed symbolic and Christian-Rosicrucian study after the earlier work on thinking and imagery.

The Christian-Gnostic Path

John’s opening and the seven Christian stages

Understand a specifically Christian contemplative path.

When to use it: Choose devotional practice when the Christ and Gospel framework is meaningful; otherwise study the chapter as an account of this path.

The Christian-Gnostic Path

Christian morning and evening programmes

Follow the relationships within a particular historical programme.

When to use it: Consider when you already understand the component practices and want a coherent Christian daily rhythm.

The Christian-Gnostic Path

The Our Father versions and inward renewal

Read prayer with its particular theology and purpose.

When to use it: Use an understood prayer as devotional practice when its theological setting is meaningful. Study the variants comparatively before changing between them.

Working with the Dead

Reading to and remembering the dead

Understand the proposed spiritual relationship and its preparation.

When to use it: Consider for voluntary remembrance or devotional practice when the source’s framework is meaningful. You can also study it without undertaking communication practices.

Working with the Dead

Verses of love, warmth and continuing connection

Choose a remembrance verse with its original circumstances visible.

When to use it: Choose a verse when its relation and language fit a voluntary act of remembrance; it is not an obligation to repeat all the collected texts.

The Foundation Stone Meditation

The Foundation Stone as an integrating meditation

Relate the whole meditation to the book’s paths.

When to use it: Consider after understanding the basic method, the I/soul/spirit language and the Christian setting.

Meeting the Guardians

The Lesser Guardian: responsibility made visible

Read the threshold account as a demand for self-knowledge.

When to use it: Study after the ethical and cognitive groundwork; it can clarify why character preparation matters.

Meeting the Guardians

The Greater Guardian and the refusal of isolated perfection

Understand why the path returns to service.

When to use it: Use as a later study of the motive for the whole course and as a review of why you practise.

Appendices

The oldest programme of German idealism

Understand the appendix’s historical voice.

When to use it: Use as contextual study when you want to understand the anthology’s meeting of philosophy and spiritual practice.

Appendices

Imagination, Inspiration and Intuition

Understand the spiritual horizon without confusing it with ordinary impressions.

When to use it: Use to understand the purposes claimed for the practices and to review your interpretations of experience.

Lesson explanations, examples, questions and teaching arrangements are original. Selected English practice texts and source-page images come from the supplied anthology; Portuguese practice passages are new study translations. Readers identify exact book sections and retain historical instructions as historical instructions.

Source readers include the longer meditation sequences, Calendar verses, prayers and historical figures. The source images preserve difficult layouts; searchable OCR transcriptions are supplied beside them with their limitations stated. The full uploaded PDF and audit files are not published.
