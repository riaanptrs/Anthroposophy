Follow reasons into action

Course exercise

Connect a small purpose with its reasons, practical steps and room for correction.

Optional course suggestion: three minutes to consider a small action and a brief review afterward.

  1. Choose a manageable action, such as organizing a shared space or returning something borrowed.
  2. State why it is worth doing. Distinguish that reason from the praise or satisfaction you might receive.
  3. Check whether the proposed steps actually serve the purpose, and notice any missing information.
  4. If you choose to act, review what happened. Keep, adjust or abandon the plan according to its reasons and the situation.

What guided the action: its recognized purpose, a passing preference, or a mixture you can now distinguish?

Reveal course notes

Organizing a shared table can serve the recognized purpose of making it usable for everyone. Wanting praise for being helpful is a personal preference that may accompany the action. The two are not identical: the purpose can remain worthwhile even if no one notices, while praise alone does not establish that your chosen arrangement is useful.

If someone explains that they need materials you planned to put away, a revised arrangement may serve the purpose better. Thinking examines the reason and changes the sequence; willing carries the revised plan into action. Persistence then means remaining faithful to the purpose, rather than protecting the first plan simply because it was yours.

Chapter IV links coherent thinking with willing guided by a recognized good instead of private reward alone. This ordinary planning and review activity is a course adaptation, not the complete discipline of spiritual investigation.
