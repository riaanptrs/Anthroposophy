Notice a memory and a retained skill

Course exercise

Distinguish a recollection of learning from the capacity that remains.

Optional course suggestion: two to five minutes, or simply notice the distinction during an ordinary activity.

  1. Choose a familiar learned activity, such as reading a short sentence or tying a knot.
  2. Notice what you can do now. Then recall, if a memory is available, one occasion when you were learning it.
  3. Compare the present ability with the remembered scene. Notice what can remain even when details are missing.
  4. You may reflect silently or make a private note; stop when the distinction is clear enough for you.

Which part is a remembered event, and which part is a present capacity?

Reveal course notes

Remembering how a teacher showed you a knot gives you a scene from the past. Tying the knot now is a usable capacity: you can carry out the sequence even if the teacher’s words, the place and the first attempts have faded. The recalled event and the present skill answer different questions about what remains from learning.

This is the source’s distinction between memory and fruits of experience. The comparison concerns the transformation of an accessible learning history into capacity; it does not identify a former incarnation as the origin of a skill.

The course adapts Chapter II’s distinction between memories and the lasting fruits of learning. The chosen activity and brief timing are course arrangements, not a prescribed spiritual exercise.
