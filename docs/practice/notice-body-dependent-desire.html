Notice a body-dependent desire

Course exercise

Clarify the difference between fulfillment requiring a particular bodily encounter and a capacity that can be carried into another situation.

About 3–5 minutes; stop after one useful distinction.

  1. Choose a neutral pleasant experience, such as hearing music, making something by hand or sharing a meal. You may use an imagined example.
  2. Identify what would be required to repeat its bodily pleasure: an organ of sense, an activity and a present encounter.
  3. Identify something that might be learned through the experience and used elsewhere, such as attentiveness, skill, patience or consideration.
  4. Write two short statements: one describing the wish to repeat the encounter, the other describing the capacity that can be used in a different encounter.

How does this distinction help you understand Steiner's contrast between bodily-dependent attachment and a retained fruit of experience?

Reveal course notes

Repeating the pleasure of a melody or meal requires another bodily encounter with its particular sounds or flavors. The attention, skill or consideration developed through that encounter can become useful while learning a different subject, making something else or caring for another person. The first statement seeks renewed enjoyment; the second identifies a capacity available beyond its original occasion. This provides a limited everyday comparison for Steiner's distinction, not evidence about life after death.

The exercise examines an ordinary distinction; it does not reproduce or verify an after-death process. There is no need to deprive yourself of a pleasure, delay food or disregard bodily needs.

Use a neutral example rather than a personal bereavement if that is more comfortable. Nothing in the exercise establishes the spiritual condition of a deceased person.

Course adaptation of Steiner's distinction between bodily-oriented desires and the extract of experience that can accompany the spirit in Chapter III. It is not a source-prescribed preparation for death.
