Sensation, response and action

Course exercise

Distinguish an experienced sensation, its personal significance and the action that may express an impulse.

A short pause after an ordinary encounter, as a course suggestion; no diary or repeated schedule is required.

  1. Recall a recent ordinary sound, smell or sight, or use the fictional bakery encounter from the lesson.
  2. Name the sensation and then the feeling or desire related to it. Do not treat them as the same thing.
  3. Identify an action that followed, or that could have followed. Consider whether a thought changed the response.
  4. If considering another person, separate what they told you from your interpretation. Leave their unreported inner experience open.

How did the encounter become personally significant, and why was its expression in action not inevitable?

Reveal course notes

The source relates sensation to feeling and outward willing. A desire can be experienced without immediately becoming an action.

Naming your own response clarifies the concept. It does not classify a temperament or disclose another person’s spiritual constitution.

This is a course arrangement of the source’s distinction between sensation, personal response and willing. It is ordinary self-observation, not a prescribed supersensible exercise.
