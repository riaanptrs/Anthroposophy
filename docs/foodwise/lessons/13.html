Foodwise · Lesson 13 · Understanding nourishment

Why is digestion a transformation rather than a transfer?

A slice of bread does not become an unchanged slice inside a person. Chewing and successive digestive processes change its form and relationships.

Cook’s account · attributed paraphrase

Cook follows food through chewing, digestive juices, intestinal processing and absorption. Alongside these physiological descriptions she interprets incorporation as overcoming the food’s foreign organization and making it appropriate to the individual. The expressions anti-chemistry and anti-physics identify the spiritual explanation she draws from Steiner; they should not be read as a kitchen experiment disproving physical laws. Cook also acknowledges the limits of her own understanding. The central idea is active assimilation: nourishment depends on what the living person does with food, not only on what the food contains.

Connect it with everyday food

Course interpretation and expansion

Cooking already begins some transformations outside the body. This connects the kitchen with the book’s discussion of inward work.

Original course activity
