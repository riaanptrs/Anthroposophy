Foodwise · Lesson 19 · Meet the grains

Where are maize flowers and seeds?

Maize makes its flowering structures unusually easy to distinguish.

Cook’s account · attributed paraphrase

Cook places maize in its American cultural setting and notices its contrasting upper and lower structures. The tassel at the top bears male flowers; the developing ears lower on the stem bear the structures that produce kernels. Protective leaves surround the ear. The familiar cob thus makes more sense when viewed as part of the whole plant. Maize also reminds us that a crop travels with knowledge: selection, preparation and uses developed in Indigenous cultures before global adoption. A modern supermarket product reveals only a fraction of that relationship. Cook’s attention to form invites readers to reconnect a prepared food with the growing organism and the people who cultivated it.
