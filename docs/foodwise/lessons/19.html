Foodwise · Lesson 19 · Meet the grains

Where are maize flowers and seeds?

Maize makes its flowering structures unusually easy to distinguish.

Cook’s account · attributed paraphrase

Cook places maize in its American cultural setting and notices its contrasting upper and lower structures. The tassel at the top bears male flowers; the developing ears lower on the stem bear the structures that produce kernels. Protective leaves surround the ear. The familiar cob thus makes more sense when viewed as part of the whole plant. Maize also reminds us that a crop travels with knowledge: selection, preparation and uses developed in Indigenous cultures before global adoption. A modern supermarket product reveals only a fraction of that relationship. Cook’s attention to form invites readers to reconnect a prepared food with the growing organism and the people who cultivated it.

Find the part before interpreting it

The tassel is at the top of the stem and bears the male flowers. Ears develop lower on the stem; kernels are borne on the ear. These labels let you reconnect loose kernels with the whole plant. The diagram shows relative positions, not a botanical scale drawing.

Cook moves from visible plant form to further correspondences, including a Saturn association. The position of a tassel can be inspected; that position alone does not demonstrate the correspondence. Keep those two statements separately labelled.

A bag of kernels is shown without a plant. A learner labels each kernel “tassel” and says its shape proves Cook’s planetary association. What should be revised?

Choose a response, then open its explanation.

A. Only the spelling: kernels and tassels are the same structure.

Reconsider this inference. A kernel and a tassel are different parts. Recovering the whole plant corrects the relationship, not merely a word.

B. Identify kernels with the ear, locate the tassel separately, and treat the planetary association as Cook’s interpretation.

Supported conclusion. Yes. The label correction concerns visible structure. The additional correspondence needs separate consideration; a shape label does not establish it.

C. The diagram proves every statement in Cook’s maize discussion.

Reconsider this inference. A schematic selects a few relationships. It neither verifies every historical statement nor establishes an interpretive cause.
