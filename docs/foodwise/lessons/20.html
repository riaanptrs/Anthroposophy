Foodwise · Lesson 20 · Meet the grains

Why distinguish millet from sorghum?

Cook groups these grains in her sevenfold picture, but a grouping is not a botanical identity.

Cook’s account · attributed paraphrase

Millet names several small-seeded cereal grasses; sorghum is a distinct cereal. Cook’s arrangement combines them within one of seven grain groups. This is useful for understanding her symbolic scheme, but it should not erase practical differences between plants or packets. Grain size, variety and processing affect water absorption and cooking time. The lesson is to move carefully between a broad cultural grouping and the particular ingredient in hand. A weekly menu can use either without assuming identical behaviour in the saucepan. Read the packet, observe tenderness, and choose a preparation appropriate to that ingredient rather than deriving a recipe from a symbolic association.

Millet

Plant part: Small seeds of several cereal grasses

Cook’s account: Cook groups millet and sorghum in her sevenfold grain arrangement.

Kitchen application: Identify the exact product; grouping does not imply identical cooking times.

Sorghum

Plant part: Seed; cereal grass distinct from millet

Cook’s account: Sorghum shares a symbolic group with millet in Cook’s arrangement.

Kitchen application: Observe whole grain versus flour; use a preparation appropriate to the form.
