Foodwise · Lesson 25 · Meet the plant on your plate

Can one plant family feed us different parts?

A turnip, cabbage and broccoli challenge the idea that all vegetables are equivalent shapes.

Cook’s account · attributed paraphrase

Cook uses plant families to sharpen observation. Within the brassicas, edible structures include roots, leaves, enlarged stems, buds and flowering structures. Kohlrabi is an enlarged stem; cabbage offers leaves; broccoli and cauliflower involve developing flowering structures. The distinctions show why the word vegetable hides many forms. Families can share related growth patterns while presenting different edible organs. Do not let the threefold scheme override what the specimen actually is: a stem is not a root because it looks rounded. Understanding the part helps a cook anticipate texture, cleaning and cutting. The relationship between form and preparation is practical before it becomes symbolic.

Cook’s account: Leaf form supports Cook’s middle-region plant picture.

Kitchen application: Use familiar culinary greens; thick stems can need longer cooking than delicate leaves.
