Foodwise · Lesson 09 · From living soil to food

What does it mean to feed the soil?

Cook’s childhood compost heap changed scraps into material that could support another season’s growth. Waste and nourishment were not separate worlds.

Cook’s account · attributed paraphrase

Cook’s agricultural argument begins with relationships: roots, organic matter, water and organisms form a living context for growth. In her account, humus does more than provide isolated ingredients; it helps the soil hold and organize what plants need. The discussion of mycorrhizal fungi gives a concrete example of exchange: the plant supplies sugars while fungal networks help it access resources. She extends this biological picture into a biodynamic understanding of vitality. Distinguish the observable soil relationship from the spiritual interpretation built around it. Both have roles in the book, but they answer different questions.

Connect it with everyday food

Course interpretation and expansion

A vegetable’s story includes the soil that supported it, not only the fertilizer named on a bag.

An optional observation

Original course activity

Observe purchased garden compost or a maintained compost system without handling unknown waste. Notice texture and how it differs from dry mineral soil.

Check your understanding

Why does Cook resist viewing soil as a container for chemical inputs?

Reveal answer and explanation

Because she emphasizes interactions, structure and ongoing transformation that support the plant, rather than an ingredient list alone.
