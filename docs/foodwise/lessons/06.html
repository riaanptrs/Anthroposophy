Foodwise · Lesson 06 · Food has a story

What do banquets and monasteries reveal about food?

A display of extravagant dishes and a garden worked through the seasons represent different ways of organizing a meal.

Cook’s account · attributed paraphrase

Cook contrasts Roman luxury with simpler diets and later turns to monasteries as places preserving gardening, baking, brewing and learning. Her examples show that cuisine reflects institutions and values, not just available ingredients. A feast can display power; a shared work rhythm can sustain skills and community. Her account of Rome is a selected cultural portrait, not a description of every Roman household. Read the contrast as a question about priorities: what is the meal for, who does the work, and what knowledge is passed on?

Course interpretation and expansion

A family celebration can be generous without making abundance a competition. Passing on a technique may matter as much as serving a spectacular dish.
