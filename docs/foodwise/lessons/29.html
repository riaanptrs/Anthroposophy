Foodwise · Lesson 29 · Foods that transform a meal

Where do different sweeteners come from?

Nectar, cane stems, beet roots and tree sap enter different routes to sweetness.

Cook’s account · attributed paraphrase

Cook begins carbohydrates with plants and their transformation of light, then considers starch, structural material and sugars. Sweeteners reveal different plant parts and processing: cane sugar comes from stems, beet sugar from roots, maple syrup from concentrated sap, and honey involves bees collecting nectar. These origins are not interchangeable stories. Concentration, refining and flavour also affect a recipe. Cook’s preferences for particular sweeteners belong to her culinary and interpretive approach; they do not establish that every less-refined sugar is a health treatment. In this course, sweetness is considered through origin, taste and function in food. No infant-feeding instructions are drawn from the book.
