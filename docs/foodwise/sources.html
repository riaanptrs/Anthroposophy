Sources, chapter map and limits

The book being studied

Wendy E. Cook, Foodwise: Understanding What We Eat and How It Affects Us — The Story of Human Nutrition.

The supplied OCR copyright page reports a first publication in 2003 and an edition in 2009, Clairview Books in association with Temple Lodge, ISBN 9781905570584. These metadata have not been checked against the PDF.

What this edition establishes

This edition uses readable passages from the supplied OCR. The PDF was unavailable for visual checking; omitted columns, figures, quantities and uncertain wording are not treated as verified.

The source has 272 ordered OCR page captures. This course explains selected readable passages and represents all twenty chapters plus the opening. It is not a claim that every full page or every column has been read and verified. Page numbers throughout are PDF-capture locators inferred from the OCR, not verified printed book pagination. Chapter boundaries below are likewise provisional OCR-derived boundaries.

What remains uncertain

The attached PDF could not be transferred because it exceeds the tool’s 32 MiB limit. No Foodwise PDF page was visually inspected. Damaged menus on capture 228, inconsistent recipe quantities (including a pound-to-kilogram mismatch on 235), uncertain temperature wording in the bread chapter, illustrations and omitted columns remain unverified. No book quotations, original illustrations or reconstructed book recipes are published here. A future accessible PDF can support a fuller page and column audit.

How to read the teaching

“Cook’s account” means an attributed paraphrase of readable OCR, not a direct quotation. “Course interpretation and expansion” connects that account with an original explanation. Activities and recipe cards are original course material; every recipe is untested. Uncertain source material is identified here and is not silently completed. Cook’s views are attributed to her; descriptions she connects with Steiner are not presented as direct Steiner quotations. Spiritual correspondences are taught as interpretations, not demonstrated physiological mechanisms.

Current guidance for practical activities

The course does not adopt the book’s disease-treatment, supplement, fasting or infant-feeding claims as advice. The OCR’s statements about B12 from algae, miso or the colon are excluded as nutrition instructions. A vegan diet needs reliable B12 provision; use qualified current guidance. Botanical clarifications distinguish millet from sorghum, identify wild rice as another grass genus, and identify saffron’s culinary part as stigmas rather than stamens.

  • NHS · The vegan diet

    Reliable B12 provision includes appropriately fortified foods and, where needed, supplements; general guidance does not replace individual care.

  • FDA · Safe Food Handling

    Handwashing, produce washing, prompt chilling, shallow containers and reheating temperatures. The 24-hour leftover limit is a conservative course choice, not the FDA’s general shelf-life rule.

  • NIDDK · Celiac disease: eating, diet & nutrition

    Wheat, barley and rye contain gluten; cross-contact and oat suitability require attention.

  • CDC · Raw milk

    Kitchen suggestions use pasteurised dairy, rather than raw-milk recommendations.

  • CDC · Botulism prevention

    Do not give honey to children younger than 12 months. This course excludes infant-feeding recipes and the book’s infant advice.

  • FDA · Microwave ovens

    The course does not turn Cook’s appliance preferences into claims that correctly used microwaves cause cancer.

Public guidance consulted 5 October 2026. Original activities use metric quantities and estimates; practical safety notes are editorial additions, not verified book instructions.
