Theosophy

What is a human being, and how does a human life belong to a wider world?

Understanding the human being and the spiritual worlds

Learn the principal ideas of Rudolf Steiner's Theosophy through connected questions, clear explanations and ordinary experience. Begin with body, soul and spirit, then follow the relationships between individuality, destiny, spiritual existence and knowledge.

Begin with the first questionContinue studying

What this course develops

The course helps you build a coherent picture of the book. Each lesson develops a concept, distinguishes it from neighboring ideas and connects it with the whole. References let you return to Steiner's own argument whenever you want.

Who it is for

Newcomers can follow the explanations without prior knowledge of Anthroposophy. Readers who already know the book can use the concept connections and expandable deeper study to examine its distinctions more closely.

What you can learn

  • Explain body, soul and spirit and the different meanings of physical, etheric and astral body.
  • Distinguish the I, the three soul functions and the spiritual transformation of the human being.
  • Explain Steiner's relationships between memory, individuality, reincarnation, heredity and karma.
  • Compare the soul world with Spirit-land and understand the account of life between death and rebirth.
  • Distinguish understanding, agreement and firsthand spiritual knowledge, and practise appropriate forms of attention and thought.

How to study

You do not need to read the original book alongside this course. Follow the modules in order for a first introduction, or return to a concept when you want to review it. Try explaining the main distinction before revealing the course notes.

Observe identifies ordinary experience. Steiner's account identifies his interpretation and spiritual-scientific claims. Try it in life offers an optional exercise related to the idea. You can reflect aloud or in your own notes; no score, account or declaration of belief is required.

Mark a lesson Exploring, Studied or Reviewed if that helps your study. Saving is optional and stays in your browser. Earlier reading notes remain available in the separate reading commentary.
