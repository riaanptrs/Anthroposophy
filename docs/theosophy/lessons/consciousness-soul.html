Bringing the whole picture together

Observe

Begin with experience

You are pleased with a calculation until someone points out an error. Disappointment is real, yet it does not make the old answer correct. Acknowledging the correction distinguishes your response to a result from the reasons that support it.

Steiner’s account

The central idea

Steiner calls the soul’s bearing of truth and goodness with significance independent of personal liking consciousness-soul. It is a particular function of the soul, not merely being awake or aware.

Useful terminology

Consciousness-soul
The member of soul life in which truth and goodness become alive with significance independent of personal liking or aversion.
Intellectual or mind soul
The differentiated soul member in which thinking permeates sensation and desire, giving personal life a reasoned organization.
Spirit-self
Spiritual content received and individualized through the I, and described in the transformation account as the spiritualized astral aspect.

Building the concept

A thought can delight us, disappoint us or leave us indifferent. Those responses belong to our inward life. Whether its content is true requires a different criterion. Steiner argues that truth concerns the thing understood and retains its significance beyond the pleasure of the person thinking it. Changing a mistaken conception does not mean that truth itself was created by our earlier satisfaction or destroyed by our disappointment.

He applies a related distinction to goodness. A reason to keep a promise or treat another fairly may oppose a present inclination. In his account, the morally good has significance that is not commanded by liking or aversion. This does not make any opinion called duty automatically right. A person still needs to understand what is being recognized. The ordinary example illustrates a question about motives and justification; Steiner’s claim about the eternal significance of truth and goodness goes further.

Consciousness-soul names this spiritual significance becoming alive within the soul. It does not name all consciousness: Steiner explicitly includes ordinary sensation and animal consciousness in a broader use of that word. Nor is the function a demand to eliminate feeling. A person can feel joy in a correct understanding, and an inclination can become consonant with a recognized good. The important distinction is that feeling does not create the criterion of truth or goodness.

Consciousness-soul and spirit-self are closely related but have different explanatory roles. The first bears truth with significance independent of sympathy and antipathy. The second concerns the same spiritual content received and individualized through the I. Individualization means becoming the spiritual life of this person, not changing truth into a private opinion. Steiner joins the two in one sevenfold grouping while preserving their distinction in the finer account. The transformation lesson will show why both descriptions are useful.

Keep this distinction

Consciousness-soul is not ordinary consciousness, emotional numbness or certainty that one is right. Its defining relationship is to truth and goodness beyond personal preference.

Try it in life

Reasons and preferences

Course exercise

Compare thought organizing a desired outcome with thought examining whether a judgment or aim is justified.

About five to ten minutes, as a flexible course suggestion; use a small decision or a fictional case.

  1. Choose a modest planning question, such as a travel route, or use the friend-visit example. Name the desired result.
  2. Give a reasoned plan for reaching it. Distinguish the facts you know from assumptions or convenient guesses.
  3. Ask what could justify revising your plan or aim, even if you dislike the revision. Consider one possible correction.
  4. Explain the difference between wanting the outcome, organizing its means and accepting a sound reason. You may reflect without writing.

Where did thought serve a preference, and where did it let a reason challenge that preference?

Reveal course notes

Organizing means is a real use of thought. It does not by itself establish the truth of every assumption or the goodness of the aim.

Accepting a correction illustrates the independence of a reason from liking. The activity does not certify spiritual rank or make every claimed duty correct.

The activity is our adaptation of Steiner’s transition from thinking in the service of personal life to truth and goodness beyond preference. Its steps and timing are not a historical exercise prescription.

Open this practice on its own

Reflect in your own words

What remains different between feeling certain, understanding a reason and being correct?

Reveal course notes

Certainty is an experienced state. Understanding follows a reason. Correctness concerns whether the reason and judgment hold. The three can coincide, but one does not guarantee the others.

The source’s consciousness-soul concerns truth becoming alive in soul life, not a license to declare personal conviction spiritually authoritative.

What to remember

  • Consciousness-soul has a more specific meaning than ordinary awareness.
  • A response to a claim differs from the reasons for accepting it.
  • Steiner attributes independent significance to truth and goodness.
  • Feeling can accompany understanding without determining validity.
  • Consciousness-soul and spirit-self relate to the same truth in different ways.

Optional deeper study

The three soul functions together

An unpleasant message can evoke a response, provoke a plan and lead to reconsideration in light of a sound reason. Sentient-soul, intellectual-soul and consciousness-soul distinguish these functions without dividing the person. Steiner describes them as interpenetrating; the distinctions are not rigid personality types, anatomical layers or a timetable by which to judge another person.
