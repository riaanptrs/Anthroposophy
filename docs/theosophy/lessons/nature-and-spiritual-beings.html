The spiritual worlds

Nature and spiritual beings

The Question

What are we describing when we describe a living form or a shared activity?

Begin with Experience

A seedling forms leaves, and several plants develop recognizable patterns. We can describe those forms and investigate their ordinary causes. Steiner asks a further question about their spiritual origin.

Steiner’s Idea

Steiner explains nature’s formation through spiritual beings and connects human understanding with that origin. His three Elementary Kingdoms are classes of proposed formative beings, followed in the list by mineral, plant, animal and human kingdoms. Elementary here names those classes. Elemental beings, such as the gnomes and undines mentioned later, are another term in the account; the book does not supply a one-to-one assignment of those names to the three kingdoms.

The account distinguishes physical form, living formation, sensation and thought through the I. It does not simply deny thought in animals. Note 3 of the course’s source describes animal thought through a group ego: a spiritual center belonging to a group rather than an individual animal’s human-like I. This is Steiner’s proposed explanation of its mode of thinking, not a finding established by observing animal behavior.

Steiner further describes Folk Spirits, the Spirit of the Age and elemental beings as independent beings. Shared customs or a plant's growth do not establish their existence. Nor should the account become a fixed character or hierarchy assigned to peoples. Keep the observed event, an ordinary explanation and the proposed spiritual explanation distinct.

Important Distinction

An observed form, an inference about its ordinary causes and Steiner’s account of an independently active spiritual being are different kinds of statement.

Apply It

If a group sings the same song, what is publicly observable, and what further claim does Steiner make when he speaks of a Folk Spirit?

Consider an approach

We can observe people singing, words, rhythm and shared participation. Ordinary explanations may concern learned habits and social relationships.

Steiner additionally asserts an independent being whose activity manifests in souls but does not originate in their aggregation. The shared song can illustrate the question, but cannot by itself establish that being.

Optional: try the related practice

Check Your Understanding

Does an orderly plant establish a spiritual being as its cause?

Explore the reasoning

No. It supplies an observable form and process. Steiner's proposed spiritual cause is a further explanation, not contained in the observation alone.

Are the seven kingdoms the same as the seven soul regions?

Explore the reasoning

No. The lists answer different questions. One concerns beings and manifestations; the other concerns soul affinities and activities.

Points to revisit

  • Form, formative life, sensation and independent human thought have different roles.
  • The three Elementary Kingdoms differ from the four physically manifest kingdoms.
  • The seven-kingdom account should not be merged with either seven-region account.
  • Folk Spirit and Spirit of the Age are independent beings in the source, rather than products of aggregated human psychology.
  • Different beings are described with physical, soul-world or purely spiritual embodiment.
  • Ordinary observation should be distinguished from interpretation and from the source’s spiritual claims.

Source questions and qualifications

Why the plant and crystal comparison matters

The source contrasts a completed crystal shape with a plant that retains its shaping activity through growth and reproduction. This comparison develops the distinction between a formed object and formative life. Steiner attributes that continuing activity to spiritual formative beings; a record of plant growth can clarify the distinction without establishing the beings he describes.

Animal thought and the human I

Note 3 in the supplied 1910 edition qualifies the contrast in the chapter. Thought is woven through animal experience, according to Steiner, while the group ego acts from the soul world. Human thoughts acquire independent existence in the I. This attributed account should not be substituted for a claim about what contemporary animal-cognition research has or has not found.

Elementary Kingdoms and elemental names

The three Elementary Kingdoms organize the account of primal archetypal, formative and soul beings. The later names salamanders, sylphs, undines and gnomes occur in a discussion of beings without physical bodies. The passage does not assign those four names one by one to the three kingdoms or give them ordinary physical appearances; a diagram that did so would add a scheme not taught here.
