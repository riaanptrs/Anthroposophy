Bringing the whole picture together

Observe

Begin with experience

Two people look at a blue cup. Their eyes are involved in the encounter, and each has an experience of blue. Describing the eye’s activity and describing what blue is like for the person are related tasks, but they are not the same description.

Steiner’s account

The central idea

Steiner distinguishes the sentient body, which mediates bodily sentience, from the sentient soul, which inwardly experiences sensation. In his fourfold constitution, astral body names these two members considered together.

Useful terminology

Sentient body
The member through which bodily organization mediates the sentient soul’s activity in Steiner’s account.
Astral body
In Chapter I’s fourfold constitution, sentient body and sentient soul considered together as one member.
Sentient soul
The inward activity of sensation and related pleasures, aversions, desires, impulses and passions in Steiner’s account.

Building the concept

Begin with the path from an object to a sensory organ. Light reaches the eye and bodily processes occur. Steiner’s argument is that tracing those processes gives further bodily processes, rather than the inwardly experienced blue as such. The living organization also belongs to this bodily side in his account. He introduces sentient soul for the activity through which an inward experience comes about. The distinction is between the conditions of experience and experience as lived.

Those sides must nevertheless relate. The kind and condition of the sensory organization affect what can be experienced. Steiner names the mediating member soul-body or sentient body. It belongs on the bodily side of the relationship, while the sentient soul concerns inward sensation, desire and impulse. Calling both sentient does not make them synonyms. The shared word identifies their close connection; the different nouns preserve the distinction between an organization and the inward activity related to it.

Astral body is introduced in the chapter’s compact fourfold picture: physical body, life body, astral body and I. Here the astral member joins sentient body and sentient soul. The compact view helps relate physical existence, living organization, sentience and selfhood without listing every later distinction. It does not mean that astral body is the whole soul, the I or a personality type. Nor does astral mean an astronomical body; the term belongs to Steiner’s constitution vocabulary.

A person can know their own sensation ordinarily and hear another person describe theirs. Steiner goes further by claiming that a developed spiritual organ can perceive another’s sensation-world. That is a separate claimed mode of access, not an ability established by observing expressions or confidently interpreting a mood. The ordinary example helps us understand the difference between bodily mediation and inward experience. It provides no warrant for diagnosing someone’s astral constitution.

Keep this distinction

Sentient body and sentient soul are related members, while astral body groups them in this particular account. Changing the scale of the description does not erase the finer distinction.

    The fourfold account distinguishes the I from the astral member.

Reflect in your own words

Why can astral body include two members without making sentient body and sentient soul identical?

Reveal course notes

The sentient body concerns mediation through bodily organization; the sentient soul concerns inward experience. The fourfold account treats their close relationship as one member.

A grouping describes a relationship at a broader scale. It does not supply evidence of another person’s inner life.

What to remember

  • The sensory event and its inward experience should be distinguished.
  • Sentient body is also called soul-body.
  • Astral body here joins sentient body and sentient soul.
  • The fourfold view keeps the I distinct.
  • Experiencing your own sensation differs from receiving another person’s account of theirs.

Optional deeper study

Why terms change with the account

Chapter I first differentiates nine members, then joins two pairs for one sevenfold presentation. The sentient body/sentient soul pair is one of those joins. It later uses the same pair as the astral member in the fourfold account. Read the function of the grouping before transferring a term from another work or school of terminology.
