Six preparations: differentiated roles and qualified claims

The preparations are not introduced as six interchangeable nutrients. Steiner assigns each plant a relationship among substances, organic processes, animal structures and the year's influences. Here those procedures are historical content for reading, rather than a practical preparation exercise.

Course explanation

What the author means

Yarrow relates sulfur to potash and is enclosed in a male red-deer bladder; summer and winter exposures enter its cosmic explanation. Chamomile relates sulfur to calcium as well as potash, and cattle intestines receive significance. The contrasting enclosures are integral to the reasoning, not packaging details. He permits possible plant substitutes, though prefers the named plants.

Stinging nettle is the exception he declares irreplaceable. It involves sulfur, potash, calcium and iron and is described without an animal enclosure. Its addition supposedly restrains wrong decomposition and nitrogen loss. The selected sentence calls manure and soil intelligent: here the claim concerns responding to particular plants' needs, not conscious human thought. Large swollen produce is contrasted with true nutritive content.

Oak bark supplies organic calcium, described as moderating excessive etheric activity and permitting astral influence. Its historical enclosure is a domestic-animal skull in a wet, decaying environment. Dandelion in bovine mesentery is said to mediate cosmic silica and potassium, sensitizing plants even to neighboring areas. Valerian blossom extract completes the six through phosphorus. The editor identifies this group as preparations 502–507; that numbering is editorial information, not part of Steiner's spoken list.

Discussion matters. Growth promotion also benefits weeds, so these preparations do not by themselves suppress weeds. Rain, soil character and continuing ordinary manuring enter the replies. Some combinations are left uncertain, and contradictory fodder opinions are asked to be tested. Gardner qualifies the oak's 77% calcium as evidently an ash analysis and says the exact mesentery portion is unclear. These notes restrict the reading without erasing Steiner's asserted radiating and bodily effects.

Key concept

Each preparation has a distinct proposed role within one living process; a named ingredient, its historical treatment and its claimed effect must be distinguished.

Course example

Create six reading cards with plant, enclosure if named, substance relationship and predicted effect. Put no animal enclosure on the nettle card and no preparation timetable on the valerian card. Add the editor's qualifications on separate cards.

Keep this distinction

A map of the six claims demonstrates understanding of the text. It does not demonstrate disease prevention, improved nutrition or cosmic radiation. The ash-analysis note also prevents treating the oak percentage as a whole-bark measurement.
