Applying the complete compost set: 502–507

Place the five finished solids and the valerian liquid into a suitable manure or compost pile. The preparation set does not replace making and managing the pile properly.

Check the pile and the set

Confirm that you have finished 502, 503, 504, 505 and 506 plus finished liquid 507. Raw yarrow, chamomile and nettle are not a substitute for the mature set. Check the supplier’s declared pile capacity; sets from different makers need not contain the same amount.

Assess moisture and aeration before insertion. A bone-dry pile or saturated, airless mass needs management first. Suitable composting also requires an appropriate material balance, pile design and decomposition process. Adding preparations does not correct every structural problem or sanitize animal manure.

Use dedicated tools and labelled portions. If the material is intended for a food crop, comply with the applicable compost-process and manure-derived-input rules. A dark colour, earthworms or a pleasant smell alone cannot establish a validated pathogen-reduction process.

A weighed alternative for mature homemade preparations

Bucher’s later manual gives this separate example: 2 g of each solid 502–506 and 5 mL of finished 507 for a heap about 1.5–2 m wide, 1.5 m high and 5–10 m long. Stir the valerian in 3–5 L water for 10–15 minutes; use part inside and the remainder over the surface. See manual pages 65–66.

Distribute the five weighed solids separately inside the pile and close the positions as described in the classical method. Use this as a complete named example with a maker who has assessed your material. Sloping heap sides and bulk density mean its outside dimensions are not a universal grams-per-cubic-metre rule. Do not substitute these amounts for the contents of the JPI set below.

One labelled protocol: the JPI complete set

  1. Make six distributed holes to about mid-depth. Place the five solids separately, one per hole.
  2. Stir the supplied 507 dose in 1 US gallon (about 3.8 L) of water for 10 minutes.
  3. Pour half into the sixth hole; close every hole with mature compost or soil. Mist the other half over the pile.
  4. JPI uses its whole set on smaller piles too, up to a stated 15 tons. Confirm the ton unit with the supplier; do not use the field calculator.

Why the positions and closing matter

The source describes preparations surrounded by manure, distributed rather than all dropped into one exposed hollow. The practical result is a retrievable application record: each named material has a place, and the hole is closed so it is not left as a drying channel. The selected JPI example is one method, not a universal rule for every product. Other makers specify a set by cubic metres, a weighed portion or a different valerian distribution. Do not convert tons into cubic metres without the pile’s bulk density, and do not combine half of one protocol with half of another.

Care after application

  • Mark the pile with date and preparation batch. Maintain moisture, aeration and the composting process appropriate to the material and intended use.
  • Monitor temperature and condition using the composting plan. If a pile needs turning for sound process management, do not abandon that need because preparations were added. Whether to reapply after rebuilding belongs to the chosen maker’s protocol.
  • Allow the pile to mature for its intended use. A preparation application date is not a compost-ready date and is not the start of a universally valid harvest interval.
  • For slurry or liquid manure, use a specific protocol for that system. The six-hole heap method cannot simply be poured into any tank without regard to mixing, volume or handling hazards.

Worked decision: a very small garden heap

If you bought a maker’s garden-sized set, follow its stated pile range. If you bought the JPI set described above, do not invent an area-based fraction because the heap occupies only a few square metres. A pile dose and a field-spray dose answer different questions. Measure the pile in the unit required by the supplier.
