506: dandelion in bovine mesentery

Dandelion flowers are wrapped in the specified bovine membrane and matured through winter burial. The membrane is not the stomach lining called tripe.

Identify flower and membrane separately

  • Dandelion flowers, classically Taraxacum officinale, accurately identified and harvested at an appropriate flowering stage. Use clean, sound flowers and dry them gently if needed.
  • Prepared bovine mesentery or the membrane specified by the chosen making tradition, from a permitted traceable source. The supplied edition notes uncertainty over the exact portion: record what the maker actually uses.
  • Thread, a label, dedicated tools, a protected winter burial location and a separate storage jar.

Form the flower parcel

  1. Prepare flowers and membrane by the group’s established procedure. If dried flowers are moistened before filling, use only the amount needed for that method rather than making a watery mass.
  2. Lay out the prepared membrane, place the flowers inside and fold it into a contained parcel. Secure with thread so the contents remain together during burial.
  3. Label and bury in autumn in the marked fertile-soil location. Protect it from animal disturbance, flooding and invasive roots.
  4. Leave through the local winter and retrieve in spring. Locate the parcel from the site record rather than probing blindly through it.
  5. Separate the matured flower material from outside soil and surviving membrane. Assess and store as 506. Keep an incomplete or questionable batch separate for review rather than adding it to the established supply.

How and when to use the finished preparation

The source relates this preparation to silica and potassium reception. Its membrane and seasonal process distinguish it from raw dandelion leaves, root tea or an ordinary compost ingredient.

Use the finished material as one member of the complete 502–507 set in newly assembled manure or compost, following the shared compost-placement guide. Keep the five solids separate until each is placed in its own position. The quantity is a measured small addition, not a layer of raw flowers or bark. Do not give this solid an hour of stirring or spray it directly over vegetables.

Store a reserve separately, cool, dark and moist under the maker’s directions. Record the batch, retrieval date and pile receiving it. The month of manufacture is different from the time of compost application: a finished stored preparation can be used when a suitable new pile is assembled.

Common mistake: substituting tripe

Tripe and mesentery are anatomically different. Ask the maker to identify the sheath and write it into the batch record. A local variation should be named as a variation, not silently presented as the book’s exact specification.
