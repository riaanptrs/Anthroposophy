501: horn silica — making and foliar application

Quartz in a cow horn is exposed through the local summer. Its very small application dose is used as a fine mist on established plant growth.

Use the right mineral and control dust

The classical material is finely ground quartz, not sand of unknown composition, lime or diatomaceous earth. Steiner also mentions orthoclase, but a mineral substitution changes the recipe and should not be made casually. Horn silica is intended as the plant-side complement to horn manure; its proposed light-related role does not make it a general nutrient spray.

For a beginner, obtain mature 501 or professionally prepared quartz paste through an experienced making group. Grinding, transferring and cleaning up dry quartz can create respirable crystalline silica. The traditional method is described below, but this guide does not turn uncontrolled dry grinding into a home exercise. Use dust-controlled equipment and appropriate occupational guidance.

Make the summer preparation

  1. Use an intact, prepared cow horn reserved for the batch and accurately identified finely ground quartz. Keep the mineral wet during the appropriate controlled preparation process.
  2. Mix the quartz with clean water into the thin paste described in the book. Fill the horn consistently. Let the maker assess settling, excess water and how the opening is secured; these details vary between making methods.
  3. Bury for the local summer at the group’s marked, well-drained site. This reverses 500’s winter exposure. Keep a separate record so 500 and 501 horns are not confused.
  4. Retrieve in autumn. Recover the mineral without adding outside soil or fragments of horn. Label it 501 with making and retrieval dates.
  5. Store the finished mineral dry according to the maker’s instructions, generally in a glass vessel in a light position. Retain dust precautions whenever handling dry material; dry storage is not a licence to disperse powder.

Apply to established growth, in a suitable morning window

  1. Confirm the product and crop-specific instructions. Use after the soil-side 500 application in the practitioner sequence and when roots and leaf growth are established; a newly transplanted or stressed plant is not an automatic candidate.
  2. Use a sufficiently precise balance for the metric example in the application guide. This is a very small quantity. Do not estimate by a household teaspoon when following a mass-based protocol.
  3. Add the measured preparation to clean water and stir with alternating vortex and reversal for one hour. Filter without allowing the measured mineral to be lost as a large settled residue; follow the supplier’s mixing and filtering directions.
  4. Spray promptly as a fine, evenly distributed mist over foliage in the early morning, using equipment calibrated for that mist. Avoid dripping saturation, drift and inhaling the spray.
  5. Choose a calm window appropriate to the crop, with no impending frost or rain and no severe heat or water stress. Number of repeats depends on crop and protocol; do not turn “several applications” into a daily treatment rule.

Worked decision: the crop is newly transplanted

The morning may be calm, but crop stage is a separate condition. Give the plant time to establish and check the maker’s directions before choosing 501. Do not copy a neighbouring mature orchard’s spray programme onto nursery trees or young transplants. Early morning describes a window, not permission to ignore plant age or weather.
