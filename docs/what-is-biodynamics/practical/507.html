507: valerian flower liquid

Valerian is the liquid member of the compost set. It is made from flowers, not valerian-root medicine, and it is not buried in an animal organ.

Begin with the flower method

Use correctly identified Valeriana officinalis flowers and clean, dedicated crushing, pressing, straining and bottling equipment. Root tinctures sold for human use are not interchangeable with 507. The supplied introduction describes flower juice allowed to ferment for a few weeks; later makers use several extraction methods. Record whether a batch is pressed juice or a specified water extraction, because the resulting concentrations may differ.

A classical pressed-flower route

  1. Collect sound flowers from an identified clean source and remove stems or foreign material according to the maker’s method. Work promptly so a heap of flowers does not heat and spoil.
  2. Crush the flowers with dedicated clean equipment and press out the juice. Strain into a labelled non-food vessel, recording flower source, date and yield.
  3. Use the maker’s controlled fermentation procedure. Keep the vessel protected and cool, and permit fermentation gas to escape safely. Do not seal an actively fermenting batch in a rigid airtight bottle.
  4. The book’s few weeks is a broad indication, not a universal finished-on-day-21 specification. Temperature, extraction and batch condition matter. Have a first batch assessed before use.
  5. Once the process is complete and gas production has ceased, transfer to the maker’s specified labelled storage bottle, generally brown glass in a cool dark place. Follow directions about headspace and closure. Recheck for renewed pressure and contamination.

Use as the liquid part of the compost set

The intended role in Steiner’s account relates to phosphorus. In compost application, a measured amount of finished 507 is diluted and stirred for the short time in the chosen compost protocol, then introduced to the pile. It is not automatically stirred for an hour like the field sprays. The shared compost page gives one complete-set method and identifies its source.

Use the supplier’s dose for the actual liquid, because juice and water-extract methods are not necessarily equivalent. Keep concentrate and diluted application mixture in distinctly labelled containers. Do not drink or use the liquid as a human remedy.

Later frost-related uses and mixtures with 500 are separate practitioner protocols. This course’s core compost instruction does not establish frost protection and does not replace frost cloth, irrigation designed for frost management or crop-specific weather planning. Do not add 507 to every field spray merely because a different supplier combines it in one product.

Common mistake: confusing concentrate and spray

A small bottle of 507 concentrate and several litres of diluted compost mixture are different stages. Label the first with its extraction method and batch; label the second with dilution and time of mixing. This prevents using a diluted mixture later as though it were the original concentrate.
