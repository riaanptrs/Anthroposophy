A seasonal calendar for making and applying

Plan by the local season and crop cycle. The season for making a preparation is not automatically the season for applying it.

Four dates for one preparation

Record ingredient harvest, enclosure or burial, recovery and field or compost application as separate dates. For example, 500 is made through winter but applied when an appropriate soil-management window occurs. A stored compost preparation can be inserted whenever a suitable new pile is made. Do not confuse six months of preparation-making with the maturation time of that pile.

The table below describes temperate seasonal correspondences. Northern autumn is approximately September–November; Southern autumn is approximately March–May. Actual frost, soil moisture, rainfall pattern and growing season can shift the work. At high altitude or near the equator, obtain a regional making calendar instead of merely swapping month names.

A first-year route that does not require waiting for every batch

  1. Before the growing season: obtain correctly stored mature preparations, learn the measuring and application method, and plan ordinary soil and compost work.
  2. At the chosen soil-preparation window: use the selected 500 protocol when soil and crop conditions fit. Record it; do not set a compulsory interval to 501 without the crop-specific protocol.
  3. Once plants are established and a suitable morning arrives: consider 501 under the maker’s crop guidance. Keep a record of stage and conditions.
  4. When building a suitable pile: insert the complete compost set by its own protocol. Continue compost management and allow maturity before use.
  5. Through the year: collect correctly identified ingredients in their harvesting seasons and join a making group for the summer and winter preparations. Plan 504 a full year ahead.
  6. When wet conditions or symptoms raise a plant-health question: inspect and diagnose first. If choosing a horsetail protocol, identify fresh versus fermented use and its target rather than following a fixed weekly calendar.

Moon and zodiac calendars: identify the system

Some biodynamic schools add Moon phases, ascending/descending Moon or constellation categories to their timing. These systems are not interchangeable, and the supplied introduction itself records disagreement and incomplete reproducibility. If you use one, record the calendar, edition, time zone and category. Explain that it is an additional practitioner framework.

This guide does not invent universal “root-day” or full-Moon instructions for every preparation. A frozen or flooded bed, strong wind, an unestablished crop or a food-safety restriction is not corrected by an auspicious calendar symbol. Choose a usable agronomic window and follow the specific preparation protocol.

Worked decision: summer in January

In a temperate Southern Hemisphere location, January is normally part of summer. Treating it as winter because a European example uses January would reverse the intended making exposure. Start with the local season, then check soil and climate; tropical wet/dry cycles require a local adaptation rather than an automatic six-month shift.
