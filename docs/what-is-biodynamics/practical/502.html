502: yarrow in a stag bladder

Yarrow flowers pass through a summer exposure and then winter burial. The matured contents become a solid compost preparation.

Identify the plant and sheath

  • Yarrow flower heads, Achillea millefolium, positively identified and collected from a suitable uncontaminated site. Dry thinly in shade if they cannot be used at once.
  • A legally sourced stag bladder, prepared for this use by a knowledgeable maker; thread, filling tool, label and a protective hanging basket that admits light and air.
  • A marked winter burial site, batch log and separate storage jar for the final contents.

Follow both phases

  1. Prepare the flowers and bladder with the making group. If the sheath is dried, soften it using the group’s procedure. Moisten dried flowers only as that procedure specifies; do not turn the contents into a soaking slurry.
  2. Fill the bladder with flower heads, close the opening and label it. Keep enough structure to contain the flowers without tearing the sheath.
  3. Hang in a protected, light and airy position through the local summer, guarding against pests, loss and destructive weather. Summer exposure is part of this method; it is not yet the final compost product.
  4. In autumn, bury the filled bladder in the marked soil site through winter. Record its position and date so it can be recovered without cutting through it.
  5. Lift in spring and separate the matured contents from soil and surviving sheath. Have the first batch assessed before storing and portioning it. Do not treat the fresh hanging flowers as finished 502.

How and when to use the finished preparation

The source links this preparation with sulfur and potassium relationships. That intended role explains its place in the set; the method is more specific than putting a yarrow plant beside a heap.

Use the finished material as one member of the complete 502–507 set in newly assembled manure or compost, following the shared compost-placement guide. Keep the five solids separate until each is placed in its own position. The quantity is a measured small addition, not a layer of raw flowers or bark. Do not give this solid an hour of stirring or spray it directly over vegetables.

Store a reserve separately, cool, dark and moist under the maker’s directions. Record the batch, retrieval date and pile receiving it. The month of manufacture is different from the time of compost application: a finished stored preparation can be used when a suitable new pile is assembled.

Common mistake: stopping after summer

A dry, flower-filled bladder after summer has completed only the first phase. Label the next task as winter burial, not compost application. The two-season sequence is the distinguishing making process.
