508: horsetail — fresh and fermented decoctions

Learn two distinct horsetail methods. “Fresh tea” means freshly made decoction; it does not necessarily mean the starting herb was freshly harvested.

Identify the herb before making a batch

The named plant is Equisetum arvense. Obtain positively identified material from a reliable grower or supplier; other horsetail species are not interchangeable and some are toxic. Do not identify it from this course’s symbolic comet drawing. Use a dedicated cooking vessel, clean water, strainer, labelled containers, timer and appropriate sprayer.

Horsetail is proposed in the source for excessive water-mediated vitality and fungal problems. A wet period is a reason to inspect a crop, not enough to diagnose every leaf mark. Check crop identity, symptoms, weather, drainage and appropriate plant-health advice before relying on a spray.

JPI fresh-decoction protocol

  1. Use 1.5 oz dried herb (about 42.5 g) in 1 US quart water (about 0.95 L). Simmer 1 hour; cool and strain.
  2. Add 2 US gallons water (about 7.6 L); stir 20 minutes.
  3. Apply as a fine foliar spray. The stated batch covers up to 1 acre (about 4,047 m²).

JPI fermented-decoction protocol

  1. Simmer the same herb amount in 1 US gallon water (about 3.8 L) for 1 hour; cool.
  2. Ferment under a loose lid in a cool place for 10–14 days; filter.
  3. Dilute 1 part concentrate with 9 parts water; stir 20 minutes. Direct this version to soil.

Choose a window and keep the recipes separate

These are JPI’s later practitioner recipes, not numerical directions quoted from Steiner. US units and approximate metric conversions are shown together so the two methods cannot be accidentally combined. Evaporation changes the recovered volume; do not promise an exact final volume from the starting water alone.

Prepare the fresh version for prompt use after cooling. For the fermented version, label the concentrate with its making date and dilution; use the maker’s storage and expiry directions. Fermentation gases require a safe venting arrangement. Keep all material out of food and animal feed.

Choose conditions that permit the intended target to be covered without runoff, wind drift or immediate wash-off. A repeated-treatment schedule belongs to a crop-specific protocol; do not assume a daily spray is needed throughout the season. Do not add the fermented concentrate to a foliar batch merely because both containers say 508.

Worked dilution: one plus nine is ten parts

For the fermented method, 100 mL of concentrate plus 900 mL of water makes 1 L of application mixture. It does not mean 100 mL topped up with 1 L of water. A ratio describes dilution; area coverage still depends on the chosen protocol and calibrated sprayer.
