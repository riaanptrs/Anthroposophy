Source anthology · English and Portuguese

What Is Biodynamics?

Learn biodynamics from the beginning: understand a living farm, the spiritual view of nature and the different roles of preparations through 14 bilingual lessons, worked examples and guided book illustrations. This anthology brings together GA 136, GA 230 and GA 327; the complete eight-lecture Agriculture Course is now available as a companion course.

Learn step by step: 14 lessons

  1. Reading an anthology with three voices

    Explain biodynamics through one farm relationship and the additional spiritual ideas it brings.

  2. Farm individuality, rhythms and preparations

    Connect farm individuality, timing and the distinct preparation families.

  3. Courtney’s wider interpretation of Earth healing

    Read the plant–human comparison and explain why four regions correspond to three groupings.

  4. Learning to perceive nature: the first spiritual beings

    Distinguish observation, inward response and Steiner’s further claim about nature beings.

  5. From nature beings to rhythms and planetary spirit

    Explain the different roles of nature spirits, cycle spirits and planetary spirit.

  6. Elementals I: four activities in the plant’s cosmic circuit

    Explain all four elemental tasks and the proposed plant circuit without confusing it with botanical fertilization.

  7. Elementals II: complements, consciousness and displaced forces

    Explain complement and displacement as relationships within the lecture’s spiritual model.

  8. Fertilizing as enlivening the soil

    Explain why the lecture connects fertilization, living soil, animal digestion and the horn.

  9. Two preparations, one larger inquiry

    Compare the two horn preparations by substance, season, direction and intended role.

  10. Living soil: substances, forces and replenishment

    Distinguish material presence, living-force claims and evidence of a crop response.

  11. Six preparations: differentiated roles and qualified claims

    Compare the six compost preparations by intended role and form, including the source’s qualifications.

  12. Desired growth, unwanted plants and the question of verification

    Explain selective restraint, the historical seed-ash claim and the lecture’s position on verification.

  13. Pests, plant abnormalities and shared responsibility

    Distinguish organism-specific claims and explain why identification and cooperation matter.

  14. Explaining biodynamics with its sources in view

    Give a clear beginner explanation of biodynamics, its preparation families and the questions still open.
