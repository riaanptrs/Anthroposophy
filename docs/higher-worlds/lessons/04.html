Course 2 · Lesson 04

Enlightenment and the seed examples

Explain the distinction between visible appearance and developmental potential.

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · How to Know Higher Worlds · GA 10 · § 21

Place a small seed of a plant before you. What matters is to make the appropriate thoughts intense before this inconspicuous thing and, through these thoughts, to develop certain feelings.

Original German text; new English and Brazilian Portuguese study translations.

Read the original chapter or lecture (German) →

Compare with the German passage

Man lege ein kleines Samenkorn einer Pflanze vor sich hin. Es kommt darauf an, sich vor diesem unscheinbaren Ding die rechten Gedanken intensiv zu machen und durch diese Gedanken gewisse Gefühle zu entwickeln.

What this passage means

Steiner next contrasts a real seed with an outwardly identical imitation: one has a future plant within its possibilities. He uses that contrast to direct thought and feeling toward development that is not yet visible, before describing a spiritual perception.

The focus of this lesson

Explain the distinction between visible appearance and developmental potential.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

Understand the idea

The enlightenment passages compare mineral, plant and animal life and connect sustained attention with differentiated feeling. Steiner then asks the learner to contemplate a real seed and imagine the plant that may develop from it, contrasting it with a convincing imitation.

The recovered opening on page 27 makes the sequence clear: describe the visible seed, imagine its development, and consider what is present as potential but not yet visible as a plant. A footnote explicitly says the purpose is not physical investigation. A microscope or germination test would answer a different question.

Steiner connects this contemplation with a claimed spiritual perception described through colour and flame. That conclusion must not be confused with the ordinary fact that viable seeds can grow. The later plant example extends the theme to decay and continuation through seeds. Our activity studies the reasoning rather than prescribing a programme for producing visions.

The rosebud in GA 4 offers a useful preliminary comparison: one momentary appearance does not show the whole course of a plant’s development. Compare a closed bud, a partly opened flower and an open blossom. Thinking connects these appearances as stages of development; merely naming the flower does not explain that relationship. The conditions matter: an expected stage may not occur. Chapter 11 distinguishes lawful development from a represented aim guiding an action. The later blossom does not reach backward and make the root grow. Its 1918 addition says that rejecting a human-like purpose imposed on nature does not mean reducing reality to matter; Steiner maintains a spiritual account. A sequence of observations can support or challenge an expectation, so distinguish what was observed from what was only predicted. GA 10’s further account of supersensible perception is an additional claim, not a consequence established by that comparison.

Additional connection: The Philosophy of Freedom (GA 4), markers 44–45, 96–99 in the 147-page Basis transcription. Locate by chapter in another edition. Find the book.

A sequence helps us understand

Consider these three imagined records of the same flower at different times. They are a study example, not a guaranteed prediction.

  1. First record

    A closed bud.

  2. Second record

    The same bud begins to open.

  3. Third record

    The blossom is open.

If you had only the first record, which details of the later ones would be expectations? What further records would help check the development? Attend to conditions too. Thinking connects the records; it does not make the flower open.

An image and a claim of spiritual knowledge

The veil of Isis offers a comparison for the work of knowing. Use it to distinguish ordinary imagery from Steiner’s technical claim about Imagination. A compelling image may clarify a question without verifying a supersensible perception.

See the idea in an example

A real seed and a model can look alike while behaving differently when planted.

What this helps explain

Similar appearance need not imply identical capacities. This familiar distinction helps explain the example; it does not establish an etheric body or a spiritual flame.
