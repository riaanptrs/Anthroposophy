Course 2 · Lesson 10

Spiritual organs and the language of colour

Read the lotus descriptions without turning them into ordinary anatomy.

Recall from Course 1: Lesson 17 →

Start with an example

A reader hears that thoughts have spiritual forms and draws a coloured outline around each classmate according to their personality.

What this helps explain

The drawing converts a complex claim into an unsupported classification of real people. A more faithful study task is to explain what the author means by form and how he relates it to habits.

Understand the idea

Steiner calls the organs of spiritual perception lotus flowers or wheels because of a perceived resemblance. The terms are figurative, but he presents the realities described as supersensible organs. Similarly, a spiritual colour is explained by comparison with the quality of an ordinary colour experience. Neither explanation reduces the entire account to a poetic mood.

The sixteen-petalled lotus is connected with eight activities: forming ideas, resolutions, speech, action, management of life, endeavour suited to capacity, learning from experience, and self-review. The recovered text on pages 56–58 allows these to be read continuously. They concern how a life is conducted, not simply memorizing a list.

For the twelve-petalled lotus, pages 60–62 discuss control of thought, control of action, perseverance, tolerance, impartiality or faith, and equanimity. The chapter then develops attention and bodily-soul-spiritual harmony. Keep the proposed habits distinct from the claim that they develop spiritual organs; these descriptions are not diagrams of physical anatomy.

Keep this idea

Keep the descriptive image, the cultivated habit and the claimed organ distinct.

Reading: Rudolf Steiner, GA 10, Some results of initiation. Page markers 54–66.

References use the 107-page transcription; locate by chapter in other editions. Explanations use original wording. About the source.

Words for the reading

  • Lotus flower — a comparative name for a purported spiritual organ.
  • Impartiality — readiness to encounter something beyond one’s existing opinion.
Try it

Work with the idea

Choose two of the eight activities and two of the six qualities. Explain each in ordinary language, then identify the additional spiritual claim attached to them.

A study activity created for this course. Assess understanding of the text; personal or spiritual experiences need not be reported.

Check your understanding

Does figurative language mean the organs are fictional for Steiner?

Show a suggested answer

No. He uses comparison to describe what he regards as a spiritual reality.

Are the eight activities identical to the six qualities?

Show a suggested answer

No. They are related lists associated with different organs in this account.

Does a habit of careful speech verify a spiritual organ?

Show a suggested answer

No. It illustrates the habit; the proposed spiritual effect is a further claim.

How to assess your response

Give yourself 0–2 points for each criterion: accurate meaning, clear distinctions, support from the text and quality of reasoning. 0 = missing; 1 = partial; 2 = clear and supported. Revisit incomplete points. Well-supported disagreement can receive full marks.
