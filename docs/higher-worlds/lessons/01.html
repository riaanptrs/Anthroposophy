Course 2 · Lesson 01

Reverence and independent judgment

Distinguish receptivity to knowledge from submission to a person.

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · How to Know Higher Worlds · GA 10 · § 6

A particular fundamental disposition of the soul must form the beginning. The spiritual researcher calls this disposition the path of reverence, of devotion toward truth and knowledge.

Original German text; new English and Brazilian Portuguese study translations.

Read the original chapter or lecture (German) →

Compare with the German passage

Eine gewisse Grundstimmung der Seele muß den Anfang bilden. Der Geheimforscher nennt diese Grundstimmung den Pfad der Verehrung , der Devotion gegenüber der Wahrheit und Erkenntnis.

What this passage means

Notice the object of devotion: truth and knowledge. Steiner makes the learner’s attitude part of knowing, rather than treating knowledge as information alone. The chapter then asks how this attitude changes inner life.

The focus of this lesson

Distinguish receptivity to knowledge from submission to a person.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

Understand the idea

Steiner begins with capacities he says lie dormant in every human being. Their development, in his account, depends on how the learner approaches the world. Reverence is an attitude of valuing truth and knowledge, rather than making a teacher’s personality the object of devotion.

The opening strongly criticizes habitual fault-finding while acknowledging the achievements of critical judgment in modern life. Do not make these statements identical: an attitude that looks only for faults differs from a reasoned examination, but the passage still places demanding limits on criticism. Read both sides of that tension.

For our study, receptivity means making a serious effort to understand a passage before assessing it. It does not make the passage true in advance. Steiner links reverence with spiritual development; that further link is his claim, not something proved by courteous reading.

Brian’s lectures on the GA 4 prefaces invite readers to work with an idea rather than submit to it. Try explaining a passage fairly before asking whether its reasons persuade you. Feeling certain, respecting a teacher and understanding an argument are different matters. A reasoned disagreement can demonstrate careful study.

When an obstacle appears

  1. What happened?

    The study group’s room is unavailable. This information limits the present plan.

  2. What am I inferring?

    “This means I should give up” adds an interpretation. The unavailable room alone does not establish a spiritual command or decide the value of the aim.

  3. What response can I justify?

    Ask participants whether another time would help. Examine resources, commitments and your reason for continuing. New information can also justify postponing, changing or abandoning the plan.

Steiner acknowledges real limitations while investigating the possibility of free action. Persistence can be valuable; insistence alone does not demonstrate ethical intuition.

Check your understanding

What is the intended object of reverence?

Show a suggested answer

Truth and knowledge, rather than a person simply because they hold authority.

Is listening equivalent to agreement?

Show a suggested answer

No. Understanding what a claim means leaves open whether its reasons are sufficient.

What tension should remain visible?

Show a suggested answer

The book values knowledge and judgment while speaking strongly against criticism and doubt in preparation.

How to assess your response

Give yourself 0–2 points for each criterion: accurate meaning, clear distinctions, support from the text and quality of reasoning. 0 = missing; 1 = partial; 2 = clear and supported. Revisit incomplete points. Well-supported disagreement can receive full marks.
