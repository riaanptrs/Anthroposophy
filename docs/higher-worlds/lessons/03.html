2 · Work through the explanation

A worked example

A dog whines by a closed door. You hear a wavering sound, feel irritated, and suppose that the dog wants to go outside.

What this helps explain

The heard sound, your reaction and the interpretation are distinguishable. The interpretation could change if the dog is waiting for someone.

Understand the idea

The three stages introduced here—probation, enlightenment and initiation—can overlap. They are not a checklist in which every part of one must be completed before anything of the next becomes possible. Probation names preparation through directed attention and feeling.

Steiner considers growth and decay, then distinguishes attending to a sound from attending to the experience expressed by a living being. He also describes occasions of listening while withholding both assent and objection. This is a selected exercise, not a rule forbidding judgment throughout daily life.

Notice the movement of the argument: ordinary attention is followed by claims about the formation of spiritual capacities. Our examples help you follow that movement, but seeing a leaf change or listening patiently does not itself establish the supersensible results he describes.

GA 4 distinguishes the content of observation from our judgments about it. An appearance can depend on a viewpoint without being an arbitrary invention. Comparing perspectives can help us revise a judgment. With people, the same care means asking rather than supplying motives: a quiet participant may be considering the discussion, feeling uncertain, or simply preferring to listen. Silence alone does not tell us which. A shared conclusion also needs reasons; agreement by itself does not establish truth.

Additional connection: The Philosophy of Freedom (GA 4), markers 31–34, 126–127 in the 147-page Basis transcription. Locate by chapter in another edition. Find the book.

One shape, two views

Imagine the same circular card viewed face-on and then at an angle. The drawings represent its visual appearance; the card keeps its circular shape.

What changed: the card, or its relation to the observer? Separate the perceived appearance from the judgment about the object’s shape. Saying “it looks narrower” is different from saying “the card shrank”.

You can try this with a card or imagine the change. Comparing views can clarify an appearance; it does not by itself establish a complete theory of perception.

When the observer changes

Koepke ends the constructed Peter conversation by asking whether the child changed, the parents’ perception changed, or both. Extend the three-column exercise with a fourth column: what new information would change my interpretation? A child’s criticism might concern a real inconsistency. Listening includes checking the adult’s account.

Check your understanding

Must the stages be entirely separate?

Show a suggested answer

No. The book allows their developments to overlap.

Does attending to another being mean knowing its experience directly?

Show a suggested answer

Not in our example; the interpretation may be wrong.

What goes beyond ordinary observation in the text?

Show a suggested answer

The asserted development of spiritual organs or supersensible perception.

How to assess your response

Give yourself 0–2 points for each criterion: accurate meaning, clear distinctions, support from the text and quality of reasoning. 0 = missing; 1 = partial; 2 = clear and supported. Revisit incomplete points. Well-supported disagreement can receive full marks.
