Four temperaments: contrasting portraits

Question: How does predominance among the human members shape each of Steiner’s four temperament portraits?

  1. Reading 1: Choleric: purpose meeting resistance

    Understand the movement from forceful insistence toward capable action.

  2. Reading 2: Sanguine: interest that moves quickly

    Explore how an existing interest can become a starting point for attention.

  3. Reading 3: Phlegmatic: finding an invitation to participate

    Explain how inner well-being and reflected interest connect in Steiner’s phlegmatic account.

  4. Reading 4: Melancholic: sensitivity and compassionate attention

    Follow Steiner’s movement from bodily resistance and personal pain toward another person’s destiny.

Course grouping

Mixtures, development and education

Question: How do mixed temperaments qualify the portraits and inform Steiner’s different educational proposals?

  1. Reading 1: Mixtures, appearance and the limits of a label

    Read the mixed-type qualification alongside Steiner’s outward portraits and their practical purpose.

  2. Reading 2: Education: work with what is already present

    Compare the four child-education routes and the principle that holds them together.
