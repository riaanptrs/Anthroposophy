Self-education through an indirect route

Distinguish useful arrangement from repeated self-reproach.

2 · Work through the explanation

A worked example

A learner repeatedly says “I must stop changing subjects.” Instead, they choose a five-minute comparison task in which looking at three examples is useful, followed by one written conclusion.

The arrangement gives variety a place and still asks for completion. Whether it helps must be assessed from the result, not assumed from a temperament label.

The reading’s key idea

In the final practical passage, Steiner argues that simply ordering oneself to have a different temperament accomplishes little. Reason can instead arrange suitable situations: short-lived interest where it has a place, a task where rage is ineffective, concern directed toward another, or, in his phlegmatic example, boredom brought to an extreme.

We preserve the distinction between direct self-reproach and indirect arrangement without prescribing every historical suggestion. Our activity uses a small chosen task and a review point. It does not deliberately provoke anger, seek distress or impose prolonged boredom. The question is how to give a tendency a workable expression while retaining responsibility for action.

Keep this idea

Use thought to arrange a useful practice, then learn from the result.

Words for the reading

Self-education: working on one’s own habits and capacities. Indirect route: arranging conditions rather than relying only on commands to oneself.

Check your understanding

Does the lecture say reason has no role?

Show a suggested answer

No. It gives reason an indirect role in choosing situations and experiences.

Does a tendency excuse harmful behaviour?

Show a suggested answer

No. Understanding a tendency does not remove responsibility for how one acts toward others.

What makes the small task reviewable?

Show a suggested answer

It has an identifiable action and stopping point, such as comparing three examples and writing one conclusion, so the learner can assess what happened.

How to assess your response

Check four things: correct attribution, a clear distinction, a reason supported by the reading and recognition of the example’s limits. Revisit anything vague. Reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement and reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
