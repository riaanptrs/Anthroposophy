Self-education through an indirect route

Explain the role of reason in arranging experiences, and distinguish the adult proposals from the child-education routes.

What useful role does Steiner give reason in self-education?

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · The Four Temperaments · GA 57 · Berlin, 4 March 1909 · Lecture, PDF capture 13, column 1; printed folio not visible.

Our logical minds can accomplish little when pitted against stronger forces such as the temperaments, but working indirectly, on the other hand, can achieve a great deal.

Sophia Books / Rudolf Steiner Press, published 2012; translated by B. Kelly (first English publication 1987), revised by Matthew Barton, translation revision copyright 2008. Selected wording from the supplied PDF; line breaks normalized.

What this passage means

Steiner assigns reason an indirect arranging role rather than excluding it. The four adult examples specify the conditions to be arranged; they are distinct from the course’s limited fictional comparison task. Exact wording visually checked in the left column’s opening paragraph.

The focus of this lesson

Explain the role of reason in arranging experiences, and distinguish the adult proposals from the child-education routes.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

The reading’s key idea

Steiner argues that direct reproach and exhortation accomplish little against temperament. Reason instead creates experiences suited to what is already present. He proposes brief suitable situations for sanguine attention, situations where choleric rage leads nowhere, seeking life’s pain through compassion for melancholics, and thorough boredom through uninteresting occupations for phlegmatics. These are his concrete adult examples, followed by a return to the principle of reckoning with what one is.

The whole passage is present in capture 13. The adult phlegmatic example deliberately differs from the varied interests of child playmates. Our five-minute comparison task is a chosen course adaptation of indirect arrangement. It does not reproduce the choleric, melancholic or phlegmatic prescriptions or turn the source’s language of cure into medical guidance.

Reason arranges an indirect route

From the educator to the adult self

At the end of the child-education passage, Steiner says that the principles can also be used for self-education. The following examples concern adults arranging experiences for themselves. He opens with the sanguine person who gains little by reproaching himself. Direct logical argument, he says, accomplishes little against stronger forces such as the temperaments. This does not remove reason from the task. Reason and intelligence receive an indirect role: selecting or creating circumstances in which an existing tendency can work in a suitable way.

PDF capture 12, right bottom; 13, left.

Give brevity a fitting place; let rage find its limit

The sanguine example asks the person to accept the tendency rather than keep issuing useless exhortations. Even small experiences can be designed in which short attention is appropriate. The choleric example asks reason to find situations where rage is of no use and leads into a self-prolonging dead end. Both arrangements make a tendency encounter particular conditions, but they do so differently. One gives brief responsiveness a fitting task; the other confronts forceful anger with its inability to achieve anything in the selected situation.

PDF capture 13, left.

Compassion and tedium are the other examples

The melancholic adult is told to face and seek life’s pain, then redirect suffering outward through compassion. This echoes the child passage while assigning the arrangement to the person himself. The phlegmatic adult is told to occupy himself with uninteresting things and surround himself with many sources of tedium until thoroughly bored. This differs sharply from giving the child playmates with varied interests. Steiner claims that boredom will break the habit of ‘phlegma’. His language of cure belongs to this historical proposal and supplies no medical treatment instruction here.

PDF capture 13, left bottom → middle.

Keep source proposal and course exercise legible

The course’s five-minute comparison task illustrates one limited version of indirect arrangement: variety has a useful place, followed by a written conclusion. The time limit, chosen materials and review question are original course decisions. They should not conceal the stronger details of Steiner’s other examples, nor be presented as his actual wording. The common principle is to reckon with what one is rather than what one is not. Read each arrangement by asking what tendency it assumes, what reason changes, and what result Steiner intends.

Connect the parts of the reading

How does the indirect role of reason differ from a command to change one’s temperament?

An explained answer: A command confronts the tendency directly through reproach or exhortation. The indirect role uses reason to find and create suitable conditions: a fitting place for brief attention, a limit to rage, outward compassion, or the source’s proposed tedium. The arrangement, rather than the command alone, is meant to bring change.

Keep this idea

In this argument, reason works indirectly by arranging conditions rather than merely issuing commands to the self.

Words for the reading

Indirect route: changing arrangements so that an existing tendency works in a suitable situation. Exhortation: a command or appeal to change directly. Self-education: the adult application distinguished from educating a child.

Read → understand → explain → apply. The chapter explanation is open from the start.

See the idea in an example

A learner repeatedly says “I must stop changing subjects.” Instead, they choose a five-minute comparison task in which looking at three examples is useful, followed by one written conclusion.

The arrangement gives variety a place and still asks for completion. Whether it helps must be assessed from the result, not assumed from a temperament label.

Check your understanding

What useful role does Steiner give reason in self-education?

Reveal the explanation

Reason can find and create situations indirectly, even though direct reproach often achieves little. It works by arranging circumstances rather than simply commanding a tendency to disappear.

Original open question and answer

Does Steiner deny reason any useful role?

Show a suggested answer

No. He says reason can find and create situations indirectly, even though direct reproach often achieves little.

What changes between the phlegmatic child and adult examples?

Show a suggested answer

The child is brought into companions’ varied interests; the adult is told to surround himself with tedium until thorough boredom breaks the habit.

What links the four adult examples?

Show a suggested answer

Each reckons with a tendency already present and rearranges its circumstances rather than trying to argue it away directly.
