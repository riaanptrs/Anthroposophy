Where the ego-bearer predominates, a choleric temperament results. When the astral body predominates we find a sanguine temperament.

What this passage means

These are the first two pairings in the complete fourfold map. The same column continues with etheric/phlegmatic and physical/melancholic. All four members belong to the same person; the pairings name predominance.

The focus of this lesson

Learn the mapping without confusing predominance with exclusive possession.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

The reading’s key idea

Steiner describes the same four members in every earthly human being: physical body, etheric or life body, astral body and bearer of the I. Their relationship is configured by the meeting of heredity and reincarnating individuality. One member stamps the combination through predominance: I-bearer/choleric, astral/sanguine, etheric/phlegmatic, physical/melancholic. Predominance is relational, not exclusive possession of a member.

The physical body belongs to bodily existence; the etheric is a supersensible life sheath integrated into it; the astral bears drives, passions, desires and changing sensations and thoughts; the I-bearer enables self-awareness. The lecture then links I, astral and etheric respectively with blood circulation, nerves and glands, and extends its account to bodily appearance. These are its spiritual physiological and physiognomic claims. The course uses the map to understand the text, while its ordinary example corrects the mistake that only one type has a given member.

Understand predominance within four shared members

The same four members in each person

Steiner’s explanation of temperament first needs a constitution in which different relationships are possible. He names four sheaths belonging to the earthly human being. The physical body is shared with the mineral world. The etheric or life body is a supersensible sheath integrated into the physical body. The astral body bears drives, passions, desires and changing sensations and thoughts. The bearer of the I enables self-awareness. These are four constituents of one person in his account, not four groups of people who each possess only one member.

Native capture 8, columns 1–2.

How the mapping follows

The preceding synthesis of hereditary and reincarnating streams determines their combination. Steiner says that one member gains predominance and gives the others its particular stamp. Predominance of the I-bearer produces the choleric temperament; the astral, sanguine; the etheric or life body, phlegmatic; the physical body, melancholic. The crucial word is relationship: a predominant member acts within a constitution that still includes the others. Although the text calls the I the highest member in its account of self-awareness, predominance is not a ranking of people’s worth or spiritual attainment.

Native capture 8, column 2.

From members to bodily expressions

Steiner next assigns bodily expressions to these members: the I to blood circulation, the astral to the nervous system, the etheric to glands, and the physical body to itself. He uses these correspondences to explain forceful will, shifting impressions, inward well-being and bodily heaviness. He then extends the account to build, facial expression, eye colour and gait. These claims reveal his proposed route from a spiritual constitution to observable form. They remain the book’s spiritual physiology and physiognomy; the course does not use anaemia, gland activity or appearance as measurements of a temperament.

Native captures 8, columns 2–3; 9, columns 1–3; 10, columns 1–2.

Read the map alongside mixtures

The later qualification that pure types never occur changes how the map must be used. Each person has a basic temperament mingled with degrees of the others, so a diagram of predominance summarizes a proposed organizing tendency, not an entire biography. The course’s mistaken reader, who thinks only sanguines have an astral body, demonstrates a conceptual error. Correct it by giving all four members to the same person and identifying the proposed emphasis. Then compare the mapping with the lecture’s portraits and educational routes, without treating every passing behaviour as evidence of one fixed spiritual cause.

Native capture 10, column 3; revisit capture 8, columns 1–2.

Connect the parts of the reading

What is lost if the four temperament pairings become four separate kinds of human being?

An explained answer: The shared fourfold constitution and the relation of predominance are lost. Every person has all four members in Steiner’s account; the two streams configure their relationship, and one stamps the combination. Mixed temperaments further qualify how that organizing tendency appears in a particular life.

Words for the reading

Etheric or life body: formative life in this framework. Astral body: bearer of inward responsiveness. I-bearer: member associated with self-awareness.

See the idea in an example

A reader says, “The sanguine person has an astral body, so the others do not.” The classification has been turned into four different kinds of human being.

The correction is relational: all four members are present, but the source assigns a different emphasis within their combination.

What does predominance mean here?

Show a suggested answer

One member gives the combination its particular stamp. All four remain constituents of the same human being; the pairing does not assign exclusive possession or greater human worth.

How does the mixture passage qualify the map?

Show a suggested answer

A basic temperament is mingled with degrees of the others. The map organizes proposed tendencies; it does not describe a pure type or the whole person.
