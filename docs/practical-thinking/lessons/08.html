Reach a proportionate conclusion

Compare alternatives before choosing a reversible next step.

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · Practical Training in Thought · GA 108 · § 36

Have the patience not to burst out immediately with a decision, but first set out different possibilities and, without deciding between them yet, let them remain quietly before you.

What this passage means

The chapter recommends deliberate comparison before judgment. In the course activity, relate each possibility to the observations that support it and explain why your conclusion remains provisional.

About the sources

See the idea in an example

After the sign moves, fewer people ask questions. One volunteer concludes that its old location caused every problem.

A conclusion should say how much the available observations support. Fewer questions may reflect a better location, fewer newcomers or someone informally helping. Compare the alternatives and ask what additional observation would distinguish them. You can still act under uncertainty: keep the new location for another session and review it. Distinguish the practical decision from the strength of the explanatory claim. A reversible choice need not wait for perfect knowledge.

What would be a proportionate conclusion?

Show a suggested answer

“Questions decreased after the move; the location may help. We will compare another session with similar attendance.” This preserves the result without claiming a single established cause.

How to assess your response

Explain the concept accurately, identify its source, distinguish an observation from an inference and show what changed after practice. A reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding. Compare a specific early entry with a later one; no change is also a result worth examining.
