Understand the key distinction more deeply

GA 108 · §§ 33–36 · Adams English translation

Compare actions, pause and return

Here the possibilities are courses of action, not just rival explanations of an event. First picture what taking each course would involve. Then set the question aside for a defined interval and return to it. The pause follows preparation; it does not replace it.

Course example: the workshop could keep one large sign or add small labels on each shelf. For each option, consider visibility, clarity and the work required. Write the alternatives before your break, then revisit them the next day if the decision can wait. Note which reasons remain and which you would revise.

Is “I will sleep and hope the answer arrives” the full instruction?

A hint

What must be done before the question is set aside?

Show an explained answer

No. First work through at least two possibilities. On returning, examine them again and give reasons for your choice.

Inner necessity and provisional judgment

In the lecture, inner necessity refers to connections belonging to the things and circumstances, rather than merely to personal preference. It does not mean that an intense feeling must be an instruction from the universe. A provisional conclusion is the best-supported conclusion you can presently give while naming what might change it.

After the pause, you might choose shelf labels because newcomers miss the single sign. State what supports that choice and when you will review it. A pressing decision may not allow an overnight wait; our course applies the exercise to an ordinary decision that can wait and uses a definite return time to distinguish pausing from avoidance.

If you feel more certain tomorrow, is that sufficient reason to choose?

A hint

Compare the strength of the feeling with the reasons and circumstances.

Show an explained answer

No. Explain what you now understand better. If only the feeling changed, acknowledge that and revisit the actual alternatives.

See the idea in an example

The workshop can keep one large sign or put labels on the shelves. You prefer the sign but have not examined what each option involves. The choice can wait until tomorrow.

Write what each action would involve, what supports it and what remains uncertain. Set a time to return and take the pause. On returning, compare again and record your reason for choosing and a condition for review. Pausing thus follows preparation of the decision.

Practise across three occasions

Use three entries: 1) set out two possible actions for an ordinary decision that can wait, with reasons and consequences; 2) after a defined pause, return and record what changed in your assessment; 3) after acting, examine the outcome and say whether you would revise the choice. These three moments are the course’s teaching structure. Do not delay an urgent decision just to fulfil the exercise.

Check your understanding

What distinguishes pausing a decision from simply avoiding it?

Show a suggested answer

You prepare alternatives and reasons, set a return time and resume the assessment. Avoidance need not include those steps.

How to assess your response

Explain the concept accurately, identify its source, distinguish an observation from an inference and show what changed after practice. A reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding. Compare a specific early entry with a later one; no change is also a result worth examining.
