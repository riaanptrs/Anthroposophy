Choose and sustain a line of thought

Practise returning to a chosen question without treating distraction as failure.

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · Practical Training in Thought · GA 108 · § 28

Whenever I have time, I will think about something I choose myself, something I bring into consciousness through my own deliberate choice.

Original German text; new English and Brazilian Portuguese study translations.

Compare with the German passage

Ich will, so oft ich Zeit habe, über etwas nachdenken, was ich mir selbst auswähle, was ich nur durch meine Willkür in mein Bewußtsein hereinbringe.

What this passage means

The contrast is between a chosen train of thought and being carried from concern to concern. The object may be ordinary; the capacity practised is directing and sustaining attention.

The focus of this lesson

Practise returning to a chosen question without treating distraction as failure.

See the idea in an example

While planning where to put the sign, you begin thinking about paint colours, then lunch, then a message you need to send.

Association can generate ideas, but it can also carry you away from the question you chose. Write the question at the top of the page. When you notice a diversion, briefly name it and return to one relevant connection. The measure here is your ability to notice and return, not an empty mind or an unbroken stretch of concentration. A deliberately short session helps you review what actually happened.

3 · Return to the source

Find the connection in the reading

Return to the passage at the top. Identify the words that support your explanation and give the source reference. If you have the book, read the surrounding section and add one point that the short excerpt leaves out.

Practise across three occasions

For three days, spend three minutes on one question about a familiar object’s use. Write connected reasons, not just a list of things associated with it. Afterward record a diversion and the connection that brought you back. Shorten the session if needed.

How to assess your response

Explain the concept accurately, identify its source, distinguish an observation from an inference and show what changed after practice. A reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding. Compare a specific early entry with a later one; no change is also a result worth examining.
