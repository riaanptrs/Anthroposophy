Thinking Must Meet Reality

A model boat crosses a basin when someone pushes it. If we explain its movement by describing only the boat, we have left out a condition that made the demonstration work.

Steiner makes a related objection to a rotating oil-drop demonstration used to illustrate planetary formation: the demonstrator supplied rotation. His broader spiritual interpretation is a further claim, and this historical objection does not refute present-day astronomy.

A model can reveal a relationship while omitting other features. Ask which conditions produced its result and which of those conditions also belong to the situation it represents.

Take an everyday plan, such as moving a heavy table. Picture the people, space and equipment it requires. An orderly plan on paper becomes practical only when those necessary conditions are considered.

Course explanation

What Steiner is saying

The student’s machine in paragraphs 7–8 shows a way of becoming enclosed in thought. Each imagined step may seem to follow, while a needed condition remains outside the picture. Steiner’s truck comparison brings the proposal back to how the arrangement would actually work. The task is to examine the relation between a thought and its object, rather than judge the thought only by how convincing it sounds.

His oil-drop demonstration makes this problem visible in a model. A person rotates a drop, and smaller drops separate from it. Steiner objects to transferring the result to a planetary explanation while forgetting the person who supplied the rotation. A complete account of this demonstration must include that action. He connects the objection with his spiritual interpretation of nature. This historical example is not a refutation of present-day astronomy; the practical lesson is to notice which conditions a model uses and whether the proposed situation shares them.

Steiner returns to his premise that thought can be drawn from a world already structured by thought. Paragraph 13 then introduces repeated practice intended to bring thinking into a fuller relationship with real events. Agreement with the premise does not finish the work. The learner must attend to details, connections and the correction of particular ideas. We begin with a small check of conditions; the next part follows an actual process carefully before attempting to explain it.

Key idea

A workable explanation includes the conditions that make the process possible. A neat model needs to be brought back to the situation it is meant to explain.

Course application

The workshop opens at 10:00. Laying out tools takes 15 minutes and checking them takes 10. A diagram shows two volunteers working in parallel, so someone proposes starting at 9:45. That timing assumes both jobs can begin together. If checking requires tools to be laid out first, the diagram has omitted a dependency. The arithmetic can be correct while the proposed arrangement does not match the work. Ask how the tasks actually connect before accepting the diagram. With the stated dependency, allow 15 + 10 = 25 minutes and begin at 9:35. Starting at 9:45 would finish at 10:10. The correction changes the model of the work, not merely the arithmetic.

Try it

Choose one familiar plan or model. Name a condition it needs, then check whether that condition holds in the actual situation. State what remains uncertain if you cannot check it yet.

Check your understanding

What condition does Steiner say is omitted from the oil-drop explanation?

Reveal the explanation

The rotating action is part of what produced the demonstration and must be included when its result is explained.

What should follow from accepting Steiner’s account of thought in reality?

Reveal the explanation

Paragraph 13 begins the practical training. The premise motivates that work rather than replacing it.

A meeting needs 8 minutes to arrange chairs, then 7 minutes to number them. Numbering can begin only after all chairs are placed. What is the minimum preparation time?

Reveal the explanation

8 + 7 = 15 minutes. More workers do not remove the stated dependency. The shorter calculation would be appropriate only if the tasks could actually run in parallel; that condition must be checked rather than imagined.

Connection
