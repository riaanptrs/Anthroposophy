See the idea in an example

A community workshop always puts instructions beside the door. Visitors repeatedly ask where the tools belong. The organizer says the instructions must be clear because they have always been there.

Familiarity tells us that a practice has a history; it does not tell us whether it achieves its purpose. A useful reason connects a choice to an aim and to information we can check. In this example, questions from visitors are relevant evidence. They do not yet tell us whether the problem is wording, location, language or something else. A better opening is to ask what visitors actually encounter. Keep that distinction throughout this course: a plausible story is a starting point for inquiry.
