Observe before explaining

Distinguish percept, mental picture and concept; understand where the astral body enters Steiner’s interpretation.

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · Practical Training in Thought · GA 108 · § 14

He observes the arrangement of clouds in the evening, the way the sun has set, and so on, and forms an exact mental picture of what he has observed.

Original German text; new English and Brazilian Portuguese study translations.

Read the original chapter or lecture (German) →

Compare with the German passage

Er beobachtet die Wolkenkonfiguration am Abend, die Art, wie die Sonne untergegangen ist und so weiter, und er bildet sich nun genau das Bild ein von dem, was er beobachtet hat.

What this passage means

The observation supplies the content of the mental picture. Accuracy comes before explanation, so describe what is present before attributing a cause.

The focus of this lesson

Distinguish percept, mental picture and concept; understand where the astral body enters Steiner’s interpretation.

A guided example: from seeing clouds to understanding

Work through one situation before starting the independent exercise. These steps are a teaching adaptation. They connect two readings without claiming that Steiner identifies a percept with the astral body. You may use the imagined scene below or observe clouds from a comfortable place.

1. Observe: what is a percept?

Imagine a broad grey cloud with a bright edge. You watch its outline change. A percept is the content given in observation: here, the particular colours, shapes and changes you see. Percept names what is perceived; perception names the activity of perceiving. We begin with a visual example, although Steiner’s use of percept is broader than sight.

Check your understanding: Which is an observation: “The edge became less distinct” or “A storm is coming”?

A hint

Ask which statement describes what appeared and which predicts something beyond it.

Show an explained answer

The changing edge is observed. A coming storm is a prediction requiring further grounds. Describing a percept uses words and thought; the exercise asks you to distinguish the observed content from the explanation you add.

2. Remember: what is a mental picture?

Look away and recall this particular cloud. That remembered image is a mental picture. Compare it with the scene when you look back: did you remember the bright edge or invent a darker centre? If the cloud has changed meanwhile, that change limits the comparison. In The Philosophy of Freedom, Steiner explains a mental picture as a concept retaining its connection to a particular percept. It is more than a photograph stored inside the head.

Check your understanding: Is the cloud you remember the same as the cloud currently before your eyes?

A hint

Distinguish remembering a previous encounter from observing what is present now.

Show an explained answer

The remembered cloud refers to your earlier perception. Looking again gives a present percept, which may differ because the cloud changed or because your recollection was incomplete.

3. Understand: what does a concept contribute?

A concept is an intelligible connection grasped through thinking. For example, understanding change involves relating an earlier condition to a later one. Two observations of the cloud give you something to compare. Thinking connects them as a change in outline. Explaining why that change happened requires more than naming it: an attractive explanation about wind is still a hypothesis until adequately supported.

Check your understanding: Does remembering two cloud shapes tell you why one became the other?

A hint

Separate recognizing a change from identifying its cause.

Show an explained answer

No. You can understand that the outline changed without knowing its cause. Keep both observations clear and leave the causal question open. Steiner’s exercise in GA 108 §§ 14–16 asks for this patience.

4. Read Steiner’s spiritual interpretation

In GA 108 § 16 Steiner refers to invisible vehicles or members of the human being. In § 17 he explicitly names the astral body as a bearer of thought and mental imagery. Vehicle here means a bearer or means of activity. In § 18 he claims that faithfully forming images of events, while holding back arbitrary speculation, lets the thoughts active in the universe impress themselves upon the astral body. This is his spiritual explanation of the exercise; an accurate memory alone does not establish it.

Check your understanding: Is the astral body another name for the percept or the remembered cloud?

A hint

Ask what is experienced, what is remembered, and what Steiner says bears the inner activity.

Show an explained answer

No. The percept is what observation presents; the mental picture retains a relation to that encounter. The astral body belongs to Steiner’s account of the human being who thinks and forms images. It is not a visible anatomical organ. The broader expression “invisible vehicles” need not always mean only the astral body, but §§ 17–18 focus on it.

5. Connect the two readings

The Philosophy of Freedom, chapter VI §§ 4–6, distinguishes percept, concept and mental picture to explain knowing and individual experience. Practical Training in Thought, §§ 14–18, gives an observation exercise and its spiritual interpretation. Our teaching comparison is that accurate observation and faithful recollection provide material for understanding. The chapter in The Philosophy of Freedom does not establish the lecture’s claim about the astral body merely by distinguishing those three terms.

Check your understanding: Which reading would you return to for the mental-picture distinction, and which for the astral-body claim?

A hint

One passage examines the relationship between percept and concept; the other interprets a practice.

Show an explained answer

Use GA 4, chapter VI §§ 4–6, for the mental picture’s connection to a particular percept. Use GA 108 §§ 17–18 for the astral body. This keeps the source of each insight clear.

6. Try a short complete explanation

Before practising independently, complete these sentences aloud or in your notebook: “What I observed was …”; “What I remembered was …”; “The connection I understood was …”; “What I still do not know is …”. Then explain in a separate sentence what Steiner claims about the astral body. You do not need to report a spiritual experience.

Check your understanding: Can you connect all four parts without turning a guess into a fact?

A hint

Use the cloud’s outline. You may understand that it changed while leaving the cause undecided.

Show an explained answer

For example: “I saw a broad cloud with a bright edge. I recalled that particular shape after looking away. Comparing two observations, I understood that its outline had changed, but I do not yet know why. Steiner says this attentive work with images can act upon the astral body.” The last sentence reports his interpretation, rather than an additional observation of the cloud.

See the idea in an example

You observe a broad cloud with a bright edge. You look away, recall its shape, and then look again. The edge looks less distinct. You wonder whether the cloud changed, whether your memory was incomplete, and what could explain the difference.

Separate the present percept from the mental picture connected with your earlier observation. Compare concrete details before deciding what caused the difference. Understanding a change does not require already knowing its cause. Steiner’s account of the astral body is a spiritual interpretation of this activity, given in GA 108 §§ 17–18.

3 · Return to the source

Find the connection in the reading

Return to the passage at the top. Identify the words that support your explanation and give the source reference. If you have the book, read the surrounding section and add one point that the short excerpt leaves out.

Practise across three occasions

On three occasions, observe clouds from a comfortable place or use a familiar object. Note three details, look away and recall them. Look again and record a match or difference, allowing for the object having changed. Distinguish what you observed, remembered, understood and still do not know. Finally, explain Steiner’s astral-body interpretation separately. This immediate comparison is a teaching adaptation; in GA 108 §§ 14–16 Steiner proposes comparing observations on successive days.

Show a suggested answer
