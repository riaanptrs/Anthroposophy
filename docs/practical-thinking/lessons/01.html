The Attitude Needed for Thinking

What attitude toward thinking does Steiner ask us to develop?

Steiner source

Read Steiner

Rudolf Steiner · Practical Training In Thought · GA 108 · 18 January 1909

Read the numbered web paragraphs 9–12. One continuous lecture; the lesson titles are course divisions.

The thoughts must be there in the very plan and structure of the things.

George (Kaufmann) Adams translation; excerpt from paragraph 9.

Open the full lecture → · Source and edition notes

Course explanation

What Steiner is saying

Steiner says we cannot train thought properly if we regard it as something occurring only inside our own head. His starting point is explicitly spiritual: if thinking can understand things, thoughts must already belong to their plan and structure. Human thinking can discover that content. This is his account of the relation between spirit, thought and world, rather than a claim that whatever we happen to imagine becomes true.

He illustrates the idea with a watch. Its parts were arranged through a maker’s thought, and someone studying it can understand those relationships. He then extends the comparison to nature: natural things, he says, are works of spiritual beings, and our thinking can rethink what has been laid into them. The watch helps explain the proposed relationship; its construction alone does not establish the additional claim about nature. Thinking about a plant does not, in this passage, make the plant grow.

The attitude Steiner seeks is confidence that the world has thought-content to be discovered, with a willingness to let things guide our thinking. Confidence in that possibility differs from confidence in a first answer. We still have to encounter what actually happens. Our application makes this receptive attitude concrete by attending to an inconvenient observation; its outcome can show something about our inquiry, but cannot by itself establish the lecture’s spiritual foundation.

Key idea

For Steiner, thought can discover a spiritual order already active in things. That confidence calls for inquiry; it does not settle each explanation in advance.

Course application

You designed a new workshop sign. One visitor likes it; another still cannot find the tools. You are tempted to count only the compliment. Giving the difficulty equal attention does not require accepting every criticism as correct. It means asking what the visitor encountered before deciding whether the problem was the sign. Your satisfaction with the design and your investigation of its use can both be acknowledged.

Try it

Take one explanation you would like to be right. Name one relevant observation that would be inconvenient for it, and one fair question you could ask to understand that observation.

Check your understanding

What is Steiner’s spiritual premise in this passage?

Reveal the explanation

Paragraphs9–12 place thought in the structure of things and describe nature as the work of spiritual beings.

What does the watch example establish on its own?

Reveal the explanation

The watch illustrates thought embodied in a human creation. Steiner’s extension to nature is an additional claim.
