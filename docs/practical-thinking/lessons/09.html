How Thinking Changes Through Practice

What change does Steiner seek beyond agreement with his teachings?

Steiner source

Read Steiner

Rudolf Steiner · Practical Training In Thought · GA 108 · 18 January 1909

Read the numbered web paragraphs 42–48. One continuous lecture; the lesson titles are course divisions.

It is more important that he should develop the faculty for seeing things and penetrating things correctly.

George (Kaufmann) Adams translation; excerpt from paragraph 48.

Open the full lecture → · Source and edition notes

Course explanation

What Steiner is saying

The conclusion returns to the opening problem of habit. In paragraphs 42–45, Steiner argues that reasons can conceal feelings and accustomed ways of thinking. He locates change within the anthroposophical movement: shared spiritual study, in his account, widens both thought and feeling. He is describing a transformation of the outlook from which we judge, beyond acquiring arguments.

Paragraph 46 asks us to learn to love what is real and objective so that reasons can become decisive. Having a feeling about an outcome does not itself settle what happened. You may care deeply about your workshop and still reconsider your explanation. The task is to let thought follow the thing, instead of making the thing support a favorite thought.

Steiner explicitly recommends practising with things of Nature least altered by human civilization. His final emphasis is the faculty of seeing and understanding correctly, rather than assent to a doctrine. Our review applies this aim to one observable change in practice. Such a change can be modest or still incomplete; it does not by itself establish every spiritual claim made in the lecture.

Key idea

Look for a specific change in how you see and think, then continue practising with the things themselves.

Steiner’s exercise

In paragraph 47, Steiner asks for the exercises to be practised with natural things least altered by human civilization. Return, for example, to observing a natural process and holding its successive appearances clearly in mind. This continues the earlier source exercise; compare what is observed without imposing an explanation before you know enough about the process.

Course application

Your first workshop note said the instructions were clear. A later observation distinguishes the wording, sign position and where visitors look. That is a specific change in observation and judgment. You may still feel disappointed that the sign failed. Recognizing the feeling lets you ask whether your reasons fit the observations without having to stop caring.

Try it

Recall or reread one early observation and a later one of similar difficulty. Name one change in observation, patience, mental pictures, causal reasoning or judgment, and choose one exercise to continue. A remaining difficulty is also a useful result.

Check your understanding

What does Steiner emphasize at the close of the lecture?

Reveal the explanation

Paragraph 48 gives greater importance to the faculty of seeing and penetrating things correctly than to accepting a teaching.

What material does paragraph 47 especially recommend for practice?

Reveal the explanation

Steiner makes this preference for things of Nature explicit, while relating the resulting training to ordinary occupations.

Connection

The four parts form one practice: thinking meets reality through observation, sustained attention, mental representation and judgment. Use the part synthesis to review one whole sequence, then the optional exercise map to select what to practise next.
