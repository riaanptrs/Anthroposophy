2 · Work through the explanation

A worked example

The workshop opens at 10:00. Setting out tools takes 15 minutes and checking them takes 10. Someone says to begin at 9:35 but cannot explain why.

A remembered answer may be right while its reasoning remains unavailable. Reconstruct the relation: both tasks must finish before opening; if they happen one after the other, 25 minutes are needed. Now change the conditions: two people can work simultaneously, but only if checking does not depend on setting out. The changed answer depends on that relationship, not just on subtracting different numbers. A sequence diagram is useful because it makes dependencies explicit.

Practise across three occasions

On three days, reconstruct one small completed task as a sequence. Mark which step depended on another. Then change one condition and explain how the sequence would change. Do not invent missing facts; mark uncertain steps.
