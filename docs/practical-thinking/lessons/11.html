Pause Before Judgment

Why does Steiner ask us to pause after examining the possibilities?

Steiner source

Read Steiner

Rudolf Steiner · Practical Training In Thought · GA 108 · 18 January 1909

Read the numbered web paragraphs 35–36. One continuous lecture; the lesson titles are course divisions.

If the circumstances permit, defer your action to the next day, and then once more bring the two possibilities before your mind.

George (Kaufmann) Adams translation; excerpt from paragraph 35.

Open the full lecture → · Source and edition notes

Course explanation

What Steiner is saying

The impulse to finish a decision can be so strong that we mistake relief for understanding. Having considered two actions, we want to settle the matter immediately. Steiner asks us to tolerate the unfinished question instead. Here, uncertainty is a stage of the work, rather than a failure to think.

The preceding exercise has already supplied the preparation: imagine what each action would involve. In paragraphs 35–36, Steiner then asks us to set the matter aside and return. He describes the possibilities as continuing to work within us when the conscious Ego is absent. In his account, this lets the inner necessity of the things enrich our thinking.

The condition matters: defer action if the circumstances permit. Sleeping alone is insufficient, and a pause does not replace comparing the actions. Return to them and examine what you now understand. This is a training of patience in ordinary judgment; treating every delay or stronger feeling as a correct answer would miss its purpose.

Key idea

Prepare the alternatives, pause when possible, and return to examine them.

Steiner’s exercise

Steiner asks you to stop thinking about a question after laying out its possibilities. If circumstances allow, defer the action until the next day and bring the possibilities before your mind again. He also applies this method when someone asks your advice: think through two or more alternatives before setting them aside, then return to the matter.

Course application

The workshop can keep one large sign or add labels to the shelves. You have considered where newcomers look and what each change requires. Because the choice can wait, leave it until tomorrow. On returning, compare those same actions: has a relevant connection become clearer, or have you merely become more attached to your favorite?

Try it

Choose one small, prepared decision that can wait. Set a time to return, take the pause, and then say what supports each alternative before choosing. One sentence about what became clearer is enough.

Check your understanding

What must come before the pause?

Reveal the explanation

Steiner says sleeping on a question is insufficient: the alternatives must first have been considered.

When does Steiner suggest deferring the action until the next day?

Reveal the explanation

The source makes the delay conditional on the circumstances, then asks us to return to the possibilities.

Connection

The pause prepares a renewed judgment. Next, we consider how to reach a conclusion without confusing a plausible story with the actual connection between events.
