What this passage means

“Where we understand” limits the exercise. Predict from known conditions, compare with the event, and investigate any mismatch. It is a way to correct a model, not practise guessing.

About the sources

See the idea in an example

You move the sign beside the tool shelf and predict that fewer visitors will ask where things belong.

A prediction becomes useful when you state what would count as its outcome before observing it. “The workshop will improve” is too vague. “In the next comparable session, fewer visitors will ask where to return tools” can be checked, though visitor numbers and experience also matter. A correct prediction does not prove your explanation: several explanations may predict the same result. An incorrect prediction helps locate a missing condition. Record both outcomes without rewriting your original expectation.

3 · Return to the source

Find the connection in the reading

Return to the passage at the top. Identify the words that support your explanation and give the source reference. If you have the book, read the surrounding section and add one point that the short excerpt leaves out.

Practise across three occasions

Make one low-stakes prediction about your own routine on each of three occasions. Write the expected result, its reason, the actual result and one changed condition. Use a familiar task rather than predicting another person’s private intentions.

How to assess your response

Explain the concept accurately, identify its source, distinguish an observation from an inference and show what changed after practice. A reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding. Compare a specific early entry with a later one; no change is also a result worth examining.
