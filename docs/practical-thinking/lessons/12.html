Arrive at Conclusions

The room is dark and a lamp is unplugged. You conclude that the unplugged lamp caused the darkness. But the room may already have been dark because the bulb failed before someone unplugged it.

Steiner's example of a fatal fall makes the same logical issue sharper: an apparent cause might instead follow an earlier event. His alternative is a possibility to consider, not an established diagnosis.

After comparing possibilities and allowing a suitable pause, return to what distinguishes them. A conclusion needs reasons related to the case, rather than merely a vivid story or a stronger feeling.

For the lamp, check the bulb and supply before settling the explanation. For your own decision, state why one course now fits better and what new information could change that judgment. A responsible conclusion can remain revisable.

Course explanation

What Steiner is saying

In paragraphs 35–36, the decision follows a return to possibilities already considered. Steiner attributes a more thorough decision to allowing the inner necessity of the things to work in us. The aim is to understand their connections better, rather than to choose whichever thought now feels most forceful. Paragraph 37 brings this inward work back to everyday occupations.

His tree example makes the danger concrete. A man falls from a tree and is found dead. The nearby conclusion is that the fall killed him. But Steiner proposes another possibility: a fatal heart attack could have caused the fall. The outward sequence can look the same while the causal connection runs differently. He presents a possible reversal, not a verified medical finding.

A conclusion therefore needs attention to what would distinguish the alternatives. The example continues the lecture's training in patience and thinking with the things themselves. It does not supply a formal decision formula. In the course application, naming your reasons and what would change the judgment makes that renewed examination visible, without turning personal certainty into evidence.

Key idea

Return to the possibilities and let the actual connections guide the conclusion.

Steiner’s exercise

Return to the possibilities after the prepared pause, as Steiner directs in paragraphs 35–36. Examine them again before deciding. Paragraph 38 adds an example to think through: the same observed fall may be an apparent cause or the effect of a prior event. This causal example is an illustration, rather than a separate prescribed exercise.

Course application

After considering the workshop sign and shelf labels, you choose labels because newcomers have been looking directly at the shelves. That observation supports your choice. It does not yet prove that labels will solve the problem. Say what you expect them to change and which later observation would make you reconsider the decision.

Try it

Return to the decision from the previous lesson. State your conclusion, one observation supporting it, and one fact that could change it. If a cause is involved, ask whether your proposed cause might instead be an effect.

Check your understanding

What does Steiner's tree example show?

Reveal the explanation

He offers a possible prior heart attack to show how cause and effect can be reversed, rather than establishing what happened in a particular case.

What should happen when we return to a prepared decision?

Reveal the explanation

The pause belongs to a sequence of preparation and return. Its purpose is a more thorough judgment.

A room gets noisy and a teacher closes the door. Someone concludes that closing the door caused the noise. What is missing?

Reveal the explanation

In the supplied sequence, noise preceded and prompted closing the door. The proposed cause is a response to the event. Doors can affect sound in other circumstances, so examine this case rather than replace one hasty generalization with another.

Connection

A conclusion is one moment in a continuing practice. The final lesson asks how repeated attention changes observation, patience, mental pictures, causal reasoning and judgment.
