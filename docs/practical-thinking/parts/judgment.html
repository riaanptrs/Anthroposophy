Part 4 of IV · Synthesis

Practical Judgment · synthesis

Key ideas

  • A useful pause follows the examination of alternatives and ends with a return to them.
  • A convincing sequence of events can still reverse cause and effect.
  • Practice aims at seeing things more faithfully, beyond agreement with a teaching.

How they connect

Observation supplies the material; chosen attention and mental pictures let you work with it; comparison, patience and renewed examination prepare a practical judgment. The conclusion remains connected to what actually happens.

One deeper exercise · course application

Course application: revisit one ordinary decision that can wait. Describe two possible actions and the circumstances supporting each. Set it aside, return at an agreed time, and give your reason for choosing. After the action, compare the result with what you imagined and name one point you would now revise. You may keep a short note, but a notebook is optional.

Optional model

A workshop compares one large sign with labels beside the materials. After a pause, the organizer chooses labels because newcomers look at the shelves. Later, a visitor still misses one label: the organizer changes its position. The review concerns a specific observation and adjustment, rather than a claim to have become a perfect thinker.
