Part 2 of IV · Synthesis

Learning to Observe · synthesis

Key ideas

  • Keep observations distinct from explanations.
  • Relate clear successive pictures without inventing missing events.
  • Construct a possible cause, then inquire, compare and correct.

How they connect

Observation supplies the material; comparison reveals what changed; inquiry tests an account of how it arose. These are distinct tasks that help thinking remain answerable to the event.

One deeper exercise · course application

Course application: choose one accessible natural process and observe it across several days. On the first day, preserve concrete details without requiring a cause. On later days, add new observations and compare the pictures. At the end, revisit any initial explanation. If you can check a proposed cause, do so; if you cannot, leave it open. Record only what you need to distinguish an observation, an imagined transition and a checked or unchecked account.

Optional model

One possible example: on Day 1 a leaf is tightly folded; on Day 2 it is more open. The imagined opening relates the two pictures but was not witnessed. “It opened because the sunlight increased” remains a possible explanation unless relevant information supports it. A precise change can be recorded even while its cause remains unknown.
