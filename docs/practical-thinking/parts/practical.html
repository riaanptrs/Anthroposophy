Part 1 of IV · Synthesis

What Makes Thinking Practical? · synthesis

Key ideas

  • A familiar habit and a clever new plan both need to meet actual circumstances.
  • Steiner grounds inquiry in his claim that thought belongs to the world, not only to the observer.
  • A model becomes useful when we examine the conditions connecting it to the real situation.

How they connect

These ideas lead to observation: attend to a process carefully before imposing an explanation.

One deeper exercise · course application

Course Application: Choose one routine whose usefulness you usually take for granted. Explain its purpose and the reason you normally give. Observe one occasion on which it is used, then identify a detail your explanation included and a condition it overlooked. Decide what to examine next; you do not need to replace the routine immediately.

Optional model

In a workshop, “a sign by the door tells everyone where tools belong” assumes that people notice and understand it. Observing one visitor may reveal that the sign is hidden by an open door. The routine’s purpose can be retained while its assumed visibility becomes a question to check.
