Practical Training in Thought

Ten lessons for observing carefully, testing expectations and revising conclusions.

Read the book with guidance

Every lesson starts with a selected source passage and explains its meaning and place in the chapter. Read the core ideas, then use the example and questions to check your understanding.

Short excerpts are starting points for close reading. The source credit identifies the edition; the lesson’s reading assignment gives the wider section.

Begin the course →

A rhythm you can return to

Read → understand → practise → return and revise. Allow about 15–25 minutes for a lesson, then three short practice sessions on separate occasions. These are planning estimates; source reading takes additional time. Work at your own pace.

Keep dates, observations and revisions in the lesson notebook. The final lesson returns to your earlier entries. Saving is optional and stays in this browser; export a copy for safekeeping.

Which exercise fits your question?

Follow the lessons in order on your first reading. Afterwards use this map to revisit a specific difficulty. Observing, predicting, investigating a cause, directing attention, remembering and deciding are related tasks with different aims.

Sources and method

Source companion: Rudolf Steiner, Practical Training in Thought, Karlsruhe, 18 January 1909, GA 108. Reading references use the numbered paragraphs in George Adams’s online translation.

The situations, journal prompts and feedback here are original adaptations. They are not Sophia Institute assignments or a reproduction of Brian’s lectures. Read Steiner’s spiritual explanations as attributed claims; practising an exercise does not establish them.
