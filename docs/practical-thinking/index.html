Practical Training in Thought

A guided study of Rudolf Steiner’s exercises in observation, concentration, mental representation and practical judgment.

This course follows Steiner’s Practical Training in Thought and turns the lecture into a progressive series of readings and exercises. Each lesson begins with Steiner’s source, explains the idea, and then asks you to practise it in ordinary life.

The four parts

Part 1 / IV

What Makes Thinking Practical?

Begin with Steiner’s contrast between habit-bound thinking and thought that learns from things. Examine the spiritual attitude he gives this training and the real conditions that explanations must include.

  1. Practical and Impractical Thinking

    Distinguish inherited habits and clever plans from thinking that can meet real circumstances.

  2. The Attitude Needed for Thinking

    Understand Steiner’s spiritual account of thought in the world and the receptive attitude he connects with it.

  3. Thinking Must Meet Reality

    Examine the real conditions an explanation depends on rather than trusting its internal neatness.

Part 2 / IV

Learning to Observe

Exact observations give thinking something definite to follow. Comparing successive states prepares causal inquiry without turning an imagined interval into a fact.

  1. Observe Before Explaining

    Form a faithful picture before imposing an explanation on an unfamiliar process.

  2. Follow Change Through Time

    Relate successive pictures while keeping observed states distinct from an imagined transition.

  3. Investigate Causes Without Guessing

    Construct a possible earlier cause, inquire into the facts and correct the account.

Part synthesis and deeper practice →

Part 3 / IV

Training Thought

Learn to choose a subject, form a detailed mental picture and prepare alternative actions before deciding.

  1. Sustain a Chosen Thought

    Deliberately choose and sustain a subject outside your habitual concerns.

  2. Build an Accurate Mental Picture

    Reconstruct details and distinguish remembered material from deliberate invention.

  3. Compare Possibilities

    Picture alternative actions before committing to a decision.

Part synthesis and deeper practice →

Part 4 / IV

Practical Judgment

Return to prepared possibilities, form a conclusion that fits the circumstances, and notice how practice changes your way of seeing.

  1. Pause Before Judgment

    Set a prepared question aside when circumstances allow, then return to the alternatives before deciding.

  2. Arrive at Conclusions

    Return to the alternatives and form a judgment that follows the circumstances, while checking the direction of cause and effect.

  3. How Thinking Changes Through Practice

    Recognize one specific change in your thinking and choose further practice that stays attentive to the things themselves.
