Soul Development and Individuality

Lesson 7 / 28

What changes when a person examines impulses and asks what is true or right?

You accuse a colleague of missing a deadline, then find a receipt showing the work arrived on time. Reasoning can defend your irritation or help correct your accusation. The standard guiding thought makes a difference.

Steiner distinguishes sentient soul, intellectual soul and consciousness soul. Sentient life includes sensations and desires; intellectual activity organizes experience and seeks ways to meet needs.

Consciousness soul concerns orientation toward truth and goodness beyond personal preference. It does not mean all conscious experience, just as intellectual soul is not an IQ category.

The I gathers these capacities within one developing life. Correcting the accusation illustrates a changed relationship among feeling, thought and truth. Development need not eliminate feeling or practical intelligence; it changes how they cooperate.

Course explanation

What Steiner is saying

The sentient soul includes inward sensations, desires and impulses. When thinking works with this life, organizing experience and finding ways to meet needs, Steiner speaks of the intellectual soul.

Thinking can also ask whether something is true or good even when the answer displeases us. Consciousness soul names this orientation beyond personal preference; it does not mean every ordinary conscious experience.

The I gathers these connected capacities and can work upon them. Development concerns their changing relationship within one person. Here individuality means this developing inward centre; the next part examines Steiner’s further claim about its continuity.

Suppose you accuse a colleague of missing a deadline, then find a dated receipt showing that the work arrived on time. Clever reasoning could defend your original irritation rather than correct your accusation. The consciousness-soul distinction concerns the standard guiding thought: is the person seeking a justification for a preference, or allowing truth to revise it? The I brings these activities into an individual life. Development changes their relationship; it does not remove feeling or practical intelligence.

Key concept: Soul development

The I’s developing work with impulses and thought, including orientation toward truth and goodness beyond preference.

Course example

You feel irritated, reconsider what happened and discover your accusation was mistaken. Correcting it despite reluctance distinguishes the feeling, reflection and a standard beyond your preference.

Important distinction

Intellectual soul is not an IQ category. Consciousness soul is not all awareness, and development does not eliminate sentient life.
