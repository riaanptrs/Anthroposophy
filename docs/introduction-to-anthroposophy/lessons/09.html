Karma

Lesson 9 / 28

How does Steiner connect deeds with the circumstances of renewed lives?

After breaking a promise, someone learns to become more reliable. The new habit is an inward fruit, while the earlier disappointment still belongs to the relationship. Learning does not automatically repair that consequence.

Steiner extends both continuations of deeds across earthly lives. Karma concerns their relation to destiny; reincarnation concerns repeated embodiment. They are connected teachings with different explanatory roles.

The spiritual account makes a strong claim about earlier deeds and later circumstances. The ordinary promise example cannot disclose such a history. Present hardship does not justify declaring that someone deserved it or withholding help.

Ask what changed for each person and what repair is possible now. Present action still creates consequences. Karma is also distinct from the post-death release of bodily attachments discussed next.

Course explanation

What Steiner is saying

A deed has two continuations in Steiner’s argument. It changes the surrounding world, and it also changes the person who acts. What one has learned and what one has brought about can remain significant after the original situation has passed.

He extends this relation across earthly lives. Karma names the soul’s self-created relation to destiny, while reincarnation names the spirit’s repeated embodiment. The supplied chapter makes a strong claim that later circumstances, joys and sorrows depend on earlier deeds. This is Steiner’s account, not a diagnosis of someone’s life.

Present action still matters: people continue acting within relationships and creating new consequences. As a course distinction, we should not treat suffering as evidence of deserved punishment or as a reason to withhold help. The selected passage supplies no method for identifying a person’s supposed earlier wrongdoing from a present misfortune.

The supporting passage in Esoteric Science develops the interpersonal aspect. Steiner describes love and friendship acquiring a spiritual bond that can continue after death and bring people together again. This is separately identified support, not an expanded quotation from the supplied Theosophy chapter.

Consider someone who learns to keep a promise after having broken one. The changed habit is an inward fruit, while the earlier disappointment still belongs to the relationship with the other person. Learning does not automatically repair that relationship. This makes the two continuations of a deed distinct before Steiner extends them across lives: acquired development and an altered field of relationships.

Key concept: Karma

Steiner’s account of the continuing relation between deeds, soul-life and the circumstances of renewed embodiment.

Course example

The source compares a mechanic and a bank clerk moving to another country. Changed surroundings do not erase their different acquired capacities. Steiner uses this ordinary continuity as an analogy for his further cross-life account.

Important distinction

Karma concerns continuing consequences across lives. It differs from reincarnation itself and from the post-death release of desires dependent on bodily organs.
