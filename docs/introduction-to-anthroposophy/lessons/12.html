The Soul World

Central question

What does Steiner mean by a world related through inward affinity?

Why this matters

Feelings and desires have already appeared in the account of soul life. Steiner connects them with a wider soul world, whose relationships he distinguishes from physical distance and impact.

Course explanation

What Steiner is saying

Steiner describes the soul world as a reality connected with desires, feelings and inward responses. It is wider than one person’s private experience or remembered pictures. Its formations relate through affinity, rather than being ordinary physical objects whose interaction is described only by position and bodily impact.

He calls the attractive, connective tendency sympathy. Antipathy names the tendency to exclude and maintain separation. A formation’s relation to its surroundings depends on how these forces interact. Here the words identify directions of relationship; their everyday social meanings are too narrow.

Attraction can remain directed toward one’s own satisfaction. Sympathy therefore does not automatically mean kindness, just as antipathy is not simply a verdict of moral evil. The source differentiates their combinations and development, but learning the two tendencies is sufficient for this introductory map.

The soul world is not placed outside the physical world as a distant destination. Steiner says the worlds are not spatially separated and their different regions interpenetrate. Adding this world means proposing a different kind of reality and relationship, rather than adding rarefied physical matter to a map.

Key concept: Soul world

In Steiner’s account, a wider reality related to soul experience, organized through connective sympathy and separating antipathy.

Course example

Course illustration: attraction draws you toward an experience; aversion turns you away. These directions help explain the technical words. The familiar feelings illustrate their meaning without establishing the independent soul world Steiner describes.

Important distinction

Technical sympathy is broader than friendliness, and technical antipathy broader than hostility. They describe relationships rather than sorting people into good and bad categories.
