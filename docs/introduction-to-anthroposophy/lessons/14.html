How the Three Worlds Relate

Lesson 14 / 28

How does one human being participate in all three worlds during earthly life?

You see that a shared chair is broken, feel concern and understand how to repair it. An understood intention can then become a personally meaningful act carried out through the body.

Steiner describes human participation in physical, soul and spiritual worlds simultaneously. The soul mediates bodily encounter and understanding, and also intention and bodily action.

The worlds are more than three subjective viewpoints. He proposes interpenetrating realities, with sensible forms expressing soul and spiritual formative activity. The ordinary repair example prepares the distinction without proving that constitution of the world.

Keep coexistence separate from the teaching about death. The first concerns relationships within embodied life; the second concerns a proposed change in bodily participation. A diagram of one should not silently become an explanation of the other.

Concern and the personal significance of helping concern soul life; understanding the joint and the reason for repair illustrate participation in intelligible relationships. Steiner describes simultaneous participation, not travel through three locations; the example clarifies that account without proving the three worlds.

Course explanation

What Steiner is saying

Steiner says a human being participates in the three worlds simultaneously. Through the body, a person encounters sensible forms and acts upon them. Through the soul, that encounter has a personal response involving feeling, desire and affinity. Through the spirit, understanding opens a relation to meaning and formative reality.

The soul mediates between bodily encounter and spiritual understanding, and between an intended idea and bodily action. These relationships belong to one human life; distinguishing them does not divide the person into three separate physical objects. The different descriptions make their connections easier to examine.

The account also makes a claim about the world beyond personal responses: Steiner presents sensible forms as expressions of soul and spiritual formative realities. The three worlds therefore do not mean only three subjective viewpoints on a material object. He describes interpenetrating kinds of reality.

The parallel fields below are an original teaching arrangement created for this course. They show coexistence and distinct relationships. The separate death panel concerns a change in bodily participation and attachment, preserving the distinction between a simultaneous-life map and the claimed transition beyond physical life.

Receiving and acting show the mediation in opposite directions. A bodily encounter can become inward experience and an understood relation. An understood intention can become personally significant and be carried into bodily action. This does not identify the three worlds with those psychological steps; it illustrates the human relationships that prepare Steiner’s further account of interpenetrating realities.

Key concept: Simultaneous participation

One human being’s related bodily, soul and spiritual life within the three interpenetrating worlds described by Steiner.

Three worlds in Steiner’s account

DomainRelationship to the human being
Physical worldThrough the body, one encounters sensible forms and acts in earthly surroundings.
Soul worldThrough the soul, one responds with feeling, desire, attraction and aversion, participating in a wider world of affinities.
Spiritual worldThrough the spirit, one understands meaning and participates, in Steiner’s account, in creative formative realities.

A changing relationship after death

In Steiner’s account, bodily death ends the instrument of sensory perception and earthly action. Longings dependent on bodily organs are gradually relinquished, while spiritually assimilable fruits of experience remain and develop as faculties. This release of bodily attachment is distinguished from karma.

Course example

Course adaptation of the source’s meadow example: you see a flower’s colours, feel joy, and understand relationships in its form and growth. Sensory encounter, personal response and understanding are distinguishable within one experience; this illustrates the relationships without proving the whole world-description.

Important distinction

The diagram’s parallel boxes organize the lesson; they do not indicate three destinations, physical distances or levels of a person’s worth.
