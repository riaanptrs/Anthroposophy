Course explanation

What Steiner is saying

The developmental account in GA 13 asks what capacities are emerging in a human life. For the question of Waldorf education, this supplies an anthropological background: teaching is considered in relation to the growing human being, rather than only to the material to be learned.

In his 1909 temperament lecture, Steiner adds a further educational question. Learners of a similar age can differ in their interests and responses. He argues against trying to homogenize these differences and proposes working with capacities already present.

His examples give relationships a role in education: affection, confidence in a teacher’s competence, compassion and the interests of other children can help engage different learners. These are proposals in this particular lecture, not instructions to classify a child by appearance or assign a fixed personality label.

Development and individual responsiveness are therefore two useful starting questions for further Waldorf study. The concrete account of artistic teaching, classroom rhythm and curriculum belongs in the specialist educational sources identified below.

Key concept: Responsiveness to the learner

Considering both developing capacities and individual interests when asking how teaching can engage a learner.

Sources needed for fuller study

  • The Education of the Child (GA 34) and a Waldorf-specific primary educational lecture series are needed for artistic and imaginative teaching, classroom rhythm, curriculum and school chronology. Educational effectiveness requires separate evidence.

Course example

A pupil repeatedly loses interest. Instead of only repeating “pay attention,” a teacher asks which existing interest or trusted relationship could provide a starting point for the subject. This course example illustrates the lecture’s principle without assigning the pupil a temperament.

Important distinction

A shared developmental stage does not make learners identical. The 1909 temperament lecture gives educational context; a Waldorf-specific primary text is needed to explain a school programme.
