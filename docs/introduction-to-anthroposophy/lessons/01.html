What Is Anthroposophy?

Central question

What does Steiner mean when he calls Anthroposophy a path of knowledge?

Why this matters

Before learning particular anthroposophical ideas, we need to understand what Steiner says his project is. His opening definition places the activity of knowing at its centre. That gives us a question to carry through the course: what kind of knowledge does he think can connect a person with the wider world?

Anthroposophy is a path of knowledge, to guide the Spiritual in the human being to the Spiritual in the universe.

Related source sections
  • The Story of My Life · GA 28 · XXXI · Digital PDF pages 170–171
Course explanation

What Steiner is saying

Steiner begins with a path, rather than a catalogue of teachings. A path of knowledge involves seeking, understanding and developing a way of knowing. The definition gives it a direction: from the spiritual in the human being toward the spiritual in the universe. Later lessons will ask what these terms mean within his account.

The two sides of the definition belong together. Steiner wants questions about our own being to lead toward understanding a reality wider than ourselves. His proposed spiritual knowledge is therefore more than a description of private feelings, even though personal experience supplies the questions that motivate it.

In Thought 1, he describes those questions as an inner need, comparable to hunger and thirst. This explains why someone might undertake the path. It does not mean that finding an answer comforting makes the answer true. The need to understand and the work of knowing have different roles.

Thoughts 2–3 then state the possibility on which the path depends: Steiner argues that the limits of knowledge gained through the senses need not be the limits of all insight. He believes further capacities can arise within human soul life. That is his starting claim, whose proposed method we will examine in Lesson 3; this definition alone does not demonstrate the spiritual realities he describes.

Key concept: Anthroposophy

Steiner’s proposed path of knowledge relating the spiritual dimension of the human being to the spiritual dimension of the universe.

Course example

A person knows many facts about a landscape but still asks what their own experience of it means and how they belong within nature. The question can motivate further inquiry; the strength of the question does not settle which answer is correct. This illustrates the role of an inner need in starting a search.

Important distinction

An inner need can motivate a search for knowledge. It is different from evidence that a particular answer is true. Keep the question that starts the path distinct from the knowledge Steiner claims the path can yield.
