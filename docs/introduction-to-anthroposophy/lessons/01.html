What Is Anthroposophy?

Overview reference · Topic 1 / 28

The Question

What does Steiner mean when he calls Anthroposophy a path of knowledge?

Begin with Experience

Anthroposophy is a spiritual philosophy and movement developed by Rudolf Steiner. It considers the human being in terms of body, soul and spirit: bodily life, inward experience and a spiritual individuality capable of seeking understanding. These aspects will be developed step by step throughout the course.

Steiner’s Idea

Wisdom about the human being

The name joins two Greek words: anthropos means “human being” and sophia means “wisdom”. Anthroposophy therefore means “human wisdom”, often explained as “wisdom about the human being”. Its central interest is how a fuller understanding of ourselves can change our relationship with the world.

Inner development in daily life

It connects inner development — cultivating attention, clear thinking and responsible choices — with everyday life. In a painting lesson, a learner coordinates hand and eye, notices a feeling in response to colour and thoughtfully chooses the next brushstroke. One activity can engage the whole person.

Practical expressions

Anthroposophy inspired Waldorf education, which brings intellectual, artistic and practical learning together, and biodynamic agriculture, which approaches the farm as an interconnected whole. Later lessons introduce these fields and the spiritual ideas that inform them.

A path of knowledge

In the first of his Anthroposophical Leading Thoughts, Steiner defines anthroposophy as a path relating the spiritual in the human being to the spiritual in the universe. A path suggests development: the person who seeks knowledge also works on the capacities through which they seek it.

A question that matters to you

He connects this path with questions experienced as an inner need. For an adult learner, such a question might be: What makes a choice free? How can I understand another person more fully? The need gives inquiry a personal starting point, while the search for an answer asks for careful thought.

How to study this course

This course explains Steiner’s spiritual teaching and invites you to examine it. You can understand an idea without first accepting it. Everyday examples clarify meanings; they do not establish supersensible claims. Read the main explanation, try the two questions, and open the sources whenever you want to investigate further.

Important Distinction

An inner need can motivate a search for knowledge. It is different from evidence that a particular answer is true. Keep the question that starts the path distinct from the knowledge Steiner claims the path can yield.

An ordinary example can clarify a distinction without establishing Steiner’s further spiritual claims. Understanding the account does not require accepting it as established fact.

Check Your Understanding

What two sides does Steiner’s definition bring into relationship?

Compare with an explained answer

The spiritual in the human being and the spiritual in the universe. The path involves both a developing knower and a reality wider than personal feeling.

What role does an inner need play in beginning the path?

Compare with an explained answer

It gives a person a reason to ask and pursue a question. It motivates inquiry, while the grounds for an answer still need examination.
