Introduction to Anthroposophy
Part III of VII · Lesson 8 of 28 · Human Destiny

Reincarnation

Central question

What does Steiner say continues from one earthly life into another?

Why this matters

The preceding lessons described a developing individuality. Reincarnation extends that account beyond one earthly life and prepares the question of how deeds continue to matter.

Read Steiner

Primary source

Theosophy

Rudolf Steiner · GA 9
Elizabeth Douglas Shields · 1910 · Delhi Open Books reissue

Section: Chapter 2. Re-Embodiment Of The Spirit And Destiny — Continuous chapter; no author subsections
Reading: PDF captures 33–38

Read the learning example in capture 33, then the retained fruits in 38 left column. Captures 34–35 develop the individuality argument; 37 qualifies heredity. The short excerpt is from 38.

These experiences themselves pass away, but their fruits remain.

Course explanation

What Steiner is saying

Steiner begins with something familiar: learning leaves capacities that outlast particular experiences. Someone can read without remembering every lesson through which reading was learned. The experiences have been assimilated into an ability, rather than preserved only as scenes in memory.

He then extends this relationship beyond one life. Spiritual individuality, he argues, brings fruits of earlier development into a new embodiment. Capacities present at the beginning of a life are interpreted through an earlier history of that individuality. This is his further argument, beyond the ordinary learning example.

The continuing subject is therefore spiritual individuality. Personality means the particular configuration of an earthly life: its circumstances, habits and personal expression. The account does not require an unchanged personality or conscious recollection of every earlier experience. Retained development and episodic memory are different kinds of continuity.

Steiner also distinguishes spiritual continuity from bodily heredity, while allowing some personal tendencies to be inherited. The distinction is not that everything inward must come from a previous life. His argument concerns the individuality and the development he attributes to it.

Key concept: Reincarnation

In Steiner’s account, repeated earthly embodiment of a spiritual individuality carrying fruits of earlier development.

Course example

Adaptation of the source’s learning example: you retain the ability to read although many practice sessions have faded from memory. This illustrates capacity versus recall; Steiner’s extension to earlier lives is a separate claim.

Important distinction

The learning analogy makes Steiner’s proposed continuity intelligible. It does not independently establish a particular previous life or make reincarnation an observed consequence of learning to read.

Check your understanding

What are “fruits of experience” in this account?

Reveal the explanation

An acquired ability can remain even when particular experiences are no longer recalled.

What does Steiner describe as reincarnating?

Reveal the explanation

He distinguishes continuing individuality from the particular personality and body of one earthly life.
