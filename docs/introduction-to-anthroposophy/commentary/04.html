Central question

How can one encounter involve sensory presence, a personal response and an understood relationship?

Why this matters

Steiner begins his account of the human being with three relationships to the world. Understanding them prepares us for his more detailed distinctions.

Course explanation

What Steiner is saying

Consider Steiner’s meadow example. The eye encounters flower colours; their beauty gives someone joy; thinking discovers relationships in their growth. These concern the same flowers, but contribute differently to the encounter.

He calls sensory access body, personal inward experience soul, and understanding beyond personal liking spirit. A flower’s relationship to its surroundings does not depend on whether I enjoy it.

When the meadow is revisited, the old flowers have gone and the earlier joy remains in memory. The relationships understood can be recognized again. This contrast explains why Steiner distinguishes the three.

The return to the meadow separates three kinds of continuity. The original bodily encounter cannot be repeated exactly; its joy can remain as this person’s memory; an understood relationship can be recognized in new flowers and discussed with others. Soul therefore does more than feel a passing emotion, while spirit does more than hold a personal opinion. Their difference appears through what each preserves from the encounter.

Key concept: Body, soul and spirit

Three relationships: sensory encounter, personal inward experience and understanding whose significance goes beyond personal liking.

Course example

You hear a warning bell, feel startled and understand that it signals danger. Another listener can understand the warning while feeling differently.

Important distinction

The three terms distinguish relationships within one human life; they do not name three separate physical objects inside us.

Check your understanding

Which relation does feeling startled illustrate?

Reveal the explanation

The feeling gives the encounter personal significance: the soul relation.

Two listeners feel differently about the bell. Can they understand the same warning?

Reveal the explanation

The warning’s meaning is not decided by either listener’s liking.
