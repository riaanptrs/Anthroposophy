The supplied witness and the existing primary passages

The uploaded PDF has 33 captures: cover 1, title 2, lecture discussion 3–31 and advertisements 32–33. Its cover and title identify the work and Rudolf Steiner. No visible imprint identifies a translator, publisher, publication year, lecture date or GA number. The separately catalogued Karlsruhe lecture of 19 January 1909, GA 68d, in Dawson’s translation remains a parallel reference. Those catalogue details do not identify the supplied English wording.

This limitation applies to the new Mystery copy. It does not remove the verified identity of the combined course’s existing Berlin translation. All twelve main-course source assignments remain unchanged: ten Berlin selections from the 2012 Kelly/Barton edition and two Childs selections. Their original English, Portuguese study translations, locators and edition metadata are retained. Separately credited German reference passages and earlier Childs capture provenance are also retained.

New Mystery passages belong to the Mystery companion, with their own capture and column locations. A quotation from that supplied copy keeps its printed English wording; the course’s explanatory prose may clarify the intended concept. A close overlap in argument is grounds for comparison, rather than proof that editions have interchangeable wording or metadata.

Terminology that needs explanation

The copy alternates etheric and ethereal, and uses I, self and me for the fourth member. Captures 10–11 explain the intended life, astral and self-consciousness concepts. Captures 9–10 describe temperament as the mediation of inherited embodiment and individuality; it is not a fifth member added to the human being.

Its irregular blood wording needs particular care. Native capture 17, column 3, describes a light step in the sanguine portrait using blood language; capture 21, column 3, calls the child a blood child within the discussion of sanguine interest. These are printed-copy wording problems. In explanation, the intended portrait is sanguine where the surrounding argument is astral mobility and changing interest. This does not change the distinct physiological correspondence: in native capture 13, columns 2–3, the I/choleric portrait is associated with literal blood circulation, the astral/sanguine portrait with nerves, the etheric/phlegmatic portrait with glands, and the physical/melancholic portrait with the bodily foundation. Study explanations retain those pairings.

Original prescriptions and adapted practice

The four relationship routes summarized in capture 27, columns 1–2, are love and fidelity for the sanguine child, esteem for the educator’s abilities for the choleric child, compassion for another’s painful destiny for the melancholic child, and the influence of others’ interests for the phlegmatic child. Captures 21–26 develop the principle of working with an existing disposition rather than trying to graft on an absent quality.

The source’s choleric proposal is stronger than the course’s ordinary challenge activity. Native capture 23, columns 2–3, says that the educator must never appear incapable of advice and that the child must believe the educator knows. Native capture 24, columns 1–2, speaks of obstacles overcoming the child’s strength. The melancholic proposal includes arranging obstacles for justified pain and suffering in native capture 25, column 3; column 1 adds a qualification against exaggeration. These elements remain part of the attributed source argument.

The existing practical teaching deliberately uses reachable challenges, honest correction of a failed test, acknowledgment of disappointment and voluntary care. Naming them as adaptations keeps the source argument visible and allows the activity’s result to be reviewed on its own terms. The full Mystery companion contains the differentiated source discussions; the combined course gains a concise comparison without another full set of repeated portraits.

Printed repetitions and excluded material

Long repeated passages occur in the supplied native copy, including captures 11–12, 18, 20, 22, 24, 28 and 31. For example, the choleric obstacle proposal repeats across capture 24, and the self-education passage repeats across capture 28. Repetition is a feature of this witness, not a reason to invent additional lessons or additional arguments. The end of capture 31 concludes the discussion. The other-work advertisements in 32–33 are reviewed as endmatter and excluded from the temperament curriculum.
