Teaching implications and visual evidence

The source first develops heredity and enduring individuality, then temperament as their reciprocal colouring, the four members, portraits and mixtures, education, indirect self-education, and the social art of living. The course should explain these relationships before asking the learner to apply them.

The PDF contains no instructional temperament diagram to reproduce. Its cover illustration is decoration. Two original teaching tables can make the source easier to study: a four-member correspondence table drawn from capture 13, column 2, and a child-education comparison drawn from the four bullet points spanning capture 27, columns 1–2. Captions must identify these as course tables summarizing the text, not illustrations copied from the book.

The educational prescriptions also need a distinction between historical teaching and course practice. The source recommends love and fidelity for sanguine children, respect for an educator’s competence for choleric children, compassion for another’s suffering for melancholic children, and contact with peers’ interests for phlegmatic children. The source separately describes challenging obstacles, justified pain and adult boredom. Activities that use attainable challenges, voluntary care and modest practice are explicit course adaptations. They must not replace the account of what the source actually recommends.

The conclusion returns to the particular person. Knowledge should affect feeling, judgment and conduct, and the other person should help shape a fitting response. The final claim that life supplies the test belongs to the source’s argument; the usefulness of one educational example does not establish all of its spiritual or physiological premises.
