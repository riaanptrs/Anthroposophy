11Peter conversation begins: physical manner, lively friendship and criticism; teacher interprets a threshold from imitative immersion toward stronger self-experience and more conscious observation.All three native columns checked. Teacher holds Peter’s hand until eye contact; peers use disparaging nicknames. Distinguish observable movements from the narrator’s “connecting himself with the ground” interpretation. These constructed descriptions do not establish a diagnosis or justify compulsory gaze/touch.
12Criticism can accompany newly conscious observation, but explanations require connections and experience. Shoes/slippers illustrate perceived unfairness. Teacher reconsiders environmental influence and parental self-knowledge.All three native columns checked. Source generalizes that boys criticize while girls withdraw; retain as the teacher’s claim, not a sorting rule. The father’s unquestioned authority and household pressures complicate an age-only explanation.
13Teacher examines his own fear of criticism; father acknowledges modelling a know-all manner. Peter wants true stories and notices the table’s veneer. Teacher says fairy tales would now hold him back.All three native columns checked. Dialogue shifts from correcting the child to adult self-education. Source’s blanket fairy-tale prescription is stronger than the course’s invitation to ask about changing interests. Teacher also admits having neglected updated contact for more than two years.
14Stories of farms/crafts and bread’s journey from grain to baking connect factual interest, meaningful work and inner feeling. Nature’s elements, sunlight and Creator frame reverence for food.All three native columns checked; verse continues on 15. Religious imagery is part of the author’s educational account. Explain the link among human labour, natural processes and gratitude without presenting the verse as an uncredited Koepke quotation.
15Teacher accepts meal deprivation for wasted bread; mother recalls a farmer following seasonal sunlight and inward experience she could not verbalize. Father’s Crusoe memory prompts objection about self-preservation and trust in destiny; Joseph introduced.All three native columns checked. The withholding-food statement is plainly present in column 1 and must be named as a source prescription before course adaptation. Silence need not mean absence of inner life. The teacher’s claim about Steiner/Crusoe remains an attributed reference, not independently checked original text.
16Joseph’s destiny contrasts with the purported self-made Crusoe; tools reveal dependence on other workers. Father challenges the teacher using his collaborative tree-house experience. Practical work can balance abstract learning.All three native columns checked. Debate is essential: the father’s testimony complicates the teacher’s one-sided literary conclusion. Neither spirituality nor social connection is reducible to individual self-sufficiency. Contribution can include girls as well as boys, as the father explicitly adds.
17Practical education raises timing of technical explanations. Teacher traces electricity through a bicycle dynamo/waterpower toward sunlight/nature and calls technology a dead fourth kingdom.All three native columns checked. Source defers details, starts physics in sixth grade, says narrow scientific instruction fruitful only after fourteen, and attributes a severe warning about early locomotive explanation to Steiner. These are historical educational assertions, not demonstrated barriers to a child’s questions. Improvement in written work is posed as something still to be seen.
18Garden delight and Psalm 104 foster inner nourishment; father recognises his contribution to Peter’s criticism. Teacher emphasizes sustained parent conversation and parental harmony.All three native columns checked. Psalm is identified as Psalm 104, not Koepke’s composition. Mother-father harmony is the dialogue’s family model; the course can translate collaborative care into other family settings while naming that adaptation.
19Adult self-education improves relationship; class builds a small wooden house and asks for a closing door; father lends skill. Closing question asks whether Peter changed, adults see him differently, or both.Native two-column ending checked. Useful work joins belonging with privacy. Selected passage is Peter’s father’s spoken question in column 2, not an empirical finding. Multiple changes prevent a single-cause conclusion.

Supporting capture 72 was also read and visually inspected for its note on these conversations. Its first native column explicitly calls Chapters 1 and 2 “a free representation of the essential characteristics of the transition in the ninth year,” with any resemblance to actual people unintentional. This resolves how the course should describe Peter and Monica even though the foreword loosely speaks of “actual examples.” The note ledger for capture 72 is maintained with the closing material, so it is not counted twice in this 19-row table.

For lessons 0–1, retain the distinction among the author’s anthroposophical account, voices within constructed dialogue, the supplied edition’s prefatory contributors and the course’s contemporary exercises. The latter preserve adult accountability and meaningful shared work while keeping food available, respecting bodily choice and answering questions without blanket age or gender restrictions.
