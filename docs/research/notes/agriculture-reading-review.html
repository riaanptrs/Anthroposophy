Agriculture — completing the biodynamics source study

Reviewed 1 October 2026.

The Agriculture course adds the complete eight-lecture GA 327 sequence to the existing biodynamics learning path. It complements the fourteen readings in What Is Biodynamics?, whose agriculture selections cover only lectures 4–6. The anthology and the Agriculture Course keep their own identities and source numbering.

Sources and reading coverage

Every marked page block was read in both supplied Markdown witnesses: 192 captures in Agriculture.md and 178 in Agriculture course.md, including front matter, all eight lectures, four discussions and the available additional material. The primary text is Spiritual Foundations for the Renewal of Agriculture, translated by Catherine E. Creeger and Malcolm Gardner, edited by Gardner, published by the Bio-Dynamic Farming and Gardening Association in 1993. The comparison is George Adams’s classic translation, first published in 1958 and reissued by Rudolf Steiner Press in 2012 as Agriculture Course: The Birth of the Biodynamic Method.

Both PDFs were supplied but exceeded the 32 MiB transfer limit. The review uses their Markdown companions. It does not claim visual verification of PDF pages, handwriting, drawings or diagrams. Creeger/Gardner’s final capture begins the bibliography at the displayed 88% position; the remainder of the bibliography, index and colour plates is absent. All eight lecture bodies and their discussions are present. Poorly extracted handwritten Appendix A material is documented without inventing its illegible details.

What the course teaches

Eighteen readings in English and Brazilian Portuguese follow an orientation, two readings for each lecture, and a synthesis. Each contains a short credited source passage, an explanation, a key concept, an original course example, two retrieval checks and a reflection.

Source fidelity and unresolved differences

The course distinguishes Steiner’s lectures and replies from Shouldice’s foreword, Gardner’s editorial notes, Pfeiffer’s retrospective writing, preparatory notes and remembered supplementary indications. Later preparation numbers are editorial terminology, not original spoken numbering. Portuguese quotations are identified as course study translations.

Comparison exposed conflicting measurements and terms: silica percentages, a sentence about above/below activity, drawing colours, horn-burial depth, quicklime wording and cabbage clubroot/root-fly. The notes retain the differences; the course does not manufacture precise recipes by combining conflicting witnesses. The historically recalled indications carry their own reliability and situation-specific limits.

Spiritual, planetary, agronomic and health claims remain attributed to the source. Habitat observations do not establish the proposed spiritual mechanisms. The course explains the argument without supplying medical treatment, animal rations or instructions involving historical lead/mercury indications. Source proposals, trial results and established effects remain different claims.
