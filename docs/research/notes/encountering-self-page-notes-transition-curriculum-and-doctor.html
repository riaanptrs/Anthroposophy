Encountering the Self: transition, curriculum and school-doctor page notes

Reviewed 2026-10-01. Every Markdown capture from 53 through 64 was read, and every corresponding native PDF image was visually inspected. These are PDF capture positions, not printed folios. The images have three text columns, read from left to right; OCR occasionally mistakes the italic I for other characters and damages diagrams and word endings. The native images govern quotation and reading order. Hermann Koepke is the main author; captures 63–64 form the signed contribution of Walter Holtzapfel. Translation credit follows the volume's title and copyright evidence, not a separately identified translator for this contribution.

53Opens “The Child in the Ninth Year Transition” and “The Move Within One's Own House.” The comparison of ages seven and twelve becomes a proposed metamorphosis: head qualities reappear in limbs and conversely; feelings become inwardly experienced. Descending breathing and ascending circulation are presented as its bodily expression.All three columns inspected. The stream disappearing and reappearing is a Steiner analogy. The four-pulses-to-one-breath ratio is an attributed developmental claim, not a course test of self-awareness or a breathing exercise.
54Completes Hadumoth Rotges's pulse–breath proposal. A circle, crescent and line are described as mutually transformable forms. Two opposed figures map ascending limb-to-head and descending head-to-limb movements; feeling is represented by incoming and opposing arrows.All three columns and the middle-column figure inspected. Koepke explicitly says the metamorphosis is observable only on the soul-spiritual, not physical, level. The original schematic is legible; an original verbal teaching table can explain its relations without presenting it as anatomy.
55Native diagram compares “First Grader” and “Sixth Grader,” with crossed directional lines. The accompanying argument links the I to independent thought, purposive transformation through the limbs, and perception of sympathy and antipathy. Imitation gives way to the eternal individuality taking hold of the body.All three columns and left-column diagram inspected. The crossed lines are part of Koepke's spiritual account, not measured neurological pathways. Teach all three soul capacities, not just independent thinking. Preserve the source's strong claims as claims.
56Finishes the house metaphor: soul separation compared with birth; sadness, loneliness and privacy; adult love, protection and gladness. Koepke makes strong claims about later ego strength. Column 3 opens “How The Curriculum Helps”: house building, farming and poetry as a sheath for the child's I.All three columns inspected. ID 7 ends before the curriculum heading in column 3; ID 8 begins there. Quote on privacy is in column 2. The seed-and-I quotation in column 3 is the author's represented inner picture, not a documented statement by a pupil.
57Poetry gives inward experience expressive form. Musical proposal moves from pentatonic to major/minor thirds, soprano recorder around nine, deeper-breath wind instruments and strings around twelve; music is described as therapy. Curriculum reversal is illustrated by first-grade arithmetic through movement, knitting and eurythmy, then sixth-grade scientific observation.All three columns inspected. Native text restores the italic I in the musical passage where Markdown is damaged. Present instrument and age prescriptions faithfully as Koepke's proposal; distinguish them from the course's modest optional language, making and listening activities.
58Completes limbs-to-head grasping in science; imagined fairy-tale forms are proposed to reappear as purposeful crafts, gardening and bodily formation at twelve. Koepke criticizes premature intellectual content and prolonged immobility. “The Story of the Violet” begins the feeling-sphere example.All three columns inspected. The “seed rots away” claim is metaphorical source language. Do not reduce the argument to generic creativity or treat it as established causal evidence about car travel. The violet story's religious content continues on capture 59.
59Completes the violet story: later reflection relates the small flower and sky to soul and God. Teaching should remain alive and grow rather than force fixed definitions. Column 2 opens “Disturbances in Thinking, Feeling, and Willing”: bodily complaints, Steiner's claims about lifelong predispositions and anemia, then Snow White as an analogy for performance pressure.All three columns inspected. ID 8 ends before the disturbances heading in column 2; ID 9 starts there. The violet interpretation is explicitly theological. Separate that interpretation, reported symptoms and the later causal claims.
60Completes the bodice/breathing, comb/nervous trouble and apple/will comparison. Koepke criticizes technology, heavy homework and forced eating; proposes reduced work, digestible portions, warm adult words and educator self-development amid children's new criticism.Both occupied columns inspected; column 3 is blank. The proposed long-term effects and blanket technology judgment remain attributed source positions. Course practice is an ordinary fictional workload adjustment, with symptoms considered separately rather than diagnosed through Snow White.
61Opens “The Incarnation of the Ego.” First motif: divine origin, Genesis and an inverted plant image linking head roots, lung leaves and downward blossoms. Parentage questions are interpreted as spiritual-origin questions; “The Sturdy Sapling” is referenced. Christophorus begins by serving a king.All three columns inspected. This is a Christian-anthroposophical interpretation. Source-faithful explanation does not require replacing an actual family-history question with its proposed spiritual meaning. The legend continues directly into capture 62.
62Restores the entire Christophorus ending: king, devil, Cross, hermit's counsel, carrying travelers, Christ-child baptism, planted staff, death and blossoming. Koepke reads life beyond death. Third motif: becoming a servant of art as creative forces are released. Summarizes divine origin, life in death and art through Father, Son and Holy Spirit.All three columns inspected. No old missing-page claim remains warranted for this section. Quote about the blossoming staff and death is in column 1. Teach the sequence and interpretation together; religious imagery is not a universal description of every child's experience.
63Opens “A Word from the School Doctor.” Holtzapfel links pulse-volume and a proposed temporary blood-sugar decrease to ego development. Describes fluctuating physical complaints, fatigue and concentration difficulty as “school-illness” or “periodic syndrome.” Insists symptoms be taken seriously and begins discussing appendicitis.All three columns inspected. The native italic I is readable despite OCR errors. These bodily and therapeutic claims belong to Holtzapfel's historical contribution. The selected sentence about taking symptoms seriously is in column 3, not Koepke's own speech.
64Completes serious abdominal possibilities and physician consultation. Attributes complaints chiefly to outside stresses, school pressure, sensory stimulation and diet, while saying there is no scientific explanation or specific treatment for the proposed syndrome. Reports Steiner's iron remedies and closes with an incarnation claim and Holtzapfel's signature.All three columns inspected. Complete doctor text is present; remove previous gap notices. Teach the stated uncertainties and serious-cause qualification alongside the historical iron claims. No remedy-to-symptom chart or treatment protocol is created.

Lessons 7–11 follow the source boundaries above. A useful original teaching table would place Koepke's three directional relations beside their proposed soul capacities: younger awake limbs → older awake thinking; younger imaginative head → older purposeful forming; surrounding impressions → inwardly perceived feeling. Its caption should name captures 54–55 and identify it as an explanation of the author's schematic, with no anatomical or diagnostic interpretation. No source figure was copied or altered.
