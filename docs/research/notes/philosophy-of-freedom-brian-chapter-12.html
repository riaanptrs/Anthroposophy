Book material that improves the course

  1. 100–102: three distinguishable capacities. Understanding an ethical idea, imagining a particular expression, and knowing how to realize it are related but not interchangeable. Nature's conditions are not suspended by imagination.
  2. 102–104: development and deduction. Steiner distinguishes the actual emergence of later forms from deriving their complete content from an earlier concept alone. Applied to ethics, inherited arrangements do not mechanically supply a new deed. He challenges the analogy between normative ethics and ready-made organic laws. The course presents these as arguments to examine, not contemporary biological findings.
  3. 104–105: continuity and observation. Steiner proposes moral individuality as a spiritual continuation of evolution. Historical ancestry alone does not describe the determining ground of a particular action; that action requires its own examination. Do not turn the compatibility claim into a scientific proof of freedom.
  4. 105–106: wanting and doing. Practical opportunity and technical skill can enable execution without settling authorship of a motive. A blocked plan does not automatically erase a self-understood reason.
  5. 106–107: 1918 addition. Include the explicit stronger account of organic activity withdrawing for ideal activity. Attribute it to Steiner and connect it to Chapter 9. The ordinary classroom example does not establish the physiological/spiritual claim, and this is not a bodily suppression exercise.
