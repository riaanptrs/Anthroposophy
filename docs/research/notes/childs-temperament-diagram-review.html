Understand Your Temperament!: figures and teaching tables

Reviewed 30 September 2026 against the newly supplied 97-capture PDF. All positions below are PDF capture indices and columns; printed folios are not visible. The new copy supplies readable original figures and tables, superseding the previous damaged-figure notices. Original illustrations remain in the private book; the course uses accessible tables and explanations.

Original itemVerified locationMeaning and teaching treatment
Elements and humours table11, leftCholeric/fire/yellow bile; sanguine/air/blood; phlegmatic/water/body fluids; melancholic/earth/black bile. Historical humoral associations, not modern bodily measurements. Explained in Chapter 1.
Arousal and perseverance chart13, leftMelancholic: attention not easily aroused, strong perseverance; phlegmatic: least easily aroused, strong perseverance; choleric: most easily aroused, strongest perseverance; sanguine: easily aroused, little perseverance. New lesson 1 table keeps these two dimensions distinct.
Introvert/extrovert and difficulties tables16, left; 18, middlePhlegmatic/melancholic are reflective introverts; choleric/sanguine active extroverts. The difficulties table divides inward personality difficulties from outward behavioural difficulties. These are Childs's categories.
Figure 119, middleStable at top, unstable at bottom; introvert/convergent left and extrovert/divergent right. Phlegmatic and sanguine occupy the stable half; melancholic and choleric the unstable half. Elements and qualitative traits surround the quadrants. Expanded lesson 2 table preserves the mappings, with a caption explaining the author's specific use of stability.
Unnumbered quadrant20, rightReverses the vertical display: unstable at top, stable below. It keeps melancholic/choleric above and phlegmatic/sanguine below, with the same introvert/extrovert allocation. Do not infer changed associations from the changed placement.
Figure 221, leftFour stereotyped bodily silhouettes, ordered melancholic, sanguine, phlegmatic, choleric. Described as the author's outward-recognition proposal; not reproduced as a tool for assigning a person a temperament.
Chapter portrait motto/theme tables26, 31, 36 and 42Short humorous framing for each portrait. The source guides explain their role without substituting slogans for the chapter's argument.
Fourfold constitution table73Fire/ego/choleric, air/astral/sanguine, water/etheric/phlegmatic, earth/physical/melancholic. New lesson 9 table presents Childs's spiritual interpretation, separately from the retained contemporary genetics context.
Historical body-type tables86, left and middleCompare Martigny's mesoblastique/chordoblastique/entoblastique/ectoblastique terms and the proposed Sheldon/temperament correspondences. The early mapping gives choleric a mesomorphic association; the prose and final proposal later change or reject this terminology. It is not flattened into one unchanging table.
Figure 387, middle; caption at right topMartigny's four bodily sketches within the discussion of three main types. Childs describes where the attempted correspondences fail. This is a stage of the argument, rather than the final scheme.
Figure 487, right; caption at 88, left topChilds's adaptation adds temperament labels and eurymorphic/leptomorphic groupings to Martigny's arrangement. Kept distinct from the later interface proposal.
Figure 588, middleEctoderm, mesoderm and endoderm layers with mesoderm interfaces; severe and moderate leptomorphic and eurymorphic variants. The text introduces its links as a proposed explanation. Lesson 11 explains the model before judging its evidential support.
Composite Table of Attributes89, leftColumns phlegmatic/choleric/sanguine/melancholic combine origin, physique, somatotype, orientation, thinking, balance, time, direction, difficulties and social attitude. Moderate choleric/sanguine rows name mesoderm, while Figure 5 also discusses interfaces with the neighbouring layers. Their different presentations remain explicit.
Germ-layer and organ-system table89, right → 90, leftChilds connects ectoderm with head/sensory organization, mesoderm with chest, and endoderm with digestion. The surrounding discussion acknowledges interdependence and loose destinations. The course attributes the temperament inference instead of teaching it as established causal anatomy.
Figure 691, rightProposed head/melancholic, lung/sanguine, heart/choleric and digestive/phlegmatic organization, with severe/moderate form labels. New lesson 11 table includes the functional regions.
Figure 792, leftFour form variants with orientation, cognitive pattern, stability and difficulty labels. The final lesson 11 table retains the author's two families and two variants, without copying the silhouettes.
Final body-type table94, middleMelancholic = severe ectomorphic/leptomorphic; sanguine = moderate ectomorphic/leptomorphic; choleric = moderate endomorphic/eurymorphic; phlegmatic = severe endomorphic/eurymorphic. The text rejects a separate mesomorphic type. This final scheme differs from the earlier comparison table.

The four source-based course tables are original teaching summaries: attention/perseverance (lesson 1), the psychological dimensions (2), the fourfold constitution (9), and the final body-type proposal (11). Semantic column/row headers, labelled focusable table regions and matched Portuguese versions support reading on small screens and with assistive technology.

The existing ten-pair table in lesson 8 remains an original set of discussion questions for all ten pairings. It is explicitly distinct from the author's exaggerated compatibility sketches. The existing argument map in lesson 11 and separate genetics context in lesson 9 are preserved.

The imprint at capture 4 acknowledges permission for illustrations from H. J. Eysenck's The Structure of Human Personality (1970). It does not individually identify every figure's provenance, so no more specific permission attribution is invented.
