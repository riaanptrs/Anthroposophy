The revised course teaches Childs's precise working meanings of temperament, individuality, personality and character; his prediction and outward-recognition claims; historical humours; attention and perseverance; the three psychological classifications; all four portraits; original child-education proposals; developmental overlays; workplace interdependence and aptitude; all ten partnership sketches; and the explicit soul, spirit, reincarnation, fourfold-constitution and practical-love argument. Both appendices receive full explanations of their reasoning, qualifications and proposed models.

The practical course adaptations remain distinct from the author's stronger prescriptions. His advice to withhold choleric advice until requested, prolong tragic stories for melancholic children, narrate real or fictitious woes, use graphology, match occupations by temperament and infer temperament from appearance is explained with attribution. The course's feedback agreements, honest voluntary care and demonstrated-skill exercises are identified as later applications rather than presented as his own instructions.

Important source tensions are retained: formal sanguine stability means consistent changeability even though later rhetoric calls it instability; capture 16 contains an apparent refuge-sentence slip; Chapter 7 rejects changing temperament while Appendix I explicitly allows modification and even change. Appendix II's earlier mesomorphic mapping differs from its final rejection of a separate mesomorphic type. The revision records these differences rather than silently harmonizing them.
