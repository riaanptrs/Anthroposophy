Agreed direction

Build a beginner course around Rudolf Steiner's Theosophy / Teosofia (GA 9), with matching lessons in English and Brazilian Portuguese. Read and analyse short, consecutive passages from the supplied edition together, then draft and review lessons grounded in them.

The book guides the sequence; the lessons help beginners understand it. This replaces the earlier proposal to start with a broad six-topic survey. That survey is not the agreed syllabus.

Starting outline

Lesson 0 — How to use this study space / Como usar este espaço de estudo

Retain the existing website study guide as orientation: attentive reading, source references, reflection, and distinguishing the author's statements from interpretation and personal response.

Lesson 1 — Body, soul, and spirit: a first reading of Theosophy

Lição 1 — Corpo, alma e espírito: uma primeira leitura de Teosofia

Working title; the detailed plan also uses “Encountering the world / Nosso encontro com o mundo.”

  • Reading: the opening of “The Essential Nature of Man,” printed pp. 1–5, scan pages 29–33 of the supplied edition, including the flower/meadow example. The brief transition at the top of printed p. 6 may be included; stop before subsection 1. The detailed plan provides reading and assessment guidance.
  • Focus: Steiner's introductory distinction between sensory experience, personal response, and the pursuit of knowledge, leading into his terms body, soul, and spirit.
  • Learning objectives: distinguish these aspects in the selected passage; explain the three terms in their introductory context; identify one question that needs further reading.
  • Activity: observe a flower or leaf, record perceptions, personal feelings, and questions about it, then compare these notes with the passage. This is a teaching adaptation, not proof of Steiner's spiritual account.
  • Discussion: What does the text say? What is our explanation? What remains unclear? Understanding is distinct from agreement.
  • Review: a short summary in the learner's own words and comprehension questions tied to the selected reading.
  • Length: approximately 25–30 minutes, adjustable after we read the source together.

Following lessons

Continue through Theosophy in short, consecutive readings. The detailed plan proposes Lessons 1–6 for Chapter I, 7–9 for Chapter II, 10–17 for Chapter III, and 18–21 for Chapter IV, followed by a synthesis. Adjust pacing to beginner feedback. Preserve the book's conceptual distinctions rather than equating body/soul/spirit automatically with thinking/feeling/willing.

Reusable lesson structure

Use content/lesson-template.md for each lesson:

  1. Precise reading reference and learning objectives.
  2. Selected passage where reproduction is permitted, or a reading assignment.
  3. Plain-language explanation grounded in that passage.
  4. English–Portuguese vocabulary with consistent terminology.
  5. Everyday example or observation activity where useful.
  6. Reflection questions.
  7. Brief comprehension check with suggested answers and source locations.

Keep the English and Portuguese versions equivalent in meaning. Clearly attribute spiritual teachings to Steiner and distinguish them from historical facts, empirical claims, and our teaching interpretations.
