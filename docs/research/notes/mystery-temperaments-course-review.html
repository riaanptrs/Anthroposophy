The Mystery of Temperaments — completed course revision

Reviewed 1 October 2026.

All 33 PDF captures and all 33 corresponding Markdown pages were read, with every native PDF capture visually inspected. Captures 1–2 establish the title and Rudolf Steiner as author. The continuous discussion occupies 3–31; the advertisements in 32–33 were reviewed and excluded from the teaching. The fifteen lessons are original teaching divisions of that discussion, not source chapters or fifteen lectures.

What changed

All fifteen lessons were revised in English and Brazilian Portuguese. Each now teaches the argument through a four-section reading guide, a short verified English passage with a PDF capture/column locator, a labelled Portuguese study translation, and three substantive source-comprehension checks. Page references and reading assignments use the new 33-capture upload. The original fictional examples and explanations are preserved; lesson goals, essential concepts, vocabulary, activities and takeaways now follow the supplied argument.

The sequence explains the individual human enigma, heredity and continuing spiritual individuality, temperament as reciprocal mediation, the four members and relative predominance, the four portraits, bodily correspondences and qualifications, mixtures and the value of diversity, four distinct child-education routes, indirect adult self-education, and understanding expressed through social conduct. Historical analogies, physiological claims and extreme psychiatric characterizations remain attributed to the source. Understanding a claim and evaluating its support are treated as distinct tasks.

Two accessible original teaching tables clarify the four member–temperament pairings (Lesson 04, source capture 13) and the four educational points of contact (Lesson 07, source capture 27). They have captions, row/column headers and focusable scroll regions. They are course summaries of the text, not copied source illustrations.

The twelve-lesson combined Understanding Temperaments course received only three concise bilingual additions: source identity in Lesson 00, terminology and mediation in 01, and the four educational routes with stronger original prescriptions in 07. Lesson 07 gained a source-comprehension check. Three companion links were added. All twelve primary passages, all earlier Four Temperaments and Childs teaching, and the existing practical cases, activities, explanations, transfer scenes and checks were preserved. Companion comparison takeaways now reflect the revised Mystery lessons.

Source identity and limitations

The upload does not identify its translator, publisher, publication year, ISBN, lecture date/place or GA volume. These remain unidentified. The previously linked Frances E. Dawson translation, The Mystery of the Human Temperaments, is separately catalogued as Karlsruhe, 19 January 1909, GA 68d. It remains an explicitly credited parallel; those particulars are not attached to the uploaded English quotations. The Berlin GA 57 Kelly/Barton edition and Gilbert Childs book retain their own verified identities.

Repeated paragraphs, awkward terminology and an incomplete sentence occur in the native PDF. The course teaches repeated arguments once and records each affected page rather than silently repairing quotations or inventing a missing continuation. Explanatory prose uses consistent I/etheric/sanguine/choleric terminology; short English quotations preserve the actual supplied wording. Partial selections are identified as fragments where necessary.

The source proposes stronger educational measures than the course activities: the choleric educator should appear able to advise and arranged obstacles should overcome the child’s strength; the melancholic discussion proposes justified pain and suffering, with a limit against exaggeration. Adult prescriptions include particular outlets for anger, sorrow, passing interest and boredom. The lessons explain these actual prescriptions. Reachable tasks, honest correction, voluntary care and brief study experiments are explicitly identified as course adaptations.
