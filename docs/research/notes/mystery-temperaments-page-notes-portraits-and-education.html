The Mystery of Temperaments: portraits and education, captures 14–23

Reviewed against the supplied Markdown and every corresponding native PDF image. The numbers below identify PDF captures, not printed folios. Each capture has three columns, read left to right. The upload does not identify its translator, publication date or edition; this ledger makes no assumption about those details or the lecture's venue/date. The course uses standard temperament names in explanations, while exact quotations retain the upload's wording.

14Choleric: the ego's predominance is said to express itself through blood, force of will and outward assertion. Sanguine: astral predominance is linked with nervous activity, changing sensations and images; the ego/blood is described as a regulator of this movement. The closing column begins a claim about anaemia and uncontrolled images.Lesson 5 compares the proposed relation among member, bodily expression and behaviour. Blood/nerves and anaemia claims remain attributed explanations, rather than findings inferred from a classroom scene. Native text uses “angry” for choleric in places and contains an awkward “repressions.entations.”
15The anaemia claim continues into illusions and hallucinations. Sanguine interest is readily awakened but quickly moves to another object. Phlegmatic: the etheric/vital body maintains inner life and well-being, producing inward comfort and reduced inclination to outward will. Melancholic: the physical body, intended as an instrument, becomes dominant and resistant.Lesson 5 teaches the full four-part correspondence, not only quick attention versus slow attention. The inner-life/outward-interest distinction is essential. The upload alternates “etheric” and “ethereal”; explanation consistently uses etheric/vital.
16Melancholic bodily resistance is said to require inward energy, producing distress, pain, persistent impressions and introspection. The lecture turns to outward appearance, explaining choleric constrained growth through the ego's regulation of formative forces; Fichte and Napoleon are examples.Lesson 5 completes the melancholic portrait. Lesson 6 separates the proposed spiritual cause from its proposed visible expression and identifies historical examples as illustrations of the argument. “Johann Gotlieb” is the upload's spelling; explanatory text can use Johann Gottlieb Fichte.
17Choleric stature, firm gaze/dark eyes and forceful step are described. A qualification states that the issue is height in relation to configuration, not a rule that cholerics are short and sanguines tall. Sanguine mobile gaze, flexible/slender form and light, bouncing gait are linked to the astral body.Lesson 6 gives the proportion qualification its own reading question and examines the chain appearance → inner explanation. Native “blood astral body” and “blood flow” in the sanguine description are translation oddities, not corrected within quotes.
18The sanguine eye-colour argument associates blue eyes with astral inner light; the paragraph repeats several times. The fourfold account is presented as knowledge of the whole person and as a basis for pedagogy. Phlegmatic inner comfort and etheric/glandular activity are connected with bodily fullness/fat formation.Lesson 6 covers eye-colour and bodily-form claims as source claims without an appearance-based classification activity. The repeated eye-colour paragraphs are already present in the native PDF, so they are studied once, not counted as additional concepts.
19Phlegmatic shuffling, static expression and inward-looking gaze are contrasted with choleric force. Melancholic lowered head, dull gaze and heavy, measured step complete the appearance portraits. Educational and self-educational application follows. The types are explicitly said never to occur in schematic purity: a basic type is mixed with others; Napoleon is also called phlegmatic.Lessons 6–7 connect appearances with the mixture qualification and the proposed practical aim. Preserve the distinction between dominant pattern and a complete person. Native “angry” in the Napoleon example is explained as choleric, not quoted as a new category.
20Temperamental variety is valued as a source of beauty, richness and achievement. Education should guide temperament rather than level all differences. One-sidedness is connected with extreme anger, fickleness, indifference, depression and more severe mental states using historical psychiatric terms. The degeneration paragraph repeats in the PDF. The sanguine educational example begins at the end.Lesson 7 teaches both variety and redirection, then attributes the historical causal predictions. It does not turn them into learner diagnoses. Repetition is recorded as a source feature; one explanatory treatment suffices.
21The foundational educational principle is to work with what is present. For the sanguine child the available movement of interest should be addressed, rather than trying to install its opposite by force. Careful observation is said to reveal a real and potentially lasting interest.Lesson 8 distinguishes a present capacity from a missing capacity. The awkward proposal about “mutual contact with other boys” occurs in the rejected recipe; do not treat that sentence as the lecture's positive sanguine prescription.
22Interest in a suitable loved person is contrasted with fleeting interest in objects and with punishment or sermons. The educator is to become worthy of affection; the upload repeats this paragraph. A second route arranges attractive objects and periods in which brief interest is appropriate, including withdrawal and return.Lesson 8 teaches personal affection and environmental arrangement as two connected source proposals. Its bird-drawing sequence is a course adaptation, not Steiner's example or an attachment requirement. Native “blood child” means sanguine child in the surrounding argument. Repetition exists in the PDF itself.
23The sanguine conclusion says indirect work with existing tendencies can concentrate interest. Adult sanguine self-education then turns toward things in which brief interest is appropriate. The choleric educational route begins: respect for competent authority, never appearing unable to advise, and resistance/difficult goals through which force can act.Lesson 8 uses the opening column as its conclusion and marks adult self-education as a later topic. The following authority and obstacle proposals belong to Lesson 9, not sanguine teaching. Their historical demand for educator infallibility must be distinguished from a practical adaptation.
