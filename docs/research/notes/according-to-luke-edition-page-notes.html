Edition evidence

The new title and copyright captures identify the English edition more precisely than the older upload:

  • Title: According to Luke: The Gospel of Compassion and Love Revealed, a cycle of ten lectures by Rudolf Steiner, with an introduction and descriptive outline by Robert A. McDermott (capture 2).
  • Publisher and copyright: Anthroposophic Press, © 2001, Great Barrington, Massachusetts (capture 3).
  • Translator: Catherine E. Creeger; book design and typesetting credited to White Cloud Press (capture 3).
  • ISBN: 0-88010-488-0. The cataloging block also gives BP595.S894 L8513 2001 and control number 2001018172 (capture 3).
  • German source: the copyright page identifies Das Lukas-Evangelium, a cycle of ten lectures in Basel, and Rudolf Steiner Verlag, Dornach, Switzerland, 1955. Its date phrase visibly reads “15. bis 24. September 1909.” The final number is 24, not an OCR misreading of 26. This differs from the September 15–26 range in the existing GA 114 lecture chronology, whose final lecture is 26 September 1909. Preserve the printed date when transcribing this page; do not silently substitute 26. The supplied captures do not resolve the bibliographic discrepancy.

These are normalized bibliographic facts, not a full transcription of the copyright page. The upload does not supply the body of “About This Edition,” so no further edition history is established here.

Coverage and the Lecture One boundary

Captures 5–13 contain McDermott's introduction, ending with his recommendation of the descriptive outline; capture 14 begins Steiner's Lecture One. Captures 14–21 contain the supplied Lecture One text, ending with the Akashic-record paragraph. The new introduction and lecture prose cover the same text as old captures 3–10 and 11–17, respectively. The new upload adds a cover and copyright page, changes the reflow and pagination, and removes the three old desktop/application captures. It supplies no additional lecture prose and reveals no intervening prose gap between the two uploads.

The earlier description of Lecture One as only an opening, with a remainder definitely missing, is not established by these captures. Capture 21 ends in a complete sentence at the same point as old capture 17, with only a short continuation at the top of its left column and most of the canvas blank. This is visually consistent with the end of a chapter; the introduction's concluding capture 13 has a similar blank remainder. The final paragraph also reads as a coherent closing statement. There is no unfinished sentence or visible mid-paragraph interruption after it. However, neither an explicit end marker nor the following Lecture Two heading is supplied, and the end has not been checked against a complete primary copy of this Creeger edition. Lecture One may be complete in both uploads; its chapter completeness remains unverified. The defensible scope label is “supplied Lecture One text.”

The bodies of Lectures Two–Ten, McDermott's descriptive outline, “About This Edition,” and the explanatory footnotes are absent from this upload. The contents list those sections but does not supply their bodies. The cover bears no visible sample label, and no capture displays a sample-limit message, retailer interface, or other explicit indicator that this was distributed as a book sample. A partial upload is certain; its origin as a publisher or retailer sample is not proven. The chapter-like blank ending alone cannot establish that origin.
