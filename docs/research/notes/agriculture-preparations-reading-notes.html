Agriculture: preparations, manuring and pests — reading notes

These are source-review notes for the complete GA 327 course. Every supplied Markdown capture in the ranges below was read. The PDF files exceed the transfer limit, so illustrations and OCR against page images were not visually verified. Capture numbers identify the supplied Markdown page anchors, not inferred printed pages. Displayed locators are transcribed only where explicitly present.

Primary text: Catherine E. Creeger and Malcolm Gardner translation, Spiritual Foundations for the Renewal of Agriculture (1993), supplied as Agriculture.md. Comparison: George Adams, Agriculture Course: The Birth of the Biodynamic Method, supplied as Agriculture course.md; the classic translation was first published in 1958 and this edition in 2012.

Comparison and course decisions

  • Full coverage: The anthology already teaches GA 327 lectures 4–6 extensively. The full books add a continuous lecture/discussion sequence and fresh source locators; these six lessons need source remapping and stronger full-discussion integration, not an invented replacement doctrine.
  • Ordinary manure: Creeger 74 and 75; Adams 87 and 88 repeatedly say the preparations enhance ordinary manuring. This must remain prominent.
  • Trial and quantity: Creeger 68,73 and Adams 80,86 explicitly leave measures to investigation and request figures/tables. Preserve the difference between confident spiritual claims and unfinished technical quantities.
  • Machine distinction: Hand stirring favored spiritually (Creeger 66–67/Adams 78–79), fine mechanical spraying accepted (70/83), farm machinery generally accepted (71/83). No general machine ban.
  • Numerical conflict: Horn burial: Creeger 63 says 3/4–1 1/2 m [2 1/2–5 feet]; Adams 75 says 18 in.–2 ft.6 in. Preserve difference and avoid a course recipe. No PDF image check available.
  • Editorial numbering: Adams 77 labels 500/501 and 502–507 as editorial/current terminology and declines adding later technicalities to the lecture. These numbers are useful but not Steiner’s spoken numbering.
  • Quicklime wording: Creeger 74 says acids in moorland; Adams 86 says oxygen. Record OCR/translation discrepancy without silently correcting quoted source.
  • Six distinct treatments: Teach plants, animal enclosure where named, seasonal exposure and attributed relationship. Nettle has no animal casing and whole-year burial; valerian is flower extract. Full Q&As distinguish separate burial from later addition to manure.
  • Q&A corrections: Quercus robur, outer living bark, Urtica dioica, nettle aerial flowering parts without roots; mesentery/peritoneum is not tripe; botanical substitution replies are tentative.
  • Agronomy boundary: Food-substance through senses, microbes as only indicators, potash/lime to nitrogen alchemy, silicon unnamed element, metallic cosmic substances, disease prevention and insect/star effects are Steiner’s assertions. Do not present as established mechanisms or field prescriptions.
  • Weeds and evidence: Growth preparations also support weeds (Creeger 87/Adams 102). Lecture 6 treats suppression separately and asserts four-year effects. Experiments invited but spiritual truth viewed as intrinsically certain (97/112–113), not left wholly conditional.
  • Animal classification: Nematodes are put with insects/lower animals by the speaker; this is historical grouping, not current zoology. Species and larval/adult timing still require testing.
  • Clubroot translation: Creeger 108 uses cabbage clubroot; Adams 123 cabbage root-fly. Distinct conditions; do not merge the translation into a single modern diagnosis.
  • Ethics/cooperation: Creeger 98 favors shared understanding with neighbors;106–107 audience misuse question and moral improvement circle are substantive. Vineyards explicitly untested(108/Adams 123), and ordinary proven methods retained(109/124).
