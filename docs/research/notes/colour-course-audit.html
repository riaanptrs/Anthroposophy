Colour — audit before the PDF revision

Reviewed 30 September 2026, before integrating the new PDF and matching Markdown. This audit concerns the existing fourteen bilingual lessons; the new page ledgers record the source reading itself.

The existing course already distinguishes three central lectures from nine supplementary talks, teaches image and lustre colours, and includes original examples, accessible colour studies and a final portfolio. These useful elements should be retained. The current main explanations are brief relative to the arguments in the book, however, and two of the three comprehension questions in nearly every lesson repeat generic questions about the example and activity.

The revision should add connected explanations of each lecture's argument and require source-based answers before application. Important relationships include the image-colour classification, image and lustre in material kingdoms, loss and renewal of colour experience, moral colour and tone experience, light/thought/past and darkness/will/future, the proposed significance of moral action, the relationships among arts and space, Titian's composition, qualitative colour versus measure and weight, and the hierarchy/rainbow account. These claims require attribution without replacing them with ordinary perception exercises.

The current capture ranges do not match the newly supplied PDF. The new page ledgers establish the actual headings and endings. All source assignments and quotation locations should follow that evidence. The whole collection and its supplementary lessons should not carry an unqualified GA 291 label: only the first three lectures come from that volume.

Existing quotations are already in English, but their credits lack exact capture positions and source fingerprints. The PDF should verify their wording, columns and translator attribution. Translation copyright 1992 should not become an unsupported claim about this copy's publication year. The orientation's Lecture 1 quotation and the portfolio's Lecture 11 quotation should be explicitly identified as readings from those lectures.

Diagrams and editorial notes need particular attention. The old review reported fragmented OCR; native PDF images may preserve tables and labels that the Markdown loses. Original explanatory tables and digital colour studies should be labelled as such and should reflect the author's distinctions accurately. Documentary shorthand gaps must remain visible; colour or medical procedures should not be invented to fill them.

Preserve lesson numbers, English/Portuguese partners, notebook identities, the interactive colour study, practical examples and existing course connections. Rebuild the entire site and check source coverage, passage credits, reading-before-practice order and the unchanged output of other courses.

Source claims, later commentary and original course adaptations remain separately attributed.
