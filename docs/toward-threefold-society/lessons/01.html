Course explanation

What the author means

Smith identifies the calamity in Steiner’s closing warning as the First World War. He calls the warning prophetic, argues that decision-makers ignored Steiner’s suggestions, and connects subsequent calamities with unresolved social problems. These are the translator’s forceful historical judgments. The introduction offers his case for relevance; it does not demonstrate that Steiner’s proposal would have prevented every later disaster.

He then asks whether cultural life is free, whether an associative economy functions, and whether the political state confines itself to ensuring human rights. He answers all three questions negatively and says destructive tendencies have intensified. His criticism targets the expectation that the state should solve cultural, economic and political problems through one institutional center.

For schools, Smith explicitly defines freedom as freedom from state control and economic influence. He is not using “free” to mean tuition-free. This distinction introduces the question of who determines education’s content and administration. It also prevents a discussion of cultural autonomy from slipping unnoticed into a different discussion about who pays fees.

Smith explains that references to the Austro-Hungarian and German empires were familiar to the original readers but require historical help today. The imprint credits his historical footnotes, and his introduction mentions notes at the end of the book; those notes are not supplied here. His conclusion, signed October 2019, is that the essential principles are even more urgent.

Key concept

Smith makes a historically situated argument for continuing relevance: distinguish his evaluation from the proposal he is introducing.

Course example

As a course reading exercise, underline Smith’s question about schools and write two headings: “Who controls educational decisions?” and “Who pays for education?” Put his stated definition under the first. The exercise clarifies his wording without deciding which funding arrangement a free school must adopt.

Keep this distinction

Calling a warning prophetic is Smith’s interpretation of subsequent history. It is different from identifying the First World War as the calamity discussed, and from proving a causal explanation of later events.

He asks about a free spiritual-cultural life, an associative economy and a political state concerned with ensuring human rights. His negative answers express his assessment of their realization.
