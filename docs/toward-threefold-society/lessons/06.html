Course explanation

What the author means

After describing cultural autonomy and economic association, Steiner introduces the political state. Every person of age should be able to exercise influence and judgment in this sphere. Cultural life involves particular abilities; economic life involves associative relationships and the value of goods. The rights sphere should address the human element independently of those differences.

He places hours and conditions of labor within the political rights-state. All count equally here because the questions to be treated are those about which all are equally competent to form an opinion. Human rights and obligations belong to this member of the social organism. Economic contribution therefore cannot serve as the measure of a person’s standing in the rights sphere.

Unity, in his proposal, originates in the independent development of the three members. Independence enables each to contribute according to its character; it is not separation into unrelated worlds. Free culture can supply living ideas, economic associations can develop practical judgments, and the rights sphere can address common human standing without being reduced to either of those functions.

The final page also states that a self-structured economy can protect the weak against the strong. Steiner presents this as an expected result, not a result demonstrated here. He explicitly leaves the integration of non-producing people for later in the book and promises to show how capital, means of production and land use can be addressed through cooperation. Those later explanations are absent from the supplied material.

Key concept

The rights sphere treats people equally as human beings; social unity should emerge through cooperation among independently developed cultural, economic and political spheres.

Course example

Imagine an association discussing the price of a product while a public debate addresses the hours and conditions under which people work. In this invented course comparison, economic knowledge informs one question and equal political standing governs the other. The questions interact, but skill or output does not give someone greater human rights.

Keep this distinction

Autonomy is not isolation, and equality of rights is not a claim that everyone has identical talents or produces identical goods. Steiner distinguishes the grounds of judgment appropriate to each sphere.
