Course explanation

Understand the idea

He recognizes that transferring education from earlier controlling circles to the state was once necessary for historical development. His argument is that an arrangement appropriate to an earlier period can become wrong later. Culture, having matured toward freedom within the state, now requires complete autonomy to exercise that freedom. This is a historical claim about a next step, not a claim that the earlier transfer had no value.

His administrative proposal is demanding: active educators should both teach and administer. Economic and political considerations should be excluded from educational administration. Neither parliament nor someone who merely used to be an educator should direct it. What teachers experience in their present work should flow into decisions about that work. He extends this principle to law schools, trade schools, and agricultural and industrial colleges.

Steiner rejects the prejudice that teachers require direction from supposedly practical outsiders. He argues that imposed terms of work make teachers remote from reality, while an independently administered education can develop socially sensitive people. Antisocial conditions arise through individual behavior as well as economic structures. Education’s social effects therefore cannot be left out of the social question.

Autonomy does not mean cultural life stops contributing to society. In his example, free law schools would develop living legal ideas which the state could receive. A free cultural community could also judge creative work according to its own values and shared experience. He does not promise perfection: the aim is the best possible situation, with fuller organizational particulars deferred to the book’s absent chapters.

Teachers encounter the effects of schedules and methods; administration can then be revised in light of that experience. Autonomy is meant to keep educational judgment connected with its subject. It is not a claim that teachers never err or that the resources and rights involved in schooling cease to matter.

Key concept

Cultural autonomy means that educational judgment and administration arise within an active cultural community, which then contributes to the other social spheres.

Course example

Imagine educators reviewing a course after teaching it and collectively changing its content from what they learned. Compare this invented course example with a sponsor setting its content to satisfy a commercial target. The comparison isolates the source of educational judgment; it does not supply a full school constitution.

Keep this distinction

Steiner’s proposal is stronger than merely consulting teachers: he assigns administration to active educators. His account of social sensitivity is a claimed consequence of that arrangement, rather than a result established by this course.
