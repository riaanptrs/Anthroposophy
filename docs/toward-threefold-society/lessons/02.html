Course explanation

What the author means

He imagines someone possessing a perfect theoretical solution to the social question. Even on that assumption, announcing the solution to the public would depend on an impractical belief: that people will accept another person’s picture of society and act according to it. The difficulty concerns how social action comes about, not just how convincing a theory sounds.

Steiner rejects the charge that his own book is utopian. He argues that its critics sometimes project their own habits of abstract planning onto it. His language is sharp: he says even the most convincing utopian ideas lead nowhere and that people waste their breath offering propositions others cannot apply. These are his criticisms of the social thinking he encounters.

The deeper problem, in his account, is a divorce between thought and reality. Contemporary economic notions can fail to engage the relations through which people actually produce, exchange and live. A thought process that remains remote from those relations cannot adequately address their disorder. Recognizing that distance is therefore an uncomfortable but necessary beginning.

This does not amount to dispensing with thinking. The preface will propose communities in which people acquire insight through participation and shared experience. Its question is how judgment can become responsive to life. At the transition to page 9, Steiner links the problem to the condition of spiritual-cultural life and its dependence on political institutions and economic forces.

Key concept

For Steiner, practical social thinking must connect judgment with living relationships and people’s participation, rather than rely on an announced blueprint.

Course example

Imagine a course discussion in which one participant designs an ideal neighborhood food system without speaking to growers, distributors or households. Ask which relationships the design leaves unknown and how those participants could contribute judgment. This invented example illustrates the preface’s problem; it is not a historical case from the book.

Keep this distinction

A plan’s internal consistency and its capacity to become living social practice are different questions. Steiner challenges the leap from one to the other, while continuing to offer ideas about institutions.
