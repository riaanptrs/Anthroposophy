Spiritual content and spiritual individuality

How does Steiner relate spirit-self, life-spirit and spirit-man?

Before you read

Read the distinction between consciousness-soul and spirit-self first. Then follow the account’s correspondences with bodily perception, nourishment and form. Ask what each spiritual term contributes rather than treating the three names as synonyms.

4. BODY, SOUL, AND SPIRIT

Read PDF captures 26–29. Begin in capture 26’s right column at the definition of spirit-self. Read intuition and the spiritual members through the completion of the aura discussion; stop on capture 29 before the comprehensive reconstruction of the whole human being.

English translation: Elizabeth Douglas Shields, 1910. Digital reissue: Delhi Open Books; publication year unstated.

What Steiner is saying

Consciousness-soul bears truth whose significance is independent of liking. Spirit-self concerns that content received and individualized in the I. The truth does not become privately true; it becomes part of this individual’s being. This is how Steiner connects the preceding discussion of knowledge with spiritual individuality.

He calls spiritual revelation intuition, in contrast with sensation as bodily revelation. The passage includes even simple thought within this discussion. A plant can occasion similar sensory impressions while one person develops a fuller understanding of it than another. The change in understanding is not simply a change in the color seen, nor does intuition manufacture what the account says it reveals.

The chapter next introduces spirit-man as the independent spiritual being and describes a spirit-sheath in relation to its individuality. It speaks of receiving spiritual nourishment from a spiritual environment. Life-spirit names the spiritual life through which that being is built and develops. Spirit-self, life-spirit and spirit-man are thus connected but differentiated.

The source’s caution about the literal expression spirit-body is important. Steiner uses correspondences with bodily life to direct attention toward his proposed spiritual reality; he does not identify it with a second physical organism. The nourishment and sheath descriptions need to be understood by their role in the account, not as instructions for locating material organs.

Key concept

Spirit-self

Steiner names the spiritual content received and individualized through the I spirit-self, distinguishing it from consciousness-soul’s bearing of truth.

How the idea works

  • Truth is acknowledged beyond personal preference
  • The I receives and individualizes spiritual content
  • Life-spirit expresses spiritual life and development
  • Spirit-man names the independent spiritual being

Course example

A learner can recognize a valid principle and gradually make it part of how they judge and act. This course example helps distinguish acknowledging a truth from integrating it into a life; it does not prove the spiritual members described by Steiner.

An important distinction

Intuition here is the chapter’s term for spiritual revelation. A strong feeling of certainty, by itself, does not explain that meaning.
