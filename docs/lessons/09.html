Lesson 09 · Re-embodiment and destiny

Action and destiny

Explain karma through the inner and outer consequences of deeds and relate destiny to repeated lives and present action.

How does the lesson distinguish karma from reincarnation?

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · Theosophy · GA 9 · Monges / Church, 1971 · Printed p. 65 · PDF p. 93

Through an action that the soul has performed, there lives in the soul the energetic predisposition to perform another action that is the fruit of the first action.

Anthroposophic Press, 1971; Henry B. Monges, translator; Gilbert Church, reviser. English excerpt checked against the supplied PDF image; Brazilian Portuguese is a new course study translation. Line breaks and line-end hyphenation normalized.

Open the source or edition index →

What this passage means

The passage identifies a deed’s inner result: a disposition toward further action. Its outer result is the changed world in which later action occurs. “Fruit” therefore connects consequences and capacities rather than naming a simple reward. The chapter extends this relationship across lives and summarizes body/heredity, soul/karma and spirit/re-embodiment on p. 68. Keep that spiritual extension distinct from the ordinary example and from the qualification in Addendum 7.

The focus of this lesson

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

Understand the idea

The second movement of Chapter II returns to action. Experience becomes lasting within spirit through its fruits; a deed also becomes lasting outside the person through its consequences. Steiner calls this a second, independent life of soul in the effects of action. The question raised on pp. 43–44 now becomes central: how can something that went out from the I later meet it from the world?

On p. 61, yesterday and tomorrow supply an analogy. Sleep interrupts awareness and activity, yet someone wakes into circumstances partly shaped by earlier deeds. A house built yesterday is still there; unfinished work still calls for attention. Steiner compares this continuity through sleep with the relation of a spiritual individuality to deeds’ effects across death and re-embodiment. The analogy shows his intended pattern; the extension depends upon Lesson 8’s spiritual account.

The soul mediates in both directions. On p. 62 it brings impressions from body to spirit so that their fruits endure. It also brings capacities from spirit into earthly deeds so that their effects endure in the world. Trace both arrows: in Steiner’s account the connection of deed and destiny arises through the person’s activity, rather than an unrelated outside judge.

His example of looking back at forty asks how experiences coming from outside have become part of who someone is. He contrasts an abstract I, isolated from its life, with an I understood through its actual biography. On pp. 63–64, destiny approaching from outside is compared with memory arising from within. He extends this connection to effects from earlier earthly lives that can meet a changed individuality later.

A deed has an inward and an outward side. On pp. 65–66 Steiner describes a capacity and a tendency towards subsequent action imprinted in soul by what it has done. His historical Kentucky-cave example illustrates a being bound to conditions resulting from earlier activity. Its role is an analogy of changed conditions and capacities; it should not replace a modern account of evolutionary biology.

The Europe-to-America example answers an objection: how could earlier actions matter in a completely changed world? A mechanic and a bank clerk may move to the same place yet meet different work and surroundings because of capacities developed earlier. Steiner extends this relationship to spirit self finding surroundings related to earlier deeds in a later life. The claim concerns a continuing relation, rather than preservation of an identical physical setting.

By p. 68 the account includes relationships between people. Steiner says individuals previously joined through actions seek renewed encounters and corresponding incarnations so that those actions have their consequences. He explicitly connects joys, sorrows, possibilities, and human encounters with earlier deeds. Teach this strong claim as his explanation of destiny; the preliminary argument does not give a learner knowledge of a particular person’s earlier deeds or grounds for assigning blame.

The threefold summary gives the essential distinction: body is subject to heredity; soul lives within self-created destiny, called karma; spirit is subject to repeated earthly lives. Reincarnation concerns the continuing individuality and its embodiments. Karma concerns the relation of actions’ effects to that individuality’s later field of life. Soul joins body and spirit during a particular earthly life, so the three relations work together without being interchangeable.

Present action matters because it creates further capacities, relationships, and consequences. Concluding that effort is useless would remove the activity through which this chapter explains destiny. Revisit pp. 65 and 69 with Addendum 7: Steiner expects fuller knowledge from supersensible observation, while calling the present thinking a preparatory silhouette. Chapter III next asks about the physical, soul, and spiritual worlds presupposed by this account.

Keep this idea

Reincarnation concerns repeated lives; karma concerns the relation between deeds and destiny.

See the idea in an example

You borrow a book on Monday and go to sleep. On Tuesday, the responsibility to return it remains.

What this helps explain

The example shows how a break in awareness need not erase the consequences of an action. Steiner extends the question of continuity to death and rebirth. Sleeping and dying are different, so the ordinary example helps explain the idea without proving that extension.

Check your understanding

How does the lesson distinguish karma from reincarnation?

Reveal the explanation

A deed changes the outer world and leaves capacities and tendencies within its actor. Steiner connects these effects with later circumstances and encounters: this relation is karma, the self-created destiny of soul life. Reincarnation is the repeated embodiment of spiritual individuality carrying acquired fruits. In his account they work together, and present deeds continue to shape what follows. The reasoning prepares his claimed spiritual knowledge; it does not identify anyone’s particular past-life actions.
