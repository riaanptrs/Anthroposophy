The three worlds at work in nature and thinking

A crystal has form; a plant also continues forming new growth. An animal senses and responds. These comparisons introduce different questions about form, life and inward activity.

Steiner proposes spiritual formative beings behind these manifestations. His three Elementary Kingdoms concern formative activity; mineral, plant, animal and human kingdoms concern beings in which such activity appears. They are not seven equivalent biological categories.

Human thinking is related to the individual I. The source's note also attributes thought within animal experience to a group ego, so its position should not be shortened to “animals have no thought.”

His image of physical matter as condensed soul and spirit is partly metaphorical, not a chemical process. Observed growth and a proposed spiritual cause remain distinct kinds of statement.

Two questions — optional

Why not treat the seven kingdoms as seven peer biological categories?

See an explanation

The first three concern proposed formative beings; the others concern manifestations with physical embodiment. Their explanatory roles differ.

See an explanation

5. THE PHYSICAL WORLD AND ITS CONNECTION WITH THE SOUL AND SPIRIT LANDS

Read PDF captures 69–74. Begin subsection 5 on capture 69. Read through the constitution-to-world relationship on capture 74, stopping before the paragraph introducing beings beyond the four visible kingdoms. Read Note 3 on capture 93 with the animal/human distinction.

English translation: Elizabeth Douglas Shields, 1910. Digital reissue: Delhi Open Books; publication year unstated.

What Steiner is saying

Steiner describes embodied humans as participants in all three worlds at once. Sensory things stand out within a wider reality as ice stands out from water. He calls their condensation of soul and spirit half-metaphorical. This supplies his explanation of thought’s correspondence with nature: the object and the thought through which it is understood relate to a common spiritual archetype.

Minerals show external interactions that can be understood through laws. Plants add growth and reproduction. A crystal has a completed shape; a plant retains the capacity to produce shape. The growing lily distinguishes visible changes from the formative being Steiner proposes behind them. First Elementary Kingdom names formless primal beings; Second Elementary Kingdom names the beings active in living formation.

Animals add sensation and impulse within a comparatively enduring living form. The beings active in that soul life belong to the Third Elementary Kingdom. Formative life and soul activity thus remain distinct, even when they cooperate in the same organism.

Humans also experience thinking as thinking and can direct impulses through it. Note 3 clarifies that the account does not exclude thought from animals: it attributes their thought to a group ego acting from the soul world, while human thoughts acquire independence in an individual I. This is the author’s distinction, rather than a contemporary biological classification.

The seven-kingdom list combines three elementary formative realms with mineral, plant, animal and human existence. It is not a list of seven parallel species. The closing account reconnects physical-body, ether-body, sentient soul-body and intellectual-soul with form, life, soul and thought archetypes. In the intellectual-soul, the human being becomes a conscious participant in work on oneself.

Key concept

Elementary Kingdom

Steiner uses Elementary Kingdom for a realm of archetypal or formative beings whose activity is expressed in physical form, living formation or soul life.

How the idea works

  • Mineral: external interaction and physical form
  • Plant: form and living formation
  • Animal: living formation and sensation/impulse
  • Human: thought experienced as thought and self-directed activity

Course example

Observing new leaves appear on a seedling differs from identifying the cause of their formation. This course example separates the observed change from the additional spiritual explanation proposed in the source.

An important distinction

The three elementary kingdoms describe formative activity; the visible kingdoms describe beings in which it is expressed. They occupy different explanatory roles.
