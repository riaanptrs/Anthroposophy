Lesson 15 · The three worlds

Nature and the three worlds

Explain the relation of the worlds without confusing it with biological classification.

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · Theosophy · GA 9 · Monges / Church, 1971 · Printed p. 129 · PDF p. 157

They are only another form of the spirit beings, and when a man forms thoughts about things, his inner nature is merely directed away from the sensible form and out towards the spiritual archetypes of these things.

Selected excerpt from the supplied English edition; Brazilian Portuguese is a new course translation. Line breaks normalized.

Open the source or edition index →

What this passage means

The section connects the realms through different forms of manifestation. Read mineral, plant, animal and human being in that framework before comparing it with ordinary biological descriptions.

The focus of this lesson

Explain the relation of the worlds without confusing it with biological classification.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

Understand the idea

Steiner next considers the physical world in connection with soul and spirit. The comparison of ice and water helps express how a visible form can be understood as a manifestation within a wider reality. In his account, the spiritual is not simply placed next to the physical as a separate object.

The chapter revisits distinctions among mineral, plant, animal, and human existence through physical organization, life, sensation, and thinking. It also introduces formative elementary realms. These categories serve the book’s spiritual account of nature; they should not be treated as an alternative set of present-day biological species or laboratory measurements.

A good reading names the explanatory role of each distinction. Physical form, living formation, and inner experience are different questions. The ice-and-water analogy clarifies a proposed relation of manifestation, but physical melting does not prove that material things originate in a spiritual world. Track where the analogy ends and the supersensible claim begins.

Keep this idea

Use an analogy to understand a relationship, not to make the two things identical.

See the idea in an example

Think of ice and liquid water. Their relationship helps us consider how a visible form can belong to a wider process.

What this helps explain

Steiner uses this comparison when relating the physical world to spiritual reality. It does not mean that spirit is literally liquid, or that heating matter reveals spirit. A comparison carries a particular relationship across, not every property of the example.

Check your understanding

Why should elementary realms not be inserted into a list of biological species?

Show a suggested answer

They describe formative relationships within Steiner’s supersensible account, not biological species identified by the same methods as ordinary taxonomy. Mixing the two would obscure the kind of claim being made.
