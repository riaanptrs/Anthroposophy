Lesson 06 · The essential nature of the human being

One human being, several classifications

Explain why the ninefold and sevenfold lists differ.

How can nine become seven?

Sketch nine labelled spaces: A physical body; B life body; C soul body; D sentient soul; E intellectual soul; F consciousness soul; G spirit self; H life spirit; I spirit man. Predict which neighbouring pairs the passage brings together. This represents Steiner’s classification.

First grouping

Bring C and D together: soul body and sentient soul form one connection. Keep both names inside one bracket. Nine spaces become eight groups.

Second grouping and check

Bring F and G together: consciousness soul and spirit self. The seven groups are A, B, [C+D], E, [F+G], H, I. Nothing has been erased. Explain the relationship rather than saying two members disappeared.

2 · Work through the explanation

A worked example

Write A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I on nine cards. Group C with D and F with G. You now have seven groups, while all nine labels remain.

What this helps explain

In the book’s intermediate grouping, C + D represents soul body with sentient soul; F + G represents consciousness soul with spirit self. This card exercise explains the regrouping only. The later transformation list uses a different organizing principle.

Understand the idea

The chapter ends by regrouping distinctions already introduced. Its ninefold account names physical body, ether body, soul body, sentient soul, intellectual soul, consciousness soul, spirit self, life spirit, and spirit man. These are aspects of one human being, not nine independent objects.

An intermediate sevenfold presentation joins soul body with sentient soul, and consciousness soul with spirit self. This leaves physical body; ether body; sentient-soul-body; intellectual soul; spirit-filled consciousness soul; life spirit; spirit man. The change is a grouping decision, not the disappearance of two pieces.

A later sevenfold presentation emphasizes transformation: physical body, life body, astral body, I, spirit self, life spirit, spirit man. The last three are associated with the transformation of astral, life, and physical organization. Do not substitute this list for the earlier sevenfold grouping. Damaged OCR in the 1971 transcription was clarified through a parallel Shields translation; wording across editions differs.

Connection with the later lectures

From a list to a relationship

The terms become easier to follow when we ask how they work together. In the later lectures, Steiner describes the physical and etheric organizations as remaining connected during sleep, while the astral organization and I separate from them. He describes death differently: the physical body is cast off. This is a preview of his spiritual account, not something that ordinary observation of sleep establishes.

Now that you know the classification terms, describe what remains connected in his account of sleep and what changes at death. Physical and etheric remain connected in sleep; death involves the release of the physical body. The purpose is to explain relationships within his account, beyond remembering a list of names.

Companion source: Rudolf Steiner, What Is Anthroposophy? Three Perspectives on Self-Knowledge (2002), page markers 75–76. Numbers refer to the first study transcription (191 pages), not the pages of Theosophy.

A fourfold map used for a particular question

The Four Temperaments (4 March 1909, GA 57) uses the physical, etheric, astral and I-bearing members to explain four temperamental tendencies. All four belong to the person in that account; the distinction is predominance, not four separate kinds of human being. This is a useful comparison with Theosophy’s several classifications: ask what question a map is designed to answer before equating its terms with another map.

Check your understanding

Why do two sevenfold lists not necessarily have identical entries?

Show a suggested answer

One combines adjacent distinctions in the ninefold account; the other foregrounds the I and transformation of bodily organizations. The number alone does not tell us the principle of classification.
