Lesson 06 · The essential nature of the human being

One human being, several classifications

Reconstruct the ninefold, grouped sevenfold, fourfold, and transformation-based sevenfold accounts, explaining their connections.

Why do the chapter’s classifications have different numbers of members?

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · Theosophy · GA 9 · Monges / Church, 1971 · Printed p. 36 · PDF p. 64

Consciousness soul and spirit self form a unity.

Anthroposophic Press, 1971; Henry B. Monges, translator; Gilbert Church, reviser. English excerpt checked against the supplied PDF image; Brazilian Portuguese is a new course study translation. Line breaks and line-end hyphenation normalized.

Open the source or edition index →

What this passage means

Unity supplies a reason for grouping while the two names remain distinct. On this page consciousness soul F and spirit self G are joined, just as soul body C and sentient soul D are joined. Nine distinctions therefore become seven groups. Keep this grouping separate from the final sevenfold list, which emphasizes transformation through the I.

The focus of this lesson

Reconstruct the ninefold, grouped sevenfold, fourfold, and transformation-based sevenfold accounts, explaining their connections.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

Understand the idea

The ending first retraces the chapter’s movement: physical organization supports life, sensation, thinking, and openness to spiritual content. The soul mediates body and spirit. Steiner’s plant image on page 37 gives the relationship a shape: bodily members are the roots in the physical world, spiritual members the flowering in the spirit world, and soul the connecting stalk. Read this as an explanatory image of the account.

The ninefold list on page 36 names A physical body; B ether or life body; C soul body; D sentient soul; E intellectual soul; F consciousness soul; G spirit self; H life spirit; I spirit man. These distinguish bodily, soul, and spiritual aspects of one human being. The letter I in the list is simply the ninth label; it must not be confused with the self called the I.

The first sevenfold account joins C with D and F with G because the text describes them as unities in earthly life. Its full list across pages 36–37 is physical body; etheric or life body; sentient soul body; intellectual soul; spirit-filled consciousness soul; life spirit; spirit man. Two pairs are joined; the distinctions have not been discarded.

The fourfold account on page 37 groups soul body and sentient soul as astral body, and takes intellectual soul and consciousness soul as the related sheaths of the I. It therefore names physical body, life body, astral body, and I. This makes the I’s central role visible while retaining the distinctions developed earlier.

Pages 38–39 then describe transformation through the I’s reception of spirit. Spirit self illuminates and transforms the astral organization of impulses and desires; life spirit transforms the life body; spirit man transforms the physical body. Steiner says that the spiritualized aspect of the physical body requires spiritual perception even though it appears physical to bodily senses. The transformation is his spiritual account, rather than a visible replacement of an organ.

The concluding sevenfold list adds these transformed aspects to the fourfold foundation: physical body; life body; astral body; I as soul kernel; spirit self as transmuted astral body; life spirit as transmuted life body; spirit man as transmuted physical body. Its organizing principle differs from the earlier grouping of nine into seven. Addendum 3’s rainbow and water comparisons explain why Steiner regards the members as real differentiations within a unity, not merely convenient labels; the comparisons illustrate that claim.

From a list to a relationship
Connection with the later lectures

From a list to a relationship

The terms become easier to follow when we ask how they work together. In the later lectures, Steiner describes the physical and etheric organizations as remaining connected during sleep, while the astral organization and I separate from them. He describes death differently: the physical body is cast off. This is a preview of his spiritual account, not something that ordinary observation of sleep establishes.

Now that you know the classification terms, describe what remains connected in his account of sleep and what changes at death. Physical and etheric remain connected in sleep; death involves the release of the physical body. The purpose is to explain relationships within his account, beyond remembering a list of names.

Companion source: Rudolf Steiner, What Is Anthroposophy? Three Perspectives on Self-Knowledge (2002), page markers 75–76. Numbers refer to the first study transcription (191 pages), not the pages of Theosophy.

A fourfold map used for a particular question

The Four Temperaments (4 March 1909, GA 57) uses the physical, etheric, astral and I-bearing members to explain four temperamental tendencies. All four belong to the person in that account; the distinction is predominance, not four separate kinds of human being. This is a useful comparison with Theosophy’s several classifications: ask what question a map is designed to answer before equating its terms with another map.

How can nine become seven?

Sketch nine labelled spaces: A physical body; B life body; C soul body; D sentient soul; E intellectual soul; F consciousness soul; G spirit self; H life spirit; I spirit man. Predict which neighbouring pairs the passage brings together. This represents Steiner’s classification.

First grouping

Bring C and D together: soul body and sentient soul form one connection. Keep both names inside one bracket. Nine spaces become eight groups.

Second grouping and check

Bring F and G together: consciousness soul and spirit self. The seven groups are A, B, [C+D], E, [F+G], H, I. Nothing has been erased. Explain the relationship rather than saying two members disappeared.

See the idea in an example

Write A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I on nine cards. Group C with D and F with G. You now have seven groups, while all nine labels remain.

What this helps explain

In the book’s intermediate grouping, C + D represents soul body with sentient soul; F + G represents consciousness soul with spirit self. This card exercise explains the regrouping only. The later transformation list uses a different organizing principle.

Check your understanding

Why do the chapter’s classifications have different numbers of members?

Reveal the explanation

The first sevenfold list combines two pairs in the ninefold description of an earthly human being. The fourfold account brings related soul members together around the I as physical body, life body, astral body, and I. The final sevenfold list adds spirit self, life spirit, and spirit man as the transformed astral, life, and physical organizations. The counts arise from different ways of expressing the relationships and development of the same being.

What does the final sevenfold list add to the fourfold account?
