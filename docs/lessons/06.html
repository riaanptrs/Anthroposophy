Lesson 06 · The essential nature of the human being

One human being, several classifications

Explain why the ninefold and sevenfold lists differ.

Reading: Rudolf Steiner, Theosophy (GA 9), Chapter I, pp. 35–39; addendum/addenda 3.

Page numbers refer to the 1971 English edition (Monges, revised by Church); use the chapter to locate the passage in other editions. Explanations are original course material.

Understand the idea

The chapter ends by regrouping distinctions already introduced. Its ninefold account names physical body, ether body, soul body, sentient soul, intellectual soul, consciousness soul, spirit self, life spirit, and spirit man. These are aspects of one human being, not nine independent objects.

An intermediate sevenfold presentation joins soul body with sentient soul, and consciousness soul with spirit self. This leaves physical body; ether body; sentient-soul-body; intellectual soul; spirit-filled consciousness soul; life spirit; spirit man. The change is a grouping decision, not the disappearance of two pieces.

A later sevenfold presentation emphasizes transformation: physical body, life body, astral body, I, spirit self, life spirit, spirit man. The last three are associated with the transformation of astral, life, and physical organization. Do not substitute this list for the earlier sevenfold grouping. Damaged OCR in the 1971 transcription was clarified through a parallel Shields translation; wording across editions differs.

Try it

Work with the idea

Write the nine terms on slips of paper. Join only the two pairs described above to make seven groups. Then write the transformation list separately and mark how its organizing question differs.

Use a notebook. This is a study exercise, not a promise of spiritual experience.

Check your understanding

Why do two sevenfold lists not necessarily have identical entries?

Show a suggested answer

One combines adjacent distinctions in the ninefold account; the other foregrounds the I and transformation of bodily organizations. The number alone does not tell us the principle of classification.

Before moving on, explain the main distinction without looking at the text. Identify a passage in the reading that supports your explanation and record one open question.

How to assess your response

Give yourself 0–2 points for each: accurate meaning, clear distinctions, support from the text, and awareness of the kind of claim (observation, argument, analogy, or supersensible report). 0 = missing; 1 = partial; 2 = clear and supported. Revisit anything partial or missing. Well-supported disagreement can receive full marks.

This course presents Steiner’s account as a subject of study. Understanding his claims does not require accepting them as established facts.
