The Constitution Of The Human Being

Bodily foundation and inward activity

How does Steiner connect embodiment with sensation, willing and reflective action?

Before you read

The opening established three relationships to the world. Now read how Steiner differentiates their bodily, inward and reflective aspects. Pay attention to the movement from receiving an impression to acting upon it.

Read Steiner

Rudolf Steiner — Theosophy

1. THE CORPOREAL BEING OF MAN · 2. THE SOUL BEING OF MAN · 3. THE SPIRITUAL BEING OF MAN · 4. BODY, SOUL, AND SPIRIT

Read PDF captures 15–18. Read the first three numbered sections, beginning on capture 15. Include the opening discussion of thinking and feeling in section 4 on capture 18; stop before the detailed account of physical organization.

English translation: Elizabeth Douglas Shields, 1910. Digital reissue: Delhi Open Books; publication year unstated.

What Steiner is saying

The corporeal section locates humans within several forms of existence. Material composition links the body with minerals; growth and reproduction link it with plants; perception and inward experience link it with animals. Steiner then describes specifically human bodily organization in relation to thinking. The point is to establish bodily conditions for the capacities developed next.

The soul section turns from the observed object to the experience it occasions. Two observers can see a red table without either directly seeing the other’s experience of red. Feeling makes the impression pleasing or displeasing. Willing adds a movement outward: a deed gives the world the impress of the person’s inward life. Soul therefore includes receiving and responding, not feeling alone.

The spiritual section asks what happens when the person reflects on impressions and acts. Thinking about a perception seeks knowledge; thinking about an action introduces reasons and coherence. Steiner contrasts bodily processes, whose necessity operates in nature, with the necessity a person acknowledges in thought. An immediate impulse need not decide the action.

At the next section’s opening, he treats the brain as the bodily instrument of thinking. He also argues that persevering thought can deepen feeling rather than necessarily cool it. The account connects reflective activity with embodiment while allowing thought to change the direction of inward life.

Key concept

Willing

In the soul section, Steiner describes willing as the outward expression of inward life through action.

How the idea works

  • An impression is received through bodily organization
  • Sensation and feeling develop inwardly
  • Willing expresses inward life in a deed
  • Reflection can direct the deed through reasons

Course example

Someone hears a bell, feels irritated and wants to stop it. Checking whether the bell warns another person changes what the listener does. This course example illustrates how reflection can guide an impulse while the original irritation remains.

An important distinction

Steiner distinguishes sensation, feeling and willing. Describing soul life only as emotion leaves out both its reception of impressions and its outward activity.

Check your understanding

Which action illustrates willing in the sense of the soul section?

Reveal the explanation

Willing expresses inward life in outward action; the vibration and the inward sensation serve different roles.

Why does Steiner discuss thinking about actions as well as thinking about perceptions?

Reveal the explanation

Reflection can seek knowledge of an object and can examine how an act is directed.

Why this matters

The chapter needs bodily support and inward activity together before asking which organizations Steiner believes sustain life and sensation.

Historical anatomical comparisons

The corporeal section includes a historical Carus quotation and anatomical judgments. Their role is to connect organization with capacity. The course does not use this wording to assess people’s worth or present it as a current biological standard.
