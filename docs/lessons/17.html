Lesson 17 · The three worlds

Thought forms and aura

Explain the aura descriptions without turning them into a colour test.

Start with an example

When someone calls a voice “warm,” they are not reporting its temperature. A word from one kind of experience helps communicate another.

What this helps explain

This illustrates how language can work across kinds of experience. Steiner similarly distinguishes aura colours from colours seen by the eyes. Unlike a casual metaphor, however, he intends to report an actual supersensible perception. The voice comparison does not verify that perception.

Understand the idea

Steiner describes thoughts and soul qualities as having perceptible forms for a developed spiritual perception. He uses colour language to communicate these experiences. The chapter explicitly distinguishes such colours from ordinary colours seen by physical eyes.

The account differentiates three interpenetrating aspects of aura, relating broadly to the bodily conditioned life of drives, the person’s inward soul activity, and spiritual orientation. These are not three painted shells. A colour word is meant to convey a quality of supersensible experience through comparison with a sensory impression, not to supply a camera specification.

Read Addendum 13 before making claims about verification. Steiner defends a methodological position about spiritual perception; the passage does not supply an experimental demonstration of aura reading. A chart that assigns a personality from someone’s favourite colour would erase the distinctions the text asks readers to maintain.

Connection with Course 2 · GA 10

A second source for colour-language

GA 10 reinforces the distinction explained above: sensory colour-words are used by comparison for the experience Steiner describes. Ask: does “not ordinary visible colour” mean “only an invented metaphor”? His answer is no: he regards the described reality as spiritual. Understanding his intended meaning and verifying that claim are separate questions.

Source: How to Know Higher Worlds, markers 55; 106–107 in the 107-page transcription.

Colour language does not make aura a painted outline

Colour, Lecture 6 (5 December 1920), uses light in an expanded spiritual sense and relates it to thought. This helps explain why the colour vocabulary in Theosophy should be read in its stated supersensible context. Painting a blue halo can illustrate an idea, but its visible pigment does not verify the spiritual experience described. Avoid using a colour chart to assign someone a fixed personality or moral rank.

Keep this idea

Colour language in the aura account is not a claim about ordinary coloured light.

Try it

Work with the idea

Rewrite “a red photograph proves an angry aura” as a careful statement about what the book actually claims. Mention the mode of perception and explain why an ordinary image would not establish it.

Use a notebook. This is a study exercise, not a promise of spiritual experience.

Check your understanding

Are the colours in this account simply light that ordinary eyes could see with better attention?

Show a suggested answer

No. Steiner distinguishes the claimed supersensible experience from ordinary visual colour. His use of colour language is comparative; reading the description does not validate a photograph, a personality test, or a claim to diagnose another person.

Before moving on, explain the main distinction without looking at the text. Identify a passage in the reading that supports your explanation and record one open question.

How to assess your response

Give yourself 0–2 points for each: accurate meaning, clear distinctions, support from the text, and awareness of the kind of claim (observation, argument, analogy, or supersensible report). 0 = missing; 1 = partial; 2 = clear and supported. Revisit anything partial or missing. Well-supported disagreement can receive full marks.

This course presents Steiner’s account as a subject of study. Understanding his claims does not require accepting them as established facts.
