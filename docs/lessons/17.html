Thought-forms and the threefold human aura

A clear explanation may be offered to help someone or to win admiration. Its content, its motive and the speaker's passing mood are different features.

Steiner says thoughts and feelings have forms accessible to spiritual perception. His aura description includes temporary states and enduring dispositions. Comparing ordinary behavior helps distinguish these features but does not reveal the claimed spiritual appearance.

The three aspects concern bodily influence on soul life, the soul's own activity and eternal spiritual activity through the person. They interpenetrate rather than forming separate colored shells. Bodily influence can include selfless care, not only selfish impulse.

Color words require their context of aspect, motive and duration. They are not a simple personality chart. The account itself stresses the difficulty of observation; this lesson does not authorize judging another person's aura.

6. THOUGHT-FORMS AND THE HUMAN AURA

Read PDF captures 75–81. Read subsection 6 from its heading on capture 75 through the end of the chapter on capture 81. Pay particular attention to thought-form definiteness on capture 76 and the three aura aspects on captures 78–80.

English translation: Elizabeth Douglas Shields, 1910. Digital reissue: Delhi Open Books; publication year unstated.

What Steiner is saying

Steiner again relates perception to the appropriate organ. Feelings and thoughts, although not objects for physical eyes, are said to become objects for spiritual perception. Colour language communicates this proposed experience by analogy with sensory colour. The passage also points forward to the path of knowledge as the setting in which such perception would develop.

Thought-forms express character and definiteness. Clear thought is described as outlined; confused thought as wavering. Motive also matters: intellectual activity serving bodily pleasure differs from thought directed toward knowledge or selfless interest. The aura expresses both transient moods and more enduring dispositions, so one colour name cannot capture the whole description.

Steiner then distinguishes three kinds of appearance: mist-like relative opacity, illuminating light, and sparkling or raying activity that continually renews itself. They interpenetrate. Attending separately to them is compared with hearing rather than looking at a bell, or closing one’s eyes to attend to music.

Their explanatory roles reconnect the account with body, soul and spirit. The first aura reflects bodily influence on the soul; the second expresses the soul’s own life beyond immediate sensory influence; the third expresses the eternal spirit’s activity through the person. The first is not simply a bad aura: Steiner includes naturally selfless impulses, especially mother-love, within it.

The source stresses the difficulty of observation and description and offers the account as an occasion for thought. Its descriptions require their context: aspect, shade, form, motive and duration. They explain the human being as it would appear within the proposed higher perception, rather than furnishing a simple colour chart for judging another person.

Key concept

Threefold aura

Steiner describes three interpenetrating aura aspects as the supersensible expression of bodily influence, soul life and eternal spiritual activity.

How the idea works

  • First aspect: bodily influence on soul
  • Second aspect: the soul’s own life
  • Third aspect: eternal spiritual activity

Course example

The same thoughtful act may serve personal advantage or a concern for someone else. This course example clarifies why motive adds information beyond intellectual ability; it does not make either motive visible as a physical colour.

An important distinction

The three aspects interpenetrate rather than form three separate shells. The first aspect includes bodily-rooted selflessness as well as impulses of desire.
