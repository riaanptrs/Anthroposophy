Lesson 14 · The three worlds

Individuality, purpose, and return

Explain why development is not equivalent to worldly success.

Start with an example

A fictional person learns to listen more carefully, while their income and job title remain unchanged.

What this helps explain

This shows why outer success and inner development are different measures. It does not reveal that person’s spiritual history or explain why anyone faces hardship. Steiner’s account of purpose cannot be read directly from a bank balance or social position.

Understand the idea

The later part of this section relates spiritual individuality to intentions and future earthly activity. In Steiner’s account, the spirit does more than collect experiences: it works within larger relationships and returns to embodied life with possibilities for further development.

Do not imagine a simple ladder in which each new life must bring more wealth, status, or ease. The text’s idea of purpose concerns spiritual development, not a scoreboard of outward advantage. Addendum 12 belongs with this discussion and helps prevent a rigid, simplified reading of the relationship between lives.

This account raises difficult questions about hardship and meaning. A learner can explain Steiner’s proposal without declaring what another person intended before birth or why a particular hardship occurred. A theory about development is not personal knowledge of someone else’s spiritual history. Keep the explanatory claim and the limits of application together.

Keep this idea

Do not use outward circumstances as a spiritual scorecard.

Reading: Rudolf Steiner, Theosophy (GA 9), Chapter III, pp. 121–127; addendum/addenda 12.

Page numbers refer to the 1971 English edition (Monges, revised by Church); use the chapter to locate the passage in other editions. Explanations are original course material.

Try it

Work with the idea

Write two accounts of a fictional return to earthly life: one that equates development with higher social status, and one that explains the book’s emphasis on capacity and purpose. Critique the first without diagnosing real people.

Use a notebook. This is a study exercise, not a promise of spiritual experience.

Check your understanding

Would greater wealth in a later life be sufficient evidence of greater spiritual development in this account?

Show a suggested answer

No. Outward success is not the measure that defines spiritual development here. Nor can a person’s circumstances reveal a specific spiritual intention simply because we have read the theory.

Before moving on, explain the main distinction without looking at the text. Identify a passage in the reading that supports your explanation and record one open question.

How to assess your response

Give yourself 0–2 points for each: accurate meaning, clear distinctions, support from the text, and awareness of the kind of claim (observation, argument, analogy, or supersensible report). 0 = missing; 1 = partial; 2 = clear and supported. Revisit anything partial or missing. Well-supported disagreement can receive full marks.

This course presents Steiner’s account as a subject of study. Understanding his claims does not require accepting them as established facts.
