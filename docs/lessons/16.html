5. THE PHYSICAL WORLD AND ITS CONNECTION WITH THE SOUL AND SPIRIT LANDS

Read PDF captures 74–75. Begin on capture 74 at the paragraph introducing beings beyond the four visible nature kingdoms. Read the Folk Spirit, Spirit of the Age and elemental-being account through capture 75; stop before subsection 6.

English translation: Elizabeth Douglas Shields, 1910. Digital reissue: Delhi Open Books; publication year unstated.

What Steiner is saying

The Folk Spirit, or National Spirit, is said to act through the sensations, feelings and tendencies shared in a people’s life. Steiner intends a being, rather than merely an abstract name for these shared experiences. He states that its activity appears in human souls but does not originate solely in those souls.

This distinguishes its embodiment from a human physical body. A person forms a body from sensory materials; the Folk Spirit forms a body from soul-world substance. The cloud comparison expresses a common soul environment in which individuals participate, rather than a physical cloud over a territory.

The Spirit of the Age, or Zeitgeist, extends the proposal to an epoch. Salamanders, sylphs, undines and gnomes are named as beings cooperating in the construction of the world. Steiner explicitly denies them physical bodies visible to physical eyes. Their proposed activity therefore differs from discovering an overlooked animal species.

He also mentions beings whose vestment belongs to Spirit-land alone, without descending into a soul-world body. The distinction between physical embodiment, soul-world body and spiritual vestment extends the chapter’s central relation between a mode of existence and its corresponding perception.

Key concept

Folk Spirit / National Spirit

Steiner uses these names for a spiritual being expressed through a people’s common soul life and embodied in soul-world substance.

Course example

A song shared at a festival is an observable cultural practice. Steiner’s account proposes a further spiritual foundation for collective life. This course example distinguishes the practice from the kind of being the passage describes.

An important distinction

The collective being proposed here is not a fixed personal description of every individual in a people. Its claimed shared activity and each individual’s life are different levels of the account.
