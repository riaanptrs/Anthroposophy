The Three Worlds

Sympathy, antipathy and the seven soul regions

How do attraction and exclusion organize Steiner’s seven soul regions?

Before you read

The previous reading distinguished soul interaction from physical impact. Now read how Steiner differentiates kinds of soul formation. Track what the two forces do before memorizing the seven region names.

Read Steiner

Rudolf Steiner — Theosophy

1. THE SOUL WORLD

Read PDF captures 47–50. Begin on capture 47 at the paragraph introducing the two basic soul forces. Read through the seven-region list on capture 49 and its continuation on capture 50; stop before subsection 2.

English translation: Elizabeth Douglas Shields, 1910. Digital reissue: Delhi Open Books; publication year unstated.

What Steiner is saying

Sympathy attracts other formations and seeks union with them. Antipathy excludes and maintains separate identity. Both forces are present in the first three regions, but their proportions differ. These are explanatory terms for relations within the account, rather than synonyms for friendliness and hostility.

In Burning Desire, antipathy predominates. A formation attracts greedily but excludes so much that satisfaction remains impossible. In Flowing Excitability, the forces balance, permitting plastic and relatively neutral reception. Steiner’s red-colour example separates receiving a sensation from subsequently enjoying it. In Wishes, sympathy predominates, yet remaining antipathy directs attraction into the formation’s own sphere.

The fourth region, Attraction and Repulsion, concerns sympathy among the parts of the formation itself. It is inward feeling, expressed as liking and its diminution in disliking. This is a change of relationship, rather than one more ratio of the first three regions. The fourth links the lower three with the higher three.

Soul Light, Active Soul Force and Soul Life describe freely outward, enlivening sympathy. Another being can be received for its own sake rather than appropriated for one’s own life. Unlike reception that requires contact, this activity is described as radiation. Steiner initially explains these three together; their different roles become clearer in the following account of purification.

The seven regions interpenetrate. Solid, liquid, gas and light provide comparisons for patterns of rigidity, receptivity, expansion and illumination. The list orders kinds of relationship; it does not turn soul reality into seven physical layers.

Key concept

Sympathy and antipathy

Steiner calls attraction toward union sympathy, and exclusion that maintains separate identity antipathy.

How the idea works

  • Regions 1–3: different proportions of both forces
  • Region 4: inward life of feeling
  • Regions 5–7: freely outward, enlivening sympathy

Course example

A reader can seek a book solely for facts that confirm a preferred opinion, or attend to its argument even where it is unfamiliar. This course example clarifies appropriation versus receptive interest; it does not classify the reader into a soul region.

An important distinction

The fourth region is inward sympathy, while the higher three are freely outward sympathy. They are not simply stronger versions of the first region’s desire.

Why this matters

Steiner will use these differentiated relationships to explain how different kinds of attachment are transformed after death.

Connection

Before applying the regions, he returns to the soul’s place between body and spirit and asks what bodily death changes.
