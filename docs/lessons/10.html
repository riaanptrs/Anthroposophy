Three worlds and their organs of perception

A ringing bell can be seen and heard. Looking at its surface and hearing its sound disclose different qualities of one event. A single mode of access need not exhaust what can be known.

Steiner extends this relation between organ and manifestation to proposed soul and spiritual perception. He says developed capacities disclose qualities unavailable to physical senses. The bell clarifies the analogy, not the existence of those capacities.

The three worlds are not separate physical locations. Their qualities and laws differ: soul affinity is not explained simply by the distance and impact used to describe physical bodies.

Words such as substance and space therefore need care. They introduce relations through familiar language without making soul reality thin physical matter. A human experience can involve body, soul and spirit together rather than one isolated set of laws.

1. THE SOUL WORLD

English translation: Elizabeth Douglas Shields, 1910. Digital reissue: Delhi Open Books; publication year unstated.

What Steiner is saying

Steiner starts from the relation between a being and its organs of perception. Light and sound do not disclose themselves in the same way to every creature. An eye or ear makes a particular kind of manifestation accessible. His discussion of Goethe adds that a being is revealed through its manifestations; its richness need not be exhausted by one kind of access.

He extends this relation to soul and spirit. Bodily organs ordinarily develop through nature, while the higher organs he proposes require the person’s own development. The soul and spirit worlds would therefore disclose qualities unavailable to physical senses. They are not additional places outside physical space, and their substance is not merely physical matter made thinner.

This distinction governs the language of the chapter. Words such as substance, space and sense begin as comparisons with familiar experience. The soul’s desires and feelings are related to a wider soul world, just as bodily materials belong to physical nature. Astral names soul substantiality; kama names its forces, although their separation is less sharp than the physical matter/force distinction.

The ivory-ball and perspective examples then distinguish laws. Physical impact depends on measurable motion; the proposed meeting of soul formations depends on inner affinity. Their apparent relation is not explained by ordinary distance alone. Because the human soul is also connected with body and spirit, Steiner warns against attributing every occurrence in it solely to soul-world laws.

Key concept

Sense

Steiner uses sense comparatively for an organ of perception, including the soul and spiritual organs he describes.

Course example

Two readers see the same musical notation, but a trained musician recognizes relationships that the beginner misses. This course example clarifies the difference between having an object before oneself and developing a capacity to apprehend it.

An important distinction

The three worlds are distinguished by qualities and access, rather than placed in three separate physical locations.
