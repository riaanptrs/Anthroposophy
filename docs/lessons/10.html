Lesson 10 · The three worlds

The soul world: seven relationships

Explain sympathy, antipathy, and the seven interpenetrating soul regions in the order of the text.

What changes across the seven soul-world relationships?

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · Theosophy · GA 9 · Monges / Church, 1971 · Printed p. 86 · PDF p. 114

Throughout the first three regions, the soul formations receive their qualities from the relative proportions of sympathy and antipathy.

Anthroposophic Press, 1971; Henry B. Monges, translator; Gilbert Church, reviser. English excerpt checked against the supplied PDF image; Brazilian Portuguese is a new course study translation. Line breaks and line-end hyphenation normalized.

Open the source or edition index →

What this passage means

“The first three” is a limit on this sentence: those regions differ by force proportions. Read the next two sentences for the fourth region’s inward sympathy and the higher three’s freely outward sympathy. Explain the change of relationship, rather than calling every region a stronger degree of liking.

The focus of this lesson

Explain sympathy, antipathy, and the seven interpenetrating soul regions in the order of the text.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

Understand the idea

Steiner begins with the human being’s participation in physical, soul, and spiritual worlds. Physical senses disclose bodily phenomena; he proposes other, trainable organs for soul and spirit perception. These worlds are not spatially outside the physical world, and their substance is not thinner physical matter. His words “sense,” “space,” and “substance” extend familiar language by comparison. Read pp. 70–77 before interpreting the regional descriptions.

In soul space, formations meet according to their inner qualities rather than the physical laws of impact or perspective. Affinity can make them interpenetrate; conflicting natures repel. “Astral” names soul substantiality, characterized by excitability and desire, rather than a place among the stars. The human soul is connected to body and spirit, so Steiner also distinguishes soul processes from influences of those other worlds (pp. 78–80).

The two basic forces are sympathy, which attracts and seeks union, and antipathy, which excludes and maintains separate identity. They are technical terms, not simply friendliness and hostility. Both occur in the first three regions, but their proportions differ. A learner needs to explain what they do before memorizing the names.

Region 1, Burning Desire: antipathy predominates. A formation greedily attracts a limited range of things while excluding much else; its own exclusion prevents satisfaction. Region 2, Mobile Sensitivity: the forces balance, allowing relatively neutral, mobile reception. Steiner distinguishes simply receiving a red colour sensation from subsequently liking the colour (pp. 81–83).

Region 3, Wishes: sympathy predominates, yet remaining antipathy still directs attraction into the formation’s own sphere. Desire to possess differs from receiving another being for its own sake. The solid, liquid, and gaseous comparisons explain the different patterns of relative rigidity, receptivity, and expansion; they do not turn soul substance into physical states of matter.

Region 4, Liking and Disliking: sympathy operates among the parts of the formation itself, as an inward life of feeling. Here the author describes disliking as diminished liking, rather than simply repeating the balance of sympathy and antipathy in regions 1–3. This fourth region links the lower three with the higher three (pp. 83–85).

Regions 5–7 are Soul Light, Active Soul Force, and Soul Life. Sympathy becomes freely outward, receiving other beings as revelation and enlivening the common soul world. Their first description is mostly shared; the following section gives their different roles in purification. Do not invent separate mechanisms where this passage supplies only a collective description. All seven regions interpenetrate (p. 86). A regional sequence organizes qualities in Steiner’s account, rather than a journey through seven physical territories.

What this helps explain

This is a reading aid for the technical roles of sympathy and antipathy. Steiner describes soul-world regions through relationships between these forces. The drawing is not a picture of a place, and everyday friendliness is not the whole meaning of sympathy here.

Check your understanding

What changes across the seven soul-world relationships?

Reveal the explanation

Reducing technical sympathy to stronger liking misses a change in relationship. The first three regions involve attraction and exclusion, the fourth includes inward feeling, and the higher three describe freely outward, enlivening participation.

Why should technical sympathy not be reduced to ordinary liking?

Pause and answer in one sentence, aloud or in your notes.

See a way to approach it

It describes relations among soul formations. The first regions involve attraction and exclusion; the middle includes inward feeling; the higher regions involve freely outward, enlivening participation. This is more than a stronger personal preference.

Original open question and answer

Why is “sympathy increases, so all seven regions are just stronger liking” inaccurate?

Show a suggested answer

It misses a change in relationship. The first three involve attraction and exclusion in different proportions; the fourth concerns sympathy within a formation and inward feeling; the higher three describe freely outward, enlivening participation. The text’s technical sympathy is wider than ordinary liking.
