Lesson 05 · The essential nature of the human being

The I and spiritual individuality

Explain the I as the mediator of body, soul, and spirit, and distinguish spirit self, life spirit, and spirit man.

How can an independent truth become individualised in spirit self?

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · Theosophy · GA 9 · Monges / Church, 1971 · Printed p. 30 · PDF p. 58

The spirit self is a revelation of the spiritual world within the “I,” just as from the other side sensations are a revelation of the physical world within the “I.”

Anthroposophic Press, 1971; Henry B. Monges, translator; Gilbert Church, reviser. English excerpt checked against the supplied PDF image; Brazilian Portuguese is a new course study translation. Line breaks and line-end hyphenation normalized.

Open the source or edition index →

What this passage means

The comparison places both revelations within the I: sensation brings physical content, and spirit self brings spiritual content. The immediately preceding paragraph distinguishes contact with truth in the consciousness soul from that truth taken up and individualized by the I. Receiving a truth as part of one’s being does not make its validity depend on personal preference.

The focus of this lesson

Explain the I as the mediator of body, soul, and spirit, and distinguish spirit self, life spirit, and spirit man.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

Understand the idea

Jean Paul’s childhood recollection introduces awareness of oneself as an independent being. Steiner then uses the word I: any person can name a table, but when each says I about themselves, the reference comes from their own inward standpoint. Addendum 5 qualifies this illustration: repeating the word is different from connecting it with the idea, which develops gradually. The lesson concerns self-awareness rather than a test of how early a child speaks.

The I gathers the experiences of body and soul and acts through them. At the same time, it opens toward spirit. The text’s two directions matter: sensations bring the physical world into experience; intuition brings spiritual content. For Steiner, the I becomes more individual as it makes truth and goodness its own. This is spiritual individuality, not merely the assertion of a private preference.

On page 30 the consciousness soul is in contact with truth independent of liking, while spirit self is that same spiritual truth taken into and individualized by the I. Receiving it does not make truth privately true; it makes it part of this person’s being. The distinction connects the preceding lesson’s orientation toward truth with the spiritual individuality developed here.

Intuition names revelation of the spiritual in contrast with sensation as revelation of the bodily world. Even simple thought is called an elementary manifestation in this discussion. Addendum 6 distinguishes this manifestation from the fuller intuition of supersensible perception developed in other works. A sudden hunch or intensity of conviction therefore does not explain the term’s role in the chapter.

Pages 32–34 add spirit man and life spirit. Spirit man names the independent spiritual being; life spirit names the formative spiritual life that builds and sustains it; spirit self names spirit individualized in the I. The analogies of nourishment and sheath describe how the spiritual being receives from its world and develops its individuality. Steiner explicitly says that spatial enlargement is a picture of a spiritual reality, so do not imagine a second material skin expanding around the body.

The chapter now has three spiritual members as well as three bodily and three soul members. They are connected within one human being. The next lesson brings the names together and explains why the book can regroup them as nine, seven, or four without changing the subject.

Keep intuition tied to its context. In The Philosophy of Freedom, Steiner describes an inward apprehension of conceptual or spiritual content, not a hunch. A feeling of certainty may accompany understanding, but it is not the definition of intuition or a guarantee that a judgment is true. For example, different drawings can share the relation of three straight sides enclosing a plane figure. Grasping this relation differs from merely remembering the word triangle. Explain the relation you understand and examine its application. This clarification does not make conceptual understanding identical with every later description of higher spiritual perception.

Additional connection: The Philosophy of Freedom (GA 4), markers 46–51, 75–78 in the 147-page Basis transcription. Locate by chapter in another edition. Find the book.

Different drawings, a shared relation

Size and angles differ. What lets you recognize both as triangles? Explain the relation between the sides instead of just repeating the name. You can use words; you need not form a mental image.

In GA 4, Steiner distinguishes shared conceptual content from the individual way of grasping it. Two people can understand the same relation without identical memories or feelings. Satisfaction may accompany understanding; it is not its criterion of truth.

Individuality and the capacity to love
Connection with the later lectures

Individuality and the capacity to love

In the later lectures, Steiner associates the manifestation of the I with love: the capacity to open toward another being and the world. This extends the meaning of individuality beyond a private standpoint. In his account, being an individual also makes a relationship with another possible.

Imagine listening to a friend whose needs differ from yours. You can try to understand those needs while retaining your own judgment. This illustrates an outward orientation, not a test for perceiving the spiritual I. What would be lost if “I” meant only selfishness? The possibility of individual participation in care and understanding.

Companion source: Rudolf Steiner, What Is Anthroposophy? Three Perspectives on Self-Knowledge (2002), page markers 160–162. Numbers refer to the first study transcription (191 pages), not the pages of Theosophy.

Keep this idea

The everyday word “I” introduces a question about individuality; it does not settle the whole spiritual account.

See the idea in an example

Ana says “I am reading.” Bruno also says “I am reading.” They use the same word, but each speaks from their own standpoint.

What this helps explain

Steiner uses this special role of “I” to introduce individuality. It is a starting point, not proof of an immortal self. In the later explanation, the I can take up spiritual content and transform soul life; it is not another name for selfishness.

Check your understanding

How can an independent truth become individualised in spirit self?

Reveal the explanation

Its validity is not changed by being received. The consciousness soul is in contact with that truth; spirit self names the spiritual content made part of the individual I’s own being. Steiner joins a shared or self-existent truth with individual appropriation of it. The I can therefore develop through spiritual content without turning that content into an arbitrary personal opinion.
