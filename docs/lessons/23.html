Sensation and its bodily mediation

What distinguishes sentient-soul from the organization supporting sensation?

Before you read

Read Steiner’s account of the visual process and the experience of blue. Keep the bodily events and the inward sensation distinguishable as the argument develops. The source then introduces two terms whose relationship matters more than remembering their names.

Read Steiner

Rudolf Steiner — Theosophy

4. BODY, SOUL, AND SPIRIT

Read PDF captures 20–22. Begin in capture 20’s middle column at the move from life-body to inward sensation. Read through the relation of sentient-soul and soul-body; stop before thinking begins serving sentient life on capture 22.

English translation: Elizabeth Douglas Shields, 1910. Digital reissue: Delhi Open Books; publication year unstated.

What Steiner is saying

Light reaches the eye, occasions bodily processes and is associated with activity in the brain. Within Steiner’s account, those processes can also be considered as living processes. He then asks where the experience of blue is found. His distinction is between describing a process in the bodily organization and encountering a sensation from within.

Sentient-soul names the activity through which impressions become inward experiences. Sensation is only part of this domain: desire, aversion, instinct, impulse and passion have the same personally lived character. Steiner therefore extends the term beyond a momentary sensory registration.

This inward life does not dispense with its bodily support. The sentient-soul depends upon ether-body and, through it, upon physical organization and functioning sense organs. Soul-body, also called sentient body, mediates those relationships. It should be distinguished from sentient-soul even though Steiner describes them as closely united.

The discussion of the seer adds another distinction. Experiencing one’s own sensation-world is not the same task as perceiving another’s through the faculty Steiner describes. His reported spiritual access remains a claim about a mode of perception, not permission to assume that another person feels exactly what we feel.

Key concept

Sentient-soul

Steiner uses this term for inwardly experienced sensation and its related desires, aversions, impulses and passions.

How the idea works

  • Bodily organs receive an impression
  • The living organization supports their activity
  • Soul-body mediates the bodily relationship
  • Sentient-soul experiences sensation inwardly

Course example

A description of someone’s eye movements does not describe how a color appears to that person from within. This course example clarifies two standpoints in Steiner’s argument; it is not an independent proof of his classification.

An important distinction

Soul-body concerns organization supporting sentient activity; sentient-soul concerns inwardly lived experience. The chapter relates them without treating the terms as interchangeable.

Check your understanding

Which distinction is central to the blue-color discussion?

Reveal the explanation

Steiner preserves bodily and living processes while distinguishing the sensation experienced inwardly.

How does this section relate soul-body and sentient-soul?

Reveal the explanation

The sentient-soul depends on living bodily organization; soul-body mediates that relationship.

Why this matters

The distinctions prevent physical process, living organization and sensation from becoming one undifferentiated concept. They also prepare the later use of astral-body for a particular grouping.
