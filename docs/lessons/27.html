Chapter 2. Re-Embodiment Of The Spirit And Destiny

Read PDF captures 36–38. Begin at “In such observations as the preceding…” in the right column of capture 36. Read through the end of capture 38, including the distinction between life-body and life-spirit.

Selected passage: capture 38

These experiences themselves pass away, but their fruits remain.

English translation: Elizabeth Douglas Shields, 1910. Digital reissue: Delhi Open Books; publication year unstated.

What Steiner is saying

The soul connects body and spirit through the members developed in Chapter I. Sentient-soul penetrates soul-body and receives impressions through it; consciousness-soul brings nourishment to spirit-self. These relationships explain why the same earthly life can depend on bodily organization and contribute to spiritual development.

Steiner places physical-body, ether-body and soul-body within bodily heredity. Through the interaction of soul-body and sentient-soul, tendencies and personal characteristics can also be transmitted. Goethe’s account of stature and a serious manner from his father, and a joyous disposition and love of romance from his mother, illustrates this allowance. Steiner distinguishes these qualities from Goethe’s genius.

On the spiritual side, spirit-self brings the good and true into the I, where consciousness-soul connects them with experience. A later similar situation can be met differently because the earlier one has changed the person’s capacities. Learning therefore makes a transitory experience fruitful rather than simply keeping an unchanged scene.

Steiner compares this acquired development with capacities present early in life. Mozart’s musical retention and Lessing’s cultivated critical ability illustrate innate and developed skill. He interprets capacities not acquired in the present life as fruits of earlier experience received by spirit-self. This is the added cross-life explanation, beyond the familiar observation that learning can improve a capacity.

The ending names life-spirit in this continuity. Just as life-body is said to reproduce bodily form between generations, life-spirit is said to continue soul existence from one personal existence to another. The account thus distinguishes inherited form and tendencies from the fruits carried forward within spiritual individuality.

Key concept

Acquired capacities

Steiner describes these as enduring fruits of learning appropriated by spirit-self; he extends the account to capacities that an individuality brings into an earthly life.

How the idea works

  • Experience enters inward life through sentient-soul
  • Consciousness-soul brings its fruits to spirit-self
  • Assimilation develops capacities
  • Steiner interprets capacities carried into life as fruits of prior experience

Course example

A musician may resemble a parent in temperament while developing a distinctive skill through practice. This course example separates the questions of a family resemblance and an acquired capacity; it does not establish the source of a talent before birth.

An important distinction

Steiner does allow inherited personal tendencies. Spiritual continuity in this reading concerns the individuality’s fruits, rather than an unchanged personality or a remembered catalogue of previous lives.

Reveal the explanation

The source allows hereditary tendencies through soul-body and sentient-soul while distinguishing them from essential spiritual individuality.

How does the argument move from learning during life to capacities present early in life?

Reveal the explanation

The source extends its fruits-of-learning account across lives; the extension concerns retained capacities, not complete episodic recall.

Why this matters

This explanation gives content to the biography argument: an earthly life supplies experiences, bodily inheritance supplies conditions, and spirit receives capacities from what is assimilated.

Connection

Experience becomes enduring within the spirit. The next reading considers the other direction introduced at the chapter’s opening: deeds that become enduring outside the actor.
