Biography and spiritual individuality

Why does Steiner argue that individuality calls for a spiritual history before birth?

Selected passage: capture 35

As physical man, I repeat the shape of my forefathers. What do I repeat as spiritual man?

English translation: Elizabeth Douglas Shields, 1910. Digital reissue: Delhi Open Books; publication year unstated.

What Steiner is saying

The first step concerns bodily form. A body is built from physical materials during life, but its human form belongs to a line of descent preceding this person’s birth. Steiner asks an analogous question about spiritual form: the particular dispositions and development through which an individual meets experience.

Similar upbringing does not, he argues, produce identical spiritual forms. Biography gives the individual significance that a description of the human species or the person’s relatives cannot supply. Describing Mr. Smith’s father or son does not describe Mr. Smith’s own development. In this analogy, each spiritual individual is a species by himself: its distinctive continuity calls for its own account.

Steiner then extends the comparison. If definite bodily form requires a prior physical history, he argues that definite spiritual form requires a prior spiritual history. Parents have different spiritual biographies, so that history cannot simply be theirs. He interprets the dispositions with which someone enters life as results of work preceding birth and concludes that the same spiritual individual re-embodies.

The source also considers a demand for the kind of evidence used in physical science. Steiner replies that the proposed continuity belongs to a spiritual region and that thinking is the ordinary capacity through which the question can be approached. The reasoning therefore includes both the familiar distinction between biographies and the claimed spiritual history offered to explain it.

Key concept

Spiritual individuality

Here Steiner means the distinctive spiritual configuration and development expressed through a person’s predispositions and biography, whose continuity he distinguishes from inherited bodily form.

How the idea works

  • Bodily form has a preceding physical history
  • Individual spiritual form calls for its own explanatory history
  • Parents’ distinct biographies do not supply that same individuality
  • Steiner infers a prior history and re-embodiment of the same spirit

Course example

A family tree records how someone is related to others. A biography explains that person’s particular development and undertakings. This course example clarifies the two explanatory questions; it does not establish an earlier incarnation.

An important distinction

Calling a spiritual individual its own species is an analogy within Steiner’s argument. It does not classify each person as a separate biological species.

Why this matters

The chapter has moved from what one life leaves within a person to the origin and continuity of the individuality that receives it. The next explanation must distinguish that individuality from the personal traits Steiner allows to be inherited.
