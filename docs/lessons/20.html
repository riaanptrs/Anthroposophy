Ordered thinking and educated will

What must guide thinking and action when ordinary sensory facts no longer supply correction?

Before you read

Steiner has described how feeling could receive impressions without immediately dictating action. He now compares everyday thinking with inquiry into the higher worlds. Watch how his account of disciplined thought becomes a requirement for conduct as well.

What Steiner is saying

In ordinary life, facts can correct a confused idea. The person walking through a town must follow the actual road, and the mechanic must work according to the machine’s structure. Steiner uses these examples to identify an order that the sensory world imposes even when someone’s initial thoughts are inaccurate.

He says spiritual inquiry does not receive that same compulsion from perceptible facts. The learner must therefore enter it with thinking already regulated. One thought should follow another through the requirements of its content, rather than through an arbitrary association, a favored conclusion or a personal feeling that diverts the reasoning.

Mathematics supplies an example of this discipline because a conclusion cannot simply be altered to suit preference. Steiner immediately qualifies the example: healthy thinking can develop without studying mathematics. What matters is the relationship among thoughts, including the disciplined movement from premises to conclusions.

The same demand is extended to action. The seeker should act according to a good that has been recognized, rather than merely according to advantage or reward. Discomfort alone should not interrupt a recognized right action, and pleasure alone should not sustain an action recognized as wrong. Renunciation of self-will concerns the rule of personal preference, while the true and beautiful are to guide willing.

Steiner also claims that sincere striving for truth can redirect even a mistaken seeker, and regards an objection based on possible error as harmful unbelief. This is a strong part of his account, not a logical rule that honest intentions make every judgment correct. His concluding point joins thinking and willing: spiritual life is to be entered through living in accordance with its order, rather than through observation alone.

Key concept

Regulated thinking

Steiner requires thought connections to follow their content and laws rather than arbitrary succession, preferred conclusions or personal impulses.

How the idea works

  • Sensory facts correct everyday thinking
  • Spiritual inquiry requires inner order beforehand
  • Thought connections follow the requirements of their content
  • Action follows a good that has been recognized

Course example

A reader notices a flaw in an argument supporting a favorite opinion. They examine the steps, revise their conclusion and acknowledge the correction despite disappointment. This course example illustrates allowing a recognized requirement to govern both thinking and action.

An important distinction

Inner discipline is not a guarantee that every premise is true. The mathematical comparison concerns orderly relations among thoughts; Steiner’s further claims concern the spiritual significance he attributes to that discipline.

Check your understanding

What is the role of mathematics in this passage?

Reveal the explanation

Steiner recommends the discipline illustrated by mathematics, then expressly says healthy thinking is possible without its study.

What should guide action in the corresponding discipline of will?

Reveal the explanation

He extends the demand for inner lawfulness to willing and contrasts recognition of the good with personal advantage and the fruits of action.

Why this matters

The path educates the knower as a participant. Receptive feeling, lawful thought and action directed toward the recognized good must work together in the person who seeks the knowledge described earlier in the book.

Connection

With thinking and willing joined, Steiner returns to transformed feeling. He then connects this inner development with attention to the eternal, action from within and the final account of initiation.
