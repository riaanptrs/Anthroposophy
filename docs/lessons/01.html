Rudolf Steiner — Theosophy

Chapter 1. The Constitution Of The Human Being

Read PDF captures 13–15. Read the unnumbered chapter opening in captures 13–15. Begin at the Goethe discussion and finish before the first numbered section about corporeal existence.

English translation: Elizabeth Douglas Shields, 1910. Digital reissue: Delhi Open Books; publication year unstated.

What Steiner is saying

Goethe’s opening contrast supplies a change of standpoint. We ordinarily assess things by how they affect our lives: a plant may be attractive or useful. Inquiry into the plant asks a different question, concerning its structure and its relationships with other plants. The object remains the same; the criterion changes.

Steiner develops this contrast through two encounters with a meadow. The first flowers disclose colors and bring enjoyment. When the observer returns a year later, those flowers have gone, but the earlier enjoyment can be remembered. New flowers can be understood through a relationship already recognized in the species. The object, the private experience and the understood relationship therefore persist differently.

Body initially names the person’s access to things through the senses. Soul names the personal inward significance of what is encountered, including pleasure, sorrow, desire and aversion. Spirit names the activity through which the nature and relationships of things become intelligible. Steiner introduces these meanings from experience before differentiating the members of the human being.

His example of the stars confirms the point. The observer’s delight belongs to that person; a correctly understood relationship of the stars concerns its object. This does not make every thought true. It distinguishes a personal response from the attempt to understand something whose significance is not decided by that response.

Key concept

Body, soul, spirit

At this stage, Steiner distinguishes sensory access, inward personal significance and knowledge of relationships through these three terms.

How the idea works

  • Senses disclose the object
  • Soul life relates its impression to this person
  • Understanding seeks what belongs to the object

Course example

A listener dislikes a piece of music but can still recognize its repeated rhythm. This course example separates a personal response from understanding a relationship in the same encounter.

An important distinction

The opening presents three relationships of one human being. It has not yet introduced the later technical classification of human members.

Check your understanding

Which part of the meadow example belongs specifically to the observer’s inner history?

Reveal the explanation

The first flowers disappear, while their enjoyment can remain as this person’s memory.

What does the stars example distinguish?

Reveal the explanation

Steiner contrasts the observer’s delight with knowledge whose significance concerns the stars rather than that person’s liking.

Why this matters

The later distinctions develop this starting problem: what makes bodily access, inward experience and understanding possible within one person?

Connection

Steiner next gives separate attention to corporeal, soul and spiritual existence. Read those short source sections as developments of the opening.
