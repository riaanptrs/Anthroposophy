Lesson 04 · The essential nature of the human being

Three aspects of soul

Explain how thinking serves desire and how orientation toward truth and goodness differentiates the three soul members.

What distinguishes consciousness soul from clever reasoning serving a desire?

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · Theosophy · GA 9 · Monges / Church, 1971 · Printed p. 25 · PDF p. 53

Thus three members must be distinguished in the soul as in the body, namely, sentient soul, intellectual soul and consciousness soul.

Anthroposophic Press, 1971; Henry B. Monges, translator; Gilbert Church, reviser. English excerpt checked against the supplied PDF image; Brazilian Portuguese is a new course study translation. Line breaks and line-end hyphenation normalized.

Open the source or edition index →

What this passage means

The comparison with the body gathers three differentiated aspects within one soul life. Use the preceding argument to give each name its role: experience and desire; thinking that works within that life; and orientation toward enduring truth and goodness. The intellectual soul can reason effectively in the service of a wish, so cleverness alone does not explain the consciousness soul.

The focus of this lesson

Explain how thinking serves desire and how orientation toward truth and goodness differentiates the three soul members.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

Understand the idea

The sentient soul encounters sensation and the desires, aversions, and impulses connected with it. Thinking first enters this life as a practical helper. The child learns that fire burns; people devise tools, transport, and communication to meet needs. Steiner names the soul served by thought the intellectual or mind soul. Thinking in this role can organize a desire very effectively while the desire still supplies its purpose.

The argument then changes direction. In the starry-heavens example on page 22, delight belongs to the individual, while knowledge of the paths of heavenly bodies has a significance others can share. On page 23 Steiner distinguishes truth from our changing conceptions of it: discovering an error changes what we believe, not truth into something that depended on our enjoyment. He takes this independence as a connection between soul and an enduring spiritual reality.

Moral goodness supplies the parallel on page 24. Inclinations can be educated so that they freely accord with what is recognized as good; goodness does not acquire its value because a desire approves it. Steiner’s ideal is the refinement of inclination rather than its permanent suppression. An ordinary example is recognizing that one should correct a misleading statement even when keeping it would bring an advantage.

The consciousness soul names the part of soul life in which the enduring true and good become alive. This is more specific than being awake or having any sensation: Steiner expressly acknowledges ordinary consciousness in animals. The three members are therefore sentient soul, intellectual soul, and consciousness soul, connected and interpenetrating rather than three sealed mental rooms.

For an original application, compare hearing criticism, planning a reply that wins approval, and examining whether the criticism is justified. The second can be clever yet remain in the service of a wish; the third asks for a valid judgment even if it is unwelcome. These are movements within one person’s experience, not categories for ranking whole people.

The closing pages describe these organizations as the aura perceived by the seer, introducing the later discussion of spiritual perception. Addendum 4 also qualifies the animal contrast on page 21: Steiner does not deny thought in animal life but attributes it to a group-ego rather than an independently thinking human I. Learn that distinction as his account before proceeding to the I itself.

Keep this idea

A correction can be true even when it is unpleasant to accept.

See the idea in an example

Someone points out a mistake in your writing. First you feel hurt. Then you examine the sentence. You discover that the correction is right, although accepting it is uncomfortable.

What this helps explain

This helps illustrate immediate soul response, thoughtful elaboration, and orientation toward truth beyond preference. These movements introduce the sentient, intellectual, and consciousness souls. One real response can involve all three; the example is not a test for identifying separate parts.

Check your understanding

What distinguishes consciousness soul from clever reasoning serving a desire?

Reveal the explanation

The intellectual soul can bring thinking into the service of a personal purpose. The consciousness soul concerns the true and good with a significance independent of that purpose. In the example, devising a persuasive defense differs from examining whether the report needs correction. Steiner distinguishes the orientation of thinking, not merely how clever the reasoning is; all three members remain related in the same person.

Why do defending a report and asking whether it needs correction illustrate different orientations?

Pause and answer in one sentence, aloud or in your notes.

See a way to approach it

A defence can use thinking to serve a desired result. Examining whether correction is needed asks about truth independently of that result. The distinction concerns the orientation of thinking, not merely its skill.
