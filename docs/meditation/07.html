Working with the Foundation Stone over time

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · The Foundation Stone Meditation · first movement excerpt · Opening of the first movement, lines 1–13

Human soul!
You live in the limbs
That carry you through the world of space
Into the being of the spirit-ocean:
Practise spirit-remembering
In the depths of the soul,
Where, in the ruling
World-Creator-being,
Your own I
In the I of God
Comes into being;
And you will truly live
In human-world-being.

Rudolf Steiner, GA 260, printed German version. The English and Portuguese below are new study translations made for this course, not the Adams, Wehrle or Bamford translations. Original line structure retained where practicable.

Open the source or edition index →

What this passage means

Return to the opening of the first movement as a unit: limbs and space lead into spirit-remembering and the origin of the I. Study its relationships before repeating it meditatively, then compare it with the heart-and-lung and thinking movements.

The focus of this lesson

Connect a carefully understood passage with inward practice and a considered deed.

Why this comes here

Reading the entire text establishes its relationships; now a small part can be attended to without losing the whole.

What the source is teaching

The meditation was unfolded within a particular conference and has a history of versions and rhythmic working. Our four-session introduction below is an original learning arrangement. It must not be confused with Steiner’s historical seven daily rhythms or attributed to him as an exact timetable.

Wilson suggests that different translations may help approach the original mantric content. Compare a key word where it clarifies meaning, then keep one wording stable during the actual practice. Constantly switching translations can turn meditation back into analysis.

Spirit-remembering concerns relation to spiritual origin in the first movement; spirit-reflection is placed in balance and relation to world-becoming; spirit-beholding in quiet thought opens toward free willing. Studying these relationships prepares the practice. It does not reduce the text to three generic mood-management techniques.

How to work with it

Historical directions are identified above. The guided steps below are our teaching arrangement of the selected practice.

  1. First session: read the whole, then work slowly with the first human address and its response. Explain the relation of the individual I to its spiritual ground.
  2. Second session: return to the whole, then attend to the second movement’s balance, rhythm and relationship.
  3. Third session: attend to the third movement, quiet thought and free willing. Keep its link to the other two in view.
  4. Fourth session: return to the ending. After the meditative period, consider one modest deed: what would warmth of heart ask you to notice, and clarity of thought ask you to understand? Consult the person affected before deciding what will help.
  5. Repeat a chosen passage over time if it remains meaningful. Review your understanding and conduct rather than demand a special experience.

Original open question and answer

Does helping someone prove that the spiritual process described occurred?

Read an explanation

No. The deed is a concrete application whose motive and effects can be examined. It can express the text’s ethical direction without serving as proof of every spiritual assertion in it.
