The six supporting exercises

Understand and begin the capacities that accompany meditation: thought, initiative, equanimity, positivity, openness and harmony.

Why this comes here

Meditation is placed within the education of the whole person. Concentration alone is not the entire path.

What the source is teaching

Steiner presents these as supplementary exercises that accompany inner work. The book includes several versions. One gives an approximately month-by-month progression, adding initiative after thought, then equanimity, positivity and openness, and finally practising their harmony. Reading this lesson does not replace those months of practice.

The instructions below are an original concise retelling of the source exercises. Their ordinary purpose can be understood as self-directed attention, reliability, balance and receptivity; Steiner also assigns them a role in spiritual development. We retain both levels rather than reducing them to productivity techniques.

Thought: choose an ordinary object, such as a pin, and sustain connected thoughts about it by your own initiative. Initiative: choose a small, harmless action that is not imposed by your routine and perform it at the time you decide. Equanimity: notice joy or difficulty while preserving the capacity to respond deliberately. Positivity: seek something genuinely worthwhile without denying what is wrong. Openness: allow a new experience or another person to challenge your expectation. Harmony: bring the preceding capacities into a balanced relationship.

How to work with it

Historical directions are identified above. The guided steps below are our teaching arrangement of the selected practice.

  1. Start with one short thought exercise on an ordinary object. Distinguish a connected line of thinking from free association.
  2. If following the book’s longer programme, read its full version and introduce the further qualities gradually; do not compress all six months into six days.
  3. For a concrete course adaptation of initiative, place a bookmark in a chosen place at an agreed time. Its smallness lets you notice the act of remembering and carrying out your intention.
  4. Review a specific instance of attention or conduct. Avoid scoring spiritual organs or interpreting every unwanted feeling as failure.

Check the connection

Does positivity mean approving every situation?

Read an explanation

No. Looking for a real constructive quality can coexist with recognizing a problem and acting on it. Similarly, equanimity concerns steadiness, not the abolition of feeling, and openness is compatible with subsequent judgment.
