Four chapter reflections

  1. How does a picture of the human being change a relationship? Compare a role-based judgment with a fuller account.
  2. Classify a school’s decisions across the three spheres. Explain the spiritual relation assigned to each sphere and one institutional difficulty.
  3. Describe a fair listening practice and an act of acquired idealism. Explain why Steiner calls them paths to Christ.
  4. Use the rose analogy to examine an institution. Identify a possible living lie and defend or challenge Steiner’s diagnosis.

Final project: a small act of social renewal

Write 600–900 words, or prepare an equivalent 6–8 minute presentation with a one-page diagram. Choose a fictional or real institution and propose one change.

  1. Describe the situation and the people affected. Separate known facts from assumptions.
  2. Connect human dignity and interdependence from Lecture 1 to the problem.
  3. Map culture, rights and economy using Lecture 2; explain the esoteric foundations without treating them as established public evidence.
  4. Use the thinking and willing practices of Lecture 3 to show how participants would be heard and the proposal carried out.
  5. Use Lecture 4 to examine an omitted relationship or potentially misleading description.
  6. Present the change, an objection, a risk and a review method. State what would make you revise the proposal.

Assessment

For each criterion, use 0 = absent, 1 = partial, 2 = clear and supported. Total: 12 points. Religious agreement and personal spiritual experiences are not assessed.

  • Explain concepts accurately and attribute spiritual claims to Steiner.
  • Use material from all four lectures and identify the relevant source pages.
  • Distinguish observations, the author’s claims and your own interpretation.
  • Consider the capacities, rights, needs and perspectives of affected people.
  • Address a serious objection and a practical risk.
  • Specify a feasible action, a way to assess it and a reason to revise it.
