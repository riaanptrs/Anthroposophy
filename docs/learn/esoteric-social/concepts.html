Concept guide

Keep the vocabulary close to the argument.

These definitions explain terms as used in this course. They distinguish spiritual interpretations from observable descriptions.

Anthroposophy
Steiner’s spiritual worldview and path of inquiry, which he calls spiritual science. The course distinguishes his asserted spiritual knowledge from publicly testable findings.
Social question
The problems of how human beings live together, especially the relations between cultural activity, political rights and economic life.
Esoteric and exoteric
Esoteric here means the inner spiritual account offered to anthroposophical members; exoteric refers to the public account of social life and reform.
Microcosm
The human being understood as an expression of the wider cosmos; a spiritual conception in this text.
Consciousness soul
Steiner’s term for a phase of human development emphasizing individual consciousness, freedom and responsibility.
Karma
Connections and consequences across earthly lives in Steiner’s account; not a basis for assigning blame or identifying another person’s destiny.
Life before birth
Spiritual existence preceding earthly life, which Steiner links to cultural capacities and associations.
Threefold social organism
Distinguishing cultural life, rights life and economic life so they can coordinate without one function ruling all the others.
Cultural life
Education, knowledge, art, religion and the cultivation of individual capacities; freedom is its guiding concern.
Rights life
Relations between people as legal equals, associated with the state and life between birth and death.
Economic life
Production, distribution and consumption, involving mutual dependence and cooperation; linked here to development beyond death.
Mystery of Golgotha
Christ’s death and resurrection understood by Steiner as a spiritual event for all humanity.
Christ impulse
The continuing spiritual activity of Christ in humanity in Steiner’s account; central to his interpretation of social responsibility.
Acquired idealism
A commitment deliberately cultivated and renewed, rather than sustained only by spontaneous enthusiasm.
Living lie
An operative arrangement that contradicts the fuller relationships of human life, as Steiner argues in his example of labour as a commodity.
Supersensible
Beyond ordinary sensory perception; a term for the spiritual dimension asserted in this worldview.
Akashic record
An enduring spiritual record in anthroposophy; a metaphysical account of responsibility rather than a publicly verified archive.
Ahrimanic materialism
Steiner’s spiritual characterization of tendencies that confine thought to material and mechanical accounts of life.
