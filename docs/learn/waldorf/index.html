A suggested reading path

  1. Build the foundation. Begin with Lesson 1 and continue through Lesson 6’s developmental map. These six lessons contain draft prose; the later teaching-method lessons are outlines.
  2. Find the developmental transition. Use the developmental lessons or timeline to locate your child’s stage. These pages are outlines, with final explanations still pending.
  3. Connect development with curriculum. Open a grade dossier, then read “The Child,” “What They Learn,” and “Why Now?” The focused views overlap where a supplied explanation serves more than one question.
  4. Follow up on a subject or concern. Compare subject pathways and parent questions. These are planning outlines; Home Life is a placeholder.

Use the course’s explanatory chain: child development → developmental need → teaching method → curriculum content → classroom experience → educational intention. Where examples, explanations, or source support are absent, the course remains unfinished.

From imitation to independent judgment

This is the course’s Anthroposophical/Waldorf framework. Ages are approximate; they are not fixed diagnoses or universally accepted scientific stages.

  1. Birth to about seven

    Imitation and example

  2. School entry

    School readiness and change of teeth

  3. Seven to nine

    Imagination

  4. Around nine

    Self ↔ world; practical grounding

  5. Nine to twelve

    Increasing observation

  6. Around twelve

    Causality

  7. Puberty; approximately twelve to fourteen

    Increasing analysis and changing authority

  8. Fourteen onward

    Increasing independent judgment
