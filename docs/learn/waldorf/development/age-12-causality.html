Around twelve: explaining causes

Distinguish an observed pattern from a causal explanation.

Understand the idea

The curriculum plan places greater emphasis on cause, law and exact relationships around Grade 6. Physics, geometric construction and business mathematics offer situations where a result must be explained, not simply described.

A causal question asks how changing one condition changes another and why. Compare relevant conditions, distinguish observations from the proposed mechanism and consider what else could affect the result.

The approximate age is an organizing interpretation, not an experimentally established threshold for every child. Younger children also reason about causes. The teaching task is to make explanations more precise and testable at an appropriate level.

A worked example

Pupils compare the sound of a stretched string at different lengths while keeping other conditions as similar as possible. They describe the pattern before proposing an explanation.

Check and apply your understanding

Why does seeing two things change together not complete a causal explanation?

See a way to approach it

Other conditions may influence the result. Explain the proposed connection, compare conditions and identify what evidence would help distinguish the explanation from alternatives.

Educational movement

Interpretation The established grade outline describes geometry as a movement from beautiful form → lawful construction. The broader course sequence places causality after pictorial learning and observation, and before independent judgment.

These are curriculum explanations from the course plan; the supplied outline does not establish an experimentally measured age at which every child becomes capable of causal thinking.
