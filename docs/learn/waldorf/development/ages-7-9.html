Lesson 21 — Ages Seven to Nine

theme

Interpretation The course characterizes approximately ages seven to nine as “living through imagination.” Its curriculum characteristics are story, rhythm, movement, repetition, artistic teaching, and the teacher relationship. The outline connects this lesson principally to Grades 1 and 2.

Steiner Attribution recorded in the conversation; primary-source verification pending. The broader master plan identifies imagination and artistic education as central after approximately age seven, within the second seven-year period. This attribution is a research lead, not a checked primary-source citation.

curriculum examples

Later Waldorf curriculum Curriculum category is provisional; exact source passages remain to be checked.

  • Grade 1: fairy tales; letters developed from images; rhythmic arithmetic; straight and curved forms; art, movement, and primarily oral foreign-language work.
  • Grade 2: fables and legends; growing arithmetic fluency; symmetry, mirroring, and completion; greater writing and reading independence.

The first grade's governing question is how academic learning can begin without immediately becoming abstract. The second grade introduces contrasts within the imaginative world: one-sided human tendencies in fables and moral striving or transformation in legends.

Distinction to preserve

Conversation The planned foundation lesson on imagination distinguishes imaginative presentation from treating fantasy as fact. The course's larger sequence gradually distinguishes image → observation → concept → causal explanation.

Interpretation The function of this development lesson is to connect the educational medium—story, rhythm, movement, and art—to the two grade outlines, rather than merely repeat their subject lists. That organization follows the course's established development-to-curriculum principle.
