Grades 1–9

Each lesson explains a central relationship, works through an example and provides a question with an explained answer.

  1. Grade 1: entering school through imagination

    Connect imaginative experience with early literacy and number.

  2. Grade 2: contrast and characterization

    Use contrast to compare without turning characters into fixed labels.

  3. Grade 3: meeting practical needs

    Explain how practical work connects a child with understandable relationships.

  4. Grade 4: parts and wholes

    Connect fractions, local geography and comparison through differentiated relationships.

  5. Grade 5: observation, proportion and history

    Distinguish a curriculum theme from a fixed developmental ideal.

  6. Grade 6: cause, law and construction

    Move from describing a result to explaining how it follows.

  7. Grade 7: exploration and changing perspectives

    Relate widening inquiry to evidence, context and responsibility.

  8. Grade 8: integration and modern change

    Bring earlier concepts into a coherent explanation or project.

  9. Grade 9: contrast and reasoned judgment

    Use contrasting explanations to build a supported conclusion.

Understand the idea. Explain the relationship. Apply what you learned.
