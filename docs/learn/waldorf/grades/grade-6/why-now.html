1. Developmental transition

Around eleven to twelve, Steiner increasingly permits teaching based upon cause and effect.

The Kingdom of Childhood explicitly says cause-and-effect instruction should become prominent only around eleven or twelve, and that the mineral kingdom should also be introduced around this period. The Kingdom of childhood _ seve…

Later Waldorf curriculum calls this the “threshold of causality.” Waldorf Resources

This becomes the governing Grade 6 theme.

2. Physics begins

This is a major curriculum event.

Typical Grade 6 physics includes phenomena from:

  • acoustics
  • optics
  • heat
  • magnetism
  • electricity
  • mechanics

The method usually follows:

experience phenomenon

→ careful observation

→ description

→ discussion

→ concept

rather than beginning with a formula.

Steiner's broader educational position is that science should develop from real phenomena rather than abstract theory alone. The transition toward science around twelve is explicitly present in The Kingdom of Childhood. The Kingdom of childhood _ seve…

3. Mineralogy and geology

The curriculum now reaches the mineral world.

Contemporary Waldorf science explicitly ties Grade 6 mineralogy to the child's increasing logical capacity and the “threshold of causality.” Waldorf Resources

Children may study:

  • granite
  • limestone
  • basalt
  • quartz
  • ores
  • crystals
  • mountain formation
  • rocks and landscape
  • relationship between geology and human economy

The movement through the natural kingdoms becomes:

animal → plant → mineral

or:

living relationship → life processes → structured physical matter

4. Roman and medieval history

Grade 6 history traditionally focuses on Rome and the Middle Ages.

Contemporary curriculum emphasizes:

  • Roman Republic and Empire
  • roads
  • law
  • engineering
  • military organization
  • Christianity
  • migration
  • Islam
  • medieval society
  • feudal structures
  • monasteries
  • towns
  • knights and political power

and specifically says children can now increasingly follow rational historical causality. Waldorf Resources

Steiner's curriculum interpretation likewise contrasts Grade 5 story/biography with Grade 6 historical connections and consequences. Practical Advice to Teachers_ (…

That developmental shift is central.

Grade 5:

“What happened?”

Grade 6 increasingly asks:

“What led to it, and what happened because of it?”

5. Geometry becomes exact

Grade 6 generally introduces formal compass-and-straightedge geometry much more strongly.

Typical work:

  • perpendiculars
  • bisectors
  • triangles
  • polygons
  • circles
  • constructions
  • geometric laws
  • increasingly exact drawings

The developmental movement is:

beautiful form

→ lawful form

Again: Grade 6 is about structure.

6. Mathematics becomes practical and economic

Traditional Grade 6 arithmetic often includes:

  • percentages
  • interest
  • discounts
  • profit/loss
  • ratios
  • currency
  • business mathematics

This prepares the way toward algebra.

The curriculum source describes Grades 6–8 as the stage where algebra begins from practical commercial arithmetic, including interest. Waldorf Resources
