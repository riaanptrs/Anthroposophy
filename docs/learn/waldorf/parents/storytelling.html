Why so many stories?

Connect narrative engagement with language and conceptual learning.

Understand the idea

Stories provide a sequence pupils can follow, picture and retell. They can develop vocabulary, attention to motives and relationships and a shared context for later work.

Their teaching purpose should be explicit. Retelling may check sequence; comparing responses may develop interpretation; connecting an image with a letter may introduce a symbol. These are different learning goals.

Stories also require cultural context and appropriate discussion. Distinguish imaginative narrative from factual history or scientific explanation. A memorable account should lead to clearer understanding rather than become the answer to every question.

A worked example

After a story about contrasting responses, pupils explain the consequences and write a new ending that follows logically from a changed choice.
