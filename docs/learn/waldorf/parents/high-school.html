Preparation for high school and university

Connect educational intentions with actual transition requirements.

Understand the idea

Later study requires subject knowledge, literacy, quantitative reasoning, independent organization and the ability to explain a judgment. Projects and discussion can support these capacities when their expectations are explicit.

Preparation must also address the receiving institution’s curriculum and qualification requirements. A general claim about holistic development does not establish readiness for a particular course or examination.

Ask the school for its transition plan, subject coverage, assessment evidence and support for unfamiliar formats. The appropriate pathway varies with the country, school and pupil’s aims; avoid universal promises about admission or outcomes.

A worked example

A pupil turns a project into a structured essay, practises an examination question and uses feedback to identify a missing concept.

Optional close reading · checked excerpt included

Rudolf Steiner · Practical Course for Teachers

GA 294 · Lecture 9 · 1919-08-30 · Stuttgart

Steiner discusses pupils of different ages, the absence of a Waldorf university and preparation for other institutions.

So we bring our Waldorf pupils up to the required standards of other schools.

The passage connects the school’s own approach with external requirements. It supports examining actual transition needs, without promising a particular modern qualification or admission result.
