What if my child is struggling?

Turn a general concern into a specific, reviewed support plan.

Understand the idea

Start with the actual difficulty: which task, in what setting and with what effect? “Struggling” may involve knowledge, skill, attention, language, wellbeing or an inaccessible task. These need different responses.

Gather examples and discuss them with the teacher. Agree on a clear learning objective, appropriate adaptations, targeted instruction and who will provide each part. Specialist assessment may be needed when the cause is unclear or difficulties persist.

Set a review point and identify what improvement would look like. A developmental interpretation can inform questions, but it should not delay help or make the pupil responsible for overcoming an undefined problem alone.

A worked example

A pupil explains 24 + 13 as two tens plus one ten and four ones plus three ones, giving 37. On paper the digits drift into the wrong columns. The explanation supports place-value understanding; the layout is the immediate barrier. Use marked tens/ones columns, model one aligned calculation and check a new example. This is a specific teaching response that can be reviewed, rather than calling the pupil generally weak at mathematics.

Check and apply your understanding

If the pupil then writes 24 + 13 neatly but says the 2 means two ones, is layout support alone enough?

Pause and answer in one sentence, aloud or in your notes.

See a way to approach it

No. The new evidence reveals a place-value misconception too. Teach what tens and ones mean using quantities, then reconnect them to the written columns. Update the plan when the evidence changes.

Optional close reading · checked excerpt included

Rudolf Steiner · Practical Course for Teachers

GA 294 · Lecture 4 · 1919-08-25 · Stuttgart

Steiner is introducing suggestions for a first school lesson to teachers facing pupils with different previous experiences.

Of course here I can only give you general suggestions which you will be able to develop further.

The passage presents suggestions to be developed by teachers. It supports distinguishing an educational intention from a fixed script. It does not establish the effectiveness of a particular contemporary adaptation.

Explain:

Waldorf ideals do not replace professional diagnosis, special education or evidence-based support.

Schools vary widely in learning-support capacity.

FAQ outline — implementation specification

What If My Child Is Struggling?

Do NOT imply Waldorf methods replace special education.

Mention the importance of:

  • qualified assessment
  • learning support
  • specialist intervention where required
