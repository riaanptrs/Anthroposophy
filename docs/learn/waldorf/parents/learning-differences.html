ADHD, dyslexia and learning differences

Distinguish adapting teaching from explaining away a difficulty.

Understand the idea

Pupils may need different routes into a concept or different support in attention, reading, language, movement or organization. A general developmental framework does not diagnose ADHD, dyslexia or other learning differences.

Describe the difficulty in specific tasks and settings. Adapt instructions, materials, time or response formats while retaining a clear learning objective. Qualified assessment can identify needs that classroom observation alone cannot settle.

Teachers and families should agree on support, responsibilities and review points. Artistic or rhythmic activities may form part of an accessible lesson, but they are not substitutes for appropriate specialist assessment or targeted assistance.

A worked example

A pupil hears a story and accurately explains why the character leaves. Reading the same text independently is slow and effortful. The oral response gives evidence of comprehension; the reading difficulty concerns access to the written words. Audio can preserve participation in the discussion while the school arranges targeted reading instruction and appropriate assessment. The two responses address different needs rather than asking one strength to cancel the difficulty.

Check and apply your understanding

Would removing all reading work because oral understanding is strong meet both goals?

Pause and answer in one sentence, aloud or in your notes.

See a way to approach it

No. Access to content and development of reading are separate goals. Preserve accessible participation while teaching and reviewing the difficult skill. This observation alone does not establish a diagnosis or the cause of the difficulty.

FAQ outline — course master

ADHD, Dyslexia and Learning Differences

This section must be especially careful.

Do not imply that Waldorf pedagogy treats or cures medical or neurodevelopmental conditions.

Discuss:

  • movement
  • rhythm
  • multisensory instruction
  • individual observation
  • possible benefits

but state clearly that appropriate professional assessment and intervention may still be necessary.
