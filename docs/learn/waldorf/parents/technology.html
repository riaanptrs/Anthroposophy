Is Waldorf anti-technology?

Evaluate a policy through learning purpose, access and preparation.

Understand the idea

Waldorf policies often emphasize direct experience and thoughtful timing of digital tools. Schools and families implement this differently; the educational argument needs to be explained in relation to actual tasks.

Ask what a device enables and what process it replaces. A tool may improve access or communication while also requiring decisions about attention, privacy and reliable information.

A useful policy prepares pupils for competent use and makes accommodations possible. Home arrangements should consider the family’s circumstances rather than assume that one rule fits every learner and activity.

A worked example

A pupil uses assistive software to access a text while still discussing its meaning and producing an independent response.
