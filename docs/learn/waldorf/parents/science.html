Is Waldorf anti-science?

Ask how observation, explanation and evidence are taught.

Understand the idea

The supplied curriculum includes natural science, physics and chemistry, often starting with careful observation before introducing formal explanations. This is a sequence of teaching, not a reason to omit scientific models or evidence.

Scientific understanding requires comparing conditions, testing explanations and using accurate subject knowledge. Spiritual interpretations associated with anthroposophy should be identified as such rather than presented as established scientific conclusions.

Ask for actual work: what pupils observed, which explanation they learned, how it was supported and how questions were handled. A school’s label or an appealing demonstration alone does not establish the quality of its science teaching.

A worked example

Pupils record observations of motion, compare data and explain the model used to make predictions, noting where the model applies.

FAQ outline — course master

Is Waldorf anti-science?

No.

Science is deliberately sequenced.

Early years: relationship and observation.

Middle grades: phenomenological science.

Around twelve: causality.

Adolescence: increasingly analytical science.

Related course-draft explanation

A simple example: science

Consider the way science develops.

A younger child may meet nature through:

  • stories;
  • gardening;
  • seasonal observation;
  • animals;
  • plants.

Later the child begins:

  • zoology;
  • botany;
  • mineralogy;
  • physics.

Still later:

  • chemistry;
  • physiology;
  • mechanics;
  • geology.

The progression is roughly:

relationship

→ observation

→ differentiation

→ causality

→ analysis

This gradual movement is characteristic of Waldorf curriculum design.
