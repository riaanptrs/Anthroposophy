Foundations

Each lesson explains a central relationship, works through an example and provides a question with an explained answer.

  1. What is Waldorf education?

    Connect a learning activity with the concept it is intended to teach.

  2. Steiner and the first Waldorf school

    Distinguish the school’s historical origin from later practice.

  3. Anthroposophy and classroom teaching

    Explain how a view of the child becomes a teaching decision.

  4. The whole human being

    Relate bodily activity, inner experience and understanding.

  5. Thinking, feeling and willing

    Connect understanding, engagement and purposeful action.

  6. The three seven-year periods

    Use a developmental map while observing the individual learner.

  7. Why curriculum follows development

    Explain the connection between learning need, method and subject matter.

  8. Why begin with imagination?

    Show how an image can lead to a clear concept.

  9. Art in academic teaching

    Explain what an artistic task contributes to understanding.

  10. Rhythm, repetition and sleep

    Distinguish predictable structure from mechanical repetition.

  11. What is a main lesson?

    Explain how a subject block develops connected understanding.

  12. Continuity with a class teacher

    Assess the benefits and responsibilities of a lasting teaching relationship.

  13. Main lesson books and textbooks

    Distinguish constructing a learning record from copying one.

  14. Eurythmy

    Understand expressive movement and its educational purpose.

  15. Music and singing

    Explain how listening, pattern and expression develop together.

  16. Handwork and practical skills

    Relate a making process to planning, pattern and correction.

  17. Foreign languages

    Connect spoken experience with explicit language understanding.

  18. Assessment and reports

    Use evidence to identify understanding and the next teaching step.

  19. Waldorf and technology

    Match a tool to its learning purpose and the learner’s needs.

Understand the idea. Explain the relationship. Apply what you learned.
