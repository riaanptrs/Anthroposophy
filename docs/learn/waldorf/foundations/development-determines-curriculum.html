Why curriculum follows development

Explain the connection between learning need, method and subject matter.

Understand the idea

A developmental curriculum asks why an experience belongs at a particular point in learning. The connection runs from an observed need to a teaching method, then to content that can make the relationship tangible.

The supplied sequence moves from images and letters, through practical work and local surroundings, toward causal explanations and independent judgment. These emphases help organize progression; younger pupils can reason and older pupils still benefit from images.

A good sequence also revisits earlier ideas at greater depth. Readiness, prior knowledge and local circumstances may require adaptation. Naming an age is insufficient: the teacher needs to explain what the task develops and check what pupils understand.

A worked example

A class first describes a familiar landscape, later draws a map and eventually explains how transport and settlement influence each other. The same surroundings support increasingly explicit relationships.

Optional close reading · checked excerpt included

Rudolf Steiner · Practical Course for Teachers

GA 294 · Lecture 4 · 1919-08-25 · Stuttgart

Steiner is introducing suggestions for a first school lesson to teachers facing pupils with different previous experiences.

Of course here I can only give you general suggestions which you will be able to develop further.

The passage presents suggestions to be developed by teachers. It supports distinguishing an educational intention from a fixed script. It does not establish the effectiveness of a particular contemporary adaptation.
