Sources and interpretations

The teaching text, classroom examples and answer models are original course prose developed from the supplied plans and dossiers. They explain the educational intentions; they do not certify outcomes or turn inherited source claims into verified quotations.

Checked passages included in the course

Rudolf Steiner · Practical Course for Teachers

GA 294 · Lecture 1 · 1919-08-21 · Stuttgart

Steiner is discussing how subject materials should be used in teaching. “Them” refers to those materials. He places this within his account of soul, body and human abilities.

You will have to use them as the means of rightly developing the soul and body forces of the individual.

The educational emphasis is what the learner develops through the material. The lesson’s classroom examples apply this idea; they are original course examples, not examples quoted from this sentence.

Steiner Archive web witness, English translation credited to Harry Collison, RSPC1937 edition route.

Rudolf Steiner · Practical Course for Teachers

GA 294 · Lecture 4 · 1919-08-25 · Stuttgart

Steiner is introducing suggestions for a first school lesson to teachers facing pupils with different previous experiences.

Of course here I can only give you general suggestions which you will be able to develop further.

The passage presents suggestions to be developed by teachers. It supports distinguishing an educational intention from a fixed script. It does not establish the effectiveness of a particular contemporary adaptation.

Steiner Archive web witness, English translation credited to Harry Collison, RSPC1937 edition route.

Rudolf Steiner · Practical Course for Teachers

GA 294 · Lecture 9 · 1919-08-30 · Stuttgart

Steiner discusses pupils of different ages, the absence of a Waldorf university and preparation for other institutions.

So we bring our Waldorf pupils up to the required standards of other schools.

The passage connects the school’s own approach with external requirements. It supports examining actual transition needs, without promising a particular modern qualification or admission result.
