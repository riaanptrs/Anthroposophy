Chemistry: transformation and explanation

Distinguish observation of a change from its chemical explanation.

Understand the idea

The supplied chemistry progression begins with observable transformations and develops more explicit relationships among substances and conditions. Burning, dissolving and changes in materials raise different explanatory questions.

A careful sequence records what changed, identifies conditions and introduces concepts that account for the result. Later classification and quantitative relationships should clarify the observations rather than be learned as disconnected lists.

Scientific explanation requires evidence and appropriate models. Anthroposophical interpretations have a different status and should be identified separately. Demonstrations need suitable supervision, materials and school protocols; this lesson explains the learning sequence rather than supplying an experiment recipe.

A worked example

A teacher provides observations of a material before and after a change. Pupils separate what was seen from a proposed explanation and identify the evidence needed to distinguish alternatives.

Check and apply your understanding

Why is “it changed colour” not a complete chemical explanation?

Pause and answer in one sentence, aloud or in your notes.

See a way to approach it

It describes an observation. An explanation must account for the change in terms of relevant substances, conditions and processes, supported by evidence.

Begin with this learning purpose: Distinguish observation of a change from its chemical explanation. Name the relationship, choose an appropriate experience and describe what the learner should explain or do. Use the distinction in this lesson to assess the result.
