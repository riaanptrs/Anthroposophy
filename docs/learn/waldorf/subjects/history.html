History: story, context and evidence

Distinguish an engaging narrative from a supported historical explanation.

Understand the idea

Historical stories make people and events imaginable. As learning develops, pupils also need chronology, context, sources and explanations of change.

A pathway moves from following an account to asking how it is known and whose viewpoint it represents. Compare sources, identify causes and consequences and distinguish evidence from later interpretation.

Curriculum narratives of cultural development should not rank living peoples as more or less evolved. Historical inquiry recognizes agency, conflict and varied experiences. An imaginative presentation remains accountable to accurate information and respectful representation.

A worked example

Pupils compare two accounts of an industrial change and identify whose work, choices and consequences each emphasizes.

Check and apply your understanding

Why might two accounts disagree without one being entirely invented?

They may use different evidence or focus on different experiences. Compare their claims and support before deciding where they complement, conflict or leave questions open.
