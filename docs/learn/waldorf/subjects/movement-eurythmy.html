Eurythmy and movement across the years

Connect expressive movement with increasingly conscious coordination.

Understand the idea

Movement learning can develop balance, timing, spatial relationships and shared attention. Eurythmy adds a particular artistic intention: representing qualities of speech and music through gesture.

A pathway may move from imitation and simple shared forms toward more complex sequences and explanation of expressive choices. The teacher identifies what pupils are practising and how coordination or interpretation is developing.

Different movement activities have different goals; games, physical education and eurythmy should not be treated as interchangeable. Provide accessible forms of participation and assess the intended relationship rather than conformity of bodies.

A worked example

A group makes the return of a musical phrase visible through movement, then explains how direction and spacing distinguish its variations.
