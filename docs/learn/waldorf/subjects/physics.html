Physics: phenomenon, pattern and mechanism

Develop an explanation from controlled comparison.

Understand the idea

The physics pathway begins with phenomena that can be observed and compared, then develops models and quantitative relationships. Sound, light, heat and movement require different concepts even when their teaching shares a method.

For motion in one direction along a straight path, average speed over an interval is distance travelled divided by elapsed time. A position table must therefore be read by subtracting successive positions and times; a position is not itself a speed. The resulting pattern describes motion. Explaining why the motion changes is a further question about interactions and conditions, which the position data alone may not settle.

A vivid demonstration is not proof of every interpretation attached to it. Consider alternative causes, measurement limits and repeated observations. School demonstrations need appropriate supervision and protocols.

These are invented teaching data, not measurements from an experiment. At times 0, 1, 2 and 3 seconds, cart A is 0, 2, 4 and 6 metres from the start; cart B is 0, 1, 3 and 6 metres from the start. Subtract successive positions. A travels 2, 2 and 2 metres in the equal one-second intervals, so its average speed in each interval is 2 m/s. B travels 1, 2 and 3 metres, so its interval-average speed increases. Both cover 6 metres in 3 seconds and have the same overall average, 2 m/s. An overall average therefore hides the different patterns. The data describe motion; they do not establish which force produced it.

Check and apply your understanding

Cart C is at 0, 3, 6 and 9 metres at times 0, 1, 3 and 6 seconds. Does adding 3 metres each time show constant speed?

Pause and answer in one sentence, aloud or in your notes.

See a way to approach it

No. The time intervals are 1, 2 and 3 seconds. The interval-average speeds are 3/1 = 3 m/s, 3/2 = 1.5 m/s and 3/3 = 1 m/s. Equal distance increments indicate equal average speeds only when the time intervals are equal. Even equal interval averages alone would not rule out changes within each interval.
