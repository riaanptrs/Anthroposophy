Bees course glossary

Biological terms and Steiner’s spiritual vocabulary keep their distinct meanings.

Colony
The community of bees considered with its shared life and organization.
Organism
A living whole whose members and processes are interdependent; the guiding picture Steiner applies to a hive.
Queen
The principal reproductive female in a honeybee colony; Steiner gives her a special solar correspondence.
Worker
A female bee contributing to changing tasks of colony life; distinct from a male drone.
Drone
A male honeybee; the lectures relate its longer development to earthly forces.
Nectar
Plant secretion collected and processed by bees into honey; distinct from pollen.
Pollen
Plant reproductive material collected as food and carried between flowers in pollination.
Beeswax
Bee-produced material used in comb construction; central to Steiner’s formative comparisons.
Swarming
A process in which a queen and part of a colony leave to establish elsewhere; also a spiritual image in the lectures.
Etheric
Steiner’s term for an organizing dimension of life processes, not a synonym for a measured chemical.
Astral
In Steiner’s account, the dimension of sensation and soul activity.
I organization
Steiner’s account of the organizing activity of human individuality; invoked in his spiritual physiology.
Group soul
The supersensible unity Steiner attributes to animal communities or kinds; not equivalent to a scientific account of coordination.
Metamorphosis
In these comparisons, a process recognized through changes in form and location; also a biological term with its own specific uses.
Microcosm and macrocosm
The small world of the human being and the larger world to which Steiner relates its processes.
Formic acid
A chemical associated with some ants; Steiner extends its meaning into a proposed account of life and soul. It is not identical to bee venom.
Social sculpture
Beuys’s expanded artistic practice in which people creatively shape their shared social life.
