Spiritual Beings and Hierarchies

Lesson 19 / 36 · Builds on: The Spiritual World

Why are spiritual beings distinguished by their activities and relationships?

From creative activity to orders of beings

Lesson 18 described different kinds of creative spiritual activity. This lesson asks which orders of beings Steiner describes as participating in cosmic development. He calls these orders spiritual hierarchies. Here, “hierarchy” means an ordered grouping of spiritual beings, distinguished by their proposed activities and relationships. Begin with the kind of question this classification answers, rather than memorizing unfamiliar names.

Three classifications with different subjects

Three classifications need to be kept distinct. Human members describe aspects of the human being: physical body, life body, astral body and I. World regions distinguish qualities, relationships or kinds of activity. Spiritual hierarchies distinguish orders of beings and their roles. These descriptions can be related, but matching their entries merely because two lists have the same number does not explain that relationship. The comparison below keeps their different subjects clear.

Cooperation and different roles

Consider several musicians contributing different parts to one performance. Their cooperation can help us understand differentiated roles within a whole: asking which musicians participate differs from asking about aspects of the music. The analogy illustrates cooperation, not the specific powers Steiner attributes to spiritual beings. His hierarchy account concerns beings he presents as real. Further study: names and world conditions in Colour

Three hierarchies in Colour’s account

Colour’s twelfth lecture groups nine named kinds of beings into three hierarchies and connects them with earlier conditions of existence. The table summarizes this particular account, not every classification of spiritual beings in Steiner’s work. Saturn, Sun and Moon here name earlier world conditions, rather than the present heavenly bodies. Warmth, light and colour describe the manifestations discussed, not additional names of beings. The lecture’s further discussion of original humanity as a Fourth Hierarchy remains in the optional source study.

Course explanation

Understand the idea

Colour’s twelfth lecture names three orders, each containing three kinds of beings. The First comprises Seraphim, Cherubim and Thrones; the Second, Kyriotetes, Dynamis and Exusiai; the Third, Archai, Archangels and Angels. The names organise the account. Learning their proposed relationships matters more here than memorising every Greek name.

The lecture relates the First to warmth in Saturn existence, the Second to light and its shadow, air, in Sun existence, and the Third to mediation between light and darkness: colour, with water as its reflection, in Moon existence. The selected passage gathers the manifestations of the Second and Third. It describes the beings’ expression through nature, not simply a list of chemical substances.

Steiner then calls original humanity before the Fall, the religious account of humanity’s changed relationship to the divine, the Fourth Hierarchy, relating its activity to life and solid contours in Earth existence. This belongs to the historical spiritual account he presents. Its hierarchy language concerns kinds of spiritual activity; it supplies no ranking of the worth of contemporary peoples or a scale for assigning students spiritual status.

Key concept

Hierarchies distinguish orders of spiritual beings and their proposed creative roles. They are different from the members within one human constitution.

Keep this distinction

Three hierarchies with nine named kinds of beings are not Theosophy’s nine human members. Nor does “higher” mean that one present-day person deserves more dignity than another.
