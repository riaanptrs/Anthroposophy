What is Anthroposophy?

What does this study claim to understand about human life?

Anthroposophy is the larger inquiry we will study, rather than the name of one practical application. Its central concern is how human life belongs to what Steiner regards as a spiritual reality.

Course explanation

Understand the idea

In Theosophy, Steiner introduces spiritual science as investigation of spiritual processes in humanity and the cosmos. The book concentrates on the spiritual core of the human being and its destination. Anthroposophy develops this inquiry through accounts of constitution, consciousness, repeated lives, freedom and relationships with spiritual worlds.

These descriptions are not presented merely as useful stories. Steiner considers their beings and processes real. The course therefore explains the account in its own terms, while distinguishing a faithfully reconstructed claim from evidence that independently establishes it.

The later lectures collected as What Is Anthroposophy? approach self-knowledge through bodily and spiritual life, thinking, memory, freedom and love. They add another setting and another development of the inquiry. Their editorial introduction is not the same voice as Steiner’s lectures, and their references do not belong to Theosophy.

An inquiry can inspire institutions and practices without being identical with any one of them. Waldorf education, artistic work and the study of temperament are places where anthroposophical ideas are applied or developed. To understand an application, first ask which account of the human being it draws upon.

Start with the human being

Anthroposophy asks how bodily life, inward experience and spiritual individuality belong together. A course about the human being therefore begins with distinctions that help describe one person from several connected perspectives.

Connect an idea with an application

A conception of development can suggest different teaching at different ages. The practical activity is an application of an idea about the person; understanding the activity requires understanding that underlying idea.

Keep the whole and its parts in view

Theosophy, freedom, education and agriculture approach different questions within this wider work. One school practice cannot stand for all anthroposophy. Learn the particular concept first, then examine its connections.

Key concept

Anthroposophy is Steiner’s proposed inquiry into spiritual dimensions of humanity and the cosmos, with consequences for self-knowledge and practical life.

Everyday example

A school might organize a painting lesson using ideas about human development. Examining that lesson reveals one application; it does not yet explain the whole account of the human being or demonstrate its spiritual premises.

Keep this distinction

Anthroposophy is not simply Waldorf schooling or a personality classification. The title Theosophy also does not make this course a survey of every teaching of the historical Theosophical Society.
