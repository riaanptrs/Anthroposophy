The Astral Body

Lesson 8 / 36 · Builds on: The Etheric or Life Body

What is added when life is also inwardly experienced?

The sentient member

Astral body names the sentient member in Steiner’s account: the organization associated with inward sensations, pleasure, pain, desires and impulses. Sentient means having inward experience. In Theosophy’s grouping, astral body joins soul body, the bodily support of sensation, with sentient soul, the inwardly lived experience.

A sound and an inward response

A bell vibrates, a listener hears it and irritation arises. Vibration is the physical event; irritation is an inward experience. Sentience means this inward experiencing. In Steiner's account, the astral member makes sentient life possible; the irritation is one of its changing contents, rather than the member itself.

How desire gives direction

Desire adds a direction to experience: the listener wants the bell to stop. The same sound may delight another listener who wants it to continue. This explains why the physical event alone does not describe the whole personal response. Physical, life and sentient organization cooperate within the experiencing being.

Capacity and particular content

A remembered tune is also a particular content, whereas the capacity to experience allows many tunes, pleasures and desires. Keep capacity and content distinct. Next we ask how a person can recognize these experiences as their own and work on a response instead of simply following the strongest desire.

Two questions — optional

A bell vibrates and a listener feels irritated. Is the irritation itself the astral body?

See an explanation

No. Vibration is a physical process; irritation is one inward experience. Astral body names Steiner’s proposed member of the experiencing being, not a particular feeling. The example explains the distinction without detecting that member.

You remember a tune after it stops. Why is the remembered tune different from the capacity to experience?

See an explanation

The tune is a particular content; the capacity makes many contents possible. This is why a mental picture should not be equated with the astral member that Steiner associates with inward experience.

Course explanation

Understand the idea

Sentient means inwardly experiencing an encounter. Pleasure, pain, desire, attraction and aversion are part of this account, alongside sensation and inner images. Emotional body is therefore too narrow as a complete definition: the term does not refer only to moods.

Theosophy develops several distinctions before using the grouped name astral body. It separates physical organization, formative life, soul body supporting sensation and the sentient soul’s experienced sensation. In the later grouping, soul body and sentient soul are taken together as astral body. Substituting the name for soul body alone loses the stated relationship.

A physical change is not automatically evidence of an inward feeling. Likewise, living growth alone does not establish pleasure or pain in the author’s account. Steiner attributes human and animal sentience to the astral member; the example of hearing helps us understand the question, not detect the proposed member.

A particular mental picture is also different from its bearer. Remembering a cloud supplies a content of inner activity. Astral body belongs to Steiner’s account of the being that experiences such contents. A picture, the act of remembering and the proposed sentient organization are connected ideas, not interchangeable names.

Sensation has an inward side

A stimulus may cause bodily movement, but experiencing pleasure, pain or desire adds an inward relationship to it. Astral body names the sentient member in the account studied here.

Follow the grouping used in Theosophy

Theosophy distinguishes a soul body and a sentient soul before grouping them together as the astral body. The grouped term brings related distinctions together; it does not mean that every use of soul has the same meaning.

Separate feeling from self-directed understanding

Hearing a melody involves the bodily mediation of sound and its inward experience. Theosophy first distinguishes those contributions as soul body and sentient soul, then groups them as astral body. The grouping concerns the experiencing being. A remembered melody is one content of that experience; it is not itself the astral member, just as one desire is not the whole soul.

Key concept

Astral body names the sentient member associated with inward experience; in Theosophy’s grouping it unites soul body and sentient soul.

Everyday example

A bell vibrates, and you hear a tone that you find irritating. The vibration and bodily organs have a material description; hearing and irritation belong to the lived encounter. This course example illustrates the distinction without establishing a supersensible cause.

Keep this distinction

Astral is not every invisible thing, the whole soul or a remembered picture. An aura illustration or photograph does not define or verify this member.
