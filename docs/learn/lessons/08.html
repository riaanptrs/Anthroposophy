The Astral Body

Foundations · Lesson 8 / 36

The Question

What is added when life is also inwardly experienced?

Begin with Experience

A bell vibrates and you hear its tone. The vibration is a physical event; hearing the tone is your inward experience of it. Even before deciding whether you like the sound, you hear it. Hearing is already an inward experience. Irritation or enjoyment adds feeling. This is why “astral body” means more in Steiner’s account than an emotional body.

Steiner’s Idea

What does an encounter feel like from within?

A plant grows and maintains its living form. Hearing a sound, feeling discomfort, or wanting something introduces another question: what does the encounter feel like from within? Steiner associates this inward experience with the astral body, also called the sentient member. “Sentient” means having inward experience. The term includes sensation, feeling and desire, rather than only emotions.

Sensation, feeling and desire

Desire adds a direction to experience: an irritated listener wants the bell to stop. Another listener enjoys the same bell and wants it to continue. We can now distinguish three aspects: hearing the tone is sensation; irritation or enjoyment is feeling; wanting it to stop or continue is desire. The physical body, life body and astral member cooperate within the experiencing being. The picture compares different responses to one physical event.

Changing experiences and the member that experiences

The sound you hear and the feeling it awakens can change. Steiner uses “astral body” for the member associated with experiencing these changing contents, not for any one sound, feeling or desire. A remembered tune is another particular experience, not the whole member. Theosophy later groups bodily mediation of sensation, called soul body, with inwardly lived experience, called sentient soul, under the name astral body. Lesson 11 examines that grouping more closely. Next we ask how a person recognizes experiences as their own and works on a response.

Important Distinction

Astral is not every invisible thing, the whole soul or a remembered picture. An aura illustration or photograph does not define or verify this member.

An ordinary example can clarify a distinction without establishing Steiner’s further spiritual claims. Understanding the account does not require accepting it as established fact.

Apply It

Recall a surprising sound. Describe the sound, your experience of it and your response without treating them as three separate events or beings.

Check Your Understanding

You hear the bell without liking or disliking it. Is that still inward experience?

Compare with an explained answer

Yes. Hearing the tone is already an inward sensation. Enjoying or disliking it adds feeling; wanting it to continue or stop adds desire. Inward experience includes more than emotion.

You remember a tune after it stops. Why is the remembered tune not the astral body itself?

Compare with an explained answer

The remembered tune is one particular experience. Many sounds, feelings, desires and images can come and go. Steiner uses astral body for the member associated with experiencing them, rather than for any single experience.
