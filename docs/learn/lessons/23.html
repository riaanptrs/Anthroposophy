Imagination

Foundations · Lesson 23 / 36

The Question

How does spiritual Imagination differ from inventing a picture?

Begin with Experience

Follow the plant-colour illustration in two steps. First, picture a plant with its colours and solid form. Then imagine the colours apart from that solid form. This helps you conceive an appearance of colour without the familiar solid plant supporting it. Producing that picture remains ordinary imagining. Steiner’s further claim is that Imagination perceives living, changing expressions of spiritual beings. The illustration helps explain his description; it does not demonstrate that such perception has occurred. His fuller account also includes qualities other than colour.

Steiner’s Idea

Seeing, picturing and spiritual Imagination

Seeing a tree before you and picturing a tree in your mind are familiar, different activities. Steiner uses the word Imagination for a third activity: a proposed perception of spiritual beings through living images. This is a specialised meaning of the familiar word. As Lesson 22 explained, his account concerns perception beyond the ordinary senses; practising attention alone does not establish that this perception has developed.

What “living spiritual expressions” means

When you deliberately picture a tree, you can change its leaves from green to blue. You are making and altering a mental image. In Steiner’s account of Imagination, changing images instead express spiritual beings whose existence is independent of the observer’s wishes. “Living spiritual expressions” means those images are described as manifestations of beings, rather than pictures the observer invents. The distinction concerns what the image expresses and how it arises, rather than its vividness alone.

Understanding the description

You can therefore picture the illustration and explain what Steiner means without claiming to perceive a spiritual being. Keep those achievements distinct: making a picture, understanding a description, and having the experience it describes. The course asks you to understand the distinction. The next lesson introduces Inspiration, in which Steiner says the being’s own self-expression reveals the meaning of what Imagination perceives.

Important Distinction

Imagination here is also distinct from GA 4’s moral imagination, which gives a freely grasped ethical idea a concrete form for action. Shared vocabulary does not make the activities identical.

An ordinary example can clarify a distinction without establishing Steiner’s further spiritual claims. Understanding the account does not require accepting it as established fact.

Apply It

Describe the difference between inventing a picture and Steiner’s claimed spiritual Imagination. What could an ordinary visualization exercise establish?

Check Your Understanding

You deliberately change a pictured tree’s leaves from green to blue. How does this differ from Imagination in Steiner’s specialised sense?

Compare with an explained answer

You are making and altering a mental picture. Steiner uses Imagination for a proposed perception of living images expressing independently existing spiritual beings. Changing your picture illustrates ordinary imagining; vividness alone does not establish the further perception he describes.

What does the plant-colour illustration help us understand, and what does it leave unestablished?

Compare with an explained answer

It helps us conceive an appearance of colour apart from the familiar solid plant, making part of Steiner’s description easier to follow. Constructing that mental picture does not establish that a spiritual being has been perceived or that the learner has developed spiritual Imagination.
