Thinking, Feeling and Willing

Lesson 10 / 36 · Builds on: The I

How can understanding, inward response and action be distinguished without separating the person?

From self-awareness to three activities

In the previous lesson, you recognized your irritation and considered how to respond. What activities are involved? Thinking helps you understand the situation and examine your interpretation. Feeling concerns how the encounter matters inwardly to you. Willing concerns directing yourself toward action. These are three cooperating activities of one person, not three kinds of people or separate parts acting on their own.

When good intentions miss the actual need

A neighbour cannot reach an appointment. You feel concern, assume she needs advice and begin explaining. She tells you she already knows what to do but lacks transport. The concern is your feeling; the mistaken assumption is your interpretation. Thinking can revise that interpretation as you listen. The concern may deepen as you understand the difficulty, while willing directs your effort toward a more suitable response.

From wishing for an outcome to willing a response

Wishing that your neighbour reaches the appointment expresses an outcome you want. Willing concerns directing yourself toward doing something: checking whether a lift is possible, asking whether she wants it, and arranging it if you can. You can intend and begin to help even if an obstacle prevents the intended result. Concern alone does not provide transport; advice may address the wrong need. Understanding what would help still needs practical activity to become help.

Cooperation rather than a fixed sequence

Thinking, feeling and willing influence one another rather than following a rigid order. Concern can prompt closer attention; asking about transport can reveal a difficulty that changes your understanding and plan. These are activities of the person. They do not correspond directly to physical body, life body and astral body: the three activities and the bodily members describe different relationships. Next we use these distinctions to understand the more detailed soul vocabulary.

How can understanding, inward response and action be distinguished without separating the person?

Thinking, feeling and willing are distinguishable activities within one life. The Philosophy of Freedom asks how they belong together without making one immediate experience the whole explanation of reality.

If we are ready to experience thinking intuitively, we can also do justice to the experience of feeling and of will.

Course explanation

Understand the idea

Thinking connects what is encountered through concepts. Feeling expresses how an encounter relates to me personally; willing expresses my active relation toward what I encounter. These directions can occur in the same event. A strong feeling or immediate intention still requires understanding if its place in the event is to be explained.

Steiner criticizes theories that elevate personal feeling or will into a universal principle. His objection does not remove either activity from life. It concerns treating one kind of inward experience as sufficient to explain everything, while overlooking the conceptual relationships needed to understand it.

The selected addition introduces intuitive thinking. Steiner distinguishes actively experiencing a connection from remembering its finished formulation. He claims that living thinking does justice to feeling and will in their deeper reality, describing love as a power within it. This is stronger than saying that thought merely suppresses emotion.

The term intuition here belongs to the book’s philosophical argument about conceptual understanding. How to Know Higher Worlds later gives an account of spiritual development and separation of thinking, feeling and willing. That further account needs its own context rather than being read back into this sentence.

Thinking examines the actual need

You first assume that your neighbour needs advice. Listening reveals that she needs transport. Thinking connects what she says with the practical situation and revises your interpretation. Understanding the actual need helps you consider whether a lift is suitable and possible.

Feeling gives the encounter personal significance

Concern is how the neighbour’s difficulty matters inwardly to you. The mistaken assumption about advice is an interpretation, not the concern itself. Understanding her situation can deepen your concern, and that concern can prompt you to listen more carefully.

Key concept

Thinking understands relationships; feeling makes an encounter personally significant; willing directs the person toward activity. Their distinction does not divide the person.

Everyday example

A neighbour cannot reach an appointment. You feel concern, assume she needs advice and begin explaining. She tells you she already knows what to do but lacks transport. The concern is your feeling; the mistaken assumption is your interpretation. Thinking can revise that interpretation as you listen. The concern may deepen as you understand the difficulty, while willing directs your effort toward a more suitable response.

Keep this distinction

Thinking, feeling and willing are not a one-to-one translation of physical, etheric and astral members. Intuitive thinking here also does not mean every sudden hunch or the later stage of supersensible Intuition.
