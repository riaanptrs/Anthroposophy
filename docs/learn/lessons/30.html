Human Development

Foundations · Lesson 30 / 36

The Question

Why might two children of the same age need different help with the same task?

Begin with Experience

Suppose two children of the same age struggle with written fractions. One can share physical pieces equally but has difficulty connecting those pieces with a written fraction. Try placing the pieces beside the matching symbols and ask the child to explain the connection. The other can explain the sharing verbally but misreads the notation. Try clarifying what each number in the fraction represents, then check it against a sharing example. These are possible responses to observed difficulties, rather than fixed types of learner.

Steiner’s Idea

What a developmental map means

We now move from spiritual accounts to their proposed applications in education. A developmental map is a broad account of how capacities may change across childhood. It can suggest what to look for and what support to consider, but it is not a timetable every child follows exactly. This lesson draws on Hermann Koepke’s Encountering the Self, an anthroposophical interpretation of childhood development. Begin with the individual learner and the task as well as the map.

Check what the support changes

Review what happens after offering support. Can each child explain the fraction and use the notation in another example? If difficulty remains, ask what is confusing and revise the support. Age gives context; the learner’s experience, the task and their response provide further information. A developmental map can guide a question, while observation helps decide what to try and whether it helped. The next lesson follows this cycle of observation, teaching and review in a Waldorf example.

Important Distinction

The selected arrows explanation is Koepke’s, not a clinical finding quoted from Steiner. Neither a tooth count nor a diagram label should become a maturity decision. Its spiritual and bodily correspondences remain claims to explain and evaluate.

An ordinary example can clarify a distinction without establishing Steiner’s further spiritual claims. Understanding the account does not require accepting it as established fact.

Apply It

Imagine two learners struggling with the same task for different reasons. Propose different supports and identify what you would observe before drawing conclusions.

Koepke’s bodily-system interpretation

For closer study, Koepke describes changing emphasis among head-related activity, heart and breathing, and metabolism and limbs. His diagram’s arrows show the other two systems supporting the one receiving developmental emphasis. Emphasis therefore means changing cooperation within a whole, not that the other systems stop working. These bodily and spiritual relationships are his anthroposophical interpretation, rather than direct measurements of an individual child’s readiness.
