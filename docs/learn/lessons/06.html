The Physical Body

What does physical body name in the fourfold account?

In ordinary speech, body can mean the entire living organism. Steiner’s technical term physical body is more selective: it concerns material existence within a larger account of the human being.

Read · Identified source section

Locate the section

Rudolf Steiner, Theosophy, Chapter I, The Bodily Nature of Man, printed pp. 6–7, within the pp. 6–11 reading, Monges/Church English edition, 1971. Parallel reference: GA 13, Chapter II, The Nature of Humanity, paragraphs 1–3.

The explanation below paraphrases the identified section. It is course prose, rather than a quotation. Follow the book links for the wider reading and edition notes.

Course explanation

What the author means

Physical body names the bodily substances, forces and structures through which we belong to the material world. The bodily nature of the human being is considered alongside mineral existence, living growth and sentient experience. The comparison asks what each description contributes, rather than making human worth depend on a bodily classification.

The material member is not limited to bones or skin. A hand, an eye and the bodily instrument of thinking belong to embodiment. But describing an organ’s material constitution is not yet the same task as describing how a living organism maintains itself or how an encounter is inwardly experienced.

Steiner later distinguishes the etheric, astral and I in cooperation with the physical member. This does not create four independent machines. It proposes different organizations within one living person. A bodily event may therefore involve several members in his explanation; one need not assign every occurrence to exactly one box.

Theosophy also distinguishes natural bodily processes from reasons acknowledged in thinking. An impulse and a judgment can belong to the same encounter without being interchangeable. The physical member supplies an essential basis in this account, while the later members address further questions.

Key concept

Physical body names the material organization of the human being considered through bodily substances, structures and physical processes.

Everyday example

Your hand holds a warm cup. Tissue, the cup’s weight and transfer of heat give a material description. Feeling the warmth as pleasant supplies another aspect of the event. This course example clarifies the different questions, rather than proving the additional members.

Keep this distinction

Physical does not mean bad, spiritually irrelevant or the whole living and experiencing person in every context. Compare the everyday word body with the technical term before equating them.

Reveal the explanation

The tissues and heat transfer provide the material side. Personal feeling and deliberate response concern further aspects of the same encounter.

In the fourfold account, physical body is always a complete synonym for the whole living and feeling person.
