The I

How does the I relate to changing experiences and spiritual individuality?

You can experience an impulse and also recognize it as your impulse. Steiner introduces the I to explain self-relation and spiritual individuality within changing bodily and soul experience.

Printed p. 30 · PDF p. 58

The spirit self is a revelation of the spiritual world within the “I,” just as from the other side sensations are a revelation of the physical world within the “I.”

Course explanation

Understand the idea

The word I points to the speaker’s own standpoint: when two people say it, each refers to themselves. Steiner’s qualification distinguishes repeating the word from gradually understanding its idea. The illustration concerns self-awareness, not a test of a child’s spiritual status.

The I gathers bodily and soul experience and acts through them. At the same time, Steiner says it can receive spiritual content. The selected passage places sensations and spirit self within the I from different directions: bodily experience on one side and spiritual revelation on the other.

The surrounding discussion makes the relationship more precise. Consciousness soul turns toward truth and goodness with significance beyond private preference. Spirit self names that spiritual content taken into and individualized by the I. Receiving a truth as part of one’s being does not mean making it true merely because one prefers it.

The I can work upon other members, rather than being a detached supervisor controlling separate bodies. Steiner’s later descriptions of transformation develop this claim. Here the main question is how a changing experience can become consciously related to the one who experiences it.

Experiences belong to someone

A person can say that a memory, feeling or decision is mine. The I concerns the individuality that relates these experiences to itself, rather than one particular feeling within the stream.

The I can work with experience

Recognising anger creates the possibility of asking how to respond. In this account, individuality can transform its relationship to feelings and habits. The immediate experience and the activity directed toward it are distinguishable.

Connect individuality and spiritual content

The I is also described as receiving spiritual content and developing through it. The concept therefore joins self-recognition with the possibility of transformation, rather than naming a fixed personality label.

Key concept

The I names spiritual individuality in Steiner’s account: the self that recognizes experiences as its own and can work with them while receiving spiritual content.

Everyday example

A correction first irritates you. You then ask whether it is justified and decide how to respond. This course example illustrates a shift from having a response to examining it; it is not a detection of a spiritual I.

Keep this distinction

I or Ego does not simply mean selfishness, ordinary personality or every passing thought. The I, consciousness soul and spirit self are related but distinct terms.

Reveal the explanation

Individual appropriation is different from arbitrary private truth. Consciousness soul and spirit self are explained through that distinction.

See a way to approach it

Recognition makes the habit an object of attention. The person can examine its meaning and choose a response. This illustrates the relationship between experience and an individuality that can work with it.
