Intuition

How does Steiner distinguish spiritual Intuition from an ordinary hunch?

You have met spiritual images and Inspiration. Intuition names a further relation to spiritual beings in Steiner’s Luke lecture. Its meaning depends on that context, so the familiar idea of a sudden hunch will not explain it.

Read · Identified source section

Locate the section

According to Luke, Lecture 1: Inspiration, Intuition and love, supplied 2001 Creeger edition, PDF capture 16. Compare The Philosophy of Freedom, Chapters 5, 9 and 12, and the 1918 addition to the conclusion.

The explanation below paraphrases the identified section. It is course prose, rather than a quotation. Follow the book links for the wider reading and edition notes.

Course explanation

What the author means

In Lecture 1, Imagination perceives spiritual beings through images; Inspiration receives their inward expression. Intuition goes beyond both: Steiner describes participation in their being, made possible through developed spiritual love. He presents a claimed spiritual capacity, not merely confidence in an interpretation.

The Philosophy of Freedom uses intuition in discussing the apprehension of conceptual content. Moral intuition concerns an individually understood ethical idea. These uses have relationships within Steiner’s work, but their contexts and tasks differ. Recognizing an intelligible relation does not certify attainment of spiritual Intuition.

Read the supplied Luke section by asking what changes in the knower’s relationship: from an image, to expression, to participation. This reconstructs Steiner’s progression. Whether a reader accepts his account is a further question; understanding it requires keeping its stronger claim visible.

Key concept

Spiritual Intuition in this lecture means participation in spiritual beings through developed spiritual love, beyond their images and inward expression.

Everyday example

You understand why a friend needs quiet and choose to postpone a visit. That can illustrate understanding and considerate action. It does not establish that you have entered the friend’s spiritual being, nor that your interpretation gives complete access to another person.

Keep this distinction

A correct guess, strong feeling or useful response is not a test of this spiritual capacity. Conversely, identifying that limit should not reduce Steiner’s Intuition silently to an ordinary metaphor for empathy.
