The Soul World

Lesson 17 / 36 · Builds on: Life Between Death and Rebirth

What do sympathy and antipathy organize in Steiner’s soul-world account?

From inward experience to the soul world

Earlier lessons distinguished desires, feelings and the release of bodily attachments. Steiner uses “soul world” for a supersensible domain whose formations he describes through inward qualities and relationships. A soul formation means an organized configuration of soul qualities: a pattern of desires, feelings and tendencies that acts as a whole. The question here is how such formations relate, rather than where they would stand in physical space.

Sympathy and antipathy as technical terms

In this account, sympathy means a tendency to draw together or unite. Antipathy means a tendency to exclude or maintain separateness. They are technical terms, rather than simply kindness and unkindness or pleasant and unpleasant feelings. They can act together: attraction toward one thing can coexist with shutting out what surrounds it. Understanding their combination matters more than treating one word as praise and the other as blame.

Possessiveness and receptive interest

Consider someone who values a book only as “something that is mine” and takes no interest in what it expresses or what other readers discover. Compare that with attending to the book and another reader’s insight for their own sake. The contrast concerns possessiveness and receptive interest, not whether either reader owns or lends the book. Steiner distinguishes self-enclosed attraction, inward feeling and increasingly outward participation. These examples explain qualities of relationship; they do not identify anyone’s spiritual region. The next lesson turns to the spiritual world and creative activity.

Further study: the seven soul regions

A guide to the seven regions

Steiner names seven interpenetrating regions to distinguish these relationships more closely. The first three describe different combinations of sympathy and antipathy; the fourth concerns inward feeling; the higher three concern increasingly free outward participation. The guide below introduces the names and their broad roles. It is a course reading aid, not the complete account of each region. Their order is not physical altitude, and the regions are not ranks for judging people.

Two questions — optional

Why can strong attraction to something coexist with exclusion or separateness?

See an explanation

A person may be drawn to an object only for what it adds to their own possession or satisfaction, while shutting out other relationships. In Steiner’s technical vocabulary, sympathy toward the object can coexist with antipathy toward what surrounds it. Attraction alone is therefore not the same as receptive participation.

Do sympathy and antipathy simply mean kindness and unkindness here?

See an explanation

No. Sympathy names a tendency toward union; antipathy names exclusion or separateness. The terms describe relationships in Steiner’s account. They do not by themselves judge a person’s character, and owning or choosing not to lend a book does not establish a spiritual condition.

Course explanation

Understand the idea

In Theosophy, the physical, soul and spiritual worlds interpenetrate. The soul world is not thinner physical matter or a remote place among the stars. Its technical forces are sympathy, which seeks union, and antipathy, which excludes and maintains separateness. They mean more than friendliness and hostility.

The first three regions differ in these forces’ proportions: Burning Desire, Mobile Sensitivity and Wishes. Attraction can remain self-centred, seeking possession rather than receiving another being for its own sake. The fourth, Liking and Disliking, concerns inward feeling within a formation. The selected passage’s “first three” must not silently become a rule for all seven.

The higher three are Soul Light, Active Soul Force and Soul Life. Sympathy becomes freely outward, receiving and enlivening a shared soul world. These regions interpenetrate; their order distinguishes relationships rather than physical altitude. In the after-death account, purification loosens the attachments formed through bodily life. It is not a geographical tour with a uniform timetable.

Keep this distinction

Sympathy is not simply “more liking.” Its direction and relationship change. Nor are the seven regions seven ranks by which the course can classify people.
