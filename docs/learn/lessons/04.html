How does Steiner claim spiritual knowledge is possible?

What must change in the knower, according to Steiner?

Steiner’s account depends on a proposed development of the knower, not just on learning additional information. The eye-and-ear comparison introduces what he calls soul and spiritual organs of perception.

Read · Source passage

Rudolf Steiner · Theosophy · GA 9 · Monges / Church, 1971

Printed p. 73 · PDF p. 101

Just as in the body, eye and ear develop as organs of perception, as senses for bodily processes, so does a man develop in himself soul and spiritual organs of perception through which the soul and spiritual worlds are opened to him.

Anthroposophic Press, 1971; Henry B. Monges, translator; Gilbert Church, reviser. English excerpt checked against the supplied PDF image; Brazilian Portuguese is a new course study translation. Line breaks and line-end hyphenation normalized.

Course explanation

What the author means

Bodily senses disclose bodily processes. By analogy, the selected passage says a person can develop capacities through which soul and spiritual worlds become accessible. These organs belong to his supersensible account; the comparison does not describe another physical eye hidden in the head.

Theosophy’s path begins with thoughtful assimilation of another investigator’s account. Steiner regards thinking as a living force capable of organizing and developing the soul. He distinguishes active understanding from an agreeable stream of inner pictures, and asks for receptive attention that preserves judgment.

Feeling, will and conduct also enter the proposed preparation. The book connects disciplined attention with selflessness, perseverance, regulated reasoning and the education of impulses. Its aim is a person able to know and remain active in ordinary life, rather than someone defined by an unusual experience.

How to Know Higher Worlds develops the training account further. Its appendix insists on clear consciousness and distinguishes pure thinking as an activity from perception of further supersensible contents. Completing a schedule, passing a check or having a vivid image cannot establish that the proposed spiritual perception has occurred.

Key concept

The claimed spiritual organ is a developed capacity of the knower through which Steiner says supersensible reality can be perceived.

Everyday example

A listener learns to distinguish musical relationships that initially escaped attention. This ordinary learning illustrates a change in a perceiver’s capacity. It does not demonstrate Steiner’s further claim about perception of a spiritual world.

Keep this distinction

Thoughtful understanding, a strong feeling and direct spiritual discovery are different. The sense-organ comparison clarifies the proposal without proving it or promising attainment.
