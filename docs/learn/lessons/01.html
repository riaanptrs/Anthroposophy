Steiner’s Idea

Steiner and his work

Rudolf Steiner (1861–1925) was an Austrian philosopher, spiritual researcher, lecturer and educator who developed anthroposophy. His life’s work explored the human being and our relationship with the natural and spiritual worlds. This lesson introduces his life and the main fields of his work, so you can recognise the person behind the ideas we will study.

Nature, thinking and freedom

His early work joined two questions: what can we learn by observing nature, and what does thinking contribute to that knowledge? Editing Goethe's scientific writings gave him a setting for studying living development. His philosophical work then examined whether a person can understand the reasons for an action and act from that understanding.

A spiritual conception of human life

His later spiritual teaching extended this interest in human capacities into a claim about knowledge beyond the senses. He called this wider inquiry anthroposophy. For example, asking how a pupil learns involves attention, feeling and action; Steiner also interpreted those activities through a spiritual account of the human being. The educational question and its spiritual explanation belong together in his work.

Ideas entering practical work

The first Waldorf school opened in Stuttgart in 1919 with Steiner's educational guidance; his agricultural lectures in 1924 became a basis for biodynamics. This gives us a useful map: inquiry about knowing and freedom, a spiritual conception of human life, and practical applications. Next we will explain that conception before studying its individual terms.

Important Distinction

Anthroposophy is the broader spiritual conception. Waldorf education is one educational movement connected with it. Knowing this relationship helps you place the different courses on the site.

An ordinary example can clarify a distinction without establishing Steiner’s further spiritual claims. Understanding the account does not require accepting it as established fact.

Check Your Understanding

Who was Rudolf Steiner, and what became the name of his wider spiritual work?

Compare with an explained answer

Rudolf Steiner was an Austrian philosopher, spiritual researcher, lecturer and educator who lived from 1861 to 1925. His wider spiritual work became known as anthroposophy.

Name two practical fields in which Steiner worked, and describe his contribution to each.

Compare with an explained answer

For example, in education he helped establish the first Waldorf school in 1919. In agriculture, his lectures of 1924 became a foundation for biodynamic agriculture. You could also name his work in drama, architecture or eurythmy. These activities brought his spiritual understanding into practical life.
