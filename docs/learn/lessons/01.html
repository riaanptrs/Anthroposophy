Who was Rudolf Steiner?

Who was Steiner, and what were the main areas of his work?

Rudolf Steiner (1861–1925) was an Austrian philosopher, lecturer and educator who developed anthroposophy. His work connects questions about knowledge and freedom with a spiritual understanding of human life, and with practical work in education and the arts.

Course explanation

Understand the idea

Steiner grew up in a family connected with the railway. Machines, the natural landscape and geometry interested him from childhood. He later studied scientific subjects in Vienna and read philosophy. These interests help explain a question that runs through his work: how can we understand both the world around us and our own activity of thinking?

An important early occupation was editing Goethe’s scientific writings. Goethe studied living forms through their development: a plant changes from leaf to flower and fruit while remaining one living organism. Steiner’s work with these writings encouraged him to explore how thinking can follow a changing living process, as well as describe an isolated object.

His philosophical book The Philosophy of Freedom examines knowledge and free action. It asks what thinking contributes to understanding and how a person can act from a moral idea they have understood individually. In Theosophy, Steiner develops a spiritual account of body, soul and spirit, reincarnation, karma and the path of knowledge. These are two important parts of his work that this site teaches in separate courses.

Anthroposophy became the name of his broader spiritual work. Alongside books and lectures, Steiner worked with colleagues on education, drama, architecture and eurythmy. In 1919, he helped establish the first Waldorf school in Stuttgart. His agricultural lectures of 1924 later became a foundation for biodynamic agriculture. These activities show how he sought to bring his ideas into everyday human work.

The useful picture to retain is a thinker, teacher and initiator of practical work. His life connects scientific and philosophical interests, spiritual teaching, artistic activity and education. The courses will develop these subjects one at a time, explaining the ideas and giving you examples through which to understand them.

A question about knowing

Scientific study asks how we know the world. Philosophy also examines what happens when we think. These interests explain why knowledge and freedom became important subjects in Steiner’s early work.

A broader spiritual conception

Anthroposophy brings together his later spiritual teaching about human life and the world. Theosophy is one book within that broader work; it introduces concepts that later lessons will explain individually.

Ideas entering practical work

Education turns a conception of human development into questions about teaching. Artistic work turns questions about form, expression and experience into different kinds of activity. This is why the site offers several related courses.

Key concept

Rudolf Steiner developed anthroposophy and worked across philosophy, spiritual teaching, education and the arts.

Everyday example

One question is how a person comes to understand an idea; another is how a teacher can help a child understand it. Steiner’s philosophical work and educational work address these related questions in different settings. Studying him therefore means learning both his ideas and the activities they inspired.

Keep this distinction

Anthroposophy is the broader spiritual conception. Waldorf education is one educational movement connected with it. Knowing this relationship helps you place the different courses on the site.
