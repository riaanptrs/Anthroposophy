Glossary

62 terms, with definitions that distinguish source-specific meanings. The same word may serve different models.

Biography

An account of a particular life that asks about experience, meaning, choices and continuity as well as events.

Development

A structural transformation in the vocabulary of the book; distinguished from change generally and quantitative growth.

Psyche or soul

The changing field of thoughts, feelings and impulses where bodily conditions and spiritual aims interact in Lievegoed’s account.

Higher ego

The spiritual individuality Lievegoed understands as giving direction and meaning to biography; a claim within his worldview.

Leitmotiv

A guiding or recurring motive proposed to connect different expressions in a life; an interpretation to examine.

Wertmitte

Dominant value-centre: the concern organizing a phase in the author’s discussion of Guardini.

Fulfilment

The experience of realizing something meaningful, distinguished from recurrent satisfaction of a desire.

Disciplined introspection

Systematic attention to inner experience, tested against observations and alternative interpretations.

Creativity

Outward expression of individuality through artistic, intellectual or social activity.

Wisdom

Receptive judgement involving perspective, patience and timing; not guaranteed by age.

Sentient soul

A Steiner term Lievegoed associates with the personal shaping of inner experience in early adulthood; not a biological organ.

Intellectual or mind soul

A Steiner term associated here with reflective, organizing activity; its connection to an age phase is Lievegoed’s application.

Consciousness soul

The soul term Lievegoed associates with examining and taking responsibility for values, especially in his transition toward the forties.

Spieltrieb or play drive

The creative possibility between form and material necessity in Lievegoed’s use of Schiller.

Recognition

Attending to another person’s individuality and aims, rather than merely to what they provide for us.

Social or policy phase

The proposed possibility of shaping conditions in which others can develop; an opportunity rather than a compulsory role.

Basic orientation

A characteristic quality of engaging with tasks, distinct from aptitude, temperament and a biographical aim.

Aptitude

A capacity or possibility for learning and performance; it does not alone choose a life aim.

Temperament

A characteristic manner or energy of response, distinguished here from the task that becomes a guiding motive.

Enquiring orientation

The urge to investigate how phenomena work and relate, present in many occupations.

Thoughtful orientation

The tendency to examine concepts, coherence and wider relations rather than only accumulate findings.

28. Basic orientations: the key of a life, not its job title

Organizing orientation

Ordering responsibilities, processes and resources to enable action; it can become one-sided when functionality dominates everything.

29. Six orientations working together

Caring orientation

Attention to conditions in which living beings can grow and flourish, without necessarily occupying the foreground.

29. Six orientations working together

Innovatory orientation

The urge to change what appears inadequate and create a more fitting form.

29. Six orientations working together

Conserving orientation

Maintaining, recording and making existing achievements accessible as a foundation for further development.

29. Six orientations working together

Creative modality

A personal and responsive engagement that can appear through any of the six orientations.

Existential value

A commitment expressed in a way of living, rather than only endorsed in words.

18. The forties: values that become a way of living

Ego-imprisonment

The book's term for excessive dependence on confirming one's importance, role or achievement.

Second creative peak

A proposed later qualitative contribution through ordered experience and judgement, not an age-guaranteed result.

Detachment

Relinquishing exclusive control while potentially retaining care and responsibility.

Anima and animus

Gendered complementary symbolic figures in the Jungian account reported by Lievegoed.

Archetype

A pattern presented as wider than one biography and experienced through symbols in the model of Jung.

Persona

A social role shaping both how a person sees the world and how others see that person.

Shadow or double

Disowned or unrecognized qualities that may be concentrated into a negative image in the book's account.

Projection

Attributing an inner image or unrecognized quality to another without adequately checking the actual person.

Individuation

The lifelong integration toward a fuller self in the account of Jung, distinct from mere conformity.

Nativism

The model emphasizing inherited or internally given conditions, criticized when treated as total destiny.

Empiricism

The environmental and learning emphasis discussed in this book; its historical presentation is not all contemporary psychology.

Conditioning

Learning understood through relations among situation, response and consequences in the model under discussion.

Positive reinforcement

A consequence that strengthens a response; rewarded performance does not alone demonstrate broad understanding.

Personalism

The view emphasizing an individuality that relates to heredity and environment and sets aims; spiritually interpreted by Lievegoed.

Reductionism

Making a useful partial explanatory level an exhaustive account that dismisses other relevant dimensions.

Verstehen
