Change, growth and development

Recognize when improvement within an existing arrangement differs from reorganizing the arrangement itself.

Reading locator: supplied PDF captures 16–17. See the source map

Change is the widest term

A change is simply a difference over time. Moving a meeting from Tuesday to Wednesday changes the schedule. The word alone tells us little about direction, quality or consequence. Some changes are accidental, temporary or damaging. Calling something a change therefore does not yet justify describing it as progress.

Growth adds quantity

Lievegoed uses growth for an increase within the same system: more members, greater size or more output. A reading group can grow from six people to twenty while keeping the same discussion method. Growth can be welcome and still create difficulties. When every participant needs speaking time, simply extending a familiar method may stop working.

Development alters structure

Development involves a reorganization through which the whole functions differently. Lievegoed describes differentiation, coordination and integration: functions become more distinct, their relations are ordered, and the parts work together in a new whole. The biological examples help explain his vocabulary. Applying this vocabulary to a person's biography is an interpretive extension, not proof that human choices follow an organism's predetermined design.

What a crisis can reveal

A crisis can expose a mismatch between existing habits and new demands. It does not ensure a beneficial reorganization. A group might dissolve, reproduce its difficulties in a new structure or find a workable solution. In biographical study, ask which capacity, relation or decision pattern actually changed. Greater suffering or more dramatic events do not by themselves establish greater development.

Work through an example

A volunteer doubles the number of meals she cooks but still makes every decision herself: output has grown. When twenty volunteers join, missed orders expose a limit. The team separates purchasing, cooking and delivery, agrees how decisions are made and reviews the whole process together. That is structural development in this example. The new chart alone would be insufficient if everyone still waited for one person to decide everything.

Check your understanding

What evidence distinguishes development from growth here?

Read an explained answer

The changed relations among tasks and people. Producing more meals shows growth; distributing responsibility and integrating the tasks shows a new organization of the whole.

Apply the idea

Choose a familiar skill or group. Describe one change, one quantitative increase and one actual reorganization. Explain what became possible after the reorganization and what problem remained.

Change the case

Someone earns more while responding to every disagreement exactly as before. Must this be psychological development?

Compare your reasoning

No. Income is a quantitative indicator. To argue for psychological development, identify a changed capacity or pattern of responding, and support that account with concrete situations.
