Course explanation

What the author means

He begins with the substances joined in protein—sulfur, carbon, oxygen, nitrogen and hydrogen—but asks about their activity within the whole natural world rather than giving a fertilizer formula. Sulfur supplies the subtle mediation by which spiritual activity enters physical carriers. His “moistening” imagery describes this relationship across the other roles.

Carbon is the sculptor or framework-former. Oxygen carries etheric life and mobility into that framework. Nitrogen mediates between the carbon form and oxygen life; Steiner associates this mediation with astrality and, in the soil, sensitivity to conditions. These roles form a relationship: neither form alone nor life alone gives his account of a living organism.

Hydrogen completes the picture by dissolving the organized form back toward the surrounding cosmos, opening a possibility of renewed formation. The roles can be remembered as shaping, enlivening, mediating, releasing, with sulfur enabling their spiritual–physical interaction. They are not five interchangeable ingredients with equal functions.

The lecture calls atmospheric oxygen and nitrogen dead, and those active within organisms or soil living; it also describes sensitive nitrogen. These labels express Steiner’s account of spiritual activity. They do not identify different chemical elements or imply that laboratory nitrogen literally has human feelings. A faithful reading keeps the relation clear while recognizing that modern chemistry and physiology use a different explanatory framework.

Key concept

In lecture 3, substances are differentiated carriers within a spiritual process of formation, life, mediation and dissolution.

Course example

Make a five-part concept map: carbon—framework; oxygen—etheric life; nitrogen—astral mediation; hydrogen—release; sulfur—spiritual–physical assistance. Add arrows that show relationships. Title the map “Steiner’s lecture 3 account” so that its purpose remains clear.

Keep this distinction

This map explains the source’s conceptual structure. It is not an elemental fertilizer prescription, a chemical reaction diagram or proof that soil conditions can be diagnosed through a personified element.
