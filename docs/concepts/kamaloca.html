Kamaloca

Also called: Region of Desires

The name Steiner uses for the after-death condition in which the soul remains connected with bodily-oriented desires and undergoes their release.

Steiner's example of desire for a taste without the bodily instrument explains why death and release are not identical in his account. Kamaloca belongs to that account of the soul's condition, not to a map of physically separated destinations.

The seven soul-world regions differentiate the attachments involved; affinity determines participation in them. The term does not authorize a diagnosis of any particular deceased person's present state.

Keep this distinction

The general name Region of Desires must not be confused with the third of the seven regions, the Region of Wishes, or treated as a physical place.

Related concepts and relationships

  • Purification

    The condition is understood through the release of bodily-dependent attachments.

  • Soul world

    Steiner situates this after-death condition within the soul world rather than a geographic location.
