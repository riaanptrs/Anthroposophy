Equanimity

Steadiness that allows pleasure, pain and other feelings to be experienced without immediately letting them command the response.

In Chapter IV, Steiner seeks a refinement of sensitivity, not the destruction of sympathy and antipathy. The learner can receive the feeling and distinguish it from the action that follows.

He gives this education a spiritual purpose by claiming that developed feelings become perceptive organs. An everyday pause illustrates the distinction, without establishing that claimed higher capacity.

Keep this distinction

Equanimity is not emotional numbness, indifference to suffering or a requirement to remain in an unwanted situation.

Related concepts and relationships

  • Feeling

    Equanimity preserves feeling while steadying its relation to action.

  • Receptive attention

    A steadier response allows fuller attention to the encounter.

  • Willing

    A feeling need not determine the next deed immediately.
