Explore concepts · Steiner’s terminology

Thought-forms

The spiritually perceptible forms that Steiner describes as the expression of actual thought activity, differentiated by content, definiteness and motive.

A definite thought is described differently from a confused one, while similar intellectual capacities can serve different ends. The account concerns a real spiritual event, rather than just a picture used to explain language.

Ordinary comparison of ideas helps clarify their content and precision. It differs from the spiritual perception through which Steiner claims their forms are known.

Keep this distinction

A thought-form is not an ordinary drawing of an idea, a physical shape in the brain or an ordinary visible appearance from which a stranger’s thoughts can be read.

Related concepts and relationships

  • Thinking

    The account adds spiritual form to the content, definiteness and motive of thinking.

  • Aura

    Thought activity participates in the spiritually perceptible inner life described in the aura account.
