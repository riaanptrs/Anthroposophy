Receptive attention

An openness that gives an encounter time to be understood before established labels or immediate reactions decide its meaning.

Steiner asks the learner to make room for instruction even from a child or a person they dislike. A familiar verdict can otherwise block what a new encounter actually presents.

Receptive attention is the course’s name for this source-grounded attitude. It changes the timing of judgment; it need not remove the ability to question, disagree or set a limit.

Keep this distinction

Receptivity differs from accepting every claim, idealizing a teacher or giving up personal judgment.

Related concepts and relationships

  • Thinking

    Receiving a statement accurately precedes a considered judgment about it.

  • Equanimity

    Steadiness helps immediate reactions leave room for attention.

  • Sympathy and antipathy

    Attraction and aversion can become premature verdicts about an encounter.
