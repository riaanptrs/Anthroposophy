Explore concepts · Steiner’s terminology

Thinking

The activity of understanding meanings and following their relationships, which Steiner seeks to cultivate into an ordered capacity.

Ordered thinking follows the content rather than a desired result. It asks whether a conclusion really arises from its reasons and can distinguish coherent thought from a pleasing succession of images.

Steiner further calls thinking a living force that prepares spiritual faculties. This developmental claim belongs to his account and exceeds the ordinary ability to reason through a practical problem.

Keep this distinction

Thinking is not merely having thoughts, enjoying an inner picture or possessing information; order concerns how meanings and reasons relate.

Related concepts and relationships

  • Spiritual knowledge

    Steiner treats thoughtful understanding as preparation for spiritual investigation.

  • Willing

    Thinking examines the purpose and reasons that willing carries into action.

  • Feeling

    A satisfying feeling does not decide whether a conclusion follows.

  • Intuition

    The source recognizes elementary intuitive spiritual content even in thought.

Related practice

  • Reasons and preferences

    Compare thought organizing a desired outcome with thought examining whether a judgment or aim is justified.

  • Understand before agreeing

    Distinguish explaining a claim, judging it and personally investigating its subject.

  • Pause and attend

    Notice an immediate reaction without confusing it with the content encountered or the response chosen.

  • Follow reasons into action

    Connect a small purpose with its reasons, practical steps and room for correction.

  • Trace a creative process

    Distinguish a human intention, its changing working forms and a completed result before comparing them with Steiner’s archetype analogy.
