What this passage means

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

The reading’s key idea

Childs’s choleric portrait centres on strength of will: courage, initiative, perseverance, enterprise and practical leadership, alongside obstinacy, impatience and insistence on one’s own way. He predicts particular sensitivity to criticism and possible rage or despondency when pride is wounded. His handling rules are to let the person work, offer practical labour, keep advice until requested, appreciate real effort and avoid flattery. Our feedback agreement adds a way to name and check an actual error.

The chapter applies unstable/extrovert/divergent labels and claims recognizable physique, gait, blood-pressure risks, a spiritual lesson of self-control and a psychiatric association with a choleric/melancholic mixture. These remain attributed claims. Its business caricature shows a leader blaming staff after ignoring a warning. The course’s repair example recognizes useful preparation and proposes a shared check of an unstable support. That correction is a later practical choice, not a silent rewriting of Childs’s advice to withhold unrequested counsel.

Read choleric strength and the problem of correction

The fiery recognition portrait

The chapter’s motto celebrates toughness and its theme is My Way. Childs associates the choleric with fire and hot-blooded action, then offers a confident bodily-recognition portrait: stocky muscular build, firm features and decisive gait. Gendered descriptions of attractiveness and chivalry are also part of his rhetoric. His psychological labels are unstable, extroverted and divergent; the blood-pressure association is a medical claim. These passages show how his recognition model joins physical form with behaviour. The course explains those connections as his positions and does not classify a learner by physique, gender or illness.

Native PDF captures 26, columns 1–3; 27, columns 1–2.

Will, enterprise and one-sidedness

Considerable strength of will anchors the portrait. Courage, initiative, perseverance, resourcefulness and devotion to duty support leadership and enterprise. The same determination can become obstinacy, impatience and an insistence that a job be done one’s own way. Childs illustrates this through an energetic leader who sees opportunities and acts quickly but tolerates neither inefficiency in others nor criticism of personal decisions. The business anecdote dramatizes the danger: a warning is rejected, failure is blamed on staff, and the leader overlooks an earlier decision that helped bring the trouble about.

Native PDF captures 27, columns 2–3; 29, columns 1–3.

The source’s handling rules

Childs recommends letting the choleric get on with a task, offering practical labour and keeping advice until requested. Honest appreciation of real effort is the golden rule; flattery and fawning, he says, provoke contempt. His explanation involves pride in worth and competence: belittlement or failure can produce a collapse into rage or despondency. This is a stronger handling prescription than our feedback agreement. It also contains rhetoric excusing deserved mistreatment. The course’s concrete correction must therefore be presented as an additional practical choice, not attributed to the author as though he invites criticism.

Native PDF capture 28, columns 1–3.

Self-control and the course’s repair case

The chapter ends by calling self-control a lesson the choleric has come to Earth to learn. It links a secondary melancholic tendency with a manic-depressive picture of euphoria, failure, dejection and renewed optimism. These spiritual and psychiatric interpretations remain attributed claims, not diagnoses. In the repair example, the leader’s useful preparation is recognized while an unstable support is named and checked together. That sequence preserves determination’s contribution but makes correction possible. Its question is whether specific information and an agreed check improve this particular piece of work, rather than whether the person matches the whole portrait.

Connect the parts of the reading

How does the course’s feedback agreement relate to Childs’s handling prescription?

An explained answer: Both recognize actual effort and competence, but Childs advises withholding advice until asked and stresses sensitivity to criticism. The course adds a specific observation and a jointly agreed check so an error can be corrected. That addition is clearly a practical application, not a claim that Childs prescribes the same feedback.

Original open question and answer

What positive and difficult expressions arise from will in the portrait?

Show a suggested answer

Courage, initiative, perseverance and enterprise can support useful leadership; the same determination can become obstinacy, impatience and insistence on one’s own way.

How does Childs distinguish appreciation from flattery, and what advice rule accompanies it?

Show a suggested answer

Appreciation recognizes honest effort and worth; flattery and fawning seek favour through obsequious praise. He also recommends keeping advice until it is asked for.

What does the course add in the repair example?

Show a suggested answer

It names a specific unstable support and proposes a jointly agreed check while recognizing useful preparation. This makes correction a practical possibility, rather than attributing the same feedback rule to Childs.
