Explore the chapter’s core ideas

The reading’s key idea

Childs considers four same-type and six different-type pairings. He explicitly calls the sketches deliberately skewed verbal caricatures and says secondary tendencies, personality, health, upbringing, education and unexpected responses qualify prediction. Within that frame he explores shared force and rivalry, lively ideas and staying power, methodical restraint, conflicting control, doubled changeability, grounded counterbalance, divergent needs for novelty and security, doubled inertia, manipulation and resentment, and sensitivity seeking reciprocity. Similarity and difference can each help or intensify difficulty in his account.

All ten sketches are now present in captures 53–68. The source still makes forceful, sometimes gendered and melodramatic predictions, including manipulative and violent scenarios; its qualification does not erase them. Our existing ten-pair visual gives original discussion questions about agreements, not author quotations or success ratings. The spontaneous-outing example addresses actual needs and preserves freedom to decline.

Read all ten caricatured partnerships

The author’s opening qualification governs every pair

Childs begins with all four temperaments mixed within each person and explains the ten unordered combinations: four same-type and six different-type pairs. He calls the coming sketches deliberately skewed verbal caricatures. Secondary temperament, personality, health, upbringing, education and unexpected reactions complicate prediction. These qualifications do not erase the forceful judgments in the sketches. Read them as the frame within which he contrasts association, complementarity and friction, rather than as a success ranking. The existing course visual supplies ten original agreement questions; it does not quote these characterizations.

PDF captures 53–54.

The four pairings beginning with choleric

Choleric/choleric magnifies rival leadership and volatile will, but shared direction, exchanged responsibilities and remembered fellow-feeling can support cooperation. Choleric/sanguine combines practical determination with rapid creative ideas; shared pace excites both, while weak staying power or inner resources may undermine later years. Choleric/phlegmatic opposes heat and water: methodical restraint can temper enterprise, and divided tasks can help, provided tolerances and goodwill develop. Choleric/melancholic instead caricatures two inflated egos seeking control by force or strategy. The domestic scene lets each partner tell a story of devotion and sacrifice while perpetuating the other’s difficulties. It is explicitly exaggerated, not an ordinary description of every couple.

PDF captures 54–58.

The three remaining pairings beginning with sanguine

Sanguine/sanguine doubles changing interests, timing conflicts and mutual admiration; Childs also expects maturity to add grace and consideration, with secondary tendencies supporting an underdeveloped inner life. Sanguine/phlegmatic pairs airy imagination with patient grounding and practical steadiness, but stubbornness and excess can create friction. His gendered shopping and finance scenes illustrate the caricature, including a reversal of the sexes. Sanguine/melancholic contrasts novelty and flexibility with reserve and security. The concert-versus-book dispute dramatizes wounded attention, withdrawal and reconciliation; time and secondary mixtures may help, but Childs predicts persistent tension.

PDF captures 58–63.

The final three inward-facing combinations

Phlegmatic/phlegmatic offers stability, comfort and routine, with doubled inertia; secondary sanguine or choleric movement may provide rhythm. Phlegmatic/melancholic is the starkest control-and-resentment sketch: demands corner an accommodating partner until the author imagines melodramatic violence, while the other intensifies self-torment. Melancholic/melancholic combines sensitivity and compassion with disappointed expectations of solicitude, pride and mutual suffering; secondary tendencies mitigate extremes, and tribulation may yield later wisdom. Preserve these actual claims when comparing the course’s questions. A negotiated agreement in a fictional situation neither confirms the prediction nor makes coercion or violence something to endure because of a label.

Words for the reading

Unordered pairing: reversing the two labels does not create another combination. Verbal caricature: a deliberately skewed portrait emphasizing selected traits. Secondary temperament: the supporting tendency Childs repeatedly invokes to qualify a pair. Reciprocity: giving and receiving consideration.

Read → understand → explain → apply. The chapter explanation is open from the start.

See the idea in an example

One partner enjoys spontaneous outings while another wants advance notice. Calling the pairing incompatible settles nothing about what each needs or what they can agree.

A useful discussion specifies notice, freedom to decline and how shared plans are made. The agreement addresses actual behaviour rather than predicting the relationship’s fate.
