Understand the reading

Steiner opens this lecture series by arguing that myths can express realities inadequately grasped through abstract concepts alone. He allows several approaches to a myth. Our course investigates that proposal through careful reading. The seven lectures assume an anthroposophical background and are reports of oral teaching, not a book revised by Steiner. We will identify his claims about ancient consciousness and spiritual beings rather than quietly treat them as historical consensus. The short stories here paraphrase his retellings; they are not a survey of all ancient versions.

See the idea in an example

Two readers hear that Isis searches for the scattered body of Osiris. One hears a story of devotion; another sees an image of recovering a lost unity. Must one interpretation erase the other?

A story presents events and relationships; an interpretation proposes what they mean. Begin by describing the search before explaining it. Then ask which details support each interpretation. Devotion attends to the persistence of the search; recovery of unity attends to what has been scattered. Neither observation by itself establishes a historical event or a spiritual law. Multiple readings can be possible without all readings being equally well supported.
