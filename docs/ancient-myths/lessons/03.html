What this passage means

The surrounding argument links bodily maturity, inward development and changing access to spiritual experience. The veil of Isis gives that question a mythic form; explain the form before interpreting its modern transformation.

The focus of this lesson

Examine how a mythic image becomes a question about knowing.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

Understand the reading

Lecture III returns to Isis and presents Steiner’s new mythic treatment in a contemporary setting. He calls for renewed engagement with the force of word, thought and idea, rather than simply recovering an old involuntary clairvoyance. His technical term Imagination concerns a claimed mode of spiritual cognition; inventing a vivid scene is not evidence of attaining it. Compare the old narrative with his new use of it. Identify where the source moves from retelling into an invitation to transform the activity of knowing.

Keep this distinction

An image can open a question; it cannot answer that question merely by being compelling.

See the idea in an example

A covered object invites you to uncover it. A difficult idea invites you to develop the capacity to understand it. What changes when the veil is an image of knowledge rather than a piece of cloth?

Removing cloth changes access to an object; understanding also requires work by the knower. The veil image can direct attention to that difference. But the analogy cannot settle what lies behind the veil. A reader must still ask what is being claimed, by whom, and on what grounds. This distinction keeps a powerful image from becoming a substitute for inquiry.
