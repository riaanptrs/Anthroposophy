Humanity’s development and lifelong learning

Read Steiner’s developmental scheme without confusing collective and individual change.

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · Ancient Myths · M. Cotterell translation, 1971 · PDF / study capture p. 150

If today we wish to have a development of the soul and spirit nature after our twenties, we must seek for this development by our power of will.

What this passage means

The lecture contrasts development supplied by nature with development deliberately taken up. It places this contrast within a historical account of humanity’s ages; the course’s lifelong-learning activity explores the deliberate element.

The focus of this lesson

Read Steiner’s developmental scheme without confusing collective and individual change.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

Understand the reading

Lecture V proposes that humanity becomes younger across successive post-Atlantean epochs while an individual grows older. Steiner links the scheme to the need for consciously cultivated development beyond what bodily maturation supplies. The lecture also engages Schlegel, materialism and the renewal of education. The epoch scheme and Atlantis belong to Steiner’s spiritual history, not a chronology established by this exercise. Our practical question is narrower: what supports continued learning, and how might an educational arrangement leave room for it?

See the idea in an example

An older adult begins learning an unfamiliar craft. Someone calls this “becoming young again.” Is that an observation about learning, a metaphor, or a claim about humanity’s evolution?

The same expression can do different work. It may describe renewed curiosity, compare learning with childhood, or assert a historical law. Before agreeing, identify its use. The adult’s experience can illustrate continued learning; it cannot establish the ages assigned to whole epochs. Draw separate lines for one individual’s biography and the collective development proposed by an author. Mixing those scales makes an analogy look like evidence.
