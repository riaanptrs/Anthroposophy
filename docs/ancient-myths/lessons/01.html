2 · Work through the explanation

A worked example

In Steiner’s retelling, Osiris brings useful arts, is killed by Typhon, and is sought by Isis. Osiris later instructs Horus from beyond death. In the Greek sequence, generations of gods succeed one another.

Notice two different structures: a search through loss and a succession of divine generations. In the Egyptian account, the continued activity of Osiris after death matters as much as the search. In the Greek account, replacement and inheritance among generations matter. Reducing both to “good defeats evil” would discard much of what Steiner asks us to examine. His retelling uses Greek forms of some names, including Typhon; use this version consistently while recognizing that myth traditions contain other versions.

Understand the reading

Steiner interprets Osiris in relation to the enduring human being and changing access to spiritual experience. He reads the succession of Greek gods as a memory of earlier states of consciousness. He contrasts these structures with the creation and moral orientation he finds in the Old Testament. These are his comparative interpretations. A pattern found in a story does not independently establish what every Egyptian, Greek or Hebrew person believed. Keep the comparison specific and avoid turning cultures into fixed psychological types.
