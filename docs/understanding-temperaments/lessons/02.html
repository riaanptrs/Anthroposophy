Choleric: purpose and correction

Understand the movement from forceful insistence toward capable action.

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · The Four Temperaments · GA 57 · 4 March 1909 · § 8

He therefore appears as the person who wants to assert his I under all circumstances.

Original German text; new English and Brazilian Portuguese study translations.

Read the original chapter or lecture (German) →

Compare with the German passage

Dadurch tritt er auf als der Mensch, der sein Ich unter allen Umständen durchsetzen will.

What this passage means

The preceding sentence associates the choleric temperament with predominance of the I. The portrait concerns directed will meeting resistance; the educational question is how purpose can develop along with receptivity to correction.

The focus of this lesson

Understand the movement from forceful insistence toward capable action.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

Understand the concept

The choleric picture centres on strong will and the wish to assert a purpose against obstacles. Steiner associates it with predominance of the I-bearer. One-sidedness appears when insistence narrows into a single aim that will not listen or adjust. Purposefulness and anger are not identical, even when the source discusses them together. For the child, the lecture emphasizes respect for an educator’s competence and the encounter with real difficulty. Our teaching adaptation uses achievable challenges and demonstrable skill. It does not turn authority into infallibility or arrange humiliation and conflict. A teacher can model competence by noticing an error and correcting it honestly.

What does choleric mean?

The choleric temperament is the portrait of concentrated will: a person forms an intention and brings energy to making it happen. The central movement is from “I want to do this” toward action, especially when something stands in the way. The word is often associated with anger, but the underlying idea here is force of purpose. Anger is one possible expression when that purpose meets resistance.

Characteristics, strengths and difficulties

Look for the connected pattern: readiness to take the lead, direct speech, quick decisions, energetic action and a wish to overcome obstacles. In a constructive expression, this can bring courage, responsibility and perseverance. The person may be willing to begin a difficult task that others hesitate to undertake.

The same force can become impatience with delay, difficulty accepting correction, or pressure on others to follow one plan. The distinction is in how the purpose meets reality: perseverance adjusts the method when evidence changes; stubbornness keeps insisting even when the method fails.

A concrete example

A group cannot assemble a planter. One member says, “Let us measure it, choose the right tool and finish the frame.” This illustrates initiative directed toward a task. If the member dismisses the measurement because it contradicts their plan, the same insistence obstructs the work. What changed was the willingness to let the task correct the intention.

Why Steiner recommends competence and real difficulty

Steiner associates this temperament with the I-bearer and emphasizes respect for someone who can actually do something. In his educational account, a meaningful challenge gives strong will something worthy to meet. The adult demonstrates skill and allows the work itself to reveal what succeeds.

In our practical adaptation, acknowledge the aim, state the concrete obstacle and invite a competent next step: “You want it finished today. This joint moves. Show me how we can make it secure.” The aim is to develop purposeful strength together with judgment.

Check your understanding

What makes the choleric portrait more than a description of anger?

Show a suggested answer

Its centre is will gathered around a purpose: initiative, decisive action and persistence. Anger can arise when that purpose meets resistance. Constructive persistence allows evidence to correct the method; one-sided insistence refuses that correction.
