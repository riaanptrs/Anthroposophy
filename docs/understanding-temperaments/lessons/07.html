What this passage means

The principle concerns guiding the temperaments. The following educational examples begin with affection, demonstrated competence, sensitivity to another destiny or companions’ interests; the course activities adapt these distinct points of contact.

The focus of this lesson

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

Understand the concept

After describing possible one-sided extremes, Steiner states a governing principle: work with what is there. For the sanguine child he proposes love for an individual and deepened interest through withdrawing and restoring objects; for the choleric, esteem for competent authority and real resistance; for the melancholic, a life-tested educator and sympathy with suffering; for the phlegmatic, varied companions’ interests and appropriate objects of impassivity. These are Steiner’s differentiated proposals in captures 11–12. Our four-learner project and other ordinary examples are course adaptations. Their manageable tasks and optional invitations should remain named as adaptations, rather than silently replacing the lecture’s stronger prescriptions. The source’s stated aim is to guide existing tendencies while preserving their diversity.

The educational principle: begin with a capacity

Steiner’s four educational routes start from something already active. Choleric purpose can meet a worthwhile challenge and visible competence. Sanguine responsiveness can deepen through a trusted relationship and a recurring interest. Phlegmatic steadiness can join an activity through companions’ enthusiasm. Melancholic sensitivity can develop into understanding and care.

Each route explains why a particular invitation might work. A command to “concentrate” gives a readily shifting interest little to return to. A shared, meaningful activity can supply that connection. Similarly, a real task can give force of will a more productive direction than a struggle over who has authority.

One aim, different ways into it

Suppose every learner needs to plant and label a seed. One may begin with the challenge of constructing a sound container; another with a favourite plant; another beside an engaged companion; another through caring for a vulnerable seedling. The learning aim remains the same. These are original examples of the four routes, chosen according to the actual learner’s response.

After the invitation, check whether understanding and participation improved. This is what makes support educational: the learner develops a capacity, rather than merely complying for a moment.

Four original routes and the stronger prescriptions

The Mystery discussion gives four relationship routes into education: the sanguine child’s love and fidelity toward a person; the choleric child’s esteem for the educator’s ability; the melancholic child’s compassion for another person’s difficult destiny; and the phlegmatic child’s awakening through companions’ interests. Its common principle is to begin with an existing disposition. These distinct routes give more content to that principle than a general instruction to be supportive.

The source also makes stronger proposals. In its choleric passage the educator should never appear unable to advise, and arranged obstacles should overcome the child’s strength. In its melancholic passage it proposes preparing obstacles for justified pain and suffering, alongside a qualification against exaggeration. State these elements when explaining the text. The course’s honest correction of a failed test, reachable challenge, acknowledgment of disappointment and voluntary act of care are its own adaptations. They develop selected relationships in the argument rather than reproduce every original prescription.
