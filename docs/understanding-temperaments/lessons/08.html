A worked example

The garden needs a planner, a welcoming host and a careful checker. Someone wants to assign these jobs from temperament labels.

Discuss relevant experience, preferences, the actual task and support needed. Agree on a review point so that a role can change. Childs supplies the workplace discussion; our adaptation uses demonstrated capacities instead of deciding a role from appearance or a temperament label.

Understand the concept

Childs begins the workplace chapter with mutual dependence: an organization needs people to coordinate, and status games interfere with that cooperation. He discusses matching abilities to tasks, learning through guided experience and developing qualities beyond a dominant tendency. The individual’s application of a capacity matters alongside the capacity itself. He also recommends temperament-based job matching and discusses appearance and handwriting as clues. Our adaptation uses demonstrated skills, expressed preferences and task requirements instead. A quiet person is not automatically a back-room worker, nor an assertive person automatically a suitable leader. The useful transfer is to make expectations explicit and review actual performance.
