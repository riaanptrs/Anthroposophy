Understanding Temperaments

Understand a response, find a freer next step

Knowing a pattern can help you keep its strength while changing what makes an encounter difficult. Begin with something you actually did. These are reflective portraits, not a validated personality test, a diagnosis or a fixed identity. One person can recognize several tendencies, and context matters.

The same situation: rain cancels a gathering

The following responses are invented examples, not predictions. Compare their direction of attention and possible contribution. None is the best or worst kind of person.

Why might I respond this way?

Steiner proposes inherited constitution meeting a spiritual individuality developing through lives. That is his spiritual explanation, not an established psychological cause. Present observation should also attend to habits, experience, relationships, fatigue, stress and the task. A temperament name alone cannot tell you which influence explains this particular response. Contemporary temperament research uses different constructs and developmental observations; it does not validate the four spiritual correspondences. GA 57 · 1909 · Developmental research: Murillo et al. (2024)

Does my birth date determine one of the four?

The core temperament lectures used here give no birthday-to-temperament formula. Steiner does discuss cosmic birth relationships and horoscopes in GA 15; it would be inaccurate to say he rejected astrology generally. That separate account does not supply a tested birthday or zodiac lookup for these portraits. This course does not assign a temperament from your date, season or sign. GA 15 · chapter 3, paragraphs 14–18

A source distinction: children and adults

The 1909 lecture gives choleric/I, sanguine/astral, phlegmatic/etheric and melancholic/physical. GA 295 (21 August 1919) explicitly gives children different pairings: choleric/astral, sanguine/etheric, phlegmatic/physical and melancholic/I. These are attributed spiritual relationships, not established anatomy or a universal chart. The historical text must be read with its source and life stage named. GA 295 · Discussion One

The books remain distinct

The combined course links to Gilbert Childs’s Understand Your Temperament! and Rudolf Steiner’s The Mystery of Temperaments as separate companions. Childs’s interpretations are his own authored contribution. The Mystery upload has unidentified edition and translation details in the retained record; a parallel lecture does not identify that edition. The three books were supplied again and their main texts were reread for the current teaching revision. The new PDF captures contain 29 pages (The Four Temperaments), 32 (The Mystery of Temperaments) and 114 (Childs). Quotation locators in the detailed readings still identify the earlier 23-, 33- and 97-page source captures; they have not been relabelled as pages of the new files.

Learn from the beginning

No previous study is needed. Begin with the meaning of temperament, compare four responses to the same situation and learn how each tendency can help or hinder an action. Then explore spiritual constitution, education, work and relationships in the books. Every lesson includes an explanation and a worked example.

A rhythm you can return to

Read → understand → practise → return and revise. Allow about 15–25 minutes for a lesson, then three short practice sessions on separate occasions. These are planning estimates; source reading takes additional time. Work at your own pace.

Keep dates, observations and revisions in the lesson notebook. The final lesson returns to your earlier entries. Saving is optional and stays in this browser; export a copy for safekeeping.

Sources and method

Steiner’s Berlin lecture (GA 57), the supplied Mystery of Temperaments text and Gilbert Childs’s later book contribute to one course. Each lesson distinguishes the main explanation, a source comparison and our original practice.

The earlier book companions remain optional reading with their existing notes. Their completion marks do not automatically complete this new course. Longer discussions of spiritual constitution, heredity and historical bodily correspondences remain in those readings.
