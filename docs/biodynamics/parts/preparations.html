The Biodynamic Preparations

Why does Steiner add distinct preparations to ordinary fertility management, and what role is each intended to serve?

Source lectures: GA 327 Lecture 4 and Discussion 1, 12 June 1924; Lecture 5 and Discussion 2, 13 June; Lecture 6 and Discussion 3, 14 June. Later numbers identify the treatments; they are not Steiner's spoken numbering.

What you will learn

  • Explain preparations as supplementary measures within the farm's fertility relationships, retaining ordinary manuring.
  • Compare the materials, seasonal exposure, destination and intended effects of the two horn treatments.
  • Distinguish the six manure/compost ingredients by their enclosures, exposure and attributed roles.
  • Explain horsetail's different status and distinguish historical indications, later practice and independently measured effects.

Lessons

  1. Why Preparations?

    What problem are preparations intended to address if the farm still needs ordinary manure and compost?

  2. Horn Manure — Preparation 500

    Why does Steiner place manure in a cow horn for winter before using it as a soil-facing treatment?

  3. Horn Silica — Preparation 501

    How does the summer mineral treatment complement the winter manure treatment?

  4. Compost Preparations 502–507

    Why does Steiner condition manure with six different plant materials rather than one interchangeable additive?

  5. Horsetail / Preparation 508

    Why does the horsetail treatment address a different agricultural problem from the horn and compost preparations?

Part synthesis

The preparations have a place within a larger agricultural argument. Crops leave the farm, fertility needs replenishment, and Steiner wants manure to retain and transmit living activity. The horn treatments address complementary soil-facing and plant-facing relationships; the six ingredients work through manure or compost. Horsetail addresses a particular account of abnormal growth rather than sharing their enclosure and maturation pattern.

Their intended roles are distinct even though they belong to one worldview. The later numbers help us find them; they do not explain the mechanisms or certify modern instructions. Original indications, subsequent practical conventions and research outcomes require their own sources.

How do the horn pair, the six fertility ingredients and horsetail serve different roles in Steiner's model, and why must ordinary manuring continue?

Show a model answer

The horn pair is complementary. Winter horn manure is applied toward the soil and is intended to enliven its solid component, pushing from below. The summer mineral treatment is applied toward the plant and is intended to support development from above. The six ingredients instead condition manure, liquid manure or compost: their different plant materials, enclosures and exposures are assigned roles in retention, organization and reception of living activity.

Horsetail addresses a different problem. Steiner attributes certain fungal abnormalities to excessive water-mediated lunar activity and intends the diluted Equisetum treatment to reduce that mediation. It is not a third horn treatment or another ingredient sharing the six preparations' enclosure and maturation pattern.

Ordinary manuring continues because these measures supplement a fertility system rather than replace it. Manure and compost still provide the agricultural setting in which the small additions are supposed to work. The distinct roles explain how the preparations fit the model; a documented application or measured outcome does not by itself demonstrate its etheric, astral or planetary mechanisms.
