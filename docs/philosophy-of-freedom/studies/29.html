Optimism and pessimism are arguments to examine

Detailed study 29 / 36 · The value of life

Return to the full chapter lesson →

Reading by PDF capture: Wilson 2012, 173-capture file, 130–145; Amrine 2022, 84-capture abridgment, 49–51. Numbers identify file positions, not printed pages.

1 Steiner introduces opposing evaluations of life. Optimism regards the world as good or as yielding a favorable balance; pessimism emphasizes suffering and the failure of desire. These are positions the chapter examines, not simple labels for cheerful and unhappy personalities. Personal temperament does not settle their philosophical arguments.

2 Schopenhauer's proposed release from striving differs from Hartmann's account of participating in a cosmic process of redemption. Keep the positions distinct before evaluating Steiner's replies. In particular, Hartmann's world-purpose is an opponent's premise, not a task Steiner assigns to the student. The book's earlier rejection of imposed cosmic purpose remains relevant.

3 The chapter questions whether a theoretical calculation of pleasure and pain can dictate what a person must value. Feelings themselves must enter the account as experienced facts. Its historical argument concerning suicide is not reliable evidence about an individual's wellbeing or risk; reconstructing that argument does not make it a tool for judging another person's life.

Make it concrete

A reader calls the chapter “positive thinking.” That misses its work. It distinguishes theories of value, tests proposed ways of counting pleasure and asks what actually moves willing. The conclusion is not that painful circumstances should be ignored.

Test the distinction

Why should Hartmann's cosmic redemption not appear as the course's advice?

Read an explained answer

It is a position presented for criticism. Confusing an opponent's claim with Steiner's conclusion reverses the argument. Identifying the speaker is essential, especially where an abridgment shortens the historical discussion.

Work with the idea

Write one sentence each for Schopenhauer, Hartmann and Steiner's question to them. Do not merge their views.
