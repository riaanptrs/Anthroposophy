2 · Work through the explanation

A worked example

Imagine a circular card held directly in front of you. Now tilt it. Its visible outline appears narrower, although the card has not been cut into a different shape. “It looks narrower from here” and “the card has become narrower” are different claims. Comparing views helps you investigate which claim fits.

Understand the idea

Steiner calls the content encountered through observation a percept. The word names what is encountered, not the act of perceiving. It can include an inward feeling as well as a colour or shape. A memory that becomes an object of attention is present as an experience; that does not guarantee its accuracy.

Thinking brings concepts into relation with this content. Understanding a circle differs from seeing this particular card from this position. After the card is put away, a representation of the encounter may remain. You need not have a vivid inner photograph to remember it. The object, its present appearance, the concept and the retained representation answer different questions.

In this chapter, Steiner describes consciousness as where observation and conceptual activity meet. He also argues that thinking helps us distinguish ourselves as subjects from what we attend to as objects. This is an argument about our participation in knowing, not permission to treat every private judgment as universally true.

The imagined observer without prior experience helps isolate the question of what thinking contributes. Actual experience is already shaped by concepts; naming a colour or shape already employs them. The exercise asks for careful distinctions, not a perfectly blank mind.

Keep this idea

distinguish what appears, how you understand it and what remains in memory.

Check your understanding

Is a percept the act of perceiving?

Show a suggested answer

no; in this usage it is the encountered content.

Does a changing appearance prove the object imaginary?

Show a suggested answer

no; it shows that the appearance depends partly on the encounter's conditions.

Is a memory identical to the present object?

Show a suggested answer

no; it is a retained representation that can itself become an object of attention.
