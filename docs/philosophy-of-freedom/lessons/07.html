2 · Work through the explanation

A worked example

Imagine three dated views of one flower: bud, opening flower and blossom. What lets you understand them as a development rather than three disconnected appearances?

Understand the idea

Steiner argues that the percept is one side of reality and the fitting concept the other. We grasp the relationship through our activity, but he holds that its content belongs to the phenomenon. Thinking does not make the petals grow. Knowing a name does not yet explain a process, and a predicted next stage may fail when conditions change.

Consider two differently drawn triangles. The drawings and individual encounters differ; both students can nevertheless understand the same three-sided relationship. This illustrates shared conceptual content. It does not prove that everyone always means the same thing or settle Steiner’s wider claim of a universal conceptual whole. Ask each student to explain the connection.

In this discussion, intuition names the inward apprehension of thought content. It differs from a hunch, confidence or enthusiasm. When a drawing is removed, a representation of that encounter may remain; Chapter 6 examines its relation to the concept more closely. A memory can itself become an object of observation without being the original paper seen again.

The chapter continues the criticism of a proof that confines knowledge to mental pictures. Its positive alternative locates thinking within knowing reality. The 1918 addition asks the reader to examine assumptions about representation and the distinctive role of thinking. Keep two questions separate: can you reconstruct the proposed connection, and do its reasons justify the conclusion?

Check your understanding

Does a name explain development?

Show a suggested answer

No; explaining requires connecting the stages and conditions.

Does thinking physically open the flower in this example?

Show a suggested answer

No; it supplies a conceptual connection in the account of knowing.

Does shared conceptual content mean identical personal experience?

Show a suggested answer

No. The triangle example distinguishes a common relationship from different drawings, encounters and feelings.

How to assess your response

Check that you explained the distinction, gave reasons and used the assigned passage. Separate the example, Steiner’s argument and your assessment. Revisit what remains unclear. Well-supported disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement or reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
