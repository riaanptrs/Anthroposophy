GA 4 · Lesson 07 · Chapter 5

Knowing a changing world

Explain Steiner's proposal using an observed sequence, a conceptual connection and a possible correction.

Start here

Imagine three dated records of the same flower: a closed bud, a partly opened flower and an open blossom. Each record shows an appearance at a particular time. Calling it a rose gives us a name. Understanding that the appearances are stages in the development of one plant connects them in a different way.

Understand the idea

Steiner argues that the percept alone is not the whole reality available to knowledge. Thinking supplies the conceptual side. In his account, the fitting concept belongs to the phenomenon, even though we grasp it through our own activity. This does not mean that thinking makes the petals unfold. It concerns how we know the flower and its relationships.

The example also has limits. Conditions may prevent a bud from opening, or our records may stop. We must distinguish an observed stage from an expected one. A guess that a flower will open tomorrow can be checked against what happens. Finding the word for the flower does not settle every question about it.

The opening pages ask us to examine an argument that we know only mental pictures. Steiner distinguishes whether that conclusion is true from whether a particular proof supports it. His alternative gives thinking a role within knowing the world. Reconstruct that proposal before deciding whether its reasons convince you.

Keep this idea

Understanding connects what a single appearance leaves apart.

Reading: GA 4 Chapter 5, Basis 41–45. Read the opening argument as well as the flower example.

Try it

Make three columns: recorded appearance, proposed relationship, and further observation needed. Use the imagined flower or a familiar object that changes. Mark which entries are observations and which are expectations. State one condition under which your expectation could fail.

Check your understanding

Does a name explain development?

Show a suggested answer

No; explaining requires connecting the stages and conditions.

Does thinking physically open the flower in this example?

Show a suggested answer

No; it supplies a conceptual connection in the account of knowing.

Is every fitting-looking guess knowledge?

Show a suggested answer

No; its application requires examination and may need revision.

How to assess your response

Check that you explained the distinction, gave reasons and used the assigned passage. Separate the example, Steiner’s argument and your assessment. Revisit what remains unclear. Well-supported disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement or reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
