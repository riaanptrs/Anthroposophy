2 · Work through the explanation

A worked example

Calculate ((3 × 3) + 1) ÷ 2. Keep your steps. Before reading on, distinguish the result from the activity by which you reached it.

Understand the idea

When two billiard balls meet, you can observe a sequence of movements. An explanation connects motion, contact and the conditions of the encounter. Thinking contributes an understood relationship; it does not push the balls. A prediction based on that relationship still needs comparison with what happens.

In the arithmetic example, the steps are 3 × 3 = 9, 9 + 1 = 10, and 10 ÷ 2 = 5. Now make those connections your object of attention. Remembering “five” is different from reconstructing why it follows. Applying division only to the 1 changes the expression, not merely the wording of the answer.

Steiner distinguishes thinking about a problem from subsequently thinking about that activity. You may have been aware of your reasons while solving it; the review makes them the subject of a fresh investigation. He gives thinking a special place because we participate in producing its connections and examine them through thinking itself. This is his proposed starting point, not a guarantee that every explanation is correct.

Observation here also includes inward experiences. A dream can be noticed without establishing its imagined object as physically present. Similarly, confidence can be observed without becoming a justification. The 1918 addition distinguishes active thinking from passing thought-images and relates willing to that activity. It does not require a blank mind. The next chapter asks how this activity relates to what we perceive.

Keep this idea

first think through something, then examine its connections; keep understanding distinct from certainty alone.

How to assess your response

Check that you explained the distinction, gave reasons and used the assigned passage. Separate the example, Steiner’s argument and your assessment. Revisit what remains unclear. Well-supported disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement or reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
