2 · Work through the explanation

A worked example

Chapter 4 examines an argument that can sound persuasive: perception depends on our organization; therefore all we directly know is our own representations. Notice that the conclusion says more than the starting observation. Dependence on a perceiver and knowledge restricted to representations are not the same statement.

Understand the idea

In the account Steiner examines, an external object affects sense organs, processes occur in the organism, and an experience results. The conclusion then treats the apparent external object as a representation. Steiner asks: what status did the argument give to the eyes, nerves and brain used to explain this result? They too are known through perception. If the argument doubts all perceived objects, it needs to explain why these particular ones were allowed to function as an unquestioned foundation.

This is his consistency objection to that route of proof. It is not a reason to ignore bodily processes. Nor does a defect in an argument automatically show its conclusion false. We must distinguish a claim, the reasons offered for it and the objection to those reasons.

Use three labels while reading: “position being examined,” “Steiner's response” and “question still open.” The quotation from Volkelt's book on Kant is not a direct quotation from Kant. The accounts discussed should not be collapsed into the slogan that everything disappears when nobody looks. The chapter ends by calling for another approach to the relation between percept and representation.

Keep this idea

examine how a conclusion is supported before accepting either it or its opposite.
