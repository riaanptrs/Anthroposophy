A worked example

Knowing that a participant missed the meeting does not by itself decide what you ought to do. You might offer a summary, arrange a conversation or respect their wish to catch up independently. Understanding the situation and finding the ethical aim are distinguishable. Steiner calls the realization of individually apprehended ethical content ethical individualism. It is not simply doing whatever occurs to you.

Understand the idea

For Steiner, a free deed springs from the ethical intuition itself and love for the objective. Yet he also distinguishes good and bad application. A feeling of excitement, novelty or certainty cannot settle that question. Nor can an observer identify freedom from the outward deed alone. A person may perform a familiar action from renewed understanding, or invent a new action to win praise.

The chapter acknowledges mixed free and unfree activity and a developmental role for norms. You can examine advice, understand an existing principle and make it your own. Ask the participant what would help before preparing an unwanted summary. Consultation supports understanding; it need not transfer responsibility for judgment. This is an original practical illustration, not a demonstration of Steiner’s entire account.

Steiner connects freedom with understanding other people’s will in a shared world of ideas. His claim of harmony concerns morally free action; it does not establish that every present disagreement has a simple resolution. Understanding an aim need not mean endorsing its effects. The chapter ends by giving community a positive role in individual development.

Keep this idea

Ethical individualism concerns the source and content of action, together with life among other individuals.

Try it

Include the other person’s wishes, possible mixed motives and the limits of self-report.

Check your understanding

Does a new deed prove freedom?

Show a suggested answer

No; novelty does not identify its determining ground.

Does taking advice necessarily remove freedom?

Show a suggested answer

No; examining reasons differs from obeying authority as such.

Does ethical individualism mean every impulse is good?

Show a suggested answer

No; the chapter distinguishes intuition from blind impulses and good from bad application.

How to assess your response

Check that you explained the distinction, gave reasons and used the assigned passage. Separate the example, Steiner’s argument and your assessment. Revisit what remains unclear. Well-supported disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement or reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
