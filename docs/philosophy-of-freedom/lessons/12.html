2 · Work through the explanation

A worked example

You offer to prepare a summary for someone who missed a meeting. The intended summary, understood as a way to enable participation, is the conceptual content guiding your action: its motive. A habit of helping, concern or desire for appreciation might make this aim attractive to you. Steiner calls the relatively lasting configuration of feelings and mental pictures the characterological disposition; from it arise driving forces. Having the notes and time to write explains practical feasibility, a separate issue.

Understand the idea

Steiner distinguishes immediate responses, feeling, practical experience and pure thinking in his discussion of driving forces. He also distinguishes aims centered on personal happiness, obedience to authority, and moral insight. These are two related analyses, not a matching game with one motive permanently attached to each kind of person.

Feeling pleased now differs from picturing future praise as the goal of the action. Neither is identical with grasping an ethical reason. At the level Steiner calls free, the ethical idea itself moves the person: motive and driving force coincide. The idea does not have to be newly invented. Understanding the distinction is preparation for examining that claim, not proof that our example fulfills it.

Keep this idea

Distinguish the aim, its motivating ground and the means of execution before discussing freedom.

Reading: Basis 75–79, then 80–82. Start by explaining the spiritual characterization of thinking at 75–77 as Steiner’s argument; identify which earlier account of observation it relies on.

Check your understanding

Are the notes a driving force?

Show a suggested answer

They are a practical resource; their availability alone does not explain why the aim moves me.

Is a feeling of concern the same as an imagined future reward?

Show a suggested answer

No: distinguish a present feeling from the pictured result.

Must a free idea be new?

Show a suggested answer

No; Basis 79 makes prior possession irrelevant. Ask what determines the action.

How to assess your response

Check that you explained the distinction, gave reasons and used the assigned passage. Separate the example, Steiner’s argument and your assessment. Revisit what remains unclear. Well-supported disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement or reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
