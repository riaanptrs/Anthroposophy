Concepts you need to follow the argument

Read a definition, distinguish its nearest neighbor and test it in a case. These are working explanations for this book, not substitutes for following its argument.

Percept
Content encountered in observation, including observed inner experience; distinct from observing and later remembering.
Perception
The activity of encountering perceptual content, not already a theory of its ultimate nature.
Concept
Intelligible content or relation grasped in thinking; for Steiner it contributes to reality’s connection.
Thinking
Activity of producing and understanding conceptual connections, distinct from memorized verbal results.
Intuition
Experienced grasp of ideal content through thinking, not an unexamined hunch or intense feeling.
Mental picture / representation
Wilson’s mental picture and Amrine’s representation often translate Vorstellung: a concept individualized through a perceptual encounter. Context can concern activity, capacity or result.
Feeling
The individual’s experienced relation to content; its occurrence does not establish a complete explanation.
Motive
Conceptual content directing the deed: what to realize and the reason under which one acts.
Driving force
The motivating aspect of disposition through which a motive becomes effective; distinct from means or obstacles.
Characterological disposition
Developed habits, feelings, experiences and capacities relevant to how motives move someone.
Ethical intuition
Individually understood ideal content capable of becoming a motive; claiming it does not establish infallibility.
Ethical individualism
Free action from individually understood ideal content; not approval of every private impulse.
Love of the deed
Embracing the deed through its understood content, beyond mere submission to external command.
Moral imagination
Forming a concrete possibility for realizing an ethical ideal in a particular situation.
Moral technique
Knowledge and skill for competently changing the existing world to realize the imagined deed.
Monism
Unity known through percept and concept, with individual participation in ideal content; not simply physical materialism.
Dualism
Fundamental separation of self and world, or appearance and inaccessible reality, whose connection needs explanation.
Naive realism
Taking the initially perceived object as complete reality without explaining thinking’s contribution.
Metaphysical realism
Locating explanatory reality behind appearances; Steiner challenges claims about a beyond made inaccessible in principle.
Purpose
An anticipated result whose present idea directs action; regularity or usefulness alone does not establish this.
Genus / generic character
Features of a kind or group; insufficient to exhaust the individual’s particular ideas and purposes.
Freedom
Action from individually grasped ideal content; distinguish permission, mere choice and successful execution.

Three distinctions to keep beside you

QuestionDistinction
What contributes to knowing?Percept: encountered content. Concept: intelligible relation. Representation: individualized concept linked with experience.
How does an ethical idea become a deed?Intuition: ideal content. Imagination: concrete possibility. Technique: competent realization.
What do we mean by free?Permission, awareness of wanting, the origin of a motive and successful execution are different questions.
Understand the idea. Explain the relationship. Apply what you learned.
