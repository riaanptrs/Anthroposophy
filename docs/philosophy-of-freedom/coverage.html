The whole-book coverage map

16 chapter stages, 36 detailed studies and six optional practices. The full explanations below are also embedded in the existing chapter lessons. You can study a chapter in several sittings or open one focused study at a time.

The two new files have different roles: Wilson (2012) supplies the full book in 173 captures; Amrine’s The Essential Philosophy of Freedom: Third Expanded Edition (2022) supplies an abridgment in 84 captures with six editorial appendices. The abridgment helps compare wording but cannot replace omitted arguments. Earlier course excerpts and notes retain locators from a different Wilson capture with 166 positions. Do not use those numbers in the new 173-capture file.

The 1918 material is part of the course

Read the additions in Chapters 3, 5, 7–13 and the conclusion alongside their arguments, rather than as optional slogans. Chapter 10 has two additions. The note on women belongs with Chapter 14 (Wilson capture 166). The final two studies teach the separate appendix on other minds and Hartmann’s objections (157–161), which Amrine’s abridgment does not include.

What the editorial material adds

Wilson’s terminology notes guide the glossary. Amrine’s representation appendix informs the comparison of mental picture and representation. The Kant and Hartmann appendices help identify historical positions; Darwin and Haeckel supply context for Chapter 12. Wannamaker’s commentary and later Steiner passages remain contextual voices, not new premises silently added to the book. See the sources page for every supporting section.
