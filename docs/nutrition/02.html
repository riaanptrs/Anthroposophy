Lesson 2 Food sources and dietary variety

Printable full unitDownload the lesson plans

Duration: 50 minutes.

Learning objectives: Identify food sources and edible plant parts; trace a food journey; explain why variety helps meet nutrient needs.

Success criteria: Correctly sort at least four plant foods and explain that different foods contribute different nutrients.

Vocabulary: Root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit, seed, variety.

Materials: Plant diagram, food cards and paper.

Teacher explanation

Foods may come from plants, animals or fungi. Meals often combine several sources. For plants, we eat different parts.

Plant partExamples
RootsCarrot and beetroot
StemsAsparagus and potato stem tubers
LeavesLettuce and cabbage
FlowersBroccoli and cauliflower
FruitsTomato, pumpkin and apple
SeedsRice, beans and peas

Explain that growing underground does not automatically make something a root: a potato is a stem tuber. Botanical categories and everyday cooking categories differ. A tomato is a botanical fruit but is commonly treated as a vegetable in cooking.

Different foods provide different combinations of nutrients. Variety over meals and days helps meet our needs. Colour can encourage variety among fruits and vegetables, but colour alone does not establish nutritional value. Familiar, affordable local foods can provide variety.

Teaching sequence

  1. Retrieval — 5 minutes: Ask learners to name the three purposes of food from Lesson 1.
  2. Explicit teaching — 10 minutes: Use the plant diagram to show edible parts. Introduce the potato and tomato examples.
  3. Sorting — 15 minutes: Groups sort cards by plant part; place animal foods and fungi in separate source groups. Ask learners to explain uncertain choices.
  4. Food journey — 15 minutes: Each learner illustrates one food’s journey through growing or rearing, harvesting, transport, preparation and eating. Discuss who contributes along the way.
  5. Exit assessment — 5 minutes: Ask, “Why is a variety of foods useful?” and “Is every underground food a root?”

Questions and answer guidance

  • Why is variety useful? Different foods contribute different nutrients.
  • Is a potato a root? No. It is an underground stem tuber.
  • Must variety involve expensive imported food? No. Local grains, pulses, vegetables and other available foods can support variety.

Common misconception: A single food can reliably provide everything the body needs. Explain that mixed eating patterns help meet different needs.

Adaptation: Reduce the sorting set for younger learners. For an extension, trace how storage or transport changes a food journey.
