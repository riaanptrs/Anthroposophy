Understand the concepts

A chain rather than an isolated effect

Capture 105 begins with nicotine and circulation, then gives the familiar figures of 72 pulse beats and 18 breaths per minute. Steiner treats their four-to-one ratio as an expression of bodily regulation. Nicotine is said to accelerate circulation and heart activity. The aim is to connect a substance to a relationship among processes, rather than treat a single organ as the entire explanation.

Where the mechanism must be checked

Capture 106 imagines pulse increasing without a matching change in breathing, then derives oxygen disturbance, anxiety and damage to organs. This is a causal chain with several separate links. The stated averages are not fixed physiological laws. The accompanying claim that the heart is not a pump contradicts present cardiovascular physiology. A reader should identify both the intended whole-system account and the points where its mechanism fails.

Poison, remedy and judgement

Captures 105–107 revisit the claim that a poison may be a remedy under some circumstances. Steiner even proposes therapeutic exceptions involving nicotine and interprets smoking through capacity for judgement. These assertions must be attributed rather than converted into advice. The editor’s opening summary is another voice in the chapter; its sympathetic framing does not validate a treatment claim.

Why bone cells enter at the end

Captures 108–109 turn to bone-building and bone-resorbing activities, in connection with work and inward engagement. The larger idea is that construction and breakdown can both be necessary: not every destructive process is simply an enemy of life. This qualifies the earlier image of opposing decay. Ask which breakdown participates in renewal and which damages organization; a whole-system description must preserve that distinction.

Historical interpretation and present knowledge

The heart pumps blood, and normal resting pulse varies rather than being fixed at 72. CDC describes smoking as harmful to many organs. The historical therapeutic nicotine suggestion is not a reason to smoke or use tobacco as treatment.

Check your understanding

These questions check what the text says and how its argument works. Understanding a claim does not require agreeing with it. Choose an answer, then compare the explanation. You can also open answers without JavaScript.

What role do the 72 and 18 figures play in Steiner’s argument?

Answer and explanation

Answer: They illustrate the proposed coordination of pulse and breathing.

The ratio is used to picture coordination; it is not a fixed rule of human physiology.

Which claim conflicts with current cardiovascular physiology?

Answer and explanation

Answer: That the heart is not a pump.

The heart’s contractions pump blood; reconstructing the source claim does not require accepting it.

Why include bone-building and bone-resorbing activities?

Answer and explanation

Answer: To show that constructive and breakdown processes can jointly support life.

The closing example concerns organized renewal, not indiscriminate approval of damage.
