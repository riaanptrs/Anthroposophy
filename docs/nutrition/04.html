Lesson 4 Digestion and absorption

Printable full unitDownload the lesson plans

Duration: 50 minutes.

Learning objectives: Trace food through the digestive tract; describe each main organ’s role; distinguish digestion from absorption.

Success criteria: Label the route in order and explain that most nutrient absorption takes place in the small intestine.

Vocabulary: Digestion, enzyme, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, absorption, large intestine.

Materials: Digestive system diagram, paper chains or connecting beads and pencils.

Teacher explanation

Digestion breaks food down into smaller substances. Absorption moves digested nutrients across the intestinal wall into circulation.

PartMain role
MouthTeeth break food into smaller pieces; saliva moistens food and an enzyme begins starch digestion
OesophagusMuscular movements push swallowed food toward the stomach
StomachChurns food; acid and enzymes help digest protein
Small intestineCompletes much digestion and absorbs most nutrients
Large intestineAbsorbs more water; microbes act on some remaining material; stool forms
Rectum and anusStore and release stool

The pancreas supplies digestive enzymes to the small intestine. The liver makes bile, and the gallbladder stores it. Bile helps disperse fats so enzymes can act on them; bile is not an enzyme.

Digestible carbohydrates become simple sugars; proteins become amino acids and small peptides; fats become smaller components that can be absorbed. Much fibre remains undigested by our enzymes, although gut microbes can act on some types.

Teaching sequence

  1. Prediction — 5 minutes: Learners predict the route taken by a bite of food.
  2. Explicit teaching — 15 minutes: Walk through the diagram in order. Identify digestion and absorption separately.
  3. Protein model — 10 minutes: A paper chain represents protein. Separate its links to represent amino acids. Explain that absorbed amino acids can be used to build the body’s proteins. State that this is a simplified model.
  4. Diagram task — 15 minutes: Learners draw arrows, label organs and add a short function for each. Include the pancreas and liver as helping organs.
  5. Exit assessment — 5 minutes: Ask where most nutrient absorption happens and how digestion differs from absorption.

Questions and answer guidance

  • Does the stomach do all digestion? No. Digestion also occurs in the mouth and small intestine.
  • What is absorption? Movement of nutrients across the intestinal wall into circulation.
  • Where does most absorption happen? The small intestine.
  • Does food pass through the liver? No. The liver assists digestion by producing bile; it is not part of the food’s route through the tract.

Adaptation: Give a partly labelled diagram. For an extension, investigate how the small intestine’s folds and villi increase its absorbing surface.
