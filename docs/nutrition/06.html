Lesson 6 Planning a practical nourishing meal

Printable full unitDownload the lesson plans

Duration: 50 minutes.

Learning objectives: Plan a varied meal; explain nutritional contributions; adapt the plan to a realistic constraint.

Success criteria: Explain at least three nutrient contributions, address the assigned constraint and identify a relevant safety practice.

Vocabulary: Variety, meal pattern, substitution, affordability, dietary need.

Materials: Food cards, meal planning worksheet and optional local prices.

Teacher explanation

A nourishing eating pattern combines variety, enough food, enjoyment and practicality. One meal does not need to provide every nutrient perfectly. Patterns across meals and days matter, and individual needs vary.

A flexible meal starting point includes vegetables or fruit, a grain or starchy food, a protein source, some fat from ingredients or preparation, and water as an everyday drink. Include calcium rich foods such as dairy or suitable fortified alternatives across the day.

A meal of rice, beans, vegetables and a little oil gives a concrete example: rice mainly contributes carbohydrates; beans supply protein, fibre and other nutrients; vegetables add fibre, vitamins and minerals; oil supplies fat. A mixed dish can include these components without placing them in separate sections.

Teaching sequence

  1. Retrieval — 5 minutes: Review nutrient functions and the main site of absorption.
  2. Worked example — 10 minutes: Explain the example meal. Model a substitution, such as replacing rice with another available grain or starchy food.
  3. Meal design — 20 minutes: Groups plan a meal and complete the worksheet below. Assign one constraint: a limited budget, vegetarian eating, a specified food allergy, or limited cooking equipment.
  4. Sharing — 10 minutes: Each group explains its food choices, constraint and safety practice.
  5. Reflection — 5 minutes: Learners explain one thing they understand now that they did not understand before.

Student worksheet

  • What is your meal and who is it for?
  • Which ingredients provide carbohydrates, protein, fats and fibre?
  • What contributes vitamins or minerals?
  • What drink will accompany it?
  • What constraint were you given, and how did you address it?
  • What preparation or food safety practice matters?
  • What could you substitute if an ingredient were unavailable?

For allergy scenarios, specify the allergen and require checking ingredient information and avoiding cross contact. Do not ask learners to determine another person’s medical needs.

Assessment rubric

Award 0, 1 or 2 points in each area: 0 means missing or inaccurate; 1 means partly demonstrated; 2 means clearly demonstrated.

AreaEvidence for 2 points
Nutrition explanationCorrectly explains at least three nutrient contributions
VarietyIncludes several foods with different nutritional contributions
Practical constraintProvides a workable response to the assigned constraint
Preparation and safetyIdentifies a relevant practice and explains its purpose

Maximum: 8 points. Use the rubric to guide feedback and revision, not to rate learners’ personal diets.

Adaptation: Offer a small set of food cards and oral presentation. For an extension, compare two meal plans using local prices and discuss tradeoffs.
