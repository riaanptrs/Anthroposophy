Teacher lesson plan · English · ages 10 to 14 assumed

Lesson 1 Why the body needs food

Printable full unitDownload the lesson plans

Duration: 50 minutes.

Learning objectives: Explain three reasons the body needs food; distinguish food from nutrients; describe one cultural or social role of food.

Success criteria: The learner can explain energy, growth and repair, and normal body functions, and give an example of a food and a nutrient.

Vocabulary: Food, nutrient, energy, growth, repair.

Materials: Food pictures or optional samples, paper and pencils.

Teacher explanation

Food supplies energy and materials the body needs. Energy is needed for moving, thinking, breathing, pumping blood and maintaining body temperature. The body continues using energy during sleep.

Materials from food support growth and repair. The body replaces and repairs tissues throughout life, so this need does not end when someone stops growing. Nutrients also support normal functions, including nerves, bones and the immune system.

A food is something we eat, such as beans. A nutrient is a substance in food that the body uses, such as protein. Most foods contain several nutrients. Food also has social meaning: people share meals, celebrate, follow traditions and enjoy flavours. Choices depend on culture, cost, availability, preference and dietary needs.

Teaching sequence

  1. Opening question — 5 minutes: Ask, “Does your body use energy while you sleep?” Learners explain their predictions. Return to their ideas during teaching.
  2. Explicit teaching — 10 minutes: Explain the three purposes of food. Ask learners to connect each purpose with an example: running, healing a cut, or keeping nerves working.
  3. Observation — 15 minutes: Learners observe and draw two foods. Prompt them to record colour, shape and texture. Smelling is optional and tasting is unnecessary.
  4. Application — 15 minutes: Groups discuss a pictured meal. Ask, “What might this meal provide? Why might people choose it?” Distinguish an observation from a guess about nutritional content.
  5. Exit assessment — 5 minutes: Complete: “We need food even when resting because…” and “A food is… whereas a nutrient is…”

Questions and answer guidance

  • Does food support only movement? No. It also supports functions such as breathing and growth and repair.
  • Are beans and protein the same thing? No. Beans are a food containing protein and other nutrients.
  • Why can two families choose different meals? Culture, preference, access, cost and dietary needs may differ.

Common misconception: Food matters only when we exercise. Correct it using examples of continuous body functions.

Adaptation: Offer picture prompts and sentence starters. For an extension, ask learners to explain why adults still need nutrients for tissue repair.
