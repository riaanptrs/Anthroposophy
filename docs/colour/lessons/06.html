GA 291 · Lesson 06 · 1920-12-05

Light, thought, darkness and will

Understand the two temporal directions in Steiner’s account.

Start with a situation

A finished sketch records decisions already made. Your intention to revise it is not yet visible in the new work.

This offers a limited analogy for a formed past and an emerging future. The intention is not physically dark, and the finished sketch does not prove Steiner’s cosmic account.

The reading’s key idea

In the lecture of 5 December 1920, Steiner treats thought and will as complementary aspects of a developing world. He connects thought with light and a past that has become manifest, and will with darkness and a future still coming into being. Thought alone and will alone give partial pictures. The polarity is developmental, not a simple opposition of good light and evil darkness.

“Light” expands here beyond what a lamp emits: it names a spiritual quality of manifestation, including the inward aspect of sensory experience. Steiner describes thought seen from outside as light and light experienced inwardly as thought. This is his spiritual account, not a measurement of photons emitted by thinking. Keeping the context clear also helps when other courses use colour words for supersensible experiences.

Keep this idea

In this lecture, light and darkness express directions of spiritual becoming, not a ranking of persons or colours.

Words for the reading

Thought–light: manifestation associated with the past. Will–darkness: potential associated with the future. These are relations inside the lecture’s spiritual framework.

Work with the idea

Divide a page into “already formed” and “still becoming.” Record a completed artistic decision and a possible next action. Then explain the lecture’s light–thought and darkness–will relations in two separate sentences.

Check your understanding

Why would a device measuring room brightness not test the whole claim?

Show a suggested answer

The lecture uses light in an expanded spiritual sense. A brightness measurement concerns physical illumination and does not by itself establish or refute that wider interpretation.

How does the example help you understand the reading?

Show a suggested answer

This offers a limited analogy for a formed past and an emerging future. The intention is not physically dark, and the finished sketch does not prove Steiner’s cosmic account.

What would a well-supported response to the activity include?

Show a suggested answer

The completed decision illustrates something formed; the intended revision illustrates something still to become. Explicitly limit the analogy: neither entry is a measurement of spiritual light or darkness.

How to assess your response

Check four things: correct attribution, a clear distinction, a reason supported by the reading and recognition of the example’s limits. Revisit anything vague. Reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement and reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
