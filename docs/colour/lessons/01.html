Keeping size and ground constant makes the comparison clearer. If your experience differs from Steiner’s, record the difference precisely. The study is still useful.

The reading’s key idea

Steiner begins with a green field containing red, peach-blossom or blue. The surrounding green is physically the same in the comparison, but he describes different felt relationships: animation with red, a more balanced relation with peach-blossom, and withdrawal with blue. The important first move is to study relationships instead of treating each colour as an isolated label.

He then calls green the lifeless image of the living, peach-blossom the living image of the soul, white or light the soul’s image of spirit, and black the spiritual image of the lifeless. “Image” means that one level presents another without becoming identical to it. Painted green can picture life without itself being a living plant. This analogy helps explain the vocabulary; it does not establish the whole spiritual classification. Peach-blossom is an artistic term here, not a universal human skin colour or a standard of health, worth or identity.

Check your understanding

What does the green example clarify about “image”?

Show a suggested answer

A green painting can present the appearance of a living plant while remaining nonliving material. Steiner extends this relation into a spiritual classification, which needs to be assessed separately.

What would a well-supported response to the activity include?

Show a suggested answer

A useful entry names the changed central colour and a specific effect on the green field. It then explains that the painted image is not itself a living plant. Agreement with every proposed feeling is unnecessary.

How to assess your response

Check four things: correct attribution, a clear distinction, a reason supported by the reading and recognition of the example’s limits. Revisit anything vague. Reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement and reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
