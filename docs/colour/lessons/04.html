GA 291 · Lesson 04 · 1914-07-26

Colour as movement and a shared artistic language

Explore how fixed shapes can suggest approach and withdrawal.

Start with a situation

Two circles occupy the same positions in two compositions. In the first, the left circle is red and the right blue; in the second, their colours exchange places.

Nothing geometrical has moved, yet the direction of attention may change. This is a useful way to understand the lecture’s artistic language before assessing its wider claims.

The reading’s key idea

The 1914 lecture places colour within the creation of the first Goetheanum and the problem of renewing a shared artistic culture. Steiner wants art to express a living relation to the world, rather than rely only on inherited symbols or imitation. His examples ask us to experience colour as active: red approaches, blue withdraws, and their relations suggest movement.

This movement does not require an object to change position. It is a quality of the experience of an arrangement. Steiner extends this into spiritual accounts of animal colouring and human embodiment. Those extensions are not established by noticing a difference between two painted circles. His historical generalizations about complexion are not a basis for classifying people or prescribing how their skin should be painted.

Keep this idea

Colour can change the felt movement of a composition while its geometry remains fixed.

Words for the reading

Approach and withdrawal: descriptions of experienced colour relations. Goetheanum: the building project that provides this lecture’s artistic setting.

Work with the idea

Make the two compositions. Draw an arrow in your notebook showing where your attention goes first, or write “no clear direction.” Ask someone else for a description before showing yours. Identify one agreement and one difference.

Check your understanding

What must remain constant to make the comparison useful?

Show a suggested answer

Keep shapes, sizes, positions and background the same, changing only the two colours’ positions. Otherwise several changes could explain the different impression.

How does the example help you understand the reading?

Show a suggested answer

Nothing geometrical has moved, yet the direction of attention may change. This is a useful way to understand the lecture’s artistic language before assessing its wider claims.

What would a well-supported response to the activity include?

Show a suggested answer

State what stayed constant and compare the direction of attention in the two versions. A second observer’s different response is useful information, not a failed exercise.

How to assess your response

Check four things: correct attribution, a clear distinction, a reason supported by the reading and recognition of the example’s limits. Revisit anything vague. Reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement and reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
