GA 291 · Lesson 12 · 1924-01-04

The rainbow within a spiritual cosmology

Reconstruct the sequence of hierarchies, elements and colour.

Start with a situation

A reader summarizes the sequence as warmth, light, colour and living form. Another labels it a recipe for making water from coloured air.

The first preserves the structure of the spiritual narrative. The second changes its terms into a laboratory procedure and misrepresents the kind of account being offered.

The reading’s key idea

The final lecture describes a way of thinking Steiner attributes to medieval spiritual knowledge. The First Hierarchy manifests through warmth; the Second through light, with air as its shadow; the Third mediates light and darkness, bringing colour and its watery reflection. Humanity appears as a Fourth Hierarchy associated with life and the forming of solid contours. These are stages in his spiritual account, not the modern chemical elements or a dated history of the physical planets.

Within this picture the rainbow becomes an activity of spiritual beings, with emergence and disappearance across its colours. Steiner’s aim is to restore beings and activity behind what he regards as abstract descriptions of nature. Learning the sequence makes his argument intelligible. It does not require claiming to see beings in a rainbow or treating his reconstruction as an independently established account of what all medieval thinkers believed.

Words for the reading

Hierarchy: an order of spiritual beings in this account. Saturn, Sun and Moon: names for earlier cosmic stages here, not simply today’s celestial bodies. Fourth Hierarchy: humanity in the lecture’s account of original human powers.

Work with the idea

Draw four boxes for the four stages. Under each, name the associated activity and hierarchy. Then write two questions: one about what the source means, another about what evidence would be needed to accept its claims.

Check your understanding

What role does the Third Hierarchy have in the sequence?

Show a suggested answer

It mediates light and darkness, bringing forth colour; water is described as colour’s reflection or creation. This is part of the spiritual cosmology, not a chemical production process.

How does the example help you understand the reading?

Show a suggested answer

The first preserves the structure of the spiritual narrative. The second changes its terms into a laboratory procedure and misrepresents the kind of account being offered.

What would a well-supported response to the activity include?

Show a suggested answer

A complete map preserves the order warmth, light, colour and life/form. One question can clarify “shadow”; another can ask what supports the account’s historical or spiritual claims.

How to assess your response

Check four things: correct attribution, a clear distinction, a reason supported by the reading and recognition of the example’s limits. Revisit anything vague. Reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement and reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
