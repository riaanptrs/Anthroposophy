GA 291 · Lesson 03 · 1921-05-08

Letting form emerge from colour

Turn the image–lustre distinction into a painting process.

Start with a situation

In one study you draw a leaf and fill it green. In another, you let two green washes meet and discover a leaf-like form in the boundary.

The second process allows an unexpected edge or interval to influence the image. It need not produce a more accurate botanical illustration; its aim is to make the colour relationship participate in forming the work.

The reading’s key idea

The third lecture asks how colour becomes attached to material things. Steiner relates mineral, plant, animal and human appearance to different combinations of image and lustre. He interprets plant green through Moon influences and flower colours through Sun influences within his account of cosmic evolution. These are spiritual explanations within that account, not established explanations of plant pigments.

The practical artistic direction is easier to try: begin with moving, interacting colour and allow a form to emerge. Steiner contrasts fluid colour with starting from an outline and filling it in. He also proposes different treatments of plants, animals and minerals through light, dark and coloured veils. These are ways to explore expression, not a prohibition on drawing, palettes or other media. The question is whether colour contributes to the form’s life rather than merely decorating a shape already decided.

Keep this idea

A form can be discovered through colour relationships instead of being fully decided beforehand.

Words for the reading

Veil: a layer modifying what lies beneath. Metamorphosis: transformation. Cosmic evolution: the spiritual framework used here, not a synonym for botanical development.

Work with the idea

Make the two studies in ten minutes. Name one decision made before colouring and one decision discovered during colouring. If using pencils, build overlapping areas before outlining. Explain which process suited your intention and why.

Check your understanding

Does painting out of colour mean avoiding all form?

Show a suggested answer

No. Form develops through the colour process. The alternative is to decide the complete outline first and use colour only to fill it.

How does the example help you understand the reading?

Show a suggested answer

The second process allows an unexpected edge or interval to influence the image. It need not produce a more accurate botanical illustration; its aim is to make the colour relationship participate in forming the work.

What would a well-supported response to the activity include?

Show a suggested answer

“I planned the leaf outline first, but discovered a curved edge where the washes met” identifies two different processes. Explain which served your aim rather than declaring that one medium always wins.

How to assess your response

Check four things: correct attribution, a clear distinction, a reason supported by the reading and recognition of the example’s limits. Revisit anything vague. Reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement and reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
