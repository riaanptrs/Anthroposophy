2 · Work through the explanation

A worked example

One learner finds a blue study quiet; another finds it lonely. The painter changes the neighbouring colour and both descriptions shift.

Feeling can guide artistic choices while remaining open to revision. A report of feeling and a judgment of someone’s moral character answer different questions.

The reading’s key idea

The 1915 lecture pairs reverence with inner energy: openness needs the strength to remain actively present. Steiner then describes an immersive sequence of colour experiences, from red through orange, yellow and green to blue, giving each a spiritual and moral character. He also considers musical intervals as lived experiences rather than bare numerical relations.

The central challenge is to notice qualitative differences without reducing art to a dictionary in which one colour always means one virtue. His account associates red with an overwhelming encounter and blue with devotion; that does not make a red object wicked or a person who likes blue virtuous. The lecture’s anecdote about an advocate also shows how learning more about an event changes judgment. Its claims about fainting are not an exercise to reproduce.

Keep this idea

Attend to the quality of a feeling without using a colour as a moral verdict.

Words for the reading

Reverence: receptive respect. Inner energy: active self-possession. Interval: relation between musical pitches; this lecture does not supply a scientific frequency–colour conversion.

Check your understanding

Why is “blue means a good person” an invalid conclusion?

Show a suggested answer

It turns a described spiritual or artistic experience into a judgment about a person. Neither a colour preference nor a painted surface establishes moral character.

How does the example help you understand the reading?

Show a suggested answer

Feeling can guide artistic choices while remaining open to revision. A report of feeling and a judgment of someone’s moral character answer different questions.

What would a well-supported response to the activity include?

Show a suggested answer

“This blue beside grey feels lonely to me” locates the response in a particular relation and person. It does not assign loneliness to every blue object or moral worth to its owner.

How to assess your response

Check four things: correct attribution, a clear distinction, a reason supported by the reading and recognition of the example’s limits. Revisit anything vague. Reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement and reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
