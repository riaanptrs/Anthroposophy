Explore the chapter’s core ideas

The reading’s key idea

Begin by reconstructing the core distinction. In Steiner's classification, green is the lifeless image of the living, peach-blossom the living image of the soul, white/light the soul's image of spirit, and black the spiritual image of the lifeless. An image presents one form of existence in another, while lustre expresses an inner quality shining outward: yellow is the lustre of spirit, blue of soul and red of the living. Explain these relations using the green and shadow/illuminant discussions, rather than reciting seven names. Then compare two supplementary lectures from Lectures 4–12: state each argument, its reasons and a source example, with capture references. The comparison supplies the basis for a considered original work.

A useful pair is Lecture 9 and Lecture 11. Lecture 9 asks how painting's flat surface can regain depth through advancing yellow/red and receding blue/purple, extending the discussion into spiritual time-perspective. Lecture 11 argues from consciousness and free sense qualities toward qualitative measure and an icon–Madonna history, ending in conscious painting out of colour. Both give colour an active role beyond naturalistic imitation, but one develops perspective and the other weight, consciousness and historical renewal. Preserve these differences and attribute their spiritual explanations. A portfolio can explore a colour decision informed by that comparison; it does not supply proof of either cosmology. Other pairs are welcome when their actual arguments are reconstructed with equal care.

Build a synthesis from textual understanding

Reconstruct the core relations

The first task is an explanation of image and lustre. Steiner describes an image as one form of existence taken up in another, and develops the distinction through green, shadow and illuminant. His four formulas link green with the living, peach-blossom with soul, white/light with spirit and black with the lifeless, while distinguishing what makes the image from what it images. Lustre instead expresses an inner quality shining outward: yellow/spirit, blue/soul, red/the living. Explain each complete relation. Peach-blossom remains the historical term of the classification; it supplies no standard for actual people's complexion or value. A seven-name colour chart without these relations is incomplete.

Core Lectures 1–2, captures 22, 24, 27, 29 and 31.

Compare two supplementary arguments

Choose two of Lectures 4–12. For each, identify the claim, the reasons by which Steiner reaches it and a concrete example, all with capture references. Then name a shared aim and a difference. For instance, Lecture 9 argues from painting's flat surface to colour perspective: yellow/red advance while blue/purple recede, and spiritual time-perspective supplies a wider account. Lecture 11 argues from consciousness and free sense qualities through qualitative measure and the icon–Madonna history to renewed painting out of colour. Both make colour active beyond imitation. One develops depth and perspective; the other develops weight, consciousness and historical renewal. Treating both as “warm colours feel closer” would lose their arguments.

Worked pair: Lecture 9, captures 102–111, especially 104–106; Lecture 11, captures 123–135, especially 125–128 and 132–134.

Locate the extensions of the argument

Comparison should also name a step that goes beyond the artistic example. A depth impression does not by itself establish the spiritual history of mineral colour in Lecture 9. An icon/Madonna comparison does not independently establish the account of ego and astral body during sleep in Lecture 11. These wider claims belong to Steiner's explanations and must remain visible, with attribution. Explain why he introduces them and how they support his intended conclusion; only then assess their support. Editorial matter can clarify the reading: note 54 identifies painted performance programmes as Lecture 11's practical example. Precise reference lets a reader check the source rather than accept a general summary.

The chosen supplementary passages; note 54, capture 151.

Make an original work answer to the reading

The portfolio follows the reconstruction and comparison. Show an initial original study, a revision and a commentary explaining how the compared ideas informed a particular decision. Describe the actual arrangement changed and the effect you experienced, including an unexpected or unclear result. A written plan or spoken explanation can do the same intellectual work. The final submission therefore has three parts: seven-colour textual map, comparison of two supplementary arguments, and documented original process. The last part does not replace the first two. Its purpose is to show that a source distinction can guide a decision without becoming a formula that predetermines every picture or a proof of the source's cosmology.

How could Lectures 9 and 11 be compared without collapsing their arguments?

Show a suggested answer

Both seek an active role for colour beyond naturalistic imitation. Lecture 9 begins with painting's flat surface and colour perspective, then extends into spiritual time-perspective. Lecture 11 begins with consciousness and free sense qualities, develops qualitative measure and historical icon/Madonna contrasts, and calls for conscious painting out of colour. Their shared aim does not erase their different routes.

What must the final synthesis demonstrate before the portfolio?

Show a suggested answer

It must first explain all seven formulas and the image/lustre distinction, then reconstruct and compare TWO supplementary arguments with capture references. The original study, revision and commentary must show how that understanding informed a specific change; a list of colour meanings or a portfolio alone leaves the textual work incomplete.

How to assess your response

Check four things: correct attribution, a clear distinction, a reason supported by the reading and recognition of the example’s limits. Revisit anything vague. Reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement and reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
