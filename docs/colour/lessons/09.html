GA 291 · Lesson 09 · 1923-06-02

From linear perspective to colour perspective

Create a sense of depth through colour relationships.

Start with a situation

Two equal patches stand side by side without overlap. In a second version their colours exchange places. You consider whether either now seems nearer.

Because their sizes and positions remain fixed, any changed depth impression can be discussed in relation to the colour arrangement. The paper itself remains flat.

The reading’s key idea

The lecture of 2 June 1923 considers the different arts through their relationship with space. For painting, Steiner asks how a flat surface can offer depth through colour itself. He contrasts geometrical perspective with colour perspective: warm reds and yellows are described as advancing, while blues and violets recede.

The proposal is to give colour an organizing role instead of depending entirely on outlines, diminishing size and vanishing points. It does not require abandoning linear perspective. Hue, lightness, surrounding colours and the composition can work together, and no particular depth response is compulsory. The lecture’s wider spiritual accounts of minerals and dimensions should be distinguished from the practical comparison of painted surfaces.

Words for the reading

Linear perspective: geometrical construction of spatial appearance. Colour perspective: depth suggested by colour relations. Advance and recede describe a visual impression.

Work with the idea

Create two equal shapes on a neutral ground, one warm and one cool. Swap their colours in a second study. Then add overlap in a third version. Compare what colour contributes with what overlap contributes.

Check your understanding

What would be wrong with saying the blue patch has physically moved backward?

Show a suggested answer

Only the impression of depth has changed. Its position on the flat surface remains the same.

How does the example help you understand the reading?

Show a suggested answer

Because their sizes and positions remain fixed, any changed depth impression can be discussed in relation to the colour arrangement. The paper itself remains flat.

What would a well-supported response to the activity include?

Show a suggested answer

Describe the colour-only comparison first, then the version with overlap. This prevents attributing a depth change caused by overlap entirely to colour.

How to assess your response

Check four things: correct attribution, a clear distinction, a reason supported by the reading and recognition of the example’s limits. Revisit anything vague. Reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement and reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
