Rudolf Steiner · Lesson 06

Appearance: the source’s signs and its qualification

Explain the appearance argument, including its proportion qualification and its proposed causal link.

What qualification accompanies the source’s height descriptions?

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · The Mystery of Temperaments · PDF capture 17, middle column; printed folio not visible.

We can only compare the height of the man with his configuration. What is important is precisely this ratio.

Rudolf Steiner, The Mystery of Temperaments, supplied 33-capture English text. Translator, publisher, publication year, lecture date/place and GA volume unidentified. Selected wording checked against the native PDF; line breaks normalized.

What this passage means

This follows the explicit rejection of a simple rule that cholerics are short and sanguines tall. Configuration and proportion qualify the outward-form account; they do not independently establish that appearance reveals temperament.

The focus of this lesson

Explain the appearance argument, including its proportion qualification and its proposed causal link.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

The reading’s key idea

Steiner links choleric ego strength with constrained growth, firm gaze and a forceful step; sanguine astral mobility with flexible form, changing expression and a light gait; phlegmatic inner comfort with bodily fullness and shuffling; melancholic resistance with a lowered head and a heavy, measured step. He also proposes dark and blue eye correspondences. These are claims about how an inner constitution becomes outward form.

Capture 17 explicitly qualifies simple height rules: the relevant comparison is height in relation to configuration. That qualification belongs to the source’s account, but does not establish that appearance identifies temperament. The fragile-object scene lets us examine an observation and the additional explanation attached to it.

From an inner member to an outward sign

Choleric form as the work of a limiting ego

The appearance discussion follows the portraits rather than starting an independent catalogue of bodily signs. Steiner says the physical form follows the etheric, which follows the astral, while the ego limits and orders the formative forces. Choleric ego predominance is therefore said to restrain growth and produce a compact, forceful organisation. Fichte and Napoleon illustrate the claim. A firm gaze, dark eyes and a step that presses into the ground are further outward expressions of the same centre. State the proposed connection among member, formative activity and visible feature, rather than presenting those features as self-explanatory.

Sanguine mobility as visible elasticity

In the sanguine account, the astral body’s changing sensations and images become mobile form and gesture. Steiner describes a cheerful gaze that quickly moves to another object, an elastic figure, fine muscles and a light, bouncing gait. He also associates blue eyes with the astral body’s inner light, contrasting them with the choleric eye description. This is the lecture’s explanatory correspondence, not a separate observation proving its spiritual cause. The English upload repeats the eye-colour paragraph at capture 18; reading it once preserves the idea without mistaking repetition for several different arguments.

Uploaded PDF capture 17, columns 2–3, and capture 18, columns 1–2.

Inner comfort and bodily resistance in the other forms

Phlegmatic appearance is explained through the etheric body’s inward comfort: glandular activity and formative forces are linked to bodily fullness, an unhurried or shuffling step, a static expression and a gaze directed inward. The melancholic bodily instrument is described as resistant rather than comfortably managed. A lowered head, opaque gaze and a heavy, measured step are its proposed expressions. The source distinguishes that measured firmness from the choleric’s energetic step. Comparing these accounts shows how the same broad feature, such as a firm step, receives meaning from the wider relationship claimed, not from its name alone.

Uploaded PDF capture 18, column 3, and capture 19, columns 1–2.

The proportion qualification and the interpretation we add

Steiner explicitly says this does not mean the choleric is simply short and the sanguine tall: height must be compared with the person’s configuration. This qualification prevents a misleading account of his position. It also raises a distinct question about whether the proposed relation between form and inner life is justified. In the fictional walking scene, a slow gait stays observable after the fragile object supplies a new explanation. Use the scene to separate description from explanation. Accurate reading requires stating the source’s full correspondence and its limit before deciding what other information would support an inference about a person.

Uploaded PDF capture 17, column 2; compare the completion of the appearance discussion at capture 19, column 2.

Connect the parts of the reading

Why is the proportion passage essential to a fair account of the appearance argument?

An explained answer: It limits a simple short/tall classification: the lecture asks for height in relation to configuration. Including that limit makes the source account more accurate, while leaving the justification of its inner-to-outer causal link as a separate question.

Parallel reading · About the source

Words for the reading

Configuration: the relation among bodily features. Proportion: a feature considered in relation to the whole. Physiognomy: interpreting inner qualities from outward form. Inference: a conclusion from information.

Check your understanding

What qualification accompanies the source’s height descriptions?

Reveal the explanation

The source rejects a simple short-choleric/tall-sanguine rule and compares height with configuration. This is a qualification within its appearance argument, not evidence that appearance identifies temperament.

Original open question and answer

Does the source simply say cholerics are short and sanguines tall?

Show a suggested answer

No. It explicitly rejects that simple contrast and asks that height be compared with configuration.

How are choleric and sanguine gait distinguished in the lecture?

Show a suggested answer

The choleric step is described as forceful and impressing itself on the ground; the sanguine gait as light and bouncing, associated with astral mobility.

What changes when the walker explains the fragile object?

Show a suggested answer

The slow pace remains an observation, while the explanation changes. A visible movement and an inferred inner cause are distinct.

How to assess your response
