Temperament as reciprocal colouring

The focus of this lesson

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

The reading’s key idea

After introducing two streams, Steiner asks how qualities developed by individuality can live within inherited bodily conditions. Temperament is his answer: it stands between the hereditary line and the continuing individual core, giving their meeting a particular colouring. The streams adapt to each other rather than simply remaining side by side. Blue and yellow meeting in green illustrates that reciprocal relation. The source also describes temperament as balancing the eternal and the perishable: the individuality enduring through lives and the inherited constitution of a particular earthly existence.

The comparison is meant to clarify a relationship, not to introduce a third independent being. The source’s descriptions of family, people and race belong to its historical vocabulary of inherited conditions; the teaching diagram uses heredity and individuality. The music example illustrates interaction between an intention and an instrument’s possibilities. It does not explain the proposed spiritual process itself.

The meeting that acquires a colouring

Why mediation becomes necessary

The two streams have different sources in the author’s account. One brings inherited bodily conditions; the other brings qualities acquired by a spiritual individuality through earlier incarnations. Naming them leaves the question of their relationship unanswered. The individual enters a particular family and a physical constitution through which its capacities must appear. The text therefore asks how these qualities can be joined and brought into harmony. Temperament answers that relational question. It occupies the interval between what the person receives through ancestry and what, according to the source, the individual core brings into this life.

Supplied PDF capture 9, columns 1–2.

Reciprocal colouring

The source describes the meeting as reciprocal colouring. The hereditary conditions do not merely sit beside an unchanged individuality, and individuality does not express itself without conditions. Their encounter has a particular quality. The blue-and-yellow comparison presents a green result to make this combined expression intelligible. Treat the picture as an analogy of the proposed relationship: it helps explain what the author means by a meeting that has its own colouring. It does not establish the spiritual streams or provide a general theory of colour mixture. Return from the image to the two terms it connects.

Supplied PDF capture 9, column 3.

Eternal and perishable

The next capture describes temperament as balancing the eternal with the perishable. Within this passage, eternal refers to the individuality that persists through earthly lives; perishable refers to the inherited constitution of a particular existence. The words are parts of this spiritual explanation, not a judgement that bodily life is unimportant. The body supplies the conditions through which the inner qualities radiate outward. Temperament names how the continuing core is adapted to those conditions. This account prepares the four-member explanation, which will describe the interaction through more specific members of the same human being.

Supplied PDF capture 10, column 1.

Use the musical comparison carefully

In the course example, a musician chooses a phrase while working with an instrument’s possibilities. The performance gives intention and conditions a form of expression together. This helps describe the relation that temperament is assigned in the lecture. A musical intention is not a reincarnating individuality, and an instrument is not the complete hereditary constitution; those differences mark the comparison’s limit. Label the arrows in your diagram and explain their meeting in one sentence. Then return to the source: what would remain unexplained if temperament were treated only as an inherited trait or only as a freely chosen preference?

Parallel reading · About the source

Words for the reading

Mediation: the relationship joining the two streams. Reciprocal colouring: each affects their combined expression. Eternal: the enduring individuality in this account. Perishable: the conditions of a particular earthly existence.

Read → understand → explain → apply. The chapter explanation is open from the start.

See the idea in an example

A player chooses a musical phrase but must work with the possibilities of the instrument. The resulting performance involves both.

This is an analogy for a relationship between intention and conditions. It is not a model of how a spiritual individuality enters a body.
