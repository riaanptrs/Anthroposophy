Hermann Koepke · Lesson 10

The incarnation of the I: reading religious images

Identify the three motifs and their particular religious setting.

Start with a situation

A child asks where they came from. The caregiver first asks what prompted the question and responds to the actual concern before offering any family belief.

The question can open a conversation without surrendering factual honesty or requiring a particular theology.

The reading’s key idea

Koepke presents divine origin, life in relation to death, and art as three sources of renewed connection. He reads them through Father, Son and Holy Spirit. Genesis and an inverted plant image express origin; the legend of Christophorus enters the second discussion. The capture loses page 156, so the full development of that story cannot be recovered here.

These are Christian-anthroposophical interpretations, not neutral accounts of every child’s inner life. A question about parentage may concern real family history and deserves an honest answer appropriate to the child. Do not replace it automatically with a hidden spiritual question. Families may explore belonging through their own traditions or everyday stories.

Keep this idea

Understand a religious image in its context and leave room for the listener’s own beliefs.

Words for the reading

Incarnation: spiritual individuality entering earthly embodiment in this account. Motif: a recurring image or theme. Tradition: a particular inherited framework.

Work with the idea

Name the three motifs in the reading. Choose one and propose both a tradition-specific discussion and an alternative for learners who do not share that tradition.

Check your understanding

How to assess your response

Check four things: correct attribution, a clear distinction, a reason supported by the reading and recognition of the example’s limits. Revisit anything vague. Reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement and reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
