Hermann Koepke · Lesson 13

Half moon-nodes: a correspondence and its limits

Separate an astronomical period from a developmental explanation.

Start with a situation

Two classroom activities repeat at the same interval. Their matching calendars do not show that one causes the other.

The simple comparison isolates the missing step: a temporal correspondence needs independent evidence before it becomes a causal explanation.

The reading’s key idea

Koepke connects a reversal in the lunar nodes after approximately nine and one-third years with his account of the ninth-year transition. A node is a crossing of the Moon’s orbital plane with the ecliptic. NASA describes the nodes’ full precession cycle as approximately 18.6 years; half is about 9.3 years. This is a nodal cycle, not the monthly sequence of lunar phases.

The astronomical period and the proposed effect on childhood are separate claims. Similar timing alone does not establish a causal connection. Koepke reads the sky as an archetypal image of development; the course explains that interpretation without using birth dates or lunar positions to assess children.

Astronomical cross-check

NASA describes the nodes’ rotation over approximately 18.6 years. The childhood-development connection is an additional interpretation by Koepke. The Moon’s orbit — NASA.

Words for the reading

Node: intersection of orbital planes. Precession: slow change in orientation. Correspondence: a proposed relationship.

Work with the idea

Write the argument as three steps: astronomical period, proposed correspondence, evidence still needed. Name information that could challenge the proposed link.

Check your understanding

What does the NASA source verify here?

Show a suggested answer

The approximately 18.6-year nodal period. It does not verify Koepke’s proposed relationship to a child’s development.
