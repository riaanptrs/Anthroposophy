Hermann Koepke · Lesson 02

Monica: fear, privacy and continued connection

Separate a child’s expressed fear from an adult’s explanation of it.

Start with a situation

A child asks to read alone, then later asks a caregiver to stay nearby at bedtime. The adult agrees on a private reading period and an ordinary bedtime check-in.

Privacy and connection can coexist. The adult asks what is frightening rather than insisting that a particular spiritual explanation must be correct.

The reading’s key idea

Monica’s constructed story includes fear at night, unease before a mirror, questions about evil and death, and a wish to spend time alone. Koepke interprets thresholds and separation as images of the I entering more deeply into earthly life. The teacher himself compares his explanations to interpreting dreams and considers different possibilities.

The practical relationship changes: a parent can remain available while allowing privacy. The religious discussion gives this an image of love that releases possession. These interpretations should not be treated as predictions of death or as knowledge of a child’s hidden motive. Persistent distress that interferes with daily life warrants professional attention, not an age label.

Current care context

NIMH recommends contacting a health professional when emotional or behavioural difficulties last weeks or months and interfere with daily life. Child and adolescent mental health — NIMH.

Keep this idea

Make room for independence while keeping care accessible.

Words for the reading

Threshold: a boundary, literal or symbolic. Privacy: chosen personal space. Availability: being accessible for support.

Work with the idea

Write two responses to “I feel afraid when I am alone”: one that assumes a meaning, and one that listens. Add a way to notice whether the distress is disrupting daily life.

Check your understanding

Does the mirror scene establish a spiritual cause of fear?

Show a suggested answer

No. It belongs to the author’s interpretation of a constructed scene; the fear itself still deserves attention.

Which distinction does the example clarify?

Show a suggested answer

Privacy and connection can coexist. The adult asks what is frightening rather than insisting that a particular spiritual explanation must be correct.

What should guide your response to the activity?

Show a suggested answer

Make room for independence while keeping care accessible. Explain your choice with a detail from the example and state the limit of the conclusion.

How to assess your response

Check four things: correct attribution, a clear distinction, a reason supported by the reading and recognition of the example’s limits. Revisit anything vague. Reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement and reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
