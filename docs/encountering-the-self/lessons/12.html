Hermann Koepke · Lesson 12

The Sturdy Sapling: belonging through a story

Read the appendix’s images while leaving their meaning open to discussion.

Start with a situation

A learner draws a tree and writes “someone who listens”, “a familiar place” and “a task I can do” beside its roots.

The drawing translates belonging into identifiable supports. It need not claim that a real person will become immune to fear once rooted.

The reading’s key idea

The story follows a seed through frightening landscapes until it takes root and grows into a larch. Remembering its journey, the tree interprets earth, water and wind religiously and experiences belonging. The movement from being carried to being rooted gives a compact image of the book’s central theme.

The ending also explains hardship through divine will. A course discussion can identify that theology without telling a child that suffering is deserved, necessary or something they must endure silently. Our original activity emphasizes concrete supports and the possibility of seeking help; it is an adaptation, not a reconstruction of the story.

Keep this idea

A story can offer an image of belonging without explaining away hardship.

Words for the reading

Sapling: a young tree. Rootedness: an image of connection and support. Theological interpretation: meaning expressed through beliefs about God.

Work with the idea

Draw or describe a tree of supports for a fictional character. Include a new support the character can ask for and explain how your version differs from the source’s ending.

Check your understanding

What is changed in the course adaptation?

Show a suggested answer

It makes supports discussable and leaves room to seek help, rather than treating every hardship as something to accept because of divine will.

Which distinction does the example clarify?

Show a suggested answer

The drawing translates belonging into identifiable supports. It need not claim that a real person will become immune to fear once rooted.

What should guide your response to the activity?

Show a suggested answer

A story can offer an image of belonging without explaining away hardship. Explain your choice with a detail from the example and state the limit of the conclusion.

How to assess your response

Check four things: correct attribution, a clear distinction, a reason supported by the reading and recognition of the example’s limits. Revisit anything vague. Reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement and reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
