Hermann Koepke · Lesson 06

Seven and twelve: doing, perceiving and feeling

Compare the three strands of Koepke’s age comparison.

Start with a situation

A learner first discovers a curve with clay, then sketches a vessel and tries to make it. Later, they compare the result with the plan.

One person can use both directions of learning. The example clarifies the contrast without assigning capacities permanently by age.

The reading’s key idea

For the limbs, Koepke contrasts discovering a spiral by shaping wax with carving wood toward an imagined bowl. For the head, he contrasts imaginative listening with observing, recalling and explaining a colour experiment over successive days. For the trunk, he contrasts feelings shaped by surroundings with feelings experienced as arising inwardly. These strands prepare his later idea of reversal.

The direction of an activity is the useful comparison: learning through making and making from a plan. Koepke acknowledges that both ages also change themselves and their materials. His descriptions of head size, appearance, pulse and breath do not establish a reliable classification of a learner. The colour sequence is an educational proposal, not a ban on younger children asking scientific questions.

Exploration and purposeful making can enrich each other.

Words for the reading

Reversal: a proposed change in direction. Recollection: remembering an observation. Sympathy and antipathy: involvement and distance in the author’s vocabulary.

Work with the idea

Plan a task with three moments: explore materials, make a plan and compare the result. Add separate notes for what was seen, remembered and inferred.

Check your understanding

What changes between the wax and bowl examples?

Show a suggested answer

The emphasis shifts from encountering a form through activity to guiding activity with an anticipated form. Neither route belongs exclusively to one age.

Which distinction does the example clarify?

Show a suggested answer

One person can use both directions of learning. The example clarifies the contrast without assigning capacities permanently by age.

What should guide your response to the activity?

Show a suggested answer

Exploration and purposeful making can enrich each other. Explain your choice with a detail from the example and state the limit of the conclusion.

How to assess your response

Check four things: correct attribution, a clear distinction, a reason supported by the reading and recognition of the example’s limits. Revisit anything vague. Reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement and reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
