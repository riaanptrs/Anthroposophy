Hermann Koepke · Lesson 08

Curriculum: shelter, cultivation and expression

Turn the curriculum examples into adaptable learning experiences.

Start with a situation

A group builds a cardboard shelter for a toy. They discuss stability, an entrance, privacy and how another person could ask to come in.

A practical problem opens questions of belonging and boundaries. The adult does not need to tell children that the model proves a theory of the soul.

The reading’s key idea

House building gives Koepke an image of inner space; farming gives an image of something taking root. Poetry, storytelling and music offer ways to express experience. The later comparison returns to a central theme: an imaginative experience need not disappear when understanding becomes more reflective. Ideas should remain capable of growing with the learner.

The source connects instruments and musical intervals to particular developmental ages. Our course uses its broader educational aim without prescribing an instrument as treatment or claiming that younger children cannot experience major and minor. A model shelter, a container garden or spoken poem can make the practical themes accessible in different homes and schools.

Keep this idea

Choose activities that combine a real task with room for personal expression.

Work with the idea

Design one activity with a concrete aim, material choices, an accessible alternative and a question that permits more than one answer. State how you will revise it after observing the learners.

Check your understanding

What links building and cultivation in the chapter?

Show a suggested answer

Both offer practical images of becoming at home: forming a protected space and helping something take root and grow.

How to assess your response

Check four things: correct attribution, a clear distinction, a reason supported by the reading and recognition of the example’s limits. Revisit anything vague. Reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement and reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
