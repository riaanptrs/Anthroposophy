Hermann Koepke · Lesson 05

The second seven years: reading a developmental map

Locate the ninth-year transition within Koepke’s larger framework.

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Hermann Koepke · Encountering the Self · Jesse Darrell translation, 1989 · Study capture p. 109

A particular age in life, such as the ninth year, can best be studied if we survey the larger period in which it occurs.

Selected excerpt from the supplied English edition; Brazilian Portuguese is a new course translation. Line breaks normalized.

What this passage means

This is the book’s developmental method: interpret a transition in relation to what comes before and after. Koepke places the ninth year within the second seven-year period; the course examines that model through particular encounters.

The focus of this lesson

Locate the ninth-year transition within Koepke’s larger framework.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

The reading’s key idea

Koepke places the transition between changing teeth and puberty. His three phases emphasize the head and imitation, the rhythmic system and a new factual interest, then limbs and adolescence. He describes all three systems as working together even when one receives emphasis. The map is therefore a proposed transformation of relationships, not three disconnected bodies.

Terms such as teeth-maturity, breath-maturity and earth-maturity belong to this anthroposophical account. They are not assessment criteria for school entry or medical measurements. The missing page 110 interrupts the opening explanation. Age, grade, interests and support needs must be described separately when applying a reading idea.

Words for the reading

Second seven years: the book’s period between changing teeth and puberty. Rhythmic system: its heart-lung focus. Emphasis: prominence within a whole.

See the idea in an example

Two children in the same class prefer different routes into a task: one starts by handling materials, another by asking how the finished object will work.

A shared school year does not establish identical development. Both routes can remain available while the adult observes what helps each learner.
