Jesus and Christ: read the stages carefully

By the end of this lesson: Distinguish the prepared human bearer, the acting individuality and Christ's entry in Steiner's account.

Keep two questions separate: whose body is being prepared, and which spiritual individuality acts through it? Steiner calls the Nathanic Jesus the prepared bodily bearer. In the earlier childhood account, Buddha's Nirmanakaya, his continuing spiritual activity, influences this child while Zarathustra's I—the continuing spiritual individuality—is associated with the Solomonic child. At about twelve, he says, Zarathustra's I enters the Nathanic Jesus and develops its soul faculties until about thirty. Preparing this bearer is therefore not yet the same event as the entry of Christ.

Steiner also gives a comparative scheme of developing faculties: ways of experiencing and forming understanding. He places the Nathanic Jesus's transitions at twelve, nineteen, twenty-six and thirty-three, compared with fourteen, twenty-one, twenty-eight and thirty-five in the general scheme he uses. These pairs concern the astral or soul body, sentient soul, mind soul and consciousness soul. They are an attributed spiritual-development model, not measured age norms. Do not confuse the thirty-three marker in that comparison with the separate change of spiritual actor at the Baptism near thirty.

At the Baptism, Steiner says, Zarathustra's I departs and the Christ being enters the Nathanic Jesus. The source assigns Christ three earthly years after that entry. It gives Zarathustra a continuing role under the name Master Jesus, helping the later development of Christianity. Those two continuations should not be merged. The same prepared bearer can occupy a place in the account before and after the Baptism while the source changes the spiritual actor it names. Our chronology diagram tracks these claims; it does not establish that they occurred.

Why is the new actor so significant in this lecture? Steiner distinguishes twelve bodhisattvas, spiritual teachers with particular missions, from Christ as the spiritual substance of the wisdom they teach. He identifies this Christ with the cosmic being earlier approached under names such as Ahura Mazda and Vishva Karman. He then links the Baptism to the Word becoming flesh: capacities he says were preserved beyond human control become present in this prepared bearer. These are connected claims within his Christology, rather than definitions shared by every Christian tradition or descriptions of physical forces.

Three questions — optional

A summary says, 'Christ's I transferred from the Solomonic child at twelve.' How should it be corrected?

See an explanation

Steiner assigns the transfer at about twelve to Zarathustra's I, which then prepares the Nathanic Jesus. He places Christ's entry at the adult Baptism near thirty. The error replaces two actors and two events with one. Reinsert the names and their different actions before interpreting their significance.

The comparison pairs the general age thirty-five with Nathanic age thirty-three. Does that move the Baptism to thirty-three?

See an explanation

No. The table compares stages in Steiner's scheme of faculties. The narrative places the Baptism and change of spiritual actor near thirty, followed by three years of Christ's earthly activity. The two displays answer different questions: which faculty is placed at an age, and who is said to act at a turning point.

Why is 'Christ is just the thirteenth teacher' an inadequate summary of this lecture?

See an explanation

Steiner makes a distinction of function: the twelve bodhisattvas convey wisdom, while the central being is presented as its spiritual substance. This explains why he treats incarnation as more than the arrival of another teacher. Reconstructing that distinction does not independently verify the cosmic account.

What this passage means

The baptism marks a decisive transition in Steiner’s chronology. Jesus names the prepared human life; Christ names the spiritual being entering it. Read the earlier contributions and this event as successive stages with different roles.

The focus of this lesson

Distinguish the prepared human bearer, the acting individuality and Christ's entry in Steiner's chronology.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

The reading’s key idea

Steiner gives two related maps: a scheme of developing faculties and a chronology of spiritual actors. Zarathustra's I prepares the Nathanic Jesus from about twelve to thirty; Christ enters at the Baptism. The source distinguishes the continuing Master Jesus from Christ and interprets the Baptism as the Word becoming flesh.

These distinctions belong to Steiner’s Christology. This course does not present them as definitions accepted by all Christian traditions. The lecture’s descriptions of ether and cosmic beings should also be read within that framework, not as physical science.

Two maps: stages of faculties and changes of actor

What the age comparison does

The lecture introduces gradual transitions, then shifts four named transitions for the Nathanic Jesus. The text explicitly supplies the pairs 14/12, 21/19, 28/26 and 35/33. It does not supply a universal rule for converting every age. Read the comparison as a diagram of Steiner's stated spiritual scheme. Its purpose here is to explain the preparation of a particular bearer, not assess a child's development.

Preparation, departure and two continuations

Zarathustra's I works in the Nathanic Jesus from about twelve to thirty. After departing, that individuality has a further course as Master Jesus in Steiner's account. Christ enters at the Baptism and acts through the prepared bearer for three years. Master Jesus and Christ are therefore not interchangeable names. A relationship map needs to preserve the departure as well as the entry.

The teachers and the substance they teach

The lecture places the bodhisattvas around a central being: each brings a particular teaching, while Christ is described as the substance of their wisdom. The names Ahura Mazda and Vishva Karman are interpreted as approaches to this same cosmic being. This is Steiner's account of relationships among traditions. It does not make their actual teachings or self-understandings identical.

Why the Word belongs at the Baptism

Steiner distinguishes fire/warmth, light, sound/number and life ether, then says certain higher capacities were protected from the consequences of the Fall. He connects their preservation with the universal character of thought and meaning, and Christ's entry with the divine Word becoming flesh. These terms explain his cosmological argument; they are not names for measured physical substances. The source's immersion account remains reading material, not a practice instruction.

Connect the parts of the reading

What information is lost if the age comparison and the change of actor are drawn as one undifferentiated line?

An explained answer: A faculty marker such as Nathanic age thirty-three can be mistaken for the Baptism near thirty, and the continuing Zarathustra individuality can be confused with Christ. Keep a role chronology and a separate attributed comparison table. Both need clear source labels and neither establishes the events independently.

Words for the reading

Bodily bearer: the human constitution described as receiving a spiritual individuality. I: the continuing spiritual individuality in this account. Master Jesus: the later course Steiner assigns to Zarathustra's I. Christology: an account of the nature and significance of Christ. Chronology: the order assigned to events.

Build an attributed map

First sketch the names and relationships you remember. Reveal one stage at a time and correct the arrows. This map summarizes Steiner’s spiritual interpretation, not established biography. Later stages preview the following readings.

1 · Two lineages

In Steiner’s interpretation, the Solomon lineage is associated with Matthew’s account and the individuality of Zarathustra; the Nathan lineage with Luke’s account and a preserved Adamic stream. Do not treat this reading as an explicit statement in the Gospels.

2 · At twelve

Steiner describes the Zarathustra I passing from the Solomon child to the Nathan child. Buddha’s contribution is a distinct relationship. Name the relationships; do not merge all the figures into one identity.

3 · The baptism

In Steiner’s sequence, the baptism at thirty marks the entry of the Christ. Distinguish this from the event at twelve. Locate what the passage says about the departing individuality.

See the idea in an example

An account of a theatre production mentions its founder, the person preparing the performance and the performer who later takes the stage. If you replace all three names with “the artist,” the account becomes easy to read but inaccurate. Important relationships disappear.

Before twelve, the retained account distinguishes the Solomon child bearing Zarathustra’s I from the Nathan child. At twelve, that I begins working in the Nathan organization; between twelve and thirty, preparation continues. At the Baptism, Zarathustra departs and Christ enters. The sequence changes both who acts and what the action prepares. Calling every stage Christ’s activity would erase the difference between preparing the bearer and entering it. This is a map of Steiner’s account, not an independently verified chronology.
