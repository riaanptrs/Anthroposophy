GA 114 · Lesson 07 · 1909-09-21

Jesus and Christ: read the stages carefully

Follow a chronology without collapsing its distinct figures.

Start with a situation

An account of a theatre production mentions its founder, the person preparing the performance and the performer who later takes the stage. If you replace all three names with “the artist,” the account becomes easy to read but inaccurate. Important relationships disappear.

The example concerns accurate reading only. Spiritual incarnation is not a theatre role. In a complex text, a helpful first step is to restore names, dates and verbs wherever an ambiguous pronoun makes the sequence hard to follow.

The reading’s key idea

Steiner places Zarathustra’s preparation of the Nathan Jesus before the baptism at thirty, when Christ enters and Zarathustra departs. Christ is a cosmic being in this account. The baptism marks a change, not simply another name for the same role.

These distinctions belong to Steiner’s Christology. This course does not present them as definitions accepted by all Christian traditions. The lecture’s descriptions of ether and cosmic beings should also be read within that framework, not as physical science.

Keep this idea

Keep names, roles and stages distinct before interpreting the whole.

Reading: Lecture 7, 21 September 1909: preparation, baptism and the nature of the Christ being.

Words for the reading

Christology: an account of the nature and significance of Christ. Chronology: the sequence assigned to events.

Work with the idea

Make a timeline from the assigned lecture with three entries: before twelve, twelve to thirty, and the baptism. Identify the subject of each statement. Mark the whole timeline as Steiner’s account and list one question that would need a different kind of source.

Check your understanding

What marks the change at thirty in this account?

Show a suggested answer

The baptism; it is a distinct stage in Steiner’s chronology.

What does chronology establish on its own?

Show a suggested answer

The order an account gives to events. It does not independently establish that the events occurred.

Can a role analogy explain incarnation?

Show a suggested answer

No. It can illustrate a reading error, but should not be treated as a model of the spiritual claim.

How to assess your response

Check four things: correct attribution, a clear distinction, a reason supported by the reading and recognition of the example’s limits. Revisit anything vague. Reasoned disagreement can demonstrate understanding; agreement and reports of spiritual experiences are not required.
