GA 114 · Lesson 06 · 1909-09-20

Responsibility beyond inherited belonging

Consider conduct without reducing a person to ancestry.

1 · Read the passage

Begin with the book

Rudolf Steiner · According to Luke · GA 114 · Lecture 6 · 20 September 1909 · § 30

It was not to take away kinship or abolish what exists between father and son, brother and sister, but to add to the love bound to kinship what is called universal human love, which passes from soul to soul beyond all ties of blood.

Compare with the German passage

Nicht nehmen sollte sie die Blutsverwandtschaft, nicht aufheben, was zwischen Vater und Sohn, zwischen Bruder und Schwester steht, sondern zu der Liebe, die an die Blutsverwandtschaft gebunden ist, dasjenige hinzufügen, was man allgemeine Menschenliebe nennt, die von Seele zu Seele geht, die erhaben ist über alle Blutsbande.

What this passage means

The verb “add” matters: the passage expands the reach of love. It connects the lecture’s account of preparation and belonging with a relation to humanity that is not limited to inherited bonds.

The focus of this lesson

Consider conduct without reducing a person to ancestry.

Explore the chapter’s core ideas

The reading’s key idea

Steiner connects Moses, Elijah and John the Baptist within a spiritual history of moral development. He reads John’s call for changed conduct as exceeding inherited belonging. His account also ranks religious cultures by maturity; that hierarchy requires critical examination.

Do not turn this scheme into judgments about Jewish people or any living community. A religious tradition cannot be reduced to a single developmental label. We can examine the ethical question about conduct while challenging the lecture’s generalizations.

Keep this idea

Attend to individual conduct and examine the categories used to judge it.

Words for the reading

Stereotype: a generalized expectation imposed on an individual. Criterion: a stated basis for making a judgment.

See the idea in an example

A club gives a leadership role to someone because her family has always belonged. Another candidate has listened carefully, resolved disagreements and fulfilled commitments. Family history may explain familiarity with the club; it does not decide who will act responsibly in this role.

Compare actions and reasons without erasing people’s histories. Respecting a tradition and evaluating a particular decision can coexist. The same standard should apply to an outsider and a long-standing member.
