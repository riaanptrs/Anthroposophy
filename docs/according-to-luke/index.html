Sources and method

The 21-capture PDF identifies the 2001 Anthroposophic Press edition, with lectures translated by Catherine E. Creeger. It contains McDermott’s introduction and the supplied Lecture 1 text; Lectures 2–10 are absent. Parallel readings use The Gospel of St. Luke (1964), translated by Dorothy S. Osmond and Owen Barfield. Each passage identifies its own source. Portuguese explanations are original course material.

What was checked in your upload

All 21 captures in the new PDF were read and compared with the 21 Markdown markers. They contain the cover and title (1–2), copyright (3), contents (4), McDermott’s complete introduction (5–13) and the supplied Lecture 1 text (14–21). References are PDF positions; no printed page numbers are visible.

Capture 3 identifies the 2001 Anthroposophic Press edition and Catherine E. Creeger as the lectures’ translator. Robert A. McDermott wrote the introduction. The ISBN is 0-88010-488-0. Passages and references in lessons 0 and 1 were rechecked against this file.

The Lecture 1 text ends at the same Akashic-record paragraph as the earlier capture. The final page is mostly blank, but no next-lecture boundary or explicit ending marker confirms whether the lecture is complete. Lectures 2–10, the Descriptive Outline, About This Edition and footnote bodies are absent. Later lessons retain their parallel sources and await comparison with this edition.

The copyright page prints 15–24 September 1909 in the German title. The existing lecture links run from 15 to 26 September; this discrepancy was recorded without silently changing the edition data.

Capture review: 2026-09-30.

The file is According to Luke: The Gospel of Compassion and Love Revealed, with an introduction by Robert A. McDermott. The supplementary edition is The Gospel of St. Luke, translated by Dorothy S. Osmond and Owen Barfield. The 1964 edition marks an omission at Lecture 9, paragraph 5; the editorial review also consulted the Steiner Online Library parallel version there.

Examples and exercises are teaching inventions. We distinguish the Gospel text, Steiner’s spiritual interpretation, the editorial introduction and the learner’s assessment. Understanding does not require agreement.
