## Concrete before/after samples

**Luke 07: an assigned timeline becomes direct teaching.**

Before: “In a complex text, a helpful first step is to restore names, dates and verbs wherever an ambiguous pronoun makes the sequence hard to follow.”

After: “Before twelve, the retained account distinguishes the Solomon child bearing Zarathustra’s I from the Nathan child. At twelve, that I begins working in the Nathan organization; between twelve and thirty, preparation continues. At the Baptism, Zarathustra departs and Christ enters. The sequence changes both who acts and what the action prepares. Calling every stage Christ’s activity would erase the difference between preparing the bearer and entering it. This is a map of Steiner’s account, not an independently verified chronology.”

**Philosophy 15: the learner receives the missing comparison.**

Before: “Separate what you intended, the present representation that guided you, the practical causes through which shelves moved, and what readers actually did.”

After: “In the display example, easier access is the intended outcome; the present representation of that outcome guides arranging the shelves; bodily work moves them; readers’ later use shows what actually resulted. If nobody finds the books more easily, the purpose was present but unfulfilled. If an unplanned conversation begins beside the display, it is an effect without necessarily having been the purpose.”
