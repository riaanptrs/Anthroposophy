**Learning objective:** Steiner worked in philosophy, spiritual teaching and practical initiatives; a successful classroom activity does not establish his philosophy.

**Essential concept:** Rudolf Steiner developed anthroposophy and worked across philosophy, spiritual teaching, education and the arts.

**Prerequisites at entry:** None; the next-lesson definition link makes the unfamiliar course name recoverable.

**Before — learner difficulty:** The deeper biography names eurythmy without explaining it. The first question repeats the opening definition; neither checks a concrete new situation.

**Preserved teaching:** The growing-plant connection and philosophy/education distinction.

**Explained answer:** It helps assess this teaching activity and what the learners understood. It does not establish every philosophical or spiritual claim in Steiner’s work. The shared interest in development connects the fields without giving them identical evidence.

**Connection or practical direction:** A teacher helping a child understand a growing plant shares an interest with the philosopher examining understanding. But a successful lesson does not prove the philosopher's argument. Steiner's biography connects these activities without making them interchangeable.

**Inherited source status:** `historical-context-with-local-book-evidence`.
