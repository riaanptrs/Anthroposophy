# Philosophy of Freedom: main-course teacher review

29 October 2026. Read all **22 current English main explanations and all three question/answer pairs per unit**: sixteen core stages and six optional practices. The course makes pedagogical sense: it develops the account of knowing before applying it to ethical action, and distinguishes motive, driving force, moral imagination and practical technique. The explanation sequence is retained.

The recurring weakness was a third check that sometimes led learners toward agreement through a yes/no formulation. **Sixteen third checks now require a new-case judgment and explained reasoning**, supplied in English and Portuguese. Six units retain all existing checks because their distinctions and activities already provide useful application. No main prose, goals, first or second checks, activities or takeaways were rewritten. These judgments concern whether the lessons teach their stated argument, not whether a learner must accept it.

## Individual judgments

| [00 — Two questions to carry through the book](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/00.html) | Revise third check. The two guiding questions connect knowledge and action. The revised case separates a changed observation from different motives for the same helpful deed. |
| [01 — What makes you say yes?](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/01.html) | Retain. The obstruction to driving and fear as a motive distinguish external freedom of movement from the origin of willing; the activity asks for examination of an actual decision. |
| [02 — Why is seeing not always understanding?](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/02.html) | Revise third check. Naming dualism or monism does not yet explain the relation. The revised wind/animal case requires evidence for a connection rather than merely choosing a worldview. |
| [03 — Seeing a sequence, explaining a connection](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/03.html) | Revise third check. Optional practice: distinguishes an event from thinking about it, and inward experience from an external object. A dream and a photograph test both distinctions without treating vividness as proof. |
| [04 — Thinking, then noticing how you thought](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/04.html) | Retain. The calculation and bracket examples ask learners to reconstruct thinking, rather than confuse recalling an answer with observing its formation. |
| [05 — What you see and what you remember](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/05.html) | Revise third check. Viewpoint, percept and remembered representation are introduced before the critique of critical idealism. The tilted-card case now asks what changes when the card is no longer present. |
| [06 — Does the explanation undermine itself?](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/06.html) | Optional practice, retained. The consistency objection and the unexplained sensation/brain bridge are distinct; an unsuccessful argument does not by itself establish that its conclusion is false. |
| [07 — How a bud and a flower belong together](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/07.html) | Revise third check. The bud/flower sequence supports the percept/concept distinction. The dried-bud case now prevents a conceptual account of development from supplying an unobserved actual history. |
| [08 — Different drawings, one idea](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/08.html) | Revise third check. Optional practice: the shared triangle concept differs from the remembered individual drawing. The open three-sided drawing tests why closure matters to applying the concept. |
| [09 — Recognizing your own bicycle](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/09.html) | Revise third check. The bicycle example teaches individualized representation and feeling. A changed bicycle case now asks what supports identifying this particular object, rather than repeating its name. |
| [10 — Not knowing yet](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/10.html) | Revise third check. The explanation distinguishes a missing fact from a claimed absolute limit to knowing. Lost watering history and measurable moisture now require separate judgments. |
| [11 — Feeling moved, understanding, acting](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/11.html) | Revise third check. Feeling, willing and interpretation remain distinct. Equally moved listeners disagreeing about a musical return now test why intensity of feeling cannot settle an interpretive claim. |
| [12 — What actually moves a helpful action?](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/12.html) | Revise third check. The lesson distinguishes the conceptual motive from the driving force and asks how an intuition becomes effective. The volunteer case tests whether praise actually determined an action despite an inclusive slogan. |
| [13 — Different solutions, shared freedom](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/13.html) | Revise third check. Optional practice: harmony of free ethical intuitions does not mean immediate agreement on a practical proposal. Audio/written alternatives now require inquiry with the person affected. |
| [14 — A rule you can make your own](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/14.html) | Revise third check. An internalized command differs from an understood ethical idea. Returning a drill now tests the transition without labelling a person permanently free or unfree. |
| [15 — An aim is present before its result](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/15.html) | Retain. A purpose can be present as an idea before its result exists; an unintended conversation and a failed plan test why effect, intention and success cannot be substituted for one another. |
| [16 — From an ethical idea to a workable action](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/16.html) | Revise third check. Ethical idea, moral imagination, technique and motive are separately taught. A clear audio guide in an unusable file format now tests technical feasibility separately from the source of the deed. |
| [17 — Improve the plan without confusing the questions](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/17.html) | Optional practice, retained. The existing inaccessible-format example, novelty and collaboration already test practical realization without equating originality with freedom. |
| [18 — Why pleasure is not the whole question](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/18.html) | Retain. Pleasure, satisfaction of a particular desire and persistence are distinguished. Pleasure based on a mistaken object can still have been experienced; difficulty alone does not establish worth. |
| [19 — Partial fulfillment is still particular](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/19.html) | Revise third check. Optional practice: proportional fulfillment is distinct from value and freedom. One completed action against targets of three and six now requires explaining one-third versus one-sixth, with limits on what either ratio establishes. |
| [20 — Meet the person beyond the category](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/20.html) | Revise third check. The individual must be encountered beyond a group generalization. A quiet participant leading with questions supplied in advance now requires revising the inference while allowing reasoned disagreement with the actual proposal. |
| [21 — Bring knowing and acting together](../../../../docs/philosophy-of-freedom/lessons/21.html) | Revise third check. The concluding synthesis connects the knowing and acting arguments. A barrier/accessibility case now separates observation, concept, ethical idea, imagined form and practical technique; the illustration is not a proof that thinking is self-supporting. |
