## Provenance labels

| Label | Meaning in this project |
|---|---|
| `[CONVERSATION]` | Editorial trace label for material developed in the referenced chat. It establishes conversation provenance, not book-level verification. |
| `[BOOK]` | The prior course text presented the material as explicitly stated or clearly presented in a supplied PDF. Retained as an inherited attribution; primary text verification is pending in this package. |

| `[EARLY WALDORF]` | Practice attributed to the first school or original teacher-training period. Historical documentation must support this classification. |

| `[INTERPRETATION]` | An explanatory inference or developmental connection. It must remain distinguishable from a primary-source statement. |
| `[EXPANSION]` | Background or teaching material included in the previous course text, beyond the book's explicit claims. No new external expansion was added for this package. |
| `[MODERN ADAPTATION]` | Contemporary responses to culture, geography, inclusion, technology, national requirements, or school circumstances. |
| `[MODERN RESEARCH]` | Contemporary research explicitly included in the course material. The category alone supplies no scientific evidence. |
| `[TEXT UNCERTAIN]` | Wording or source evidence is unclear. Resolve it against the original source rather than guessing. |

**Provenance and verification are separate.** A label can record the original attribution while the actual citation remains unresolved. Anthroposophical spiritual claims must not be described as scientifically established facts. Age ranges in the course are approximate within that pedagogical framework, not fixed diagnoses or universal developmental laws.

The earlier dossiers also use inherited variants: `[WALDORF CURRICULUM]`, `[WALDORF TRADITION]`, `[CONTEMPORARY WALDORF]`, `[LATER WALDORF INTERPRETATION]`, `[STEINER + WALDORF CURRICULUM]`, and `[EARLY WALDORF / STEINER]`. Preserve those words and any combined attribution rather than silently simplifying them to `[STEINER]`. They remain conversation attributions with verification pending.

## Unresolved conversation citations

Some replies contain tokens such as `:chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}`. These are internal references in the original conversation, not portable book citations. Their indices are local to each reply: index 0 in one reply need not identify the same source as index 0 in another.

Preserve the originating message and index when recording such a token. Unless a source title or link was actually exposed, leave it unresolved. Do not substitute a plausible title, manufacture a URL, or convert an index into a book page number.

Browser or clipboard recovery can also expose displayed file-preview names, sometimes shortened with an ellipsis. Those labels remain unresolved source clues; a shortened preview is not a complete bibliographic citation or a verified passage.

This identifies named works in that reply but does not resolve all other messages' references or verify the cited passages.

Any quotation retained from the lesson draft is a quotation reproduced from the conversation, with primary-text verification pending. ## Before completing an unfinished lesson

1. Locate the relevant primary passage and the exact edition or translation.
2. Distinguish printed book pages from PDF viewer pages. Record only locators actually checked.
3. Check quotation wording and its surrounding qualifications.
4. Separate Steiner indications, early school practice, later curriculum, and developmental interpretation.
5. Treat cultural narratives and curriculum sequences as traditions with variations where the conversation does so.
6. For modern research or contemporary school policies, obtain and label actual supporting evidence rather than implying it exists.

When the supplied material does not support a proposed assertion, write: **Not established in the supplied text.**
