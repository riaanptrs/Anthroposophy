# Lesson 26 — Adolescence and Independent Judgment

**Status: DRAFT OUTLINE — established planning content, awaiting a finished lesson.**

## Established developmental framework

[STEINER] **Attribution recorded in the conversation; primary-source verification pending.** The master course plan places adolescence in the third seven-year period, characterized by judgment, truth, and growing independence. It directs research to GA 302, *Education for Adolescents*, and to the adolescent lectures in GA 303.

[INTERPRETATION] The lesson's established sequence is:

**Picture → observation → causality → judgment.**

Its role is to prepare the parent for Grade 9.

## Established Grade 9 connections

[LATER WALDORF CURRICULUM] **Provisional category; precise curriculum sources remain to be checked.** The Grade 9 outline describes independent judgment and confrontation with objective reality, a movement toward specialist teachers, and increasingly analytical work.

Named examples include modern political ideas and social questions; mechanics and applied science; geology, earth processes, and resources; increasingly abstract mathematics and proof; more complex drama; and observation, comparison, and aesthetic judgment in art.

The Grade 9 governing question is how Waldorf education supports the adolescent's growing capacity to judge for themselves.

## Distinction to preserve

[CONVERSATION] The course must identify this as an Anthroposophical developmental framework. The available conversation does not establish that its seven-year periods are universally accepted scientific stages or that particular curriculum choices demonstrate a measured developmental outcome.
