### Why handwork is educational

Consider knitting.

At first glance it may seem unrelated to academic learning.

But the child must:

- maintain attention;
- coordinate both hands;
- remember a sequence;
- notice errors;
- correct them;
- persist;
- transform raw material into a finished object.

This is an education of willing.

The same principle applies to:

- woodworking;
- sewing;
- metalwork;
- gardening;
- painting;
- musical practice.

### Handwork Pathway

Grade 1
knitting

↓

Grade 2
crochet

↓

Grade 3
larger useful items / textile processes

↓

Grades 4–5
sewing / embroidery / increasingly exact handwork

↓

Grades 6–8
sewing and crafts + woodwork depending upon school

↓

Grade 9
dressmaking / basketry / carpentry / metalwork according to facilities

Contemporary Waldorf Resources explicitly describes this progression and notes that handwork was extended to boys and girls from Grade 1 at the first Waldorf school.
