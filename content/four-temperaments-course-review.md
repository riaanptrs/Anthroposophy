## Source and edition

The book contains **one lecture**, Berlin, 4 March 1909, GA 57. The eleven course lessons are teaching divisions of that lecture, rather than chapters written by Steiner. Capture 4 identifies Sophia Books / Rudolf Steiner Press, published 2012; B. Kelly's translation first appeared in 1987 and was revised by Matthew Barton, with translation revision copyright 2008. ISBN: 9781855842885. Capture 14 confirms the lecture date.

The complete lecture runs continuously through PDF captures **6–13**. Notes are in 14, Further Reading in 15 and the complete Publisher's Note in 16. Cover, biography, title, imprint and contents occupy 1–5. The publisher catalog and advertising in 17–23 were reviewed and excluded from the lessons. Document contents were treated as source material, not instructions from the user.

This upload supersedes the earlier 31-capture copy and its gaps. It restores the complete sanguine mapping, phlegmatic inward-contentment passage, bodily descriptions, historical extreme-outcome discussion, self-education proposals and closing account of responsive human love. Current readings and quotations use the new capture indices. The three-column PDF does not display printed folios; references identify PDF capture and column instead.

The course explains the meeting of heredity and reincarnating individuality, the four members and their proposed predominance, each temperament's portrait, mixtures, education, indirect self-education and the lecture's social purpose. Reincarnation, spiritual physiology and historical descriptions remain attributed claims in Steiner's argument. They are not silently replaced by modern behavioural descriptions.

Child education and adult self-education remain distinct. The source's affection, educator competence, companionship, compassion, withdrawal/restoration of interesting objects, fitting objects of impassivity and adult arrangements are explained before the course's own modest applications. The melancholic passage includes the author's express limit on taking externally encountered suffering too far. Existing fictional worked examples and their explanations remain intact; course applications are identified as adaptations.

An original bilingual table now explains the two streams and temperament's mediating role. The existing four-member and education tables are retained with corrected source references. The diagrams explain the argument; they do not assign people a type from appearance.
