# According to Luke — course fidelity and teaching audit

Audit date: 30 September 2026. This document records the course as it stood before the current supplied-source revision. It is an editorial audit, not a newly verified digest of all ten lectures.

## Evidence and limits

Reviewed `according-to-luke.mjs`, `scripts/build-luke.mjs`, `according-to-luke-reading-review.md`, the Luke entries in `passage-study.json`, `guided-prompts.mjs`, `guided-labs.mjs`, the passage-study builder, and representative generated English lesson pages. Read the newly supplied Markdown capture, particularly markers 3–17. Its OCR warnings require checking quotations against the paired PDF before publication.

The uploaded PDF/Markdown supply title and contents, McDermott's introduction, and the opening of Lecture 1. Markers 18–20 are interface material. The contents name ten lectures but do not supply their bodies. Capture markers are not printed folios. The supplied edition's translator must remain unidentified unless a reliable bibliographic page is available.

The earlier reading review identifies the parallel 1964 English edition and a separate version for an omission in Lecture 9. Existing course excerpts instead cite numbered German GA 114 paragraphs and original course translations. Those are three different provenance layers: supplied capture, earlier parallel English reading, and separately credited German excerpt. This audit does not silently merge them.

A read-only request to the Rudolf Steiner Archive returned a proxy 403 in this session. Lectures 2–10 were therefore **not reread against their complete primary texts**. Recommendations for those lessons identify observable omissions in existing teaching, its own reading assignments, its quoted passages, and the supplied contents. They do not authorize newly asserted doctrine, invented page references, or a claim of full-source verification. The September 12 reading review is evidence of an earlier workflow, not fresh verification on September 30.

## What already works

- The course has a stable orientation, ten dated lecture lessons, and a final original synthesis, with matching English and Brazilian Portuguese coverage.
- Its source notes disclose that the uploaded capture is incomplete and that complete readings use a parallel English edition.
- It distinguishes Gospel narrative, Steiner's interpretation, McDermott's editorial voice, and the learner's assessment. Unusual spiritual identities remain attributed.
- Its ethical cases preserve ordinary agency and accountability. It does not turn healing narratives into diagnoses or initiation narratives into bodily instructions.
- The final build already teaches before practice. Although `build-luke.mjs` initially places the worked example first, `build-passage-study.mjs` moves the selected passage and an open explanation before the example and response fields. Inspected generated pages confirm that order. Reordering the base builder alone would not address the principal weakness.

## Principal gap

The course is presently a selective ethical reading companion. Its own earlier review says it does not attempt an exhaustive digest. That is a reasonable description of the existing result, but it does not satisfy a request to reformulate the course around the essential concepts and argument of the supplied book.

Each lecture has a short passage and a short explanation, followed by two brief course paragraphs. The substantial elaboration often concerns an original everyday case: volunteer help, communication, a club appointment, reception of feedback, sharing notes, or repairing a friendship. The cases are useful applications, but they leave the reader unable to reconstruct the lecture's argument. Technical glossaries often define general study words instead of the terms the source requires. Merely adding a quotation before this structure cannot supply the missing argument.

The current `sourceChecks` override creates one source-oriented check in Lessons 2–10, while the other two usually test the application or its limits. Many questions can be answered without understanding the lecture. The suggested answers are generally sensible, but successful completion is weak evidence of source comprehension. Lesson 1 has no source-check override at all. Its checks concern vividness, a friend's explanation, and analogy limits rather than the three modes of cognition or the Gospel comparison.

## Readable learning path

Preserve the familiar page pattern, but make its substantive sequence explicit:

1. Identify the author, edition or source layer, and available span.
2. Read one or more short verified passages selected for the argument, rather than only for an accessible ethical slogan.
3. Explain the author's question, premises, main distinctions, sequence of reasoning, and conclusion in connected prose.
4. Define the technical vocabulary required for that explanation.
5. Ask the learner to explain a source relation with words from the passage.
6. Use the original everyday case as an application, with its limits stated briefly.
7. Check source comprehension before checking application, then invite assessment or disagreement.
8. Connect the lesson to the preceding and following stage, and name anything unavailable in the supplied material.

For Lecture 1, a small comparison table is particularly useful: level of cognition, how Steiner describes its access, distinction from ordinary usage, and role in his Gospel comparison. A second source table can separate direct spiritual research, the Akashic record claimed as its historical source, and subsequent documentary comparison. Neither table should imply an independently verified account.

The same map currently appears in guided labs for Lessons 4, 5 and 7. It previews later stages appropriately, but it repeats instead of developing the learner's task. On a future complete-source revision, progressively change the map: identify lineage distinctions in 4; specify distinct contributions and the twelve-year transition in 5; explain the baptism transition and its larger significance in 7. Preserve names, explicit relation verbs and source references.

## Bilingual terminology findings

The English and Portuguese lesson prose is generally closely aligned. The most important issues are shared underexplanation, not a separate missing Portuguese course.

- Lesson 1's “participation in their being” / “participação no ser deles” softens the selected passage's stronger claim of becoming one with beings and immersing in their nature. Preserve the strength of the attributed claim while keeping ordinary empathy distinct.
- Lesson 2 uses `Bodhisattva` in its Portuguese reading assignment and `Bodisatva` in the selected passage. Standardize course prose or identify the spelling variation.
- Lessons 5, 8 and 9 alternate English `Ego` and `I` between prose and passages. Portuguese consistently uses `Eu`. State the terminology equivalence once and preserve the term chosen by each quoted edition.
- Lesson 8's English “spiritual understanding” becomes “conhecimento espiritual” in Portuguese. “Compreensão espiritual” preserves the wording's distinction more closely.
- The retained Lesson 9 German passage uses `überfließen`, Portuguese uses `transbordar`, and English uses “flow out.” “Overflow” preserves the image used by the lecture's own reading description more precisely. This is a comparison of the existing quoted wording, not a fresh verification against the complete lecture.

## Source and translation controls for the revision

- Use PDF/capture page numbers only as such; do not invent printed folios or transfer German paragraph numbers to the supplied English edition.
- Label selected passages from the newly supplied edition separately from retained German GA 114 excerpts and the parallel 1964 English reading links.
- A short quotation should be followed by an explanation of its wording and its position in the argument. The current one-passage-per-lesson data model is a structural constraint, not evidence that one passage captures every essential concept.
- Original Portuguese teaching and study translations should remain explicitly distinguished from a published Portuguese edition. Preserve the same argument, examples and source limits in both languages.
- Use stable technical terms: Imagination/Imaginação, Inspiration/Inspiração, Intuition/Intuição, clairvoyance/clarividência, initiation/iniciação, and Ego or I/Eu. Explain variation rather than casually equating GA 114 usage with GA 4 or GA 10.
- Preserve the course's concise critical attribution of group hierarchies, healing claims and unusual identities. Place those notes after an adequate account of the author's argument so that the learner can understand what is being assessed.

## Scope that can be completed with the present uploads

The introduction and supplied opening of Lecture 1 can be reformulated around the actual author/editor argument now, with PDF-verified short passages in English and matching Brazilian Portuguese explanations. The supplied contents can also make the index's lecture coverage more informative. Later lessons can retain their previously credited parallel excerpts and disclosed reading limits.

A claim that all ten complete lectures have now been reread page by page would be unsupported. Deeper replacement of Lessons 2–10 requires the missing lecture texts or working access to the explicitly credited parallel primary source. Structural validation can check attribution fields, language parity, source-first order and correct links; it cannot establish that an unexamined lecture is substantively complete.
