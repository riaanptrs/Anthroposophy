# Practical Training in Thought: source and teaching review

Scope: all ten lessons in the GA 108 practice course, in English and Brazilian Portuguese. The other primary courses were not substantively audited in this pass. Existing source quotations, learner-note identifiers and lesson URLs remain intact.

Sources checked: [GA 108 English Adams translation](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA108/English/Singles/19090118p02.html) and [German original](https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA108/German/19090118.html). Numbered assignments use the English page. Its paragraph 49 is marked untranslated, so the conclusion assignment ends at 48. Archive metadata and historical anecdotes are not treated as independently verified historical facts; no historical fact-check is claimed by this review.

| Lesson on the site | Gap found | Implemented teaching |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Habit or a reason? | The earlier expansion explains habit well, but can leave “new = practical” unchallenged. | Distinguish cleverness and internal consistency from a plan fitted to the situation; contrast bigger lettering with explaining an unfamiliar word. |
| 2 · An attitude of inquiry | Confidence in the spiritual premise could be confused with certainty about an individual answer. | Explain intelligibility versus a pre-decided conclusion; retain the existing watch and plant examples. |
| 3 · Observe before explaining | “Refraining from thought” could sound like suppressing all thought. | Read that phrase with the instructions to attend, form pictures and compare; define the speculation being deferred. Retain the six-step cloud guide. |
| 4 · Follow a change | The assignment to provide two causes encouraged speculation at the very stage that postpones it. | Replace that demand with dated observations, change, continuity and an unanswered question. Explain imagined transitions versus observed intervals. |
| 5 · Make a prediction you can check | The relationship between prediction, explanation and conditions needed a worked comparison. | Separate the three; compare 8/20 with 4/10 to show why raw counts can mislead; distinguish support from proof of a unique cause. |
| 6 · Investigate how something happened | Scheduling dependencies dominated a lesson whose source investigates past causes. | Replace the main example, task, goal and check with a present event, rival causal hypotheses and an available verification. Explain temporal order versus causation. |
| 7 · Choose and sustain a line of thought | The recent revision distinguishes timing and memory; returning after distraction needed further teaching. | Give a positively specified subject and a practical way of recording a diversion and return. |
| 8 · Build and check a mental picture | Source imaginative completion and the course’s immediate object check could still be conflated; promised improvement could be read as a deadline. | Label remembered, uncertain and deliberately imagined details; identify the adaptation; assess actual changes rather than guarantee perfect recall. |
| 9 · Compare, pause and decide | The lesson emphasized rival explanations, but the passage compares courses of action and prescribes a pause. | Teach alternatives, preparation, a defined pause, return, reasons and review. Define inner necessity and provisional judgment; distinguish pause from avoidance. |
| 10 · Review how your thinking changed | Assigned reading stopped at 38 despite a quotation from 48. Objective thinking and the closing argument were thinly taught. | Assign 38–48 with a review of 1–36. Explain feeling versus evidence, claims versus their support, natural-process examples and the final assessment. |
