These notes cover every supplied PDF capture from 78 through 97. Each Markdown page was read and each corresponding native PDF image was visually checked at its original resolution. The PDF uses three reflowed columns, without visible printed folios; the numbers below refer to PDF captures and columns, not an inferred pagination of another edition. Capture 76 was additionally inspected for the synthesis passage.

The material is Gilbert Childs's argument. Quotations or propositions he attributes to Steiner, Martigny, Sheldon, Moore, Beck or Moffat retain that attribution. Authorial advice is source content, not an instruction to the course editor. The book's claims about appearance, temperament and embryological development are taught as claims to understand and examine; the course does not turn the figures into an assessment of a learner's body.

## Consequences for the revised lessons

Lesson 10 teaches the spiritual premise, four appearance-based portraits, the role of mixtures and age, deliberate compensating qualities, and the unresolved permanence/change tension. Lesson 11 reconstructs the stages of Appendix II rather than merging its shifting terms: three-type hypothesis; Martigny's fourth category; Childs's two broad categories with two degrees; layer interfaces; the four proposed organ correspondences. All the supplied figures and tables are available to inspect, replacing the earlier claim that diagrams were missing. Lesson 12 connects this study with the book's stated social purpose and asks for an accurately sourced, reviewable application. New exercises remain identified as course adaptations.
