A concept carries understood content; an idea expresses ordered relationships. Brian attributes this teaching distinction to Kant; the supplied sources do not independently verify the exact formula.

## Revised notes for teaching

- Write **Immanuel Kant**. Treat the intellect/reason chart as Brian's teaching simplification and his attribution to Kant. The book itself opens this chapter with concepts and ideas, not that chart. Neither a concept reduced to a name nor an idea reduced to a pile of parts captures the intended understood relationships.
- Replace “I exist because thinking distinguishes me” with **“Through thinking, I recognize myself as subject in relation to what I observe.”** The argument concerns self-consciousness and the starting point of understanding. It does not say that naming physically creates the thinker or the world.
- At a distance the trees' apparent size changes with viewpoint. Further observations help correct the picture. This does not establish that the physical trees shrink or that the world is imaginary.
- Replace “we think universally” with **“conceptual relations can be shared and understood by different people.”** It does not imply identical opinions, automatic agreement or infallibility. A Eureka moment calls for examining the connection and its application.
- Omit the universal age-three threshold. The book describes gradual concept formation on PDF capture 48. Brian's approximate age-three/four example at 44:20–45:11 concerns the **concept of self**; it does not establish the starting age of all concepts or consciousness.
- Omit the generalization that reason is taught only in Waldorf schools. It is the lecturer's educational comment at 03:12–03:19, without support in these supplied sources.
- Keep **Logos** as a separately attributed historical/theological extension. Brian's John/Heraclitus association at 21:12–21:48 is not a conclusion demonstrated by Chapter 4. The theological use should not serve as the sole definition of the broader Greek term.
- Distinguish an **outer encounter**, a **present percept**, the **conceptual connection** and a **retained mental picture**. Inner/outer shorthand alone hides these contributions. A representation need not be visual, and memory and feeling are not interchangeable with it.
- Define **critical idealism here** as the position whose argument restricts direct knowledge to representations. It is not simply “everything is illusion.” **Naive realism** accepts perceived objects as independently existing just as they appear. The failure of one proof does not automatically establish the competing view.
