## Revised teaching notes

**English:** We encounter not only external objects but also inward feelings and memories. Thinking develops conceptual relations through which these contents become intelligible, and helps determine the self as subject. Concepts are grasped through active thinking rather than merely received with an observation; a concept already formed can later become an observed content.

My feeling is an individual experience. Its reality or intensity does not establish that others feel the same, or that it supplies a universal explanation of the world. Steiner criticizes a mysticism based on mere feeling when it gives that individual experience the status of a universal means of knowledge. He is not denying the reality or importance of feeling.

Feeling expresses an encounter's relation to me; willing expresses my active relation toward what is objective. The experienced will also requires conceptual understanding. Thelism makes this individually experienced will a universal world principle. Being the person who intends or performs an action does not, by itself, establish its freedom. Chapter 9 investigates the motive and driving force more closely; Chapter 12 develops moral imagination.

In the 1918 addition, a remembered formulation can appear lifeless beside the activity that produced it. Renewing understanding means actively reconstructing its conceptual connection, rather than recovering a former excitement. Steiner describes thinking in its essence as warm and luminous, penetrating phenomena through love in spiritual form. He finds feeling and will within this living thinking, and expressly rejects the idea that this means projecting a separate feeling of love into it.

**Português:** Encontramos não apenas objetos exteriores, mas também sentimentos e lembranças interiores. O pensar desenvolve relações conceituais pelas quais esses conteúdos se tornam compreensíveis e participa da determinação de si como sujeito. Conceitos são apreendidos pelo pensar ativo, em vez de simplesmente recebidos com uma observação; um conceito já formado pode depois tornar-se conteúdo observado.

Meu sentimento é uma experiência individual. Sua realidade ou intensidade não demonstra que outros sintam o mesmo nem que ele forneça uma explicação universal do mundo. Steiner critica o misticismo baseado no mero sentir quando atribui a essa experiência individual o estatuto de um meio universal de conhecer. Não nega a realidade ou importância do sentimento.

O sentir expressa a relação de um encontro comigo; o querer expressa minha relação ativa com aquilo que é objetivo. A vontade vivenciada também precisa de compreensão conceitual. O telismo transforma essa vontade individualmente vivenciada num princípio universal do mundo. Ser quem pretende ou realiza uma ação não demonstra, por si só, sua liberdade. O capítulo 9 investiga mais de perto motivo e força motriz; o capítulo 12 desenvolve a imaginação moral.

No adendo de 1918, uma formulação lembrada pode parecer sem vida diante da atividade que a produziu. Renovar a compreensão significa reconstruir ativamente sua relação conceitual, em vez de recuperar um entusiasmo anterior. Steiner descreve o pensar em sua essência como caloroso e luminoso, penetrando os fenômenos pelo amor em forma espiritual. Encontra sentir e querer nesse pensar vivo e rejeita expressamente a ideia de que isso significaria projetar nele um sentimento separado de amor.

## Specific corrections to the supplied notes

- **Self-concept and age:** PDF 91 describes the gradual emergence of the concept of self from an earlier feeling of existence. It provides neither a universal age-three threshold nor a beginning date for memory. Brian's examples at 02:59–04:35 are additional developmental interpretations.
- **Thelism:** use **thelism** in English and **telismo** in Portuguese, not “theolism.” Schopenhauer is Brian's example at 09:46–10:22; this is not a complete exposition of Schopenhauer's philosophy.
- **The copied AI paragraph:** replace it with the notes above. Chapter 8 does not establish that every act of will is rational, conscious, determined only from within or free of external influence. Inner impulses and dispositions can themselves determine action. Moral intuition's ethical role and moral imagination belong to later chapters.
- **Cold concepts:** the addition contrasts living thinking with the lifeless abstraction that may remain in recollection. It does not say every understood concept becomes intrinsically dead, false or unusable. Reconstruct the relation in fresh thinking; reproducing an emotion is unnecessary.
- **Light, warmth and love:** preserve these as Steiner's spiritual characterization of thinking in its essence. Neither physical heat or optical radiation nor the claim that every everyday thought is loving or correct follows from the passage. Reducing the account to an agreeable mood would also miss its stronger claim.
- **Page 132:** the lecture identifies this as its edition's page for the addition at 11:02–11:09. In the uploaded Wilson book the addition is **PDF capture 93**. Do not use uploaded PDF page 132 for this material. The motives question anticipates Chapter 9, PDF 94–108.

## Actual book passage

The 1918 addition on PDF capture **93** states:

> This penetration is brought about by a power flowing through the activity of thinking itself—the power of love in its spiritual form.

It then adds:

> If we turn towards thinking in its essence, we find in it both feeling and will, and these in the depths of their reality;

These are excerpts from Wilson's translation. They support retaining the positive spiritual-love account alongside the chapter's criticisms of one-sided philosophies. The practical course exercise can illustrate renewed conceptual activity without claiming to demonstrate the whole account.
