# Esoteric Christianity I — book-based clarification review

Reviewed 6 October 2026. This revision uses targeted passages in eight supplied
works to clarify 17 lessons in both English and Brazilian Portuguese. It keeps
all 24 lesson identities, six modules and the central endpoint at the Jordan.

## What was reviewed

The 13 uploads contain nine unique works. Four have paired PDF/Markdown files;
these are reading aids for the same source, not independent evidence. Title and
imprint information takes precedence over upload filenames. Originals and full
book text remain private and unaltered. The fingerprint and page ledger is
`resource-revision-ledger.json`; precise printed/PDF locators and scope are in
`resource-review.mjs`. This is targeted passage study, not a claim that all nine
works were read in full.

- GA 8, *Christianity as Mystical Fact*: chapter 1's description of initiation,
  chapter 6's Gospel interpretation, and chapter 10's Mysteries/Christianity
  relationship. Andrew Welburn translates the 1997 edition. Background added to
  this course; not falsely attributed as a named recommendation in Brian's talks.
- GA 11, *Cosmic Memory*: “Our Atlantean Ancestors”, pp. 42–56, for memory,
  language and judgment. The source's racial hierarchies are explicitly
  acknowledged, not silently sanitized or adopted as present anthropology.
- GA 13, *An Outline of Occult Science*: selected chapter II and IV passages on
  members, the specialized use of “body” and cosmological method. Internal title
  page is 1922, despite a 2011 upload label. Paired Markdown checked with PDF.
- GA 103, *The Gospel of St. John*: lecture I, Logos and Gospel viewpoints.
  Monges translation, revised 1962, second printing 1973. This identifies one
  supplied John cycle without claiming Brian specified this edition.
- GA 123, *The Gospel of St. Matthew*: selected lectures III, VI and VII,
  genealogy, two children, Temple and Jordan. The imprint is a 1985 reprint of
  the 1965 translation, despite the filename's January 2000 label.
- GA 131, *From Jesus to Christ*: selected lectures VI–VIII, form/substance,
  phantom and Buddha/Nathan. Revised translation 1973; supplied PDF has no
  printed pagination, so locators use lecture and PDF page.
- GA 148, *The Fifth Gospel*: lectures IV and V read directly, pp. 64–98. The
  supplied 1950 book contains five Christiania lectures, not the full GA 148.
- *The Book of the Bee*: preface, introduction and chapter 37 checked directly.
  Solomon of Akhlat is the medieval compiler; Budge edits/translates. Internal
  title page is 1886; filename label 2021 is not treated as a new translation.

The upload called *Esoteric Christianity and the mission of Christian…* is
actually *Art as Seen in the Light of Mystery Wisdom*, GA 275, first complete
English edition 1984. Both PDF and matching Markdown have this mismatch. Title
and imprint pages 5–6 were checked; the book is not used to substantiate the
Christian Rosenkreutz title or the two-child account. This is disclosed in the
student source guide as well as the private ledger.

## Source-sensitive decisions

The Essene-gate and Mary passages previously credited to the research dossier
are now directly checked in the supplied GA 148. Seven decisive PDF pages were
visually compared with extracted text. The Asita/Simeon attribution still rests
on the supplied GA 114 dossier: that book was not in this new set. GA 131
independently supports the Buddha/Nathan influence, not the specific Asita claim.

Steiner's two-mother relationship is explicitly distinguished from Brian's
Heavenly Eve conjecture. The Solomon/Nathan emphasis in GA 123 VI is explained
without implying that either child has only some bodily members. GA 123 VI–VII
speaks of the Solomon child at twelve at transfer, while Brian entertains larger
age differences; no exact harmonized age gap is invented or assessed. The six-year
encounter outline is qualified by GA 148's earlier journeys and uncertain altar
location. Association with the Essenes is not converted into formal membership.
The Book of the Bee's own identification of Zârâdôsht with Baruch is acknowledged
rather than silently equated with an established historical Persian identity.
Resurrection receives a clearer definition as a continuation lead, not a new
module beyond the course's scope.
