## Course changes

Fourteen bilingual lessons retain their existing identities and practical examples, while adding connected source explanations, revised key ideas, precise reading assignments and three source-based comprehension questions per lesson. The reading guides explain the author's actual arguments before the practical applications. The orientation identifies the collection's structure; the final portfolio requires textual reconstruction and comparison before original artistic work.

The core teaching explains the four image colours; shadow-thrower, illuminant and image; the three lustre colours and their distributions; the folded spectrum scheme; image and lustre in mineral, plant, animal and human painting; and form emerging from colour. The supplementary lessons develop colour experience and consciousness, moral colour and tone, light/thought/past and darkness/will/future, the proposed significance of moral deeds, viewing conditions, colour perspective, Titian's composition, qualitative measure/number/weight, and the hierarchy/rainbow account. Spiritual and historical claims remain attributed to Steiner rather than replaced by ordinary artistic exercises.

Selected English passages are verified against the native images and credited to their actual translator, exact capture/column and source fingerprint. Portuguese quotations are course study translations. The orientation's Lecture 1 preview and the synthesis's Lecture 11 passage are identified by their actual source.

Four accessible relationship tables restore or explain what OCR loses: the image-colour relationships, the four painting modes, qualitative weight/measure/number, and the hierarchy/manifestation/element sequence. Captions distinguish source-table formatting from original explanatory summaries. Existing digital colour studies and the interactive colour lab remain original applications, with their descriptions and controls preserved.

## What the source does not supply

Editorial Note 2 identifies an incomplete peach-blossom transcript passage. Note 22 explains limits in the original Lecture 4 drawing, including unavailable colours. Note 38 describes the earlier rainbow discussion as hints with shorthand gaps and supplies a refraction/reflection clarification. These are genuine source qualifications, distinguished from garbled OCR, and no missing words or colour procedures were invented.

Native drawings are chiefly monochrome hatch schemes; their colour assignments come from labels and prose. The source's Titian title, *Ascension of Mary*, is distinguished from the usual identification, *Assumption of the Virgin*. Historical medical, pigment and bodily claims are taught as source content rather than practical instructions.
