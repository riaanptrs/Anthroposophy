## Editorial and teaching decisions

The visual markers in 093, 097 and 099 are genuine edition gaps. Editorial note 38 (reviewed by the following-range agent at capture 150, column 3) explicitly says the rainbow account contains incomplete hints and gaps in the shorthand report; it also supplies Steiner's separate discussion of refraction and reflection. Consequently the lecture's red/blue contrast must not be labelled its complete account of rainbow formation. No missing words or procedures have been reconstructed.

Note 35 refers to a doctors' course; note 36 refers to historical plant-colour research. These references establish context and do not validate the physiological and pigment claims. The guides explain those claims because they carry the author's argument. Learner work uses ordinary commercial art materials, description and comparison: it does not prescribe bodily exposure, calcium carbonate, nerve/blood alteration, plant burning or chemical extraction. Modern atmospheric optics, retinal photoreception and pigment chemistry are briefly distinguished from this historical spiritual account.

The artwork discussed is conventionally Titian's *Assumption of the Virgin*; the source heading uses *Ascension of Mary*. Captures 112–122 contain no reproduction. Descriptions of Apostles, Mary and God the Father therefore identify Steiner's reading of the work rather than results of direct image analysis. The lesson connects three regions to virtue, beauty and wisdom and includes the two limits of artistic appearance. The argument about tragedy, karma, language and cultural context is retained rather than treating the lecture as colour composition alone.
