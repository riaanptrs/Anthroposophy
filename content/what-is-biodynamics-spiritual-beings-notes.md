## The two sections’ argument

The first lecture begins with the relation between a physical field of action and the spiritual impulses that can work within it. It then treats spiritual science as a schooling of the soul: interests and habits of attention can change, even before formal occult training. Steiner expressly rejects substituting sentimental declarations of love for this education. His examples move from blue, green, white and an octave to devotion, creative thought, matter and the agreement of wish with reason. The phrase “moral perception” encompasses these inward qualities; it must not be taught only as judging behaviour good or bad.

This movement introduces an etheric account of nature. The individual’s life body is unitary; the etheric world of nature is a plurality. The first class has definite outlines in earthly and especially metallic surroundings. The second transforms in watery phenomena and, in Steiner’s account, draws the first shoots out of the ground from above. He calls the reporting empirical because occultly observed particulars precede theoretical synthesis. That is his epistemic claim, not proof of modern experimental verification. Nor is his description of what physical science recognizes a reliable summary of present botany.

The second lecture completes the four classes. Air spirits flash and vanish and are associated with maturation and fading. Fire spirits guard the seed across generations and transform warmth into the living heat required by the germ. The account is not a simple one-to-one modern crop-stage chart: maturation appears in the air description as well as seed development in the fire description. The editorial names help recall the groups but do not replace their asserted forms and functions.

| Nature class in the lecture | Elemental setting | Asserted form/work | Common name in editorial synopsis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First | Earth, solid and metallic substance | Definite outlines; beings behind earthly substance | Gnomes |
| Second | Water, mist, spray, clouds and rain | Continuing metamorphosis; drawing forth shoots | Undines |
| Third | Air, relatively free of moisture, with sunlight and warmth | Flashing/vanishing; maturation and fading | Sylphs |
| Fourth | Fire/heat | Protecting the germ across generations; changing surrounding warmth into living heat | Salamanders/fire spirits |

Their cooperation is the Earth’s etheric body. A different order directs the right activity at the right time and place, including regional seasonal differences. The spirits of periods or cycles of time form the Earth’s astral body and are said to regulate seasons, day/night and Earth’s rotation. Steiner describes their perception through conscious sleep and immersion in soul-like thought, feeling and will. A coherence qualification follows: memory of present-life deeds and an undiminished conscience must be preserved. His criticism of assumed famous reincarnation identities and unearned future merit must remain alongside the spiritual claims, rather than disappear from a merely inspirational retelling.

The planetary spirit then relates Earth to other heavenly bodies. The final correspondence differentiates roles:

| Earth/world in the lecture | Human counterpart in its analogy | External imprint described at the ending |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sense world | Physical body | Sense-perceptible appearance |
| Nature spirits | Etheric/life body | Forces of nature |
| Spirits of cycles of time | Astral body | Laws of nature |
| Planetary spirit | I | Purpose/meaning of nature |

Steiner calls the external imprints *maya* and assigns ultimate reality to the beings behind them. This is a strong ontological and causal account, including the causes of Earth’s rotation and its cosmic relations; it is not just an invitation to enjoy nature or a metaphor for ecological interdependence. The course can make the argument understandable while keeping its evidence and scientific status open to examination. These lectures do not supply a planting calendar, a preparation recipe or proof of an agricultural effect.

## Proposed teaching sequence and aims

Two lesson drafts follow the two actual source selections rather than inventing a third source chapter. IDs **2 and 3** are provisional course positions, not the book’s chapter numbers; the source chapters are numbered 1 and 2.

1. **Learning to perceive nature: the first spiritual beings / Aprender a perceber a natureza: os primeiros seres espirituais.** Central question: *How does Steiner connect self-education with a differentiated spiritual account of nature? / Como Steiner relaciona a autoeducação a uma explicação espiritual diferenciada da natureza?* Read the source sentence first, then trace physical action/spiritual impulse, educated feeling, the sensory examples and the unity/plurality contrast. Retrieve the human/nature contrast and definite form/metamorphosis distinction. An ungraded reflection separates a shoot observation, personal response and source claim.
2. **From nature beings to rhythms and planetary spirit / Dos seres da natureza aos ritmos e ao espírito planetário.** Central question: *How does Steiner distinguish nature beings, directing cycle spirits and the planetary spirit? / Como Steiner distingue seres da natureza, espíritos que dirigem os ciclos e espírito planetário?* Complete the elemental classes, distinguish seasonal direction from cosmic relation, and preserve memory/conscience and the maya account. Retrieve the cycle/planetary distinction and the two continuity qualifications. An ungraded reflection explains one Earth/human correspondence and a limit of the garden analogy.

Each bilingual draft contains five explanatory paragraphs, a key concept, an original example, an explicit source/example distinction, a bridge to the next selection, two objective retrieval questions with feedback and one ungraded reflection. The examples clarify distinctions; they do not simulate initiation or claim to verify elemental beings. The prose keeps the editor’s synopses separate from Steiner’s speech and the course’s own teaching.
