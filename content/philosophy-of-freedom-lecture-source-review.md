## Editorial use

Keep three kinds of statement clear: a passage read from Steiner, Brian Gray's explanation, and a learner's response. Normalize unmistakable proper names only in original explanatory prose, not in purported exact quotations. Compare translation differences with the complete book rather than reconstructing a translator's identity from a garbled name.

Retain the lecture's useful read–pause–restate–example–return method. Preserve open questions and reasoned disagreement, while grounding answers in the primary argument. A felt certainty, colorful analogy, successful exercise or enthusiastic audience response does not by itself establish the philosophical or spiritual conclusion under discussion. Diagrams should state relationships they explain; absent board drawings must not be invented as historical reproductions.
