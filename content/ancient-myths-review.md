# Ancient Myths: source and teaching review

13 September 2026

## Source

User-supplied 273-page Markdown capture of Rudolf Steiner, *Ancient Myths: Their Meaning and Connection with Evolution*, M. Cotterell translation, printed 1971. Front matter identifies seven lectures delivered at Dornach, 4–13 January 1918, from GA 180, recorded in shorthand and unrevised by the lecturer. This selection is not the entirety of GA 180. The edition introduction explicitly assumes prior anthroposophical knowledge.

Reviewed front matter, contents, seven lecture boundaries, selected opening, central and concluding passages. The capture has OCR errors (including January dates rendered as “Sth”, spelling and line breaks); dates follow the contents and the matching online edition. No full transcription cleanup or verification of every historical assertion is claimed. The final page continues Lecture VII; no missing bibliography has been invented. The book remains local and is not uploaded.

Parallel source: https://rsarchive.org/Lectures/GA180/English/SBC1971/index.html

## Curriculum

One orientation plus one thematic lesson for each lecture. References are capture-page ranges, sometimes sharing boundary pages, not printed page numbers. The full source lectures are linked for deeper reading; concise lessons do not imply their full reading can be completed in a short session.

| Course lesson | Source | Capture pages | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Reading a myth | Edition introduction; opening of I | 3–12 | Separate story, interpretation and response |
| 2 Isis, Osiris and Greek gods | I, 4 January | 10–44 | Compare narrative structures without erasing differences |
| 3 Images and language | II, 5 January | 44–81 | Distinguish an illustration of abstraction from historical proof |
| 4 The veil of Isis | III, 6 January | 81–114 | Examine knowledge imagery and technical Imagination |
| 5 Individuality and freedom | IV, 8 January | 114–148 | Distinguish origins, influences and understood reasons |
| 6 Development and lifelong learning | V, 11 January | 148–181 | Separate collective epoch claims from individual biography |
| 7 Head and heart | VI, 12 January | 181–223 | Relate understood concepts to considered action |
| 8 Education and final project | VII, 13 January | 223–273 | Create and revise an adult learning activity using two lectures |

Retellings follow Steiner's reported version; the course does not claim to independently reconstruct ancient traditions. The Egyptian/Greek/Hebrew comparison, Atlantis, declining developmental ages, cosmic physiology and political judgments remain attributed. The everyday activities illuminate distinctions without claiming to establish spiritual cognition, reincarnation or historical evolution. Freedom is compared with the existing philosophical argument, not merged into it. The head/heart discussion retains its spiritual context instead of being silently rewritten as generic emotional intelligence. The final lesson notes the political conclusion of VII rather than presenting the lecture as only educational advice.
