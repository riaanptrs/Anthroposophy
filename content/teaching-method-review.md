# Teaching-method review: from reading companions to guided inquiry

13 September 2026 · Original proposal for the existing eight bilingual courses

Implementation update: the guided sequence now covers nine courses, including the new Mystery of Temperaments companion. See [the implementation record](guided-study-implementation.md) for features and limits. The proposal below records the review before implementation.

## Recommendation

Keep the eight book-based courses, but change the learner's role inside a lesson: encounter a question, attempt an explanation, receive a useful hint, examine the text, and try again in a new situation. Brian's most useful contribution is the way he helps a learner **produce a connection**, rather than simply receive the lecturer's conclusion.

The current website already contains much of the right material. The priority is to reorganize and deepen it, not add another layer of explanation to every page. Keep a complete reading view alongside guided activities, and preserve the original course numbering and English/Portuguese pairing.

This document is an editorial proposal. It does not change or publish the lessons. The [lesson-by-lesson revision map](teaching-method-lesson-map.md) covers all 130 lessons, with direct links to their current pages.

## What this review examined

- All 130 English lesson pages across eight courses: learning intentions, opening examples, practice tasks, answer arrangements and teaching structure. The accompanying lesson-by-lesson appendix identifies an adaptation for every lesson, including orientation and synthesis pages.
- Existing Brian lecture reviews, particularly the prefaces and Chapters 3–6 and 9. These contain timestamped analysis of previously supplied transcripts. This review uses those records; it does not claim a new viewing of every recording or reconstruction of unavailable board drawings.
- The Sophia Institute sample supplied by the user. Its teaching text is credited to **Conrad Rehbach**, so it should be treated as a separate teaching reference, not evidence of Brian's authorship or presentation style.
- The existing lesson template and course source-review records. This is a teaching-design review, not a fresh verification of every historical, philosophical or medical claim. A complete Portuguese editorial review belongs to the implementation of each revised lesson.

There is no learner analytics or usability-study evidence here. Statements about likely difficulty and proposed benefits are design judgments to test with learners, not measured learning outcomes.

### Sophia Institute: compare, discuss, reconsider

The sample combines several definitions with personal reflection, a written dialogue between viewpoints, and questions about how the learner's understanding has changed. It provides submission by form or email. For our self-paced site, the useful adaptations are definition comparison, a fictional dialogue, and an opening response revisited at the end. The sample does not establish what instructor feedback students receive. We should not reproduce its assumption about the learner's spiritual beliefs or its characterization of science. [Sophia Institute sample lesson](https://www.sophiainstitute.us/ic11.html)

The broader ten-lesson outline pasted by the user suggests a separate curriculum opportunity: a short introductory route linking biography, key ideas and practical fields. Our existing courses are book companions, not that broad survey. Steiner's biography, the twelve senses, seven life processes and organizational history would need their own sourced lessons. They should not be inserted casually into unrelated book lessons. The sample's credit and duration claims do not apply to this website.

## What is already working, and what needs revision

**Keep:** ordinary examples, precise reading references, invitations to use fictional cases, creative alternatives, room for reasoned disagreement, and synthesis assignments that return to an earlier question. The Colour course already asks for a changed arrangement; the two temperament courses and Koepke course already revisit an opening case. These are foundations to strengthen.

**Change the timing of the explanation.** Many opening examples explain themselves immediately. In Philosophy of Freedom 04, the arithmetic task supplies its intermediate results and answer in the opening paragraph. A reader can arrive at the reflection without having done any thinking to reflect on. Keep an accessible “Show worked example” route, but first offer an attempt.

**Improve feedback, not merely the number of questions.** Colour 02's second answer repeats its example explanation. Several later courses use recurring questions about what the example clarifies or what a strong answer includes. Those can be useful prompts, but they do not respond to the learner's particular misunderstanding. Add contrasting answers with explanations of the missing relationship and a small retry.

**Give the source a central role.** The ordinary example can become more memorable than the book's actual argument. This is especially apparent in Luke, where practical ethical exercises sometimes connect only loosely to the lecture-specific check. Every lesson needs at least one task that requires locating or paraphrasing the relevant source, not merely offering a considerate response.

**Reduce the long supplementary detours in the main route.** Theosophy 00 has approximately 1,432 words in its main content, compared with 493 in Lesson 12, counting text inside expandable sections. Word count is not difficulty, but the orientation's multiple aims deserve separation into a quick start and optional connections. Retain necessary qualifications near the claim they qualify; move repeated cross-course commentary into optional expansions.

**Separate three sorts of assessment.** A matching task can have a correct answer within an author's classification. A reconstruction of an argument needs reasons. A personal artistic response has no prescribed feeling. Using the same answer-reveal pattern for all three obscures these differences.

**Make the study actions possible on the site.** Notebook instructions are legitimate, but the pages currently depend heavily on the learner organizing that work elsewhere. A private, optional notebook, export and a clear resume point would make sustained independent study easier to manage. Do not use “Submit” where there is no receiving teacher or submission service; Colour 13 currently uses that word.

## A shared lesson rhythm, with three different forms

Use the following as a design rhythm, not seven compulsory boxes on every page:

1. **A question worth answering:** one scene, object or source problem.
2. **Your first attempt:** predict, write two sentences, arrange relationships, draw or explain aloud.
3. **A hint if needed:** one question that helps, without giving away the whole answer.
4. **Work through it together:** a short explanation and an original diagram where the relationship needs one.
5. **Return to the reading:** locate the sentence or paragraph; distinguish its claim from our analogy.
6. **Try a changed situation:** change one condition and explain what changes in the reasoning.
7. **Look back:** revise the opening response and keep one question for later.

Offer an initial 15–25-minute guided route as a design target to test, with full source reading and art-making time stated separately. Avoid presenting a uniform completion time as established fact.

| Lesson form | Best suited to | Characteristic activity | What earns useful feedback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Concept inquiry | Theosophy; Philosophy of Freedom; difficult cosmological passages | Compare cases, build an argument, explain an analogy's reach | The relation is clear, source attribution is accurate, and the learner can transfer it. |
| Reflective case | Higher Worlds; temperaments; Koepke; ethical applications in Luke | Hear a situation, choose a question, receive more information, revise a response | The response attends to what is known and changes appropriately with new information. |
| Studio inquiry | Colour; selected drawing and curriculum lessons | Make or inspect two versions, change one feature, describe and explain | A specific artistic decision is connected with an observable arrangement and the learner's response. |

## Course-by-course decisions

### 1. Theosophy — 23 lessons

**Current method:** everyday example, conceptual explanation, reading, notebook activity, answer reveal and recurring rubric. Existing additions already connect with Brian's method, particularly Lessons 00, 01 and 18–21.

**Strength:** the stars, bell, card regrouping and memory examples make difficult terms approachable. **Weakness:** classifications and spiritual-world descriptions can become lists of finished answers. The opening lesson and later supplements can distract from the main learning question.

**Revision:** make this the foundation for learning how to inquire. Build the body/soul/spirit distinction through two people's encounter with the same event; build the sevenfold grouping one relation at a time; revisit the same everyday case after each chapter. Put the reason for the classification before a memory check. Keep the main source's distinctive spiritual claims clearly explained rather than replacing them with everyday psychology.

**First revisions:** 01, 06 and 18. **Evidence of improvement:** the learner can explain why a classification changes and apply the distinction to a new event without copying definitions.

### 2. How to Know Higher Worlds — 19 lessons

**Current method:** reflective fictional situations, modest observation activities, explicit distinctions and three answer checks. **Strength:** listening and self-observation are translated into ordinary situations without requiring personal disclosure. **Weakness:** reading about an attentive response can substitute for practising the act of noticing or reconsidering.

**Revision:** use short observation and listening sequences, with text equivalents. Record a first description, add information, and revise it. Let learners distinguish what happened, how it felt and what they inferred. Give the source's stronger account a separate, visible reading task. A fictional or analytical route remains available for every inward exercise.

**First revisions:** 02, 03 and 07. **Evidence of improvement:** the learner changes an interpretation for a stated reason, and can distinguish calm attention from passive agreement. Completion records study activity, not spiritual attainment.

### 3. The Philosophy of Freedom — 21 lessons

**Current method:** concise explanations derived substantially from the Brian reviews, practical examples and questions about conceptual distinctions. **Strength:** this has the closest source connection to the style the user likes. **Weakness:** the explanatory conclusions often arrive before the student attempts the problem; several goal lines repeat the title rather than state what the learner should be able to do.

**Revision:** use this as the main demonstration of guided philosophical dialogue. Ask, pause, reveal a hint, reconstruct, raise an objection and test another case. Give percept/concept/representation and motive/driving-force/feasibility their own clear diagrams. A learner must explain the relationship represented by an arrow; putting labels in the correct positions is insufficient.

**First revisions:** 04, 09 and 12–13. **Evidence of improvement:** the learner reconstructs an argument and answers a plausible objection in a new case. Do not confuse completing the online activity with settling the philosophical argument. The book's concluding section and synthesis remain a separate content-completion task beyond the current Chapter 14 coverage.

### 4. According to Luke — 12 lessons

**Current method:** short spiritual-reading summaries coupled with ethical everyday examples. **Strength:** text, interpretation and personal judgment are explicitly separated. **Weakness:** a good answer about care may not demonstrate understanding of the particular lecture. In Lesson 02 the volunteering example develops attentive help, while a review question asks about Buddha and Jesus: an explicit bridge is needed.

**Revision:** put a passage map at the centre: Gospel narrative, Steiner's interpretation, reader's question. Use original dialogue between a reader asking for clarification and another reconstructing the interpretation. Add clearly attributed timelines and relationship arrows in 04, 05 and 07. Follow the source work with a practical ethical question, explaining exactly which relation transfers and which does not.

**First revisions:** 02, 04 and 07. **Evidence of improvement:** the learner correctly attributes a statement and reconstructs the lecture-specific sequence before evaluating or applying it.

### 5. Colour — 14 lessons

**Current method:** looking, making, comparing, short explanations, selected digital studies and a final portfolio. **Strength:** this is already the most naturally experiential course; 02, 04, 09 and 11 invite controlled visual comparisons. **Weakness:** describing a predicted visual effect before the learner looks can lead the response; generic answer checks add little to studio inquiry.

**Revision:** turn the course into a small studio. Let the learner compare arrangements before seeing the lecturer's description, change one variable, keep a version and explain a revision. Digital controls are a preparation or alternative to pigment work, not a paint simulator. Keep written descriptions and non-colour cues available. Never score the learner on feeling the prescribed emotional effect.

**First revisions:** 02, 09 and 13. **Evidence of improvement:** a portfolio commentary names a specific change, explains its role and identifies where experience differs from expectation.

### 6. The Four Temperaments — 11 lessons

**Current method:** fictional portraits, framework maps, support-design exercises and return to an opening case. **Strength:** it attends to individuality and mixtures. **Weakness:** portraits can still invite the learner to identify a type and stop thinking.

**Revision:** reveal a person in more than one situation. Ask what information is needed before proposing support; then offer a second scene that complicates the first impression. Keep framework-learning exercises distinct from decisions about people. Adapt the indirect educational approach into choices about tasks, invitations and responses, not a personality quiz.

**First revisions:** 03, 07 and 08. **Evidence of improvement:** the learner proposes a concrete response and revises it when a new scene challenges the initial label.

### 7. Understand Your Temperament! — 13 lessons

**Current method:** chapter summaries, workplace and relationship cases, an expanded classification, and critical appendix tasks. **Strength:** it transfers the topic to adult life. **Weakness:** it can feel like a repetition of Course 6 unless Childs's distinct claims and additional applications become more visible.

**Revision:** use a continuing adult project case; compare Steiner and Childs at specific source points. In feedback and compatibility lessons, let the learner write an actual question or agreement and compare alternative versions. Use the appendices to teach how to examine an attractive explanatory model and a tension within an author's account.

**First revisions:** 03, 08 and 10–11. **Evidence of improvement:** the learner names which author's claim is being used, identifies missing information and creates an agreement that people can review.

### 8. Encountering the Self — 17 lessons

**Current method:** constructed child/adult cases, developmental interpretation, curriculum design, appendix examination and a final response plan. **Strength:** dialogue and observing the observer are built into the material. **Weakness:** adult explanations still arrive before the learner has heard enough of the child's situation.

**Revision:** use staged conversations. Let the adult learner choose or write a question, then reveal the child's next line and invite a revised interpretation. Treat branches as possible responses, not predictions of what any child will do. Combine this with hands-on curriculum design and form drawing. Historical treatment claims remain objects of source study, not practice branches.

**First revisions:** 01, 03 and 15. **Evidence of improvement:** the adult's second response changes because of something the child said, and the learner can explain a drawing correction or curriculum decision.

## Online features in priority order

| Priority | Feature | How it supports the method | Minimum useful version |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Attempt → hint → explanation → retry | Creates the pause Brian makes in a live lesson | Ordinary text input or notebook option; separate accessible buttons; no forced timer or locked reading. |
| 1 | Feedback for particular misunderstandings | Replaces the teacher's response to a common question | Two or three authored routes; each explains a distinction and gives a new example. Label these as common responses, not live assessment. |
| 1 | Source-return prompt | Keeps examples connected to the book | Exact edition/page or paragraph reference, one paraphrase task, and a link to a permitted source. |
| 1 | Before-and-after notebook | Makes revision visible | Explicit opt-in save on this device, edit, delete, export; preserve before and after responses. No account needed. |
| 2 | Original diagrams built in steps | Recreates the useful part of board work | Learner-controlled reveal; keyboard operation; complete text equivalent; full diagram available at once. |
| 2 | Case conversations and contrasting interpretations | Brings discussion into solitary study | Authored dialogue with a few branches plus an open response. New information changes the next question. |
| 2 | Resume point and short cumulative review | Makes the course feel continuous | Course/lesson progress stored locally; revisit one earlier concept in a new case every few lessons. Do not equate opening a page with mastery. |
| 3 | Optional short narrated explanations | Adds human pacing when it helps | Original narration with transcript, controls and no autoplay; a visible teaching purpose for each clip. |
| 3 | Instructor or peer feedback | Provides genuinely responsive dialogue | Only advertise submission or discussion when an actual moderator, response process and storage policy exist. |

Free-text answers do not need automatic grading in the initial version. Learners can compare their response with annotated examples and a concise rubric. A deterministic choice can receive authored feedback; a reflective response should not receive an invented judgment of understanding. No AI service is necessary for the first revision.

Local saving should be transparent: “Saved on this device” means it will not automatically follow the learner to another browser or device and could be cleared with browser data. Export is therefore part of the useful minimum. Handle unavailable storage gracefully. Keep English and Portuguese progress tied to stable lesson IDs while preserving the language of written responses.

## Three concrete pilot activities

These are original design drafts inspired by the documented teaching moves, not quotations from Brian. They are ready to use as the basis for prototype lessons.

### Pilot A: Philosophy of Freedom 04 — do the thinking, then examine it

**EN opening:** “Work out ((3 × 3) + 1) ÷ 2. Keep a note of your steps. Before opening the explanation, describe how you reached your answer.”

**PT-BR opening:** “Calcule ((3 × 3) + 1) ÷ 2. Anote os passos. Antes de abrir a explicação, descreva como chegou à sua resposta.”

**Hint / Dica:** “What result does each operation act on?” / “Sobre qual resultado cada operação atua?”

**EN explanation:** “The steps give 9, then 10, then 5. Now compare the final number with the activity that produced it. Remembering ‘5’ and reconstructing the operations are different tasks.”

**PT-BR explanation:** “Os passos produzem 9, depois 10 e, por fim, 5. Agora compare o número final com a atividade que o produziu. Lembrar ‘5’ e reconstruir as operações são tarefas diferentes.”

**Feedback:** If the response only gives 5, acknowledge the numerical answer and ask for the connection between the operations. If the answer is 9.5, show which parentheses were lost and let the learner retry. If the learner correctly says clear reasoning can use an incorrect premise, acknowledge that distinction rather than marking it as a disagreement with the exercise.

**Return to the text:** use the currently assigned Chapter 3 passage and the existing Basis markers 20–23; distinguish retrospective inquiry from an assertion that one was unconscious while reasoning.

**Transfer / Aplicação:** “A meeting starts at 9:00. The journey takes 30 minutes and you want to arrive 10 minutes early. Explain your departure time. What changes if the journey takes 40 minutes?” / “Uma reunião começa às 9h. O trajeto leva 30 minutos e você quer chegar 10 minutos antes. Explique o horário de saída. O que muda se o trajeto levar 40 minutos?”

**Check:** 8:20 changes to 8:10 because the travel-time premise changes. Ask the learner to explain the dependency. The task does not establish a claim about the brain or certify ethical freedom.

### Pilot B: Colour 02 — notice before being told

**EN opening:** “Compare two yellow studies: one has a dense centre and pale edge; the other a pale centre and dense edge. Where does your attention go? Describe a difference, or say that none is clear.”

**PT-BR opening:** “Compare dois estudos amarelos: um tem centro intenso e borda clara; o outro, centro claro e borda intensa. Para onde vai sua atenção? Descreva uma diferença ou diga que nenhuma ficou clara.”

Show the arrangements without emotional labels. Offer a text description and a paint, pencil or imagined-composition alternative. A simple reversal control changes the distribution while keeping hue, size and ground constant.

**Hint / Dica:** “What stayed the same? What changed?” / “O que permaneceu igual? O que mudou?”

After the response, reveal Steiner's proposed yellow tendency from Lecture 2 and return to its assigned reading. Ask how the learner's description relates to it. Then compare the blue arrangement. Screen appearance is not pigment mixing, and a different reported impression remains a valid observation.

**Feedback:** “Both are yellow” correctly names hue but does not yet compare distribution. “The first draws my attention to the middle” identifies a specific relationship. “I notice no difference in movement” is acceptable; invite a description of density and position, not an invented feeling.

**EN closing:** “Keep the first version and a revision. Explain one artistic decision and one thing this comparison cannot establish.”

**PT-BR closing:** “Guarde a primeira versão e uma revisão. Explique uma decisão artística e algo que esta comparação não permite estabelecer.”

### Pilot C: Encountering the Self 03 — let another voice change the answer

**EN opening:** “A child used someone else's materials without asking. What would you say first?”

**PT-BR opening:** “Uma criança usou materiais de outra pessoa sem pedir. O que você diria primeiro?”

Offer three authored responses and an open alternative: a global accusation, a neutral description followed by a question, or immediate repair without hearing the account. Explain the tradeoffs; do not assign virtue scores to the learner.

Reveal a possible next line: “I thought the box was for everyone.” / “Achei que a caixa fosse para todos.” This is one invented continuation, not a prediction of a child's response.

**Prompt / Pergunta:** “What does that change? What still needs repair?” / “O que isso muda? O que ainda precisa ser reparado?”

**Model response:** “I understand why the box looked shared. These materials belong to someone else. Let's ask what needs replacing, and then make the labels clearer.” / “Entendo por que a caixa pareceu ser de todos. Estes materiais pertencem a outra pessoa. Vamos perguntar o que precisa ser reposto e depois deixar as etiquetas mais claras.”

**Feedback:** The new information changes the explanation of intention; it does not erase the effect. A good response makes room for an adult's contribution to the confusion and an appropriate repair.

Return to the assigned Dear Parents section: identify the relationship between adult self-examination, listening and guidance. Transfer to a different case where the boundary was clear and ask which part of the response must change.

## How to judge whether the revision helps

Begin with the three pilots above. Compare the existing and revised versions with a small formative learner review; this can find confusing wording but cannot establish general effectiveness. Look at the first response, the revision and a later transfer response rather than enjoyment or completion alone.

Use four observable criteria: accurate meaning, a stated connection or reason, relevant source support, and an appropriate response to changed information. Keep the existing acceptance of reasoned disagreement. For art, assess the explanation of a decision rather than emotional conformity; for personal reflection, assess the structure of inquiry rather than private beliefs.

Before wider rollout, verify keyboard navigation, screen-reader labels and feedback announcements, mobile layout, optional storage and export, English/Portuguese equivalence, correct source attribution, and a usable complete lesson when JavaScript is unavailable. Each new interactive element must support a stated learning objective. Decorative animation is not evidence of a better teaching method.

## Implementation sequence

1. Prototype the three different activity forms in GA 4 Lesson 04, Colour Lesson 02 and Koepke Lesson 03.
2. Revise Theosophy 01 and its quick-start introduction so new learners immediately encounter the method.
3. Introduce source maps for Luke and misconception feedback in the philosophy lessons.
4. Extend case-based interactions through Higher Worlds and the two temperament courses.
5. Roll out the per-lesson changes in the appendix, preserving a distinct teaching form for each course. Build cumulative portfolios from existing opening cases instead of creating unrelated homework.

The proposed result is a course that asks the learner to look, think, make and revise, with enough guidance to continue independently. The measure is the explanation the learner can now produce, not the number of features on the page.
