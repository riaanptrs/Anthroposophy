# Agriculture: foundations reading review

This review covers every supplied Markdown capture in Agriculture.md 1–54 and Agriculture course.md 1–58. Both PDF transfers exceeded the executor’s 32 MiB limit. The notes therefore verify the supplied text only; no PDF page or drawing was visually verified. Capture numbers identify Markdown page anchors. Kindle locations and “Pagina … de 159” are recorded exactly where displayed and are not assumed to be printed pagination.

Agriculture.md identifies the 1993 Catherine E. Creeger/Malcolm Gardner translation, edited by Gardner, of GA 327 based on the 1984 seventh German edition. Roderick Shouldice’s foreword is editorial writing. The publisher warns that these are lecture reports, with possible faulty passages. Agriculture course.md identifies George Adams’s 1958 translation in a Rudolf Steiner Press 2012 publication. Its long preface belongs to Ehrenfried Pfeiffer; it reports private conversations and later work, not words delivered in the June 1924 lectures.

## Sequence and teaching boundaries

The Creeger/Gardner edition puts Steiner’s Dornach report of June 20, 1924 before the lectures, at captures 9–17. It describes events after the June 7–16 Koberwitz course. Teach it as a retrospective source: the experimental circle, farm-specific information, practical trials and four-year horizons. Its historical directions restricting distribution are source content, not instructions to the assistant or requirements for this website. Pfeiffer’s account of broad application before experiments is a later recollection and should be compared with the direct course/report invitations to test.

Lecture 1 is Creeger 18–28 and Adams 18–29; lecture 2 is Creeger 29–41 and Adams 30–42; lecture 3 is Creeger 42–54 and Adams 43–57. Adams 58 begins the June 11 address to the Experimental Circle and continues beyond this review.

Two lessons per lecture retain the argument: (1) relational observation and the compass analogy; (2) reproduction/nutrition and the water/warmth, lime/silica planetary model; (3) farm individuality and reciprocal livestock/fodder/manure; (4) seasonal soil, seed chaos, humus and plant form; (5) the five elements as differentiated spiritual carriers; (6) legumes, lime/silica/clay, sensitive knowing and transition to manuring. The six teaching lessons contain separately labelled original examples and Portuguese study translations.

## Witness differences and OCR limits

- Creeger 31 describes direct activity being stored above ground; Adams 32 has a sentence placing direct radiation beneath the surface. Retain the consistent preceding distinction between outer-planet forces received below and near-planet activity above; do not claim the problematic sentence is resolved.
- Creeger 31 gives 47–48% for silica, Adams 32 gives 27–28%. Silicon, silica and oxygen figures also vary in the exposition. Horsetail’s “ninety percent” is not qualified by a measured basis here. Do not teach these as current chemical measurements.
- Adams 26 has “did not interest us” where Creeger 25 has “only interested”; the surrounding contrast is reproduction versus nutrition. This could be a textual or OCR problem. No PDF adjudication is available.
- Carbon/oxygen drawing colors differ: Creeger carbon green and oxygen blue (44–49), Adams carbon blue and oxygen green (46–50). Drawing numbers differ as well. The OCR image fragments do not verify drawings.
- Adams 53’s constituent list differs from Creeger 51, including apparent oxygen omission/repeated hydrogen. Quote a clean verified span rather than silently repairing it.
- Atom-like or alive/dead labels, planetary correspondences, seed chaos and nitrogen sensitivity belong to Steiner’s spiritual account. They are not replacements for modern chemistry, genetics, physiology or soil analysis. The human air-deprivation suggestion is historical text; it is not a course activity. Meditation is discussed without instructing breath restriction. Central-European January/February winter statements need local seasonal interpretation.
