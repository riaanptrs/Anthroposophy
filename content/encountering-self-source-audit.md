## Identity and voices

The title page at capture 2 identifies Hermann Koepke, *Encountering the Self: Transformation & Destiny in the Ninth Year*, translated by Jesse Darrell. Capture 3 supplies SteinerBooks / Anthroposophic Press, English copyright/cataloguing particulars 1989 and ISBN 9780880102797. It identifies the translated German second edition as 1985. The publication date of the captured electronic edition is not separately stated.

Sease’s preface at 5 dates first German appearance 1983 and is signed September 1988. Koepke’s acknowledgments at 6 are dated Easter 1985; his introduction dedication at 9 is Michaelmas 1982. These dates refer to different events. Jörgen Smit writes the foreword at 7. Walter Holtzapfel signs the school-doctor contribution at 64. The upload does not identify a separate chapter translator for that contribution; the verified volume credit is Jesse Darrell.

The endnote at 72, column 1, calls the two parent conversations free representations of the transition’s essential characteristics. They are constructed teaching dialogues, rather than recorded clinical cases. Teacher, mother and father speeches are attributed within those scenes. Biographical quotations, Biblical passages, poems and selections from Steiner retain their distinct quoted voices; Koepke’s connecting prose is not silently assigned to the person he quotes.

The form-drawing appendix’s completion passage is credited by note 1 at 76 to Steiner’s *A Modern Art of Education*, Lecture IX, pp. 152–167 (1972). Reflection and later inner/outer/open examples are notes 2–3 to *Kingdom of Childhood*, Lecture 4. The English wording was checked in the supplied Koepke volume. Those original lectures and their translations were not independently inspected for this revision.

Native diagrams are readable even when Markdown is severely garbled. Diagram 1 at 46, middle column, shows 7→9⅓→11⅔→14, shaded head/trunk/limbs and arrows indicating cooperation. The surrounding prose uses approximate transitions around nine and twelve; a fixed threshold for every child is not substituted for that relationship.

## Faithful source teaching and explicit adaptations

The guides retain Koepke’s spiritual interpretation of separation, authority, destiny and the I; all seven biographical motifs; cooperating threefold development; the seven/twelve contrasts and both-directions qualification; curriculum imagery and instrument proposals; bodily, pulse/breath and medical interpretations; Christian incarnation motifs; the sapling’s theological ending; nodal correspondences; and proposed bodily effects of form drawing.

Stronger prescriptions in Peter and Dear Parents are attributed before the course explains its alternatives. These include food deprivation, forced attention/touch, gendered educational expectations, strong claims about parental accord and punishment, and consequences asserted for later life. Holtzapfel’s discussion includes both a warning to take symptoms seriously and consult a physician, and his statement that no scientific explanation/specific treatment had been found before proposing iron preparations. The guides preserve that tension without producing a treatment protocol.

The existing modest activities are labelled adaptations: listening, meaningful work, privacy, voluntary support, geometric exploration and review. A useful exercise result does not validate every spiritual, physiological, therapeutic or cosmic claim. The existing NIMH, NIH and NASA context links are retained; their earlier bounded checks are not described as new network research.
